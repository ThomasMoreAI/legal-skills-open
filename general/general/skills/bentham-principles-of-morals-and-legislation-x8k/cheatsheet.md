# Cheatsheet - Principles of Morals and Legislation

## The Principle of Utility (Fundamental Principle)
**"Nature has placed mankind under the governance of two sovereign masters, pain and pleasure."**

The principle that approves or disapproves of every action based on its tendency to:
- **Augment happiness** (pleasure) of the community
- **Diminish happiness** (pain) of the community

## The Greatest Happiness Principle
**"It is for them alone to point out what we ought to do, as well as to determine what we shall do."**

Actions are right in proportion as they tend to promote happiness; wrong as they tend to produce the reverse of happiness.

## The Four Sanctions (Sources of Pain and Pleasure)

| Sanction | Source | Description | Examples |
|----------|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Physical** | Nature | Pleasures and pains directly from physical causes | Hunger, thirst, pleasure from food |
| **Political** | State/Law | Pleasures and pains from legal sanctions | Fines, imprisonment, rewards |
| **Moral** | Public Opinion | Pleasures and pains from social approval/disapproval | Praise, blame, reputation |
| **Religious** | Divine | Pleasures and pains from divine reward/punishment | Heaven, hell, divine favor |

## The Felicific Calculus (Hedonic Calculus)
**Method to measure pleasure and pain:**

### 7 Dimensions (Circumstances):
1. **Intensity**: How strong is the pleasure/pain?
2. **Duration**: How long does it last?
3. **Certainty**: How certain is it to occur?
4. **Propinquity**: How soon will it occur?
5. **Fecundity**: Likelihood it will produce more of the same kind
6. **Purity**: Likelihood it will not be followed by the opposite kind
7. **Extent**: How many people are affected?

### Formula:
```
Value = (Intensity × Duration × Certainty × Propinquity × Fecundity × Purity) × Extent
```

## Classification of Pleasures and Pains

### By Source:
- **Senses**: Pleasures of taste, touch, sight, hearing, smell
- **Wealth**: Pleasures from possession
- **Skill**: Pleasures from exercise of abilities
- **Amity**: Pleasures of social interaction
- **Good reputation**: Pleasures from esteem
- **Power**: Pleasures from control
- **Piety**: Pleasures from religious devotion
- **Benevolence**: Pleasures from doing good
- **Malevolence**: Pleasures from others' pain
- **Memory**: Pleasures from recollection
- **Imagination**: Pleasures from mental representations
- **Expectation**: Pleasures from anticipation
- **Association**: Pleasures from connected ideas
- **Relief**: Pleasures from cessation of pain

### Simple vs. Complex:
- **Simple pleasures**: Single, uniform sensations
- **Complex pleasures**: Combinations of simple pleasures

## Motives Classification

### 14 Simple Motives:
1. **Physical**: Pain, pleasure, hunger, thirst
2. **Pecuniary**: Gain, loss of money
3. **Moral Sanction**: Good/bad reputation
4. **Religious Sanction**: Divine favor/disfavor
5. **Political Sanction**: Legal reward/punishment
6. **Sympathy**: Benevolence, malevolence
7. **Love of Power**: Authority, control
8. **Love of Approval**: Desire for esteem
9. **Self-Preservation**: Survival instinct
10. ** Sex**: Sexual desire
11. **Love of Ease**: Desire for comfort
12. **Love of Variety**: Desire for novelty
13. **Curiosity**: Desire for knowledge
14. **Emulation**: Desire to excel

## Offences Classification

### 5 Classes of Offences:
1. **Private Offences**: Against individuals (theft, assault)
2. **Semi-Public Offences**: Against groups (fraud, deceit)
3. **Public Offences**: Against the community (treason, riot)
4. **Self-Regarding Offences**: Against oneself (suicide, drunkenness)
5. **Offences Against the State**: Against government (sedition, treason)

### Properties of Punishment:
1. **Proportionality**: Must fit the crime
2. **Necessity**: Must be needed to prevent greater mischief
3. **Efficacy**: Must actually prevent the offence
4. **Profitability**: Benefits must outweigh costs

## Proportion Between Punishments and Offences

**4 Rules:**
1. **Value of punishment** must not exceed **mischief of the offence**
2. **Punishment** must be **greater than the profit** from the offence
3. **Punishment** must be **proportional to the offence**
4. **Punishment** must be **certain and swift**

## Cases Unmeet for Punishment

### When punishment is:
1. **Groundless**: No real offence committed
2. **Inefficacious**: Will not prevent the offence
3. **Unprofitable**: Costs exceed benefits
4. **Needless**: Offence can be prevented without punishment

## Key Principles Summary

### Utility Principle:
- All actions should be judged by their consequences
- Maximize happiness, minimize pain
- "The greatest happiness of the greatest number"

### Hedonic Calculus:
- Quantify pleasure and pain using 7 dimensions
- Apply to individual and social decisions
- Guide legislation and policy

### Sanctions:
- Four sources of pain and pleasure
- Physical, Political, Moral, Religious
- All influence human behavior

### Classification:
- Systematize pleasures, pains, motives, offences
- Foundation for rational legislation
- Basis for penal code reform
