# Glossario - Principles of Morals and Legislation

**A**
- **Action**: Any human behavior or conduct. For Bentham, all actions should be judged by their consequences. (Ch I)
- **Asceticism**: The principle that opposes pleasure and promotes self-denial. Bentham rejects asceticism as it leads to unnecessary pain. (Ch II)

**B**
- **Benevolence**: A motive arising from the desire to increase the happiness of others. One of the two aspects of the sympathy motive. (Ch X)

**C**
- **Calculus, Felicific**: See Hedonic Calculus. (Ch IV)
- **Calculus, Hedonic**: Method for calculating the value of pleasures and pains using 7 dimensions: Intensity, Duration, Certainty, Propinquity, Fecundity, Purity, Extent. (Ch IV)
- **Certainty**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. The probability that a pleasure or pain will occur. (Ch IV)
- **Circumstances Influencing Sensibility**: Factors that affect how strongly individuals feel pleasure or pain, including health, education, climate, etc. (Ch VI)
- **Complex Pleasure**: A pleasure composed of multiple simple pleasures combined. (Ch V)
- **Consequences**: The outcomes or effects of an action. Central to utilitarian evaluation. (Ch XII)
- **Consciousness**: The awareness of one's own actions and their consequences. Essential for moral responsibility. (Ch IX)

**D**
- **Dispositions**: General tendencies or inclinations to act in certain ways. Bentham classifies various human dispositions. (Ch XI)
- **Duration**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. How long a pleasure or pain lasts. (Ch IV)

**E**
- **Extent**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. The number of people affected by a pleasure or pain. (Ch IV)
- **Evil**: In Bentham's terminology, synonymous with pain. That which should be minimized. (Ch I)

**F**
- **Fecundity**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. The probability that a pleasure will be followed by more pleasures, or a pain by more pains. (Ch IV)
- **Felicific Calculus**: See Hedonic Calculus. (Ch IV)

**G**
- **Good**: In Bentham's terminology, synonymous with pleasure. That which should be maximized. (Ch I)
- **Greatest Happiness Principle**: The principle that actions are right in proportion as they tend to promote happiness, wrong as they tend to produce the reverse of happiness. (Ch I)

**H**
- **Happiness**: The aggregate of all pleasures. The goal of human action according to utilitarianism. (Ch I)
- **Hedonic Calculus**: Method for measuring pleasure and pain to determine which actions produce the greatest happiness. (Ch IV)

**I**
- **Intensity**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. The strength or force of a pleasure or pain. (Ch IV)
- **Intentionality**: The quality of being done intentionally or willfully. Important for determining moral responsibility and appropriate punishment. (Ch VIII)

**J**
- **Jurisprudence**: The science or philosophy of law. Bentham distinguishes between civil and penal jurisprudence. (Ch XVII)

**L**
- **Legislation**: The art or process of making laws. Should be guided by the principle of utility. (Ch I)

**M**
- **Malevolence**: A motive arising from the desire to decrease the happiness of others. The negative aspect of the sympathy motive. (Ch X)
- **Mischief**: Harm or evil caused by an action. Basis for determining appropriate punishment. (Ch XII, XIII)
- **Motive**: That which induces a person to act. Bentham identifies 14 simple motives. (Ch X)
- **Morals**: Principles concerning the distinction between right and wrong or good and bad behavior. (Ch I)

**O**
- **Offence**: An act that causes mischief or harm. Bentham classifies offences into 5 classes. (Ch XII, XIII, XIV, XV, XVI)

**P**
- **Pain**: Physical or mental suffering. That which should be minimized according to utilitarianism. (Ch I)
- **Pleasure**: Physical or mental enjoyment. That which should be maximized according to utilitarianism. (Ch I)
- **Political Sanction**: Pleasures and pains derived from the operations of law. One of the four sanctions. (Ch III)
- **Preface**: Bentham's introduction explaining the purpose and scope of the work. (Preface)
- **Private Ethics**: Individual morality, as opposed to legislation which deals with social behavior. (Ch XVII)
- **Propinquity**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. The nearness or remoteness of a pleasure or pain in time. (Ch IV)
- **Punishment**: Pain inflicted by political authority for an offence. Should be proportional to the offence. (Ch XIII, XIV, XV)
- **Purity**: One of the 7 circumstances of the Hedonic Calculus. The probability that a pleasure will not be followed by pain, or vice versa. (Ch IV)

**R**
- **Religious Sanction**: Pleasures and pains derived from the belief in a divine being. One of the four sanctions. (Ch III)

**S**
- **Sanctions**: The four sources of pain and pleasure that govern human behavior: Physical, Political, Moral, Religious. (Ch III)
- **Sensibility**: The capacity to feel pleasure and pain. Varies among individuals. (Ch VI)
- **Simple Pleasure**: A pleasure that is uniform and not composed of other pleasures. (Ch V)

**T**
- **Theology**: The study of the nature of God and religious belief. Bentham is critical of theological principles that oppose utility. (Ch II)

**U**
- **Utility**: The principle that approves or disapproves of every action according to its tendency to augment or diminish the happiness of the party whose interest is in question. The fundamental principle of Bentham's philosophy. (Ch I)
- **Utilitarianism**: The ethical theory that the right action is that which produces the greatest happiness for the greatest number. Founded by Bentham. (Ch I)

**V**
- **Value of a Lot of Pleasure or Pain**: The aggregate measure of pleasure or pain calculated using the Hedonic Calculus. (Ch IV)
- **Virtue**: For Bentham, actions that produce happiness are virtuous, regardless of traditional moral categories. (Ch II)

**Termini correlati e autori**
- **Bentham, Jeremy** (1748-1832): Filosofo inglese, fondatore dell'utilitarismo. Autore di "An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation". (Tutti i capitoli)
- **Mill, John Stuart** (1806-1873): Filosofo inglese, discepolo di Bentham, autore di "Utilitarianism" (1863). Sviluppò ulteriormente le teorie benthamiane.
- **Principe di Utilità**: Il principio fondamentale che approva o disapprova le azioni in base alla loro tendenza ad aumentare o diminuire la felicità.
- **Utilitarismo**: La teoria etica fondata da Bentham secondo cui le azioni sono giuste nella misura in cui tendono a promovere la felicità.
- **Calcolo Edonistico**: Metodo per misurare il valore delle pene e dei piaceri proposto da Bentham.
- **Sanzioni**: Le quattro fonti di dolore e piacere che governano il comportamento umano: Fisiche, Politiche, Morali, Religiose.

**Termini giuridici**
- **Codice Penale**: Sistema di leggi che definiscono i reati e le pene corrispondenti. Bentham propose riforme basate sul principio di utilità.
- **Giurisprudenza**: La scienza del diritto. Bentham la divide in civile e penale.
- **Reato**: Azione che causa danno o disagio. Bentham classifica i reati in 5 classi.
- **Pena**: Dolore inflitto dall'autorità politica per un reato. Deve essere proporzionale al reato.
