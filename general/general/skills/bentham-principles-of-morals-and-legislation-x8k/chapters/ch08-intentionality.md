# Chapter VIII: Of Intentionality

## Core Idea
Intentionality determines whether an act or its consequences were willed by the agent. Bentham distinguishes multiple dimensions of intentionality: direct vs oblique, ultimate vs mediate, exclusive vs inexclusive, conjunctive vs disjunctive vs indiscriminate. These distinctions are crucial for assigning moral and legal responsibility.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Intentionality Taxonomy**: Six dimensions for analyzing intention
  1. **Object of Intention**: Act itself vs consequences of the act
  2. **Directness**: Directly intentional vs obliquely/collaterally intentional
  3. **Ultimacy**: Ultimately intentional vs mediately intentional
  4. **Exclusivity**: Exclusively intentional vs inexclusively intentional
  5. **Conjunction**: Conjunctively intentional (both A and B) vs disjunctively (A or B) vs indiscriminately (A or B or both)
  6. **Preference**: Disjunctive intention with vs without preference
- **Chain of Motives**: Series of linked intentions where each links to the next as cause

## Key Concepts
- **Intentional Act**: An act that was the object of intention
- **Intentional Consequences**: Consequences that were intended, regardless of act intentionality
- **Unintentional**: Any article (act or consequence) not the object of intention
- **Directly Intentional**: When prospect of the consequence was a link in the causal chain determining the act
- **Obliquely Intentional**: When consequence was contemplated but not a determining link
- **Ultimately Intentional**: When the consequence is the final link in the chain of motives
- **Mediately Intentional**: When there's another incident that forms a subsequent link
- **Exclusively Intentional**: When only that specific incident would satisfy the intention
- **Inexclusively Intentional**: When other incidents also share in determining the will
- **Conjunctively Intentional**: Intention to produce both of two incidents
- **Disjunctively Intentional**: Intention to produce either one or the other
- **Indiscriminately Intentional**: Intention indifferently to produce either, or both

## Mental Models
- **Trace Causal Chains**: Map how intentions link through chains of motives to actions
- **Distinguish Direct from Oblique**: Direct intentions drive action; oblique are side effects
- **Separate Intention from Act**: Act can be intentional without consequences being so, and vice versa
- **Consider All Six Dimensions**: Each dimension adds precision to intentionality analysis
- **Use William II Example**: Concrete case illustrating all intentionality distinctions

## Anti-patterns
- **Avoid Conflating Intention with Consequences**: Consequences don't determine intention; intention determines consequences
- **Don't Assume Intentional = Ultimate**: Direct intentions can be mediate, not ultimate
- **Avoid Overlooking Unintentional Stages**: An act can be unintentional at some stages but intentional at others
- **Don't Equate Good Intention with Good Outcome**: Intention goodness depends on consequences or motives, not the intention itself
- **Avoid Ambiguous Intentionality Talk**: Specify which dimension of intentionality is being discussed

## Key Takeaways
1. Intention can regard either the act itself or its consequences, or both
2. Consequences can be intentional even if the act isn't fully intentional throughout
3. Directly intentional consequences are part of the causal chain; oblique are not
4. Ultimately intentional = final in chain; mediately = intermediate in chain
5. Exclusively intentional = only one outcome; inexclusively = multiple possible outcomes
6. Conjunctive = both intended; disjunctive = either/or; indiscriminate = either/or/both indifferently
7. Good/bad intention refers either to consequences (consciousness) or motives (motives)
