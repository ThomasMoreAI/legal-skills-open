# Chapter I: Of the Principle of Utility

## Core Idea
The principle of utility is the foundation of moral and legislative reasoning: actions are right if they tend to promote happiness, wrong if they tend to produce the reverse of happiness. It is the sole sovereign master of human actions, unavoidable and universal.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Principle of Utility**: The principle that approves or disapproves of every action according to its tendency to augment or diminish happiness
- **Law of Utility**: A hypothetical dictate by which actions conformable to utility can be measured
- **Utility Test**: A 10-step self-examination to determine if one truly accepts the principle of utility

## Key Concepts
- **Utility**: That property in any object which tends to produce benefit, advantage, pleasure, good, or happiness
- **Interest of the Community**: The sum of the interests of the several members who compose it
- **Interest of the Individual**: That which tends to add to the sum total of pleasures or diminish the sum total of pains
- **Conformable to Utility**: When an action's tendency to augment happiness is greater than its tendency to diminish it
- **Partizan of Utility**: One whose approbation or disapprobation is determined by and proportioned to an action's tendency to affect community happiness

## Mental Models
- **Use Pain-Pleasure Sovereignty**: Recognize that pain and pleasure govern all human thought, speech, and action
- **Think Community as Sum of Individuals**: The community's interest is literally the sum of its members' interests
- **Apply Utility as Foundation**: Build all moral and legal systems on the principle that happiness promotion is the standard
- **Test Principles for Consistency**: If opposing the principle of utility, examine whether your alternative is intelligible or merely caprice

## Anti-patterns
- **Avoid Metaphor in Moral Science**: Moral science improves through reason, not declamation or metaphor
- **Don't Assume Utility Needs Proof**: The principle of utility, like first principles, cannot be proved but serves as the foundation for all other proofs
- **Avoid Despotism**: Judging all actions by one's own sentiment alone is despotical
- **Avoid Anarchy**: Allowing each person's sentiment to be its own standard leads to contradiction and ends all argument
- **Don't Compound Principles**: Mixing utility with other principles without clear boundaries creates inconsistency

## Key Takeaways
1. The principle of utility recognizes human subjection to pain and pleasure as the foundation for building felicity through reason and law
2. An action is conformable to utility when its net effect is to increase community happiness
3. Community interest is not a separate entity but the aggregate of individual interests
4. Words like "ought", "right", and "wrong" have meaning only when interpreted through the principle of utility
5. All attempts to combat utility ultimately borrow from it, proving it misapplied rather than wrong
6. The 10-step utility test provides a framework for resolving doubts about the principle's validity
