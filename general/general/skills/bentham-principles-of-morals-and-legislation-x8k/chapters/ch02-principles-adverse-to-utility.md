# Chapter II: Of Principles Adverse to That of Utility

## Core Idea
Bentham identifies and refutes principles that oppose utility: asceticism (rejection of pleasure) and sympathy/antipathy (subjective approval based on personal sentiment). He demonstrates that these alternatives are either inconsistent, impractical, or reduce to utility itself.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Principle of Asceticism**: Approves actions that diminish happiness and disapproves actions that augment it - the inverse of utility
- **Principle of Sympathy and Antipathy**: Approves/disapproves based on personal inclination without reference to consequences
- **Theological Principle**: Purportedly based on God's will, but shown to be merely asceticism or sympathy/antipathy in disguise

## Key Concepts
- **Ascetic Moralists**: Philosophers who reject pleasure, motivated by hope of honor and reputation
- **Ascetic Religionists**: Religious figures who reject pleasure, motivated by fear of divine punishment
- **Presumptive Will of God**: The idea that God's will can be inferred from conformity to other principles
- **Principle in Name Only**: Sympathy/antipathy is negative - it lacks external grounding and merely reflects internal sentiment
- **Regulation of Antipathy**: Antipathy must always be regulated by utility to prevent mischief

## Mental Models
- **Test Principles by Application**: To refute a principle, show it differs from utility in any case
- **Trace Motives to Sources**: Asceticism stems from either philosophical pride or superstitious fear
- **Distinguish Motive from Ground**: The cause of an action (motive) is different from its justification (ground)
- **Follow Principles to Extremes**: Asceticism cannot be consistently pursued - widespread adoption would create hell
- **Unmask Disguised Principles**: Theological principle is always asceticism or sympathy/antipathy in another form

## Anti-patterns
- **Avoid Reprobating Pleasure as Such**: Even the vilest pleasures are only wrong when outweighed by resulting pains
- **Don't Confuse Motive with Justification**: Because antipathy causes good actions doesn't make it a right ground for action
- **Avoid Principle in Words Only**: A principle that lacks external grounding is no principle at all
- **Don't Apply Asceticism to Government**: Even asceticism's partisans haven't consistently applied it to governance
- **Avoid Severity from Sympathy**: Punishment based on antipathy leads to excessive severity for trivial offences
- **Don't Err on Side of Lenity**: Sympathy/antipathy can also be too lenient when mischief is remote or imperceptible

## Key Takeaways
1. Any principle differing from utility in any case is wrong - to state it is to confute it
2. Asceticism comes in two forms: philosophical (rejecting pleasure) and religious (courting pain)
3. Asceticism has rarely been applied to government consistently - most applications are either security measures or misapplications of utility
4. Sympathy/antipathy is not a true principle but the negation of all principle - it uses sentiment as its own justification
5. Sympathy/antipathy often coincides with utility by accident, but frequently errs toward severity
6. The theological principle is never distinct - it's always asceticism or sympathy/antipathy disguised as divine will
7. Antipathy must always be regulated by utility to prevent harmful effects
