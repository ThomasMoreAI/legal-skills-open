# Chapter III: Of the Four Sanctions or Sources of Pain and Pleasure

## Core Idea
All human actions are ultimately governed by four sanctions (sources of pleasure and pain): physical, political, moral, and religious. These sanctions are the efficient causes that enforce laws and rules of conduct, with the physical sanction serving as the foundation for the others.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four Sanctions Framework**: The complete taxonomy of pleasure and pain sources that motivate human behavior
  - **Physical Sanction**: Natural consequences from the ordinary course of nature
  - **Political Sanction**: Punishments or rewards administered by law/legal authorities
  - **Moral/Popular Sanction**: Social approval or disapproval from the community
  - **Religious Sanction**: Divine rewards or punishments, present or future

## Key Concepts
- **Sanction**: Any source of pleasure or pain capable of giving binding force to a law or rule of conduct
- **Calamity**: Pain from physical sanction in natural course of events
- **Punishment**: Pain intentionally inflicted, classified by which sanction it comes from
- **Binding Force**: The efficacy of sanctions in compelling compliance with rules
- **Future Life Pleasures/Pains**: Religious sanction elements that are matters of expectation only

## Mental Models
- **Use Sanctions as Efficient Causes**: View pleasure and pain not just as ends but as means for motivating behavior
- **Think of Physical as Foundation**: The physical sanction underlies all others - they operate through natural powers
- **Map Pain Sources**: Any pain can be categorized by its sanction source (accident, law, social, divine)
- **Consider Sanction Interaction**: Political magistrates must account for moral and religious sanctions in their calculations
- **Distinguish Present vs Future**: Religious sanctions alone can reference future life consequences

## Anti-patterns
- **Avoid Ignoring Moral Forces**: Political actors who neglect moral and religious sanctions will err in their calculations
- **Don't Confuse Sanction with End**: Sanctions are means/efficient causes, not final causes or ends
- **Avoid Overlooking Physical Basis**: Even political/moral/religious pains operate through physical mechanisms
- **Don't Assume Religious Knowability**: Future life pleasures/pains are unknowable in quality - we can only form unliquidated ideas

## Key Takeaways
1. All human motivation reduces to pleasure and pain from four sources (sanctions)
2. Physical sanction operates independently; political, moral, and religious operate through it
3. Physical sanction is the groundwork included within political, moral, and religious sanctions
4. The four sanctions provide a comprehensive taxonomy for understanding all human motivation
5. Political magistrates must consider moral and religious sanctions as allies or rivals in their work
6. Religious sanctions reference both present-life and future-life consequences
7. The framework helps identify and categorize the sources of all human pleasures and pains
