# Case Citations - Bluebook Rule 10

Complete guidance for citing cases in law review footnotes per Bluebook 22nd Edition. Page cites
below are **22e** page ranges taken from the rule headings of the official Bluebook Online 22nd
edition.

## Basic Form (Rule 10.1) (22e pp. 100-01)

```
[Case Name], [Volume] [Reporter] [First Page], [Pinpoint] ([Court] [Year]).
```

**Example:**
```
Ward v. Reddy, 727 F. Supp. 1407, 1412 (D. Mass. 1990).
```

Rule 10.1 in the 22e is a table of forms keyed to the case's posture. The forms that recur most:

| Posture | Form |
|---------|------|
| Published decision | `Ward v. Reddy, 727 F. Supp. 1407, 1412 (D. Mass. 1990).` |
| Filed but not decided | `Hoshijima v. Jensen, No. 90-345 (D. Mass. filed Sep. 18, 1990).` |
| Unpublished decision | `Kitchens v. Grohman, No. 90-347, slip op. at 6 (D. Mass. Dec. 4, 1990).` |
| In an electronic database | `Yee v. Kitchens, No. 90-349, 1990 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 20837, at *6-8, *10 (D. Mass. Dec. 4, 1990).` |
| Appeal docketed | `Yee v. Grohman, 727 F. Supp. 1407 (D. Mass. 1990), appeal docketed, No. 90-567 (1st Cir. Dec. 20, 1990).` |
| Brief, record, or appendix | `Brief for Appellant at 7, Kitchens v. Scotten, 925 F.2d 314 (1st Cir. 1991) (No. 90-568).` |
| Lower-court disposition with subsequent history | `Haber v. Yee, 727 F. Supp. 1407, 1412 (D. Mass. 1990), aff'd, 925 F.2d 314 (1st Cir. 1991).` |

## Case Names (Rule 10.2) (22e pp. 101-07)

Rule 10.2.1 applies to **every** case name, in text or in citations. Rule 10.2.2 abbreviates
further, and applies only in citations. So the same case is `Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen` in a
textual sentence and `S. Pac. Co. v. Jensen` in a citation.

### Party Names (10.2.1(a))

Use the case name as it appears at the **beginning of the opinion in the cited reporter**. If no
name appears there, use a popular name or cite as "Judgment of <date>."

**Always retain in full the first word in each party's name, including a relator.**

**Omit:**
- All parties other than the first listed on each side — **but do not omit the first-listed
  relator, and do not omit any portion of a partnership name** (`Massachusetts ex rel. Alison v.
  Pauly`; `Eisen v. Spradlin, Lincoln & Amorosi`)
- All but the first listed action in a consolidation (`Shelley v. Kraemer`, not `Shelley v.
  Kraemer, McGhee v. Sipes`)
- Words indicating multiple parties ("et al.") and alternative names for the first-listed party
  ("d/b/a . . .")
- In rem: all but the first-listed item or group (`In re Three Pink Cadillacs`)
- "The" as the first word of a party's name — **except** as part of the name of the object of an
  in rem action (`In re The Clinton Bridge`), where "The King" or "The Queen" is a party, and in
  an established popular name (`The Civil Rights Cases`, though "the" is lowercased when the case
  is referred to textually)
- Given names of individuals — **subject to the exceptions in 10.2.1(g) below**
- Descriptive terms for a party already named — "administrator," "appellee," "executor,"
  "licensee," "trustee" (`Burns v. McMillen`, not `Burns v. McMillen, Administrator`; but
  `Trustees of Dartmouth College v. Woodward`)

Where real property is a party, use its common street address if available (`United States v. 6109
Grubb Road`).

**Bankruptcy and similar cases (10.2.1(a)).** Where both an adversary and a nonadversary name
appear at the beginning of the opinion, cite the adversary name first, then the nonadversary name
in parentheses, with the procedural phrase inside:
```
Wallingford's, Inc. v. Waning (In re Waning), 120 B.R. 607, 611 (Bankr. D. Me. 1990).
State v. Powers (In re Interest of Powers), 493 N.W.2d 167 (Neb. 1992).
```

### Abbreviations in Case Names (10.2.1(c), 10.2.2; Table T6)

The eight words abbreviated **in text as well as in citations**:

- & (for "and")
- Ass'n (Association)
- Bros. (Brothers)
- Co. (Company)
- Corp. (Corporation)
- Inc. (Incorporated)
- Ltd. (Limited)
- No. (Number)

**In textual sentences** (main text or footnote text): abbreviate only widely known acronyms under
rule 6.1(b) **and those eight words** — but if one of the eight *begins* a party's name, do not
abbreviate it (`Philadelphia Electric Co. v. Hirsch`, not `PECO v. Hirsch`; but `NAACP v.
Kaminski`).

**In citations (10.2.2):** always abbreviate any word listed in T6, **even if it is the first word
in a party's name**, unless the word is part of a state, country, or other geographical unit that
is the entire name of the party:
```
S. Consol. R.R. v. Consol. Transp. Co.        In re Acad. Answering Serv., Inc.
McGaugh v. Comm'r                             But: South Dakota v. Dole  (not S. Dakota v. Dole)
```
Abbreviate states, countries, and other geographical units per **Table T10** — including "United
States" — unless the geographical unit is the entire name of the party:
```
LeBeau v. Univ. of Md.          Ctr. for Nat'l Sec. Stud. v. U.S. Dep't of Just.
But: Staub v. District of Columbia    Alvarez-Machain v. United States    Fritz v. County of Los Angeles
```
Abbreviate other words of eight letters or more if substantial space is saved and the result is
unambiguous. When the textual sentence gives the case name in full, the case name may be omitted
from the citation.

### Special Parties

| Party | Citation Form |
|-------|---------------|
| United States, standing alone | `United States` — omit "of America" (10.2.1(f)); do **not** abbreviate, because the geographical unit is the party's entire name (10.2.2) |
| United States as part of a longer name | `U.S.` per T10 (`U.S. Dep't of Just.`) (10.2.2) |
| State as party (10.2.1(f)) | Omit "State of," "Commonwealth of," "People of" — **except** when citing decisions of that state's own courts, where "State," "Commonwealth," or "People" alone is retained (`Blystone v. Pennsylvania` in the U.S. Reports; `Commonwealth v. Ferrone` in a Pa. court). Which of the three you use follows the reporter, not preference. |
| Commissioner of Internal Revenue (10.2.1(j)) | "Commissioner" in text; "Comm'r" in citations |
| Relator (10.2.1(a)) | **Retain** the first-listed relator; do not omit it |
| Unions (10.2.1(i)) | Cite exactly as given in the official reporter, except: cite only the smallest unit; omit all craft or industry designations after the first full one; a widely recognized abbreviation (e.g., UAW) may be used; omit all prepositional phrases of location, including national ones |

### Geographical Terms (10.2.1(f))

- Omit "City of," "County of," "Village of," "Township of" and like expressions **unless the
  expression begins a party name** (`Mayor of New York v. Clinton`; but `Butts v. City of New
  York`).
- Omit prepositional phrases of location not following "City" or the like — **unless** the
  omission would leave only one word in a party's name, or **the location is part of the full name
  of a business or similar entity**:
  ```
  Surrick v. Board of Wardens          (not "of the Port of Philadelphia")
  Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey   (not "Planned Parenthood v. Casey")
  Shapiro v. Bank of Harrisburg        Eimers v. Mutual of Omaha
  ```
- Retain all geographical designations **not** introduced by a preposition (`Billman v. Indiana
  Department of Corrections`).
- Omit all geographical designations that **follow a comma** (`City of Arlington v. FCC`, not
  `City of Arlington, Texas v. FCC`).
- Include designations of national or larger areas, except in union names.

### Given Names (10.2.1(g))

Generally omit given names or initials — **but not** in names of business firms, and not where a
party's surname is abbreviated (`Tanya Bartucz, Inc. v. Virginia J. Wise & Co.`; `Linda R.S. v.
Richard D.`). Further:

- Do not omit any part of a surname made up of more than one word (`Van der Velt v. Standing
  Horse`; `Abdul Ghani v. Subedar Shoedar Khan`).
- Retain given names that **follow** a surname — so retain the full name where it is entirely in a
  language putting the surname first, such as Chinese, Korean, or Vietnamese (`Yao Zhen Guang v.
  Yeh Zhi An`; `Chow v. Ha Quang Jin`, not `Timothy Chow v. Ha Quang Jin`).
- For names of Spanish or Portuguese derivation, cite the surname and all names following (`Ortega
  y Gasset v. Alcala de Larosa`).

### Business Firm Designations (10.2.1(h))

Omit "Inc.," "Ltd.," "L.L.C.," "N.A.," "F.S.B.," and similar terms **if the name also contains a
word such as "Ass'n," "Bros.," "Co.," "Corp.," "Ins.," or "R.R." clearly indicating a business
firm**. The retained word is the business-type word; the dropped word is the corporate-form word:
```
Wis. Packing Co. v. Ind. Refrigerator Lines, Inc.
Not: Wis. Packing Co., Inc. v. Ind. Refrigerator Lines, Inc.
```
Read this narrowly: omit only where the name could not possibly be mistaken for some other entity,
such as a natural person.

### Procedural Phrases (10.2.1(b))

| Phrase | What it abbreviates |
|--------|---------------------|
| *In re* | "in the matter of," "petition of," "application of," and similar expressions |
| *Ex rel.* | "on the relation of," "for the use of," "on behalf of," "as next friend of," and similar expressions |
| *Ex parte* | (retained as given) |

Omit all procedural phrases except the first. **When adversary parties are named, omit all
procedural phrases except "ex rel."** (`Gorman v. Bruh`, not `In re Gorman v. Bruh`; but
`Massachusetts ex rel. Kennedy v. Armbruster`).

Include introductory or descriptive phrases such as "Accounting of," "Estate of," and "Will of"
(`In re Will of Holt`; `Estate of Haas v. Commissioner`).

**Procedural phrases are always italicized, regardless of whether the rest of the case name is.**

**Example:**
```
In re Gault, 387 U.S. 1 (1967).
```

### Common Names Different from the Reporter Name (10.2.1(k))

For a case **not known** by the reporter name, substitute the common name entirely, or give it
parenthetically in the same typeface as the reporter case name:
```
The Brig Amy Warwick (The Prize Cases), 67 U.S. (2 Black) 635 (1863).
The Prize Cases, 67 U.S. (2 Black) 635 (1863).
```
For a case **known** by the reporter name but also by a short common name, the common name may not
replace the reporter name in a full citation, but may be given parenthetically in italics:
```
Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (Steel Seizure), 343 U.S. 579 (1952).
United States v. U.S. District Court (Keith), 407 U.S. 297 (1972).
Hamdi v. Rumsfeld (Hamdi III), 316 F.3d 450 (4th Cir. 2003).
```
The identifier is given only in the primary citation; once given, it alone may serve as the case
name thereafter, even where a full citation would otherwise be required. The parentheses
themselves are never italicized (rule 2.1(f)).

## Reporters and Other Sources (Rule 10.3) (22e pp. 108-10)

### Federal Courts

| Court | Reporter | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Supreme Court | U.S. | 347 U.S. 483 |
| Circuit Courts | F., F.2d, F.3d, F.4th | 500 F.3d 123 |
| District Courts | F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, F. Supp. 3d | 100 F. Supp. 3d 456 |

**Supreme Court source hierarchy (T1.1, 22e pp. 257-60):** "Cite to U.S., if therein; otherwise,
cite to S. Ct., L. Ed., or U.S.L.W., in that order of preference." U.S.L.W. is the fourth rung, not
a stopping point after L. Ed. Within the U.S. Reports, the editor-named volumes are Dallas
(1790-1800), Cranch (1801-1815), Wheaton (1816-1827), Peters (1828-1842), Howard (1843-1860), Black
(1861-1862), and Wallace (1863-1874); 91 U.S. to date carries no editor name.

**Circuit Justices in chambers (T1.1):** "Cite to U.S., if therein; otherwise, cite to S. Ct., Rapp,
L. Ed., or U.S.L.W., if therein, in that order of preference." Rapp appears in this hierarchy and
nowhere in the Court's own; it covers 1925-date. Some Circuit Justice decisions sit in other
reporters and are cited there with the `Circuit Justice` parenthetical: `Halperin v. Kissinger, 807
F.2d 180 (D.C. Cir. 1986) (Scalia, Circuit Justice).`

**Other federal courts (T1.1), where the reporter choice is not obvious:**

- **Courts of appeals** — F., F.2d, F.3d, or F.4th, if therein. The Federal Appendix (`F. App'x`,
  2001-date) is listed under the same court.
- **Circuit courts, abolished 1912** — F. (1880-1912) or F. Cas. (1789-1880). **Citations to F. Cas.
  give the case number parenthetically:** `Oelrich v. Pittsburgh, 18 F. Cas. 598 (C.C.W.D. Pa. 1859)
  (No. 10,444).`
- **District courts** — for cases after 1932, F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, F. Supp. 3d, F.R.D., or B.R., if
  therein; otherwise Fed. R. Serv., Fed. R. Serv. 2d, or Fed. R. Serv. 3d. For prior cases, F., F.2d,
  or F. Cas. (again with the case number parenthetically: `Ex parte McKean, 16 F. Cas. 186 (E.D. Va.
  1878) (No. 8,848).`).
- **Bankruptcy courts and bankruptcy appellate panels** — B.R., if therein; otherwise a service
  (rule 19).
- **J.P.M.L.** — F. Supp., F. Supp. 2d, or F. Supp. 3d.
- **Tax Court** — T.C. or B.T.A., if therein; otherwise T.C.M. (CCH), T.C.M. (P-H), T.C.M. (RIA), or
  B.T.A.M. (P-H).

### Which Source to Cite (10.3.1)

**In law review pieces and ordinary legal memoranda, cite the relevant regional reporter if the
decision is found there:**
```
Swedloff v. Phila. Transp. Co., 187 A.2d 152 (Pa. 1963).
```
If the decision is available as an **official public domain (medium-neutral) citation, that
citation must be provided**, plus a parallel citation to the regional reporter if available.

If the decision is in neither, cite the other sources indicated in Table T1. If it is in no
official or preferred unofficial reporter and has no public domain citation, cite, **in this order
of preference**: another unofficial reporter, a widely used computer database (rule 18.4), a
service (rule 19), a slip opinion (rule 10.8.1(b)), an internet source (rule 18.2.2), a newspaper
(rule 16.6):
```
United States v. Carlisle, No. 90-2465SI, 1991 U.S. App. LEXIS 5863, at *3 (8th Cir. Apr. 10, 1991) (per curiam).
State v. McArthur, No. C4-99-502 (Minn. Ct. App. Sep. 28, 1999), https://mn.gov/law-library-stat/archive/ctapun/9909/502.htm.
```

### Parallel Citations (10.3.1(a))

Required in **documents submitted to state courts**, where local rules govern and commonly require
the official state reporter followed by a parallel regional citation. Those local rules, not Table
T1, control state court filings. See Bluepages B10.1.3 and Bluepages table BT2.2.

For law reviews, do **not** default to an official-plus-regional pair; cite the regional reporter
(10.3.1(b)). But a parallel citation is required in more than one situation, so do not read that as
"law reviews never give parallel cites":

- **Public domain citation available** — it "must be provided, as well as a parallel citation to
  the regional reporter, if available" (10.3.3).
- **A jurisdiction-named reprint whose pagination differs from the original editor-named
  reporter** — 10.3.2 requires parallel citations to the reprint and the original (`Wadsworth v.
  Ruggles, 23 Mass. 62, 6 Pick. 63 (1828)`). This is narrow: it is about a reprint versus its
  original, not about any two sources that happen to paginate differently. 10.3.2's other
  parallel-cite instruction is equally narrow — very early Pennsylvania decisions reported in the
  initial volumes of the U.S. Reports take the "U.S." cite plus a parallel cite to the appropriate
  lower court reporter.
- **State rules** — "Many state rules require that citations to state court decisions include a
  citation to the official state reporter, followed by a parallel citation to a regional
  reporter" (10.3.1(a)); that is a court-filing constraint, not a law-review one, but it governs
  the same case in a different document.

### Reporters (10.3.2)

Reporters are cited in roman type: volume, abbreviated reporter name (Table T1), first page of the
report. For spacing, see rule 6.1(a).

Early editor-named series that have been folded into jurisdiction-named series are cited by the
official series name and number only, dropping the editor (`Cobb v. Davenport, 32 N.J.L. 369 (Sup.
Ct. 1867)`, not `3 Vroom 369`). **But** for U.S. Supreme Court reporters through 90 U.S. (23 Wall.)
and a few early state reporters (see Table T1.3), give the editor's name and
that series' volume:
```
Green v. Biddle, 21 U.S. (8 Wheat.) 1 (1823).       Hall v. Bell, 47 Mass. (6 Met.) 431 (1843).
```
If the pagination of reprint and original differs, give parallel citations: `Wadsworth v. Ruggles,
23 Mass. 62, 6 Pick. 63 (1828).`

### Public Domain Format (10.3.3)

Where the jurisdiction's format fits the recommended form (Table T7), give: case name, year of
decision, the state's two-character postal code, the T7 court abbreviation (unless the court is the
state's highest), the sequential decision number, and a capital "U" after that number if the
decision is unpublished. Pinpoints are to **paragraph numbers**. A parallel citation to the
regional reporter must be provided if available:
```
Beck v. Beck, 1999 ME 110, ¶ 6, 733 A.2d 981, 983.
Gregory v. Class, 1998 SD 106, ¶ 3, 584 N.W.2d 873, 875.
Jones v. Fisher, 1998 OK Civ. App. 120U.
```
Where a jurisdiction's own format differs, follow it (Table T1): `State v. Brennan, 1998-NMCA-176,
¶ 7, 126 N.M. 389, 970 P.2d 161.`

## Court and Jurisdiction (Rule 10.4) (22e pp. 110-11)

Every case citation must indicate which court decided the case. Give the court name and its
geographical jurisdiction, abbreviated per Tables T1 or T2 where included there and per **Tables T7
and T10** otherwise, in the parenthetical that also carries the date. Ordinals follow rule 6.2(b).
For foreign cases, see rule 20.3 and Table T2.

### When to Include Court

**Omit court when conveyed by the reporter:**
- U.S. and S. Ct. → Supreme Court name omitted (but in U.S. Law Week the Court is indicated "U.S.")
- F.3d → Court of Appeals (give the circuit: "(9th Cir.)")
- F. Supp. 3d → District Court (give the district, **not the division**: "(S.D.N.Y.)", "C.D. Cal."
  not "C.D. Cal. E.D.")
- State: omit the jurisdiction and/or court abbreviation if the reporter title conveys it
  unambiguously (`DiLucia v. Mandelker, 493 N.Y.S.2d 769 (App. Div. 1985)`, not `(N.Y. App. Div.
  1985)`; `Dubreuil v. Witt, 80 Conn. App. 410 (2003)`, not `(App. Ct. 2003)`)

**Do not name the court where the court of decision is the highest court of the state** (`People v.
Armour, 590 N.W.2d 61 (Mich. 1999)`, not `(Mich. Sup. Ct. 1999)`). Where the highest court's
reporter shares the jurisdiction's name, neither court nor state need be given (`Bates v. Tappan,
99 Mass. 376 (1868)`).

**Do not indicate the department, district, or county** of an intermediate state court unless that
information is of particular relevance; where it is, give it as `(Sup. Ct. N.Y. Cnty. 1944)` or
`(Tex. App. 14th 2003)`.

A more detailed court designation than the rule specifies may be given if necessary.

### Court Abbreviations (10.4(a)–(b); Table T7, 22e pp. 337-39)

The specific federal forms below are given in **rule 10.4(a)** itself, not in T7; the state
highest-court omission is **10.4(b)**. T7 is an alphabetical word-to-abbreviation list of court
names (`Bankruptcy [Court, Judge]` → `Bankr.`, `District Court (federal)` → `D.`, and so on), from
which a composite abbreviation may be assembled when the full court name is not listed.

| Court | Abbreviation | Source |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| Numbered federal circuits (any year) | `2d Cir.` — not `C.C.A.2d`, not `CA2` | 10.4(a) |
| D.C. Circuit and predecessors; Federal Circuit | `D.C. Cir.`, `Fed. Cir.` | 10.4(a) |
| District Court | `D.` with the district, **not the division**: `D.N.J.`, `D.D.C.`, `C.D. Cal.` | 10.4(a); T7 (`D.`) |
| Old circuit courts (abolished 1912) | `C.C.S.D.N.Y.`, `C.C.E.D. Mo.` | 10.4(a); T7 (`C.C.`) |
| Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation | `J.P.M.L.` | 10.4(a); T7 |
| FISA Court / Court of Review | `F.I.S.C.`, `F.I.S.C.R.` | 10.4(a); T7 |
| Bankruptcy Court; bankruptcy appellate panel | `Bankr. E.D. Va.`; `B.A.P. 9th Cir.` | 10.4(a); T7 |
| State highest court | omitted entirely (see above) | 10.4(b) |
| Circuit Justice sitting below | `(Thomas, Circuit Justice)` | 10.4(a) |

Note: T7 does list `Sup. Ct.` — its entries are `Supreme Court (federal)` → `U.S.` and `Supreme
Court (other)` → `Sup. Ct.` — so `Sup. Ct.` is the abbreviation for any non-federal court named
"Supreme Court." What it is not is a designation you will normally see in a state high-court cite,
because 10.4(b) directs that the court not be named at all where the court of decision is the
highest court of the state (`People v. Armour, 590 N.W.2d 61 (Mich. 1999)`, not `(Mich. Sup. Ct.
1999)`). Where a state calls its trial court the Supreme Court, as New York does, the abbreviation
does appear: `Schiffman v. Corsi, 50 N.Y.S.2d 897 (Sup. Ct. 1944)`.

**Examples:**
```
Smith v. Jones, 500 F.3d 100 (9th Cir. 2007).
Doe v. Roe, 200 F. Supp. 3d 50 (E.D. Va. 2016).
```

## Date or Year (Rule 10.5) (22e p. 112)

### Year of Decision (10.5(a))

Give the **year of decision**; use the year of the court's term only if the year of decision is
unavailable. In ambiguous cases follow the year in the reporter's running head. Before Volume 108
of the U.S. Reports (1882), opinions generally list only the term; for those dates consult the
Supreme Court Librarian's list of dates of decisions.

### Exact Date (10.5(b))

Required for all unreported cases and for cases cited to:
- a looseleaf service
- a slip opinion
- an electronic database
- a newspaper

**Format:** (Month Day, Year)
```
Bedell v. Nessim, No. 90-567, slip op. 3458 (1st Cir. Jan. 19, 1991).
```

The exact date is **not** required for cases labeled "unpublished" that are nevertheless reported —
including the Federal Appendix: `United States v. Tando, 68 F. App'x 85 (9th Cir. 2003).`

### Pending Cases and Cases Dismissed Without Opinion (10.5(c))

Use the date or year of the most recent **major disposition**. Major dispositions are only: the
initial filing (trial or appellate — "filed," "appeal docketed," "petition for cert. filed"), oral
argument ("argued"), and, for cases dismissed without opinion, the dismissal ("dismissed").
Indicate the date's significance in the parenthetical unless it is explained elsewhere:
```
Bedell v. Nessim, No. 90-567 (1st Cir. argued Jan. 10, 1991).
```

### Multiple Decisions Within a Single Year (10.5(d))

Include the year only with the last-cited decision of that year:
```
United States v. Eller, 114 F. Supp. 284 (M.D.N.C.), rev'd, 208 F.2d 716 (4th Cir. 1953).
```
But if an exact date is required for either decision, give both dates.

## Pinpoint Citations (Rule 3.2)

**UNVERIFIED — rule 3.2 has no text in the 22e corpus.** This is not a coverage gap that the
expanded corpus closed: the page `rules/3-subdivisions/3-2-pages-footnotes-endnotes-and-graphical-
materials` is present in the corpus but its body is empty, while every neighbor (3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5)
carries full text. So rule 3.2's page-span, footnote, and graphical-material conventions — including
the `n.` / `nn.` forms and the rule on inclusive page numbers — remain unchecked. The forms below are
consistent with pinpoints as they appear throughout the 22e Rule 10 examples (e.g. `244 U.S. 205,
225-26`; `at *18-19`; `at *6-8, *10`), but rule 3.2's own text was not checked. Verify against
rule 3.2 before relying on the page-span and footnote conventions.

### Page Numbers

**Always include pinpoints** when citing for a specific proposition.

**Span of pages:**
```
Brown, 347 U.S. at 495-96.
```

**Non-consecutive pages:**
```
Brown, 347 U.S. at 495, 498, 501-02.
```

### Paragraph Numbers

For sources using paragraph numbers instead of pages (including public domain citations, 10.3.3):
```
Beck v. Beck, 1999 ME 110, ¶ 6, 733 A.2d 981, 983.
```

### Footnote Citations

Cite to footnote within a page:
```
Brown, 347 U.S. at 495 n.3.
```

Multiple footnotes:
```
Brown, 347 U.S. at 495 nn.3-5.
```

## Prior and Subsequent History (Rule 10.7) (22e pp. 114-17)

### When to Include

**Whenever a decision is cited in full, give the entire subsequent history**, but omit:

- Denials of certiorari and denials of similar discretionary appeals — **unless the decision is
  less than two years old** or the denial is particularly relevant. (The two-year clock runs on the
  age of the *decision*, not the age of the denial.)
- History on remand, and any denial of a rehearing, unless relevant to the point for which the case
  is cited
- Any disposition withdrawn by the deciding authority, such as an affirmance later reversed on
  rehearing

```
Cent. Ill. Pub. Serv. Co. v. Westervelt, 342 N.E.2d 463 (Ill. App. Ct. 1976), aff'd, 367 N.E.2d 661 (Ill. 1977).
Not: ... , cert. denied, 434 U.S. 1070 (1978).
```

**Prior history:** give it only if significant to the point for which the case is cited, or if the
disposition cited does not intelligibly describe the issues (as in a Supreme Court "mem."). Give
separate decisions of other issues, with their own history, only if relevant.

### Format (10.7.1(a))

Append history to the primary citation, introducing each decision with **italicized** explanatory
words:
```
Smith v. Jones, 500 F.3d 100 (9th Cir. 2007), aff'd, 555 U.S. 100 (2008).
Cooper v. Dupnik, 924 F.2d 1520, 1530 & n.20 (9th Cir. 1991), rev'd en banc, 963 F.2d 1220 (9th Cir. 1992).
```
- If a subsequent disposition occurred in the **same year** as the primary citation, omit the year
  from the primary citation's parenthetical.
- History of history is appended with another explanatory phrase: `Herbert v. Lando, 73 F.R.D. 387
  (S.D.N.Y.), rev'd, 568 F.2d 974 (2d Cir. 1977), rev'd, 441 U.S. 153 (1979).`
- To show both, **give prior history first**: `Kubrick v. United States, 581 F.2d 1092 (3d Cir.
  1978), aff'g 435 F. Supp. 166 (E.D. Pa. 1977), rev'd, 444 U.S. 111 (1979).`
- History follows any parenthetical information given for the primary citation (rule 1.5(b)).
- **(10.7.1(e))** Multiple dispositions by the same court are joined with an italicized "and":
  `United States v. Baxter, 492 F.2d 150 (9th Cir.), cert. dismissed, 414 U.S. 801 (1973), and cert.
  denied, 416 U.S. 940 (1974).`
- **(10.7.1(b))** Give the reason for a disposition that lacks its normal substantive significance:
  `vacated as moot,` `appeal dismissed per stipulation,`.

### Common History Abbreviations (Table T8) (22e p. 339)

T8 is "a partial list of explanatory phrases (as abbreviated)" (10.7.1). Phrases that take a direct
object — typically those ending in a preposition or a verbal "-ing" — are **not** followed by
commas; all other T8 phrases are followed by a comma, and the comma is not italicized.

| Action | Abbreviation |
|--------|--------------|
| affirmed | *aff'd,* |
| affirming | *aff'g* |
| reversed | *rev'd,* |
| certiorari denied | *cert. denied,* |
| certiorari granted | *cert. granted,* |
| vacated | *vacated,* |
| overruled by | *overruled by* |

Three phrases this file uses elsewhere are **not** T8 entries — they come from rule 10.7 itself, so
check them there rather than in the table: *abrogated by* (10.7.1(c)(ii)), *superseded by statute*
(10.7.1(c)(iii)), and *sub nom.* for a case under a different name on appeal (10.7.2(a)).

### Overruled, Abrogated, and Superseded Cases (10.7.1(c))

- **Overruled by** — a later decision of the same court (or a court with appellate jurisdiction
  over it) explicitly repudiates the earlier decision: `Nat'l League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S.
  833 (1976), overruled by Garcia v. San Antonio Metro. Transit Auth., 469 U.S. 528 (1985).` Where
  multiple decisions of that court overrule separate parts, join them with an italicized "and."
- **Abrogated by** — the later decision effectively but not explicitly overrules or departs:
  `Ahrens v. Clark, 335 U.S. 188 (1948), abrogated by Braden v. 30th Jud. Cir. Ct., 410 U.S. 484
  (1973).`
- **Superseded by statute or constitutional amendment** — the statute or amendment **must always be
  cited**, to the session laws (rule 12.4) where possible; a later case recognizing the supersession
  may be cited too, so long as it comes from the same court or one with appellate jurisdiction over
  it: `Wards Cove Packing Co. v. Atonio, 490 U.S. 642 (1989), superseded by statute, Civil Rights
  Act of 1991, Pub. L. No. 102-166, 105 Stat. 1074, as recognized in Raytheon Co. v. Hernandez, 540
  U.S. 44 (2003).`

### Cases Involving Enslaved Persons (10.7.1(d))

- Enslaved person **as a party**: parenthetical `(enslaved party)`.
- Enslaved person as the **subject** of a property or other dispute but not a named party:
  `(enslaved person at issue)`.
- Otherwise, an adequately descriptive parenthetical.

```
Dred Scott v. Sandford, 60 U.S. (19 How.) 393 (1857) (enslaved party), superseded by constitutional amendment, U.S. Const. amend. XIV.
Wall v. Wall, 30 Miss. 91 (1855) (enslaved person at issue).
```

### Different Case Name on Appeal (10.7.2)

When the name differs in **subsequent** history, give the new name introduced by *sub nom.* — even
where the difference is slight, such as a change in procedural phrases only:
```
Great W. United Corp. v. Kidwell, 577 F.2d 1256 (5th Cir. 1978), rev'd sub nom. Leroy v. Great W. United Corp., 443 U.S. 173 (1979).
```
For a different name in **prior** history: `Rederi v. Isbrandtsen Co., 342 U.S. 950 (1952) (per
curiam), aff'g by an equally divided court Isbrandtsen Co. v. United States, 96 F. Supp. 883
(S.D.N.Y. 1951).`

**Do not** give the different name when (i) the parties' names are merely reversed; (ii) the
differing citation is to a denial of certiorari or rehearing; (iii) on appeal of an administrative
action the private party's name is unchanged; or (iv) the change is merely stylistic ("State" to
"California").

## Parenthetical Information (Rule 10.6) (22e pp. 113-14)

### Weight of Authority (10.6.1)

Weight-of-authority information (en banc; in banc; 2-1 decision; mem.; per curiam; Brandeis, J.;
unpublished table decision) goes in a separate parenthetical following the date:
```
Webb v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 57 F.3d 1067 (4th Cir. 1995) (unpublished table decision).
Doe v. Roe, 400 F.3d 50 (5th Cir. 2005) (per curiam).
```
Where a case is cited for a proposition that is **not the single, clear holding of a majority**
(alternative holding, by implication, dictum, dissenting opinion, plurality opinion, holding
unclear), say so parenthetically:
```
Parker v. Randolph, 442 U.S. 62, 84 (1979) (Stevens, J., dissenting).
Garcia v. San Antonio Metro. Transit Auth., 469 U.S. 528, 570 (1985) (5-4 decision) (Powell, J., dissenting).
```
Information about related authority (rule 1.6) or history (10.7.1(a)) that can be given with an
explanatory phrase (T8) must **not** be given parenthetically: `Wersba v. Seiler, 263 F. Supp. 838,
843 (E.D. Pa. 1967), aff'd per curiam, 393 F.2d 937 (3d Cir. 1968).`

**"Mem." and "per curiam" (10.6.1(b)):** "mem." is used if and only if the court disposed of the
matter **without an opinion**; district court and other opinions denominated "memorandum decision"
are *not* designated "mem." "Per curiam" describes an opinion the court itself so denominated.

**Seriatim opinions (10.6.1(c)):** `(opinion of Lastname, J.)`.

### In-Chambers Opinions (10.6.2)

A Justice ruling in chambers on a stay, bail, or injunction application is cited with `(Lastname,
J., in chambers)`. Since Volume 396 (1969) these appear in the U.S. Reports: `Maryland v. King, 567
U.S. 1301 (2012) (Roberts, C.J., in chambers).` Pre-1969 opinions not in the U.S. Reports are cited
to Rapp: `Sacco v. Massachusetts, 1 Rapp 16 (1927) (Holmes, J., in chambers).`

### Quoting/Citing Parentheticals (10.6.3)

Where the cited case itself quotes or cites another case for the point, use a "quoting" or "citing"
parenthetical (rule 1.5(a)); inside it, typeface, pincite, and short-form rules apply as if the
quoted authority were the direct source:
```
Zadvydas v. Davis, 533 U.S. 678, 719 (2001) (Kennedy, J., dissenting) (citing Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei, 345 U.S. 206 (1953)).
```
**Only one level of recursion is required** — if a case quotes a case that quotes another, one
level suffices. Add a level only where the extra information is particularly relevant.

### Explanatory Parentheticals (rule 1.5(a))

**Form:** a present participial phrase, a quoted sentence, or a short statement appropriate in
context.
- Phrases **not** quoting the authority usually begin with a present participle and **never begin
  with a capital letter**.
- A shorter parenthetical may replace a full participial phrase where one is unnecessary in
  context.
- A parenthetical quoting one or more full sentences (or material reading as a full sentence)
  **begins with a capital letter and carries closing punctuation**; do not introduce it with
  "explaining that."

```
Brown v. Bd. of Educ., 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954) (holding that separate educational facilities are inherently unequal).
```

### Order of Parentheticals (10.6.4)

1. Weight of authority
2. "Quoting" / "citing"
3. Explanatory

```
Wolf v. Colorado, 338 U.S. 25, 47 (1949) (Rutledge, J., dissenting) (rejecting the Court's conception of the exclusionary rule), aff'g 187 P.2d 926 (Colo. 1947), overruled by Mapp v. Ohio, 367 U.S. 643 (1961).
```
**If an explanatory parenthetical contains text that itself needs a "quoting" or "citing"
parenthetical, nest them** rather than running them in series:
```
Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448, 519 (1980) (Marshall, J., concurring) (noting that conventional strict scrutiny is "strict in theory, but fatal in fact" (citing Regents of the Univ. of Cal. v. Bakke, 438 U.S. 265, 362 (1978))).
```
The full ordering across all parenthetical types is in rule 1.5(b): `(date) [hereinafter short
name] (en banc) (Lastname, J., concurring) (plurality opinion) (per curiam) (alteration in
original) (emphasis added) (footnote omitted) (citations omitted) (quoting another source) (citing
another source), URL (last visited) (explanatory parenthetical), prior or subsequent history.`

Where the cited material quotes or cites two or more sources, include them all in **one**
parenthetical: two sources take "first . . . ; and then . . ."; three or more take "first . . . ;
then . . . ; and then . . . ."

## Special Citation Forms (Rule 10.8) (22e pp. 117-21)

### Unpublished Opinions in Electronic Databases (10.8.1(a))

Give case name, docket number, database identifier, court, and full date of the most recent major
disposition. Cite the docket number as it appears on court documents; an initial digit preceding a
colon and a judge's initials at the end may be dropped, but no other letters or numbers. Screen or
page numbers are preceded by an asterisk; paragraph numbers by ¶.
```
Int'l Snowmobile Mfrs. Ass'n v. Norton, No. 00-CV-229-B, 2004 WL 2337372, at *3, *7 (D. Wyo. Oct. 14, 2004).
Gibbs v. Frank, No. 02-3924, 2004 U.S. App. LEXIS 21357, at *18-19 (3d Cir. Oct. 14, 2004).
Ortho-McNeil Pharm., Inc. v. Teva Pharm. Indus., Ltd., No. 2008-1549, 2009 BL 181480, at *4 (Fed. Cir. Aug. 26, 2009).
```
In 2024 LexisNexis introduced an alternative short identifier that may be used interchangeably:
`Albrecht v. Stranczek, No. 87 C 9535, 1991 LX 2672345, at *1, *3 (N.D. Ill. Apr. 15, 1991).`

If the database's name is not clear from the identifier, add it parenthetically at the end
(`(VersusLaw)`). Where no unique identifier has been assigned, include the specific collection
(`(LEXIS, Genfed Library, Dist. File)`; `(Loislaw, Ariz. Case Law)`).

### Slip Opinions (10.8.1(b))

Give the **docket number**, the court, and the full date of the most recent major disposition:
```
Groucho Marx Prods. v. Playboy Enters., No. 77 Civ. 1782 (S.D.N.Y. Dec. 30, 1977).
Ross v. Weissman, No. 90-345, slip op. at 6 (D. Mass. Dec. 4, 1990).
```
Always give the **full** docket number (`No. 75-31`, not `No. 31`), and note any renumbering. If
the case is not separately paginated, cite both the page on which it begins and the page cited:
`Bedell v. Nessim, No. 90-567, slip op. 3458, 3465 (1st Cir. Jan. 19, 1991).` If the date is not
the date of decision and its significance is not clear elsewhere, say so in the parenthetical
(`(D. Mass. filed Sep. 18, 1990)`).

In pending or unreported bankruptcy adversary proceedings, supply **both** case numbers: `Brown v.
Sachs (In re Brown), Ch. 7 Case No. 84-00170-G, Adv. No. 85-1190, slip op. at 5 (E.D. Mich. Jan.
24, 1986).`

### Pending Cases

```
Bedell v. Nessim, No. 90-567 (1st Cir. argued Jan. 10, 1991).
```

### Depublished Cases (10.8.1(d))

Where a decision is depublished with no reported order, note it parenthetically: `Mitchell v. Cal.
Fair Plan Ass'n, 260 Cal. Rptr. 3 (Ct. App. 1989) (depublished).` Where the depublishing order is
reported, give it as subsequent history introduced by *depublished by*.

### Fifth Circuit Split (10.8.2)

For the 1981 division of the Fifth Circuit: cite decisions rendered in 1981 and labeled "5th Cir."
**by month**; give unit information whenever available; and designate as "Former 5th Cir." any
nonunit Former Fifth judgment rendered after September 30, 1981.
```
Birl v. Estelle, 660 F.2d 592 (5th Cir. Nov. 1981).
Haitian Refugee Ctr. v. Smith, 676 F.2d 1023 (5th Cir. Unit B 1982).
McCormick v. United States, 680 F.2d 345 (Former 5th Cir. 1982).
```

### Briefs, Court Filings, and Transcripts (10.8.3)

All court filings take the same general form: the **full name of the document as it appears on the
filing**, abbreviated per rule 10.2.1(c), then a pinpoint if any, then the full case citation and
docket number. If no decision has yet been rendered, the parenthetical date is the **date of
filing**; if one has, cite the case as you otherwise would.
```
Complaint at 17, Kelly v. Wyman, 294 F. Supp. 893 (S.D.N.Y. 1968) (No. 68 Civ. 394).
Plaintiff's Motion for Summary Judgment at 2, In re 3M Combat Arms Earplug Prods. Liab. Litig., No. 20-CV-47213 (N.D. Fla. July 7, 2022).
Brief of Petitioner-Appellant at 48, United States v. Al-Marri, No. 03-3674 (7th Cir. Nov. 12, 2003).
```
**Always include the docket number** — parenthetically where there is a reported citation, as the
citation where there is not. The court-assigned document number (e.g. PACER's) may be added but is
unnecessary unless essential to finding the document: `..., Dkt. No. 14.`

Court-produced documents follow the same rules:
```
Transcript of Oral Argument at 11, Ayers v. Belmontes, 127 S. Ct. 469 (2006) (No. 05-493).
Transcript of Record at 16-17, Johnson v. Eisentrager, 339 U.S. 763 (1950) (No. 306).
```
**Amicus briefs** follow the same rules, with the sole exception that "et al." may be used where
there are more than two signatories. A parallel citation to an electronic database or website may
be added if helpful; where a pinpoint is required for one, it is required for the other.

**Audio recordings** of proceedings follow the same rules, except the title may describe the
recording and pinpoints are **time markers**; append a URL per rule 18.8:
```
Oral Argument at 11:38, Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 (1973) (No. 70-18), http://www.oyez.org/....
```

**Short forms.** A citation to a court document containing a case citation suffices as a citation to
the case itself. Where the case may be short-formed under 10.9, it may be short-formed inside the
document citation — **but the docket number must still be given**. The court document itself, unlike
the case, may take a *supra* form (and a *hereinafter* form, rule 4.2(b)); a *supra* form for the
document does **not** count for the five-footnote rule of 10.9(a):
```
1 Brief for the Petitioner, Demore v. Kim, 538 U.S. 510 (2003) (No. 01-1491).
2 Petition for Writ of Certiorari, Demore, 538 U.S. 510 (No. 01-1491).
3 Brief for the Petitioner, supra note 1, at 12.
```

### Court Administrative Orders (10.8.4)

Cite the official reporter if the order is there, and give the order's title if it has one: `Order
Discharging the Advisory Comm., 352 U.S. 803 (1956).`

### Foreign Cases (rule 20.3, 22e pp. 210-11)

Rule 10.4 sends foreign cases to **rule 20.3 and Table T2**. 20.3 opens: cite foreign cases
according to rule 10 as modified by 20.3's instructions and table T2.

**Common law cases (20.3.1).** Indicate the deciding court parenthetically — `R v. Lockwood (1782)
99 Eng. Rep. 379 (KB).` **But if the court is the highest court in the jurisdiction, only the
jurisdiction need be identified** — `Chase v. Campbell, [1962] S.C.R. 425 (Can.).`

**Civil law and other non-common-law cases (20.3.2).** Cite per table T2, identifying the court per
rule 20.1 with an English translation of the designation where the context does not make it clear;
cite the sources and abbreviations listed in T2, giving the full form on first use with the
subsequent abbreviation in brackets (20.2.3); always include the jurisdiction, abbreviated per table
T10, in parentheses at the end (20.1); annotations are cited per rule 3.4.

**United Kingdom (T2.46, 22e p. 328).** The UK entry confirms what 20.3.1's `(KB)` example
suggested: **UK court and reporter abbreviations carry no internal periods, and no comma follows the
case name.** The familiar `Donoghue v. Stevenson, [1932] A.C. 562 (H.L.)` is wrong on all three
counts. T2.46's own form is:
```
Donoghue v. Stevenson [1932] AC 562 (HL) 564 (appeal taken from Scot.).
```
Note the pinpoint: it follows the court parenthetical, with no "at" and no comma.

- **Jurisdiction abbreviations:** `UK`, `Gr. Brit.`, `Eng.`, `Wales`, `Scot.`, `N. Ir.` Per rule
  20.1, give one parenthetically only if the jurisdiction is not clear from context or citation.
- **House of Lords (HL):** cite to the Law Reports, to Eng. Rep., or — in Scottish civil cases — to
  the House of Lords section of Session Cases (T2.46.3), in that order of preference. The Supreme
  Court of the United Kingdom (UKSC) took over the HL's appellate jurisdiction and the Privy
  Council's jurisdiction on **October 1, 2009**.
- **Privy Council (PC):** cite to the Law Reports or to Eng. Rep. — `B.C. Elec. Ry. v. Loach [1916] 1
  AC 719 (PC) (appeal taken from B.C.).`
- **Appeal-taken-from parenthetical:** required whenever the deciding court hears appeals from more
  than one jurisdiction, as the HL and PC do.
- **Neutral citations.** Every judgment after 2001 has one. Cite the neutral citation first, then the
  official reporter: `R v. Pyrah [2002] UKHL 47, [2003] 1 AC 903 (appeal taken from Eng.).`;
  `Archbold v. Royal Coll. of Veterinary Surgeons [2004] UKPC 1, [2]-[3] (appeal taken from Eng.).`
  Court abbreviations: `UKPC`, `UKHL`, `UKSC`; England and Wales adds `EWCA (Civ)`, `EWCA (Crim)`,
  `EWHC (Ch)`, `EWHC (QB)`, `EWHC (Admin)`, and the rest of the T2.46.1 list. **Paragraph pinpoints
  go in square brackets**, not after a ¶.
- **England and Wales after 1865 (T2.46.1):** cite the official Law Reports if therein; only if the
  case is not there, prefer in order WLR, All ER, then other reporters. Here the pinpoint does take
  "at": `R v. Kelly [1999] QB 621 at 625 (Eng.).` With a neutral citation the pinpoint is bracketed:
  `Quinland v. Governor of Swaleside Prison [2002] EWCA (Civ) 174 [178], [2003] QB 306 [317] (Eng.).`
- **England and Wales before 1865 (T2.46.1):** there was no official series. Cite the English Reports
  reprint if the case is there, **with a parallel citation to the original nominate reporter**; the
  court name is optional. `Semayne's Case (1604) 77 Eng. Rep. 194, 195; 5 Co. Rep. 91 a, 93 b.` For
  Year Books, give regnal year, folio, term, and plea number, with a parallel cite to a modern
  reprint if possible: `Y.B. 17 Edw. 4, fol. 2a, Pasch, pl. 3 (1477) (Eng.).`
- **Attributing to a judge:** the judge's name goes in its own parenthetical after the pinpoint and
  before "appeal taken from" — `R v. Martin [1998] AC 917 (HL) 948 (Lord Hope of Craighead) (appeal
  taken from HM Court-Martial App. Ct.) (UK).` A Law Lord's territorial qualification is used on
  first mention and dropped thereafter ("Lord Hope of Craighead," then "Lord Hope").

## Short Forms for Cases (Rule 10.9) (22e pp. 121-24)

### The Five-Footnote Rule (10.9(a))

In law review footnotes, a short form may be used **only if** it clearly identifies a case that

1. is already cited in the **same** footnote, or
2. is cited — in full or short form, **including "id."** — in one of the **preceding five**
   footnotes.

**Otherwise a full citation is required.** (Note the direction: five footnotes is the window in
which the short form is *permitted*, not a threshold after which it becomes available.)

### Acceptable Short Forms (10.9(a)(i))

Only one party's name (or a readily identifiable shorter version of it) may be used **only if the
reference is unambiguous**; when one party's name is used alone it is **italicized**:
```
Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer, 343 U.S. at 585.
Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co., 343 U.S. at 585.
Youngstown, 343 U.S. at 585.
343 U.S. at 585.
Id. at 585.
```
Omit the case name entirely (the last two forms) only if the reader will have no doubt which case
is meant.

**Avoid using, as the one party name, the name of a geographical or government unit, a government
official, or another common litigant:**
```
NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson, 357 U.S. 449, 464 (1958)  →  Patterson, 357 U.S. at 464.
Not: NAACP, 357 U.S. at 464.       Not: Alabama, 357 U.S. at 464.
Reno v. Bossier Par. Sch. Bd., 520 U.S. 471, 480 (1997)  →  Bossier Par. Sch. Bd., 520 U.S. at 480.
Not: Reno, 520 U.S. at 480.
```
A case may be short-formed by a different name than the reporter's, so long as the full citation
gave both (10.2.1(k)): `The Steel Seizure Case, 343 U.S. at 585.`

**Citing an entire decision** in short form (no pinpoint): give the shortened case name, volume,
reporter, and first page — but **no jurisdiction/date parenthetical**: `The Steel Seizure Case, 343
U.S. 579.`

**Databases (10.9(a)(ii)):** use the unique database identifier — `Clark, 1991 WL 55402, at *3.`;
`Albrecht, 1991 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 5088, at *1.`

**Slip opinions (10.9(a)(iii)):** `Sam, slip op. at 12.` When citing multiple slip opinions in the
same case, either give each a unique identifier in its initial citation (`Clenchy II, slip op. at
5.`) or include a jurisdiction or date parenthetical in each short form (`Clenchy, slip op. at 5
(Me. Super. Ct. Nov. 20, 2012).`).

### Id. (Rule 4.1)

In law review footnotes, use "id." when citing the immediately preceding authority **within the
same footnote**, or **within the immediately preceding footnote when that footnote contains only
one authority**. The period in "id." is always italicized. Indicate where the subsequent citation
varies (e.g., a different page).

```
1. Chalfin v. Specter, 233 A.2d 562, 562 (Pa. 1967).
2. Id. at 563.
```

Sources appearing only in explanatory parentheticals, explanatory phrases, or prior/subsequent
history are **ignored** in deciding whether the preceding footnote contains one authority.

### Two Exceptions When Using "Id." for Cases (10.9(b))

**(i) Different opinion in the same case.** Where an "id." refers to a *different* opinion, say so
parenthetically — **even when the second opinion is the majority opinion**:
```
1 Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (The Steel Seizure Case), 343 U.S. 579 (1952).
2 Id. at 584.
3 Id. at 635 (Jackson, J., concurring).
4 Id. at 638.
5 Id. at 589 (Frankfurter, J., concurring).
6 Id. at 582 (majority opinion).
```
After an intervening citation to another source, the next citation to the case is presumed to be to
the majority opinion unless a parenthetical says otherwise.

**(ii) Parallel citations.** Where a parallel citation is required, "id." carries the second
reporter through:
```
Chalfin v. Specter, 426 Pa. 464, 465, 233 A.2d 562, 563 (1967).
becomes: Id. at 465, 233 A.2d at 563.
Not: Id. at 465.       Not: Id. at 465, 563.
```

### Never Use

- ***Supra* or *hereinafter* for a case** — rule 4.2 bars both for cases, statutes, constitutions,
  legislative materials other than hearings, restatements, model codes, and regulations, except in
  extraordinary circumstances such as an extremely long case name. (A court *document* citing a
  case may take a *supra* form; the case may not. See 10.8.3.)
- "Op. cit." / "loc. cit." — **UNVERIFIED**: neither string appears anywhere in the full 22e corpus
  (all 23 rules, all 16 tables). They are not among the short forms the 22e sanctions — the sanctioned
  set is *id.*, *supra*, *hereinafter*, and the case-specific forms of 10.9 — but the 22e nowhere
  states a prohibition, so this entry rests on their absence rather than on a rule.
- A case name alone with no reporter, where the reader could doubt which case is meant (10.9(a)(i)).

### Text (10.9(c))

A case cited in full in the same general discussion may be referred to in main text or footnote
text by one of the parties' names without further citation: "The issue presented in *Bakke* has not
been fully resolved."

| John Smith v. Jane Doe | Smith v. Doe |
| Microsoft Corporation | Microsoft Corp. |

## Verification Checklist

Before finalizing any case citation:

- [ ] Case name taken from the beginning of the opinion in the cited reporter, then shortened per
      Rule 10.2 — first word of each party's name retained, relator retained
- [ ] Abbreviated for a *citation* (10.2.2, T6/T10), not merely for text (10.2.1(c))
- [ ] Regional reporter cited for a law review piece; public domain citation given if one exists,
      with its parallel regional cite
- [ ] Court identified unless conveyed by the reporter; no court name for a state's highest court
- [ ] Year is the year of decision; exact date given if unreported or cited to a service, slip
      opinion, database, or newspaper
- [ ] Pinpoint page included for specific propositions
- [ ] Entire subsequent history given, less cert. denials (decision two years old or more) and
      irrelevant remand/rehearing history
- [ ] Parentheticals in 10.6.4 order, nested where an explanatory parenthetical carries a quote
- [ ] Short form used only within the same footnote or the preceding five (10.9(a))
