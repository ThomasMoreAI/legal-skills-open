# Short Form Citations - Bluebook Rule 4

Complete guidance for id., supra, hereinafter, and other short forms per Bluebook 22nd Edition. Page cites below are 22e pages.

## The Iron Law of Short Forms

<EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>
**NO SHORT FORM WITHOUT FULL CITATION FIRST.**

Every short form (id., supra, hereinafter) MUST point to a preceding full citation. If the full citation doesn't exist, the short form is meaningless.

**Verification gate:**
1. LOCATE full citation in document
2. VERIFY no ambiguity (only one source matches)
3. CHECK the reach: for id., the same footnote or the immediately preceding single-authority one; for a case or statute short form, the preceding five footnotes
4. THEN use short form
</EXTREMELY-IMPORTANT>

## Id. (Rule 4.1) (22e pp. 83-85)

### When to Use Id.

*Id.* may be used for **any kind of authority except internal cross-references** (rule 3.5).

The test differs by document type:

| Document | Use *id.* when |
|----------|----------------|
| Court documents and legal memoranda | citing the **immediately preceding authority**, and only when the immediately preceding citation contains **only one authority** |
| Law review footnotes | citing the immediately preceding authority **within the same footnote**, or **within the immediately preceding footnote** when that footnote contains **only one authority** |

Not "a recent footnote" — for *id.* the referent must be in the same footnote or the one immediately before it. (The five-footnote allowance belongs to case and statute short forms, rules 10.9(a) and 12.10(b), not to *id.*)

Indicate where the subsequent citation varies from the former, such as the specific page cited.

**If the first citation refers only to a shorter work contained within an authority, do not use *id.* for a subsequent citation to the entire authority** — use the *supra* form (rule 4.2(a)).

**The period at the end of *id.* is always italicized.**

### Basic Form

**Same page:**
```
1. Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954).
2. Id.
```

**Different page:**
```
1. Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954).
2. Id. at 496.
```

**Different section (statutes):**
```
1. 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (2018).
2. Id. § 1985.
```

### Id. Capitalization

| Position | Capitalization |
|----------|----------------|
| Beginning of citation sentence | *Id.* |
| Within citation clause | *id.* |
| After a signal | *id.* |

**Examples:**
```
Id. at 496.                           (beginning of sentence)
See id. at 496.                       (after signal)
See Brown, 347 U.S. at 495; id. at 496.  (within clause)
```

### When NOT to Use Id.

**Never use id. when:**

1. **Intervening citation exists:**
```
WRONG:
1. Brown, 347 U.S. at 495.
2. Plessy v. Ferguson, 163 U.S. 537 (1896).
3. Id. at 496.  ← Would refer to Plessy, not Brown!

CORRECT:
3. Brown, 347 U.S. at 496.
```

2. **Preceding footnote has multiple sources:**
```
WRONG:
1. Brown, 347 U.S. at 495; Plessy, 163 U.S. at 540.
2. Id. at 496.  ← Ambiguous: which case?

CORRECT:
2. Brown, 347 U.S. at 496.
```

3. **The referent is more than one footnote back.** *Id.* reaches only the same footnote or the immediately preceding one. Use the source's own short form instead.

4. **The referent is an internal cross-reference:**
```
24. See supra text accompanying note 2.
25. See supra text accompanying note 2.

WRONG:
25. See supra id.
```

### Exception: parentheticals and history are ignored

"Sources identified in explanatory parentheticals, explanatory phrases, or prior/subsequent history . . . are ignored for the purposes of this rule." A preceding footnote is still a single-authority footnote even when it quotes, cites, or carries the history of another case:

```
18. Tuten v. United States, 460 U.S. 660, 663 (1983) (quoting Ralston v. Robinson, 454 U.S. 201, 206 (1981)).
19. See id. at 664.                    ← refers to Tuten. Correct.

20. Dillon v. Gloss, 256 U.S. 368, 376 (1921), quoted in Nixon v. United States, 506 U.S. 224, 230 (1993).
21. See id. at 374.                    ← refers to Dillon. Correct.

22. Kohler v. Tugwell, 292 F. Supp. 978, 985 (E.D. La. 1968), aff'd per curiam, 393 U.S. 531 (1969).
23. See id. at 980.                    ← refers to Kohler. Correct.
```

### Id. for Different Source Types

**Cases:**
```
Id.                    (same page)
Id. at 496.           (different page)
```

**Statutes:**
```
Id.                    (same section)
Id. § 1985.           (different section)
Id. § 1983(a).        (subsection of same section)
```

**Books:**
```
Id.                    (same page)
Id. at 50.            (different page)
```

**Articles:**
```
Id.                    (same page)
Id. at 2035.          (different page)
```

**Multivolume works** — the volume number precedes *id.*:
```
10. 3 William Holdsworth, A History of English Law 255 (3d ed. 1923).
11. 1 id. at 5-17 (2d ed. 1914).
```

## Supra (Rule 4.2) (22e pp. 85-87)

### When to Use Supra

Use supra to refer to a source fully cited previously — in a previous footnote, or in the same footnote.

**Supra (and hereinafter) may be used for:**
- Legislative hearings
- Court filings
- Books, pamphlets, reports
- Unpublished materials
- Nonprint resources
- Periodicals
- Services
- Treaties and international agreements
- Regulations, directives, and decisions of intergovernmental organizations
- Internal cross-references

**Supra (and hereinafter) should NOT be used for:**
- Cases (use the case short form, rule 10.9)
- Statutes (use the statutory short form, rule 12.10)
- Constitutions
- Legislative materials or debates **other than hearings**
- Restatements
- Model codes
- Regulations

**Except in extraordinary circumstances**, such as when the name of the authority is extremely long:
```
26. In re Multidistrict Private Civ. Treble Damage Antitrust Litig. Involving Motor Vehicle Air Pollution Control Equip., 52 F.R.D. 398 (C.D. Cal. 1970) [hereinafter Air Pollution Control Antitrust Case].
```

Never use *infra* to refer to books, reports, or other nonperiodic materials (rule 15.10, 22e pp. 163-64).

### Basic Form

```
[Author's last name], supra note [#], at [Page].
```

**When there is no author:** use the title of the work. For unsigned student-written law review materials, use the appropriate designation (rule 16.7.1(b)) — e.g. `Note, supra note 16, at 1416.` **For an institutional author:** use the full institutional name.

**Same-footnote referent takes no cross-reference.** Give the footnote in which the full citation appears *unless the full citation is in the same footnote*, in which case use bare "supra":
```
34. See Samuel Issacharoff, On Political Corruption, 124 Harv. L. Rev. 118, 125 (2010) ...; Issacharoff, supra, at 130-42 ...
```

**A second work from a volume already cited** takes the collection's supra form:
```
35. Catharine A. MacKinnon, On Exceptionality: Women as Women in Law, in Feminism Unmodified 70, 70 (1987).
36. Catharine A. MacKinnon, Desire and Power, in Feminism Unmodified, supra note 35, at 46, 47.
```

**Example:**
```
1. Cass R. Sunstein, On the Expressive Function of Law, 144 U. Pa. L. Rev. 2021, 2030 (1996).
...
15. Sunstein, supra note 1, at 2040.
```

### Supra Without Page

When referring to the work generally (not a specific page):
```
Sunstein, supra note 1.
```

### Multiple Works by Same Author

When the author has multiple cited works, use title to disambiguate:
```
1. Cass R. Sunstein, On the Expressive Function of Law, 144 U. Pa. L. Rev. 2021 (1996).
2. Cass R. Sunstein, The Cost-Benefit Revolution (2018).
...
15. Sunstein, supra note 1, at 2040.        (refers to article)
16. Sunstein, supra note 2, at 50.          (refers to book)
```

Or use hereinafter for clarity.

### Supra for Multi-Author Works

```
1. William N. Eskridge, Jr. & Philip P. Frickey, Cases and Materials on Legislation 200 (5th ed. 2014).
...
15. Eskridge & Frickey, supra note 1, at 250.
```

For three or more authors, the short form keeps "et al.":
```
1. Erwin Chemerinsky et al., Constitutional Law 150 (6th ed. 2019).
...
15. Chemerinsky et al., supra note 1, at 200.
```
22e examples: `Keeton et al., supra note 30, § 2, at 4` (from "W. Page Keeton et al."); `Fallon et al., supra note 38, at 343` (from "Richard H. Fallon, Jr. et al.").

## Hereinafter (Rule 4.2(b)) (22e pp. 85-87)

### When to Use Hereinafter

Use hereinafter for authority that "would be cumbersome to cite with the usual 'supra' form or for which the regular shortened form may confuse the reader":
1. Title or authority name is unusually long or cumbersome
2. Two authorities appear in the same footnote and a simple supra form would be confusing
3. Source would otherwise be ambiguous in short form

**Do not use hereinafter when a simple supra form is adequate.**

The shortened form appears **in the same typeface as in the full citation**.

### Basic Form

Define in first citation:
```
[Full Citation] [hereinafter [Short Name]].
```

Use in subsequent citations:
```
[Short Name], supra note [#], at [Page].
```

### Examples

**Long title:**
```
1. U.S. Dep't of Health & Human Servs., The Health Consequences of Smoking—50 Years of Progress: A Report of the Surgeon General 100 (2014) [hereinafter Surgeon General's Report].
...
15. Surgeon General's Report, supra note 1, at 150.
```

**Multiple works by same author:**
```
1. Cass R. Sunstein, On the Expressive Function of Law, 144 U. Pa. L. Rev. 2021 (1996) [hereinafter Sunstein, Expressive Function].
2. Cass R. Sunstein, Cost-Benefit Analysis and the Environment, 115 Ethics 351 (2005) [hereinafter Sunstein, Cost-Benefit].
...
15. Sunstein, Expressive Function, supra note 1, at 2040.
16. Sunstein, Cost-Benefit, supra note 2, at 360.
```

### Hereinafter Placement

After the first citation of the authority, but **before any explanatory parenthetical**. It follows the date parenthetical and precedes the explanatory one:
```
37. Proposed Amendments to the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure: Hearings Before the
    Subcomm. on Crim. Just. of the H. Comm. on the Judiciary, 95th Cong. 92-93 (1977)
    [hereinafter Hearings] (statement of Prof. Wayne LaFave).

CORRECT: ...100 (2014) [hereinafter Report].
WRONG:   ...100 [hereinafter Report] (2014).
```

## Short Forms for Cases (Rule 10.9) (22e pp. 121-24)

**Cases do NOT use supra.** Use case-specific short forms instead.

### The five-footnote window (Rule 10.9(a))

In law review footnotes a case short form may be used only if it clearly identifies a case that (1) is already cited in the **same footnote**, or (2) is cited — in full or short form, including *id.* — in **one of the preceding five footnotes**. Otherwise a full citation is required.

### Basic Case Short Form

```
[One Party Name], [Vol.] [Reporter] at [Page].
```

**Example:**
```
1. Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954).
...
5. Brown, 347 U.S. at 497.
```

Using only one party's name (or a readily identifiable shorter version of it) is permissible **only if the reference is unambiguous**; when only one party's name is used, **it is italicized**. Acceptable forms, from longest to shortest:
```
Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer, 343 U.S. at 585.
Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co., 343 U.S. at 585.
Youngstown, 343 U.S. at 585.
343 U.S. at 585.
Id. at 585.
```
Omit the case name entirely (the last two) only if the reader will have no doubt which case is meant.

### Which party name to drop

Avoid using the name of a **geographical or government unit, a government official, or another common litigant** — keep the other party:
```
NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson, 357 U.S. 449, 464 (1958).
  → Patterson, 357 U.S. at 464.
  NOT NAACP, 357 U.S. at 464.  NOT Alabama, 357 U.S. at 464.

Reno v. Bossier Par. Sch. Bd., 520 U.S. 471, 480 (1997).
  → Bossier Par. Sch. Bd., 520 U.S. at 480.
  NOT Reno, 520 U.S. at 480.
```

### Alternate case names, whole decisions, databases, slip opinions

Per rule 10.2.1(k), a case may be short-cited by a different name than the reporter's, so long as the full citation gives both:
```
Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (The Steel Seizure Case), 343 U.S. 579, 585 (1952).
  → The Steel Seizure Case, 343 U.S. at 585.
```

**Citing an entire decision** in short form (no pinpoint): give the shorter case name, volume, reporter, and first page — and **no jurisdiction/date parenthetical**:
```
The Steel Seizure Case, 343 U.S. 579.
```

**Electronic databases** — carry the unique database identifier:
```
Clark, 1991 WL 55402, at *3.
Albrecht, 1991 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 5088, at *1.
Lindquist, at *2 (Loislaw, Ariz. Case Law).
```

**Slip opinions:**
```
Sam, slip op. at 12.
```
For multiple slip opinions in the same case, either give each a unique identifier in its initial citation (`Clenchy II, slip op. at 5.`) or include a jurisdiction/date parenthetical in each short form (`Clenchy, slip op. at 5 (Me. Super. Ct. Nov. 20, 2012).`).

### Two exceptions when using id. (Rule 10.9(b))

**(i) Different opinion within the same case.** When *id.* refers to the same opinion, no parenthetical is needed; when it refers to a **different** opinion, that must be indicated parenthetically — **even when the second opinion is the majority**:
```
1. Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (The Steel Seizure Case), 343 U.S. 579 (1952).
2. Id. at 584.
3. Id. at 635 (Jackson, J., concurring).
4. Id. at 638.
5. Id. at 589 (Frankfurter, J., concurring).
6. Id. at 582 (majority opinion).
```
After an intervening citation to another source, the next citation to the case is presumed to be to the majority opinion unless a parenthetical says otherwise.

**(ii) Parallel citations.** The *id.* form repeats the second reporter:
```
Chalfin v. Specter, 426 Pa. 464, 465, 233 A.2d 562, 563 (1967).
  → Id. at 465, 233 A.2d at 563.
  NOT Id. at 465.   NOT Id. at 465, 563.
```

### In text (Rule 10.9(c))

A case cited in full in the same general discussion may be referred to in main text or footnote text by one party's name without further citation: "The issue presented in *Bakke* has not been fully resolved."

### Id. vs. Case Short Form

| Situation | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| Same case, same footnote or immediately preceding single-authority footnote | Id. at [Page] |
| Same case, cited within the preceding five footnotes | Brown, 347 U.S. at [Page] |
| Same case, more than five footnotes back | Full citation |
| Different case | Its own short form, subject to the same five-footnote window |

## Short Forms for Statutes (Rule 12.10) (22e pp. 139-40)

### The five-footnote window (Rule 12.10(b))

A statutory short form may be used if the statute "has already been cited (in either full or short form, including 'id.') in either the same footnote or in a manner such that it can be readily found in one of the preceding five footnotes." Otherwise use the full citation form.

### Short citation forms by statute type

| Type | Full citation | In text | Short citation |
|------|---------------|---------|----------------|
| Named statutes | Administrative Procedure Act § 1, 5 U.S.C. § 551 | section 1 of the Administrative Procedure Act; or section 1 | § 1; or 5 U.S.C. § 551; or Administrative Procedure Act § 1 |
| U.S. Code | 42 U.S.C. § 1983 | 42 U.S.C. § 1983; or § 1983 | 42 U.S.C. § 1983; or § 1983 |
| State code (numbered) | Del. Code Ann. tit. 28, § 1701 (1999) | title 28, section 1701 of the Delaware Code; or section 1701 | tit. 28, § 1701; or § 1701 |
| State code (named) | Cal. Educ. Code § 48222 (West 2008) | section 48222 of the California Education Code; or section 48222 | Educ. § 48222 |
| Session laws | National Environmental Policy Act of 1969, Pub. L. No. 91-190, § 102, 83 Stat. 852, 853-54 (1970) | section 102 of the National Environmental Policy Act; or section 102 | § 102; or National Environmental Policy Act § 102; or § 102, 83 Stat. at 853-54 |

### "Section" spelled out (Rule 12.10(c))

**Except when referring to United States Code provisions**, spell out "section" in law review text and footnote text; the "§" symbol may be used in citations. See rule 6.2(c).

### Electronic sources (Rule 12.10(d))

Name the database in the short form: `Wis. Stat. Ann. § 19.43 (West, Westlaw through 2007 Act 242).` → `§ 19.43 (Westlaw).` For materials available only online, use the normal short form; a URL need not be repeated after a full citation: `Utah Code § 4-30-108 (2017), http://...` → `§ 4-30-108.`

### Id. for Statutes

```
1. 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (2018).
2. Id.                           (same section)
3. Id. § 1985.                   (different section in same title)
```

### Section Symbol Short Form

After full citation, within same discussion:
```
Section 1983 provides remedies for...
```

Or:
```
The statute, § 1983, was enacted...
```

### Named Act Short Form

```
1. Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e to 2000e-17 (2018).
...
5. Civil Rights Act § 703.      (within the five-footnote window)
```

## Infra (Rule 3.5) (22e pp. 82-83)

Portions of text, footnotes, and groups of authorities within the piece may be cited using *supra* or *infra*. Related limits from elsewhere: *id.* may **not** be used for internal cross-references (rule 4.1, "as described in rule 3.5"); *supra* and *hereinafter* **may** be used for internal cross-references (rule 4.2); and **"[n]ever use 'infra' to refer to"** books, reports, or other nonperiodic materials (rule 15.10, 22e pp. 163-64).

### When to Use Infra

**Use *supra* to refer to material that has already appeared within the piece. Use *infra* to refer to material that appears later in the piece.**

```
See infra note 45 and accompanying text.
See discussion infra Sections II.B.2, III.C.1.
```

### Infra vs. Supra

| Direction | Citation |
|-----------|----------|
| Earlier in the piece | supra |
| Later in the piece | infra |

### Naming subdivisions

Use a **consistent naming convention** for subdivisions that are numbered or otherwise designated in the piece. The Bluebook suggests "Part" for main subdivisions and "Section" for all smaller subdivisions; "note" for footnotes within the same piece; "p." and "pp." for other pages within the same piece; "Figure" and "Table" for figures and tables within the same piece. A variety of forms may be used.

**In a span of numbers, always retain the last two digits, but drop other repetitious digits** (`notes 305-07`).

### Cross-References

22e examples:
```
See supra text accompanying notes 305-07.
See supra notes 12-15, 92-97 and accompanying text.
See cases cited supra note 22.
But see sources cited supra note 24.
See discussion infra Sections II.B.2, III.C.1.
See supra Part IV.
See infra pp. 106-07.
See infra p. 50 and note 100.
See supra Figure 2.
See infra Table 3.
```

## Source-Specific Short Form Rules

**Books, reports, nonperiodic materials (Rule 15.10, 22e pp. 163-64).** Follow rule 4; never use *infra*. For a shorter work in a collection (15.10.1): *id.* may refer to the shorter work if it was the immediately preceding authority in the same footnote or the sole authority in the immediately preceding footnote, but **do not use *id.* to refer to the collection as a whole when citing another shorter work within it**. The supra form for the **entire volume** uses the **title of the collection** rather than an author, regardless of whether the collected pieces have one author or many; the supra form for an **individual work** uses the author's last name (or the title, if none).

**Periodicals (Rule 16.9, 22e pp. 175-76).** Include the author's last name before supra; with no author, use the title, or the designation if one is listed before or instead of a title (`Recent Case, supra note 2, at 150`; `Note, supra note 6`). If the first citation established a hereinafter form, use that in place of the name, title, or designation. Give the footnote of the full citation unless it is in the same footnote. **Always indicate the page cited except when citing the work in its entirety.**

**Unpublished and forthcoming sources (Rule 17.6, 22e p. 181).** Use *id.* and *supra* as under rules 16.9 and 15.10, substituting the name of the source for an author when none is listed, and enclosing a page cite to the manuscript of a forthcoming publication in parentheses: `Oetken, supra note 2 (manuscript at 15)`.

**Online-only materials (rules 12.10(d), 15.10, 16.9, 17.6).** Use the normal short form for the source; a URL need not be repeated after a full citation.

## Common Short Form Errors

### Id. Errors

| Error | Problem | Correction |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Id. after intervening cite | Ambiguous reference | Use full short form |
| Id. to ambiguous source | Multiple sources in prior note | Specify source |
| Id. without preceding full cite | No referent | Add full citation |
| Capitalizing mid-sentence | Wrong capitalization | Lowercase *id.* |
| Avoiding id. because the prior note carried a quoting/history parenthetical | Those sources are ignored under 4.1 | *Id.* is correct |
| Id. to a different opinion with no parenthetical | 10.9(b)(i) requires it, even for the majority | Add (Jackson, J., concurring) / (majority opinion) |
| Id. after a parallel citation dropping the second reporter | 10.9(b)(ii) | *Id.* at 465, 233 A.2d at 563 |
| *Id.* with a roman period | The period is always italicized | Italicize through the period |

### Supra Errors

| Error | Problem | Correction |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Supra for cases | Wrong source type | Use case short form |
| Supra for statutes | Wrong source type | Use statutory short form |
| Wrong footnote number | Reader can't find source | Verify note number |
| Missing "at" for pinpoint | Incomplete citation | Add "at [Page]" |
| "Chemerinsky, supra" for a three-author work | 4.2(a) keeps "et al." | Chemerinsky et al., supra note 1 |
| "supra note 8" when the full cite is in the same footnote | 4.2(a) takes no cross-reference there | Issacharoff, supra, at 130-42 |
| Supra for a restatement or model code | Not on the 4.2 list | Use that source's own short form |
| Hereinafter placed after an explanatory parenthetical | 4.2(b) puts it before | (1977) [hereinafter Hearings] (statement of . . .) |
| Hereinafter used where plain supra would do | 4.2(b) forbids it | Fallon et al., supra note 38, at 343 |

### Hereinafter Errors

| Error | Problem | Correction |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Hereinafter without supra | Incomplete short form | Include supra note # |
| Hereinafter not defined | Reader confused | Define in full citation |
| Hereinafter changed | Inconsistent | Use same short name throughout |

## Decision Flowchart

```
Need to cite a source you've cited before?
│
├── Is it a case?
│   ├── Same footnote, or sole authority in the immediately preceding footnote? → Id. at [Page]
│   ├── Cited within the preceding five footnotes? → [Party], [Vol.] [Reporter] at [Page]
│   └── Otherwise → full citation
│
├── Is it a statute?
│   ├── Same footnote, or sole authority in the immediately preceding footnote? → Id. or Id. § [#]
│   ├── Readily findable in the preceding five footnotes? → § [#] or [Name] § [#]
│   └── Otherwise → full citation
│
└── Is it a secondary source?
    ├── Same footnote, or sole authority in the immediately preceding footnote? → Id. at [Page]
    ├── Full cite in this same footnote? → [Author], supra, at [Page]
    └── Otherwise → [Author], supra note [#], at [Page]
```

## Verification Checklist

Before using any short form:

- [ ] Full citation exists earlier in document
- [ ] For id.: referent is in the same footnote or the immediately preceding one
- [ ] For id.: prior footnote is unambiguous (single source), ignoring explanatory parentheticals, explanatory phrases, and prior/subsequent history
- [ ] For id.: the period is italicized
- [ ] For a case or statute short form: the source appears in the same footnote or one of the preceding five
- [ ] For supra: correct footnote number — omitted entirely if the full cite is in the same footnote
- [ ] For supra: author name matches full citation
- [ ] For hereinafter: short name defined in full citation
- [ ] Case short forms do NOT use supra (absent extraordinary circumstances, e.g. an extremely long case name)
- [ ] Statute short forms do NOT use supra
- [ ] Multi-author supra keeps "et al."
- [ ] One-party case short form is unambiguous, italicized, and not a government/geographic/common-litigant name
- [ ] Capitalization correct for position in sentence
