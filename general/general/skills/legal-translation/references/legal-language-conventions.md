## English → Hindi (Devanagari script)

**Formal Opening:**
- "निम्नलिखित करार के पक्षकारों ने..." (The following parties have entered into this agreement...)
- For very formal deeds: "यह विलेख..." (This deed...)

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- "यतः" (Yataḥ) — the traditional Sanskrit-derived opening for recitals
- Modern legal: "चूँकि" (Chunki) is also acceptable and more readable

**Operative Language:**
- "करेगा / करेगी / करेंगे" (future, obligation) — Hindi has no direct "shall" equivalent; context determines obligation
- "है / हैं" (present) — for statements of fact
- "रहेगा / रहेंगे" — for ongoing obligations
- "同意" — NEVER use conversational Hindi for legal operative clauses

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"इसके साक्ष्य स्वरूप, पक्षकारों ने अपने हस्ताक्षर किए हैं।"
(In witness whereof, the parties have signed)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- Defined terms are typically in quotes or bold
- Clause numbering: 1, 1.1, 1.1.1 — decimal system
- Dates: DD/MM/YYYY (Indian standard)
- Currency: ₹ (Indian Rupee symbol preferred)
- All caps for defined terms that will be used throughout

**Common Errors to Avoid:**
- Translating "shall" as future tense "will" — Hindi future does not carry legal obligation
- Using dictionary Hindi instead of legal Hindi (e.g., "मृत्यु" for death in legal context vs "मौत")
- Dropping the formal "आपका" / "आप" distinction in letters

## English → Urdu (Arabic script, RTL)

**Formal Opening:**
- "مندرجہ ذیل想吃" (The following parties...) — but note Urdu legal prose uses "مذکورہ بالا" (aforementioned) frequently
- Traditional: "یہ دستاویز" (This document) for deeds

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- "جبکہ" (Jabke) — modern legal Urdu for recitals
- Classical: "چونکہ" (Chunke) — acceptable, more formal

**Operative Language:**
- "ہو گا / ہوں گے" — future for obligations
- "چاہیئے" (should/ought) — for weaker obligations
- "ضرورت ہے" (it is necessary) — for mandatory obligations
- **CRITICAL:** Urdu legal drafting convention uses "منحصر" (dependent on context) heavily — never translate "shall" uniformly

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"اس کی تصدیق میں، دونوں فریقوں نے اپنے دستخط کیے ہیں۔"
(In confirmation whereof, both parties have signed)
```

**Layout:**
- RTL throughout — the entire document must be right-to-left
- Clause numbers aligned right
- Signature blocks on the RIGHT side of the page
- Dates in Urdu numerals or DD/MM/YYYY with Urdu month names

**Key Conventions:**
- Traditional Urdu legal uses extensive Persian/Arabic vocabulary
- Prefer "متعہ" over Hindi-derived "طلاق" for divorce
- Company names: transliterate, do not translate
- Case references: keep in original script, add transliteration if needed

## English → Arabic (MSA — Modern Standard Arabic)

**Formal Opening:**
- "عقد الأطرافي ان العقود" (The parties have agreed...) — but note specific document types have their own conventions
- Court documents: "حضرة المحكمة الموقرة" (Honourable Court) at top

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- "حيثيات" (Ḥaythiyyāt) — recital/whereas clauses in legal Arabic
- "إذ أن" (Idh anna) — formal "whereas" in contracts

**Operative Language:**
- "يجب" (Yajibu) — must/shall (obligation)
- "يُجيز" (Yujīzu) — may (permission)
- "يتعهد" (Yataʿahhadu) — undertakes/agrees to
- "يلتزم" (Yaltazimu) — is obligated to
- "فسخ" (Faskh) — termination/rescission

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"و أثبتوا ذلک بالتوقيع على هذه الاتفاقية."
(And they have evidenced this by signing this agreement)
```

**RTL Layout:**
- Full RTL throughout
- Page numbers on the right
- Signature blocks on the right
- Headers right-aligned

**Key Conventions:**
- Arabic legal language is highly formulaic — follow established templates
- Quranic/religious references common in family law documents
- "الطرفان" (both parties) used where English uses "the parties"
- Date: Hijri and/or Gregorian — note which is primary in the document

## English → French

**Formal Opening:**
- "Contrat conclu entre:" (Contract concluded between:)
- "Le présent contrat" (This contract) for the main body

**Traditional WHEREAS (Préambule):**
- "Vu que" (Given that) or simply structured paragraphs without explicit "Attendu que"
- Modern French contracts often omit explicit whereas clauses

**Operative Language:**
- "Doit" / "devront" — shall/must
- "S'engage à" — undertakes to
- "A le droit de" — may/have the right to
- "Ne peut pas" — shall not

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"En foi de quoi, les parties ont signé le présent contrat."
(In witness whereof, the parties have signed the present contract)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- French legal is highly formal — avoid any colloquialisms
- "Convention" vs "Contrat" — convention is broader/more framework; contrat is specific obligations
- Numbering: article-based (Article 1, 1.1, 1.2)
- Dates: DD/MM/YYYY or le DD month YYYY (e.g., "le 15 mars 2024")
- Currency: euro symbol € or "euros"

**Common Errors:**
- Translating "force majeure" differently than French law requires (French concept is specific)
- Missing accents on French words (é, è, ê, ç) — legally significant
- Using "will" as future instead of "shall" equivalents for obligations

## English → German

**Formal Opening:**
- "Vertrag" (contract) or specific type ("Kaufvertrag" for sale)
- "Zwischen den Parteien..." (Between the parties...)

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- German legal rarely uses explicit "Whereas" clauses
- Recitals are often in a preamble (Präambel) with numbered paragraphs
- Key terms: "einigt" (agrees), "wird geschlossen" (is concluded)

**Operative Language:**
- "hat" / "sind" — has/have (for existing facts)
- "wird" / "werden" — will (future, but also obligation in legal context)
- "muss" / "müssen" — must (strong obligation)
- "soll" / "sollen" — should/shall (weaker obligation)
- "kann" / "können" — may (permission)
- "verpflichtet sich" — undertakes/obligates itself

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"Zu Urkund dessen haben die Parteien diesen Vertrag unterschrieben."
(In witness whereof, the parties have signed this contract)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- German legal is extremely precise — every word has legal weight
- Subordinate clauses in long sentences are common and expected
- Noun-heavy constructions preferred over verb-heavy
- Currency: Euro (€), amounts as "EUR 10.000,00" (German decimal convention)
- Dates: DD.MM.YYYY

## English → Spanish (Latin American & Iberian)

**Formal Opening:**
- "Contrato de [tipo] celebrado entre:" (Contract of [type] entered into between:)
- "De una parte..." (On one side...) and "De otra parte..." (On the other side...)

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- "Considerando que" (Considering that) or just "Vistos:" (Having seen:)
- Latin American contracts sometimes use "Ya que" (Since)
- Modern contracts may omit explicit whereas clauses

**Operative Language:**
- "Deberá" / "Deberán" — shall/must (obligation)
- "Podrá" / "Podrán" — may (permission)
- "Se obliga a" — undertakes to
- "Acepta" — accepts/agrees
- "No podrá" — shall not

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"En testimonio de lo cual, las partes firman el presente contrato."
(In testimony whereof, the parties sign the present contract)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- Spanish legal varies significantly between Spain and Latin America
- "Contrato" vs "Convenio" — contrato is more binding
- Numbers: Spanish uses "." for thousands and "," for decimals (opposite of US)
- "Sí" for yes, never "yea" or colloquial forms

## English → Mandarin Chinese

**Formal Opening:**
- "本合同由以下双方于[日期]签署：" (This contract is signed by the following two parties on [date]:)
- For deeds: "立字据人" (document creators)

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- Chinese legal rarely uses explicit "Whereas" clauses
- Recitals are implied or stated briefly in opening paragraph
- Key structure: parties → recitals (if any) → main body → signatures

**Operative Language:**
- "应当" (yīngdāng) — shall/should (obligation)
- "必须" (bìxū) — must (strong obligation)
- "可以" (kěyǐ) — may (permission)
- "同意" (tóngyì) — agrees
- "不得" (bùdé) — shall not

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"双方签字如下：" (Both parties have signed as follows:)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- Chinese legal uses simplified characters (PRC) or traditional (Taiwan, HK)
- **CRITICAL:** Never mix Simplified and Traditional in the same document
- Party names: keep as-is, transliterate if they appear in a different script
- Clause numbering: "第一条" (Article 1), "1.1", "1.1.1"
- Dates: YYYY年MM月DD日 (e.g., 2024年3月15日)
- Currency: RMB ¥ or "人民币元"

**Formatting:**
- Chinese text should not be justified (irregular right margin is normal)
- Line spacing is important for readability
- No italics or bold for emphasis — Chinese legal uses repetition for emphasis

## English → Japanese

**Formal Opening:**
- 「本合同は甲乙両名間に[start date]締結された。」(This contract was concluded between Party A and Party B on [date].)
- Or: "当事者は следующих сторон" (never — use Japanese conventions)

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- Japanese legal rarely uses explicit "Whereas" clauses
- Background is stated briefly in opening paragraph
- Key terms: " 체결한다" — concludes/enters into (Korean, not Japanese)

**Operative Language:**
- "なければならない" (must) — strong obligation
- "てはならない" (shall not)
- "べき" (should/ought) — weaker obligation
- "してもよい" (may)
- "同意する" (agrees)

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"上記のとおり契約締結のことを証するため、本書を2通作成し、各自署名押印する。"
(In witness whereof, to certify the conclusion of this contract, this document is made in two copies and each party signs and seals it)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- Japanese legal is extremely formal and uses classical Japanese grammar
- である体 (de aru tai) — formal written style
- である調 (de aru chō) — formal assertion style
- Never use casual Japanese (である調 vs だ体)
- Dates: YYYY年MM月DD日
- Currency: 円 (¥) or "日本円"

## English → Russian

**Formal Opening:**
- "Настоящий Договор заключен между:" (This Contract is concluded between:)
- For deeds: "Акт" (Act/Deed)

**Traditional WHEREAS:**
- Russian legal uses "Учитывая" (Considering) or "Имея в виду" (Having in mind)
- Sometimes structured as "Стороны, руководствуясь" (The parties, being guided by)

**Operative Language:**
- "обязуется" / "обязуются" — undertakes/undertakes (obligations)
- "вправе" — has the right (may)
- "должен" / "должна" / "должны" — must/should
- "не вправе" — shall not
- "соглашается" — agrees

**Closing Attestation:**
```
"В подтверждение чего Стороны подписали настоящий Договор."
(In confirmation whereof the Parties have signed this Contract)
```

**Key Conventions:**
- Russian legal uses Cyrillic script
- Case numbers, registration numbers: NEVER change
- "Экземпляр" (copy) vs "Подлинник" (original) — distinction matters
- Dates: DD.MM.YYYY or "DD" month YYYY г.
- Currency: руб. (rubles) or RUB

## Script Direction Summary

| Script | Direction | Key Notes |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Arabic | RTL | Full RTL, right-aligned, right page numbers |
| Hebrew | RTL | Full RTL, right-aligned |
| Urdu | RTL | Full RTL, right-aligned, nastaliq style preferred |
| Devanagari (Hindi, Marathi) | LTR | Standard left-to-right, but numbers may use Indian format |
| Persian | RTL | Full RTL |
| Chinese (Simplified) | LTR | Left-to-right for mixed script |
| Chinese (Traditional) | LTR | Left-to-right |
| Japanese | LTR | Left-to-right |
| Korean | LTR | Left-to-right, Hangul script |
| Cyrillic (Russian, etc.) | LTR | Standard Latin direction |
