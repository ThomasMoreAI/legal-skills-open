# Colorado — CPA

**Citation form**: Colorado Privacy Act, Colo. Rev. Stat. §§ 6-1-1301 to -1313. Effective July 1, 2023. Implementing rules at 4 Colo. Code Regs. § 904-3 ("CPA Rules"). Enforced by the Colorado Attorney General and Colorado district attorneys.

## Applicability

Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-1304. Applies to controllers conducting business in Colorado or producing/delivering commercial products or services intentionally targeted to CO residents AND:

1. Controlling or processing PD of **at least 100,000** consumers in a calendar year; OR
2. Deriving revenue or receiving discount on the price of goods/services from the sale of PD AND processing or controlling PD of **at least 25,000** consumers.

Exemptions: GLBA, HIPAA, FCRA, DPPA, FERPA, COPPA carve-outs (data-level for most); employment data; air carriers; certain state institutions; certain narrowly-defined entities.

## Consumer rights

Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-1306. Access, correct, delete, portability, opt-out of sale / targeted advertising / profiling with significant effect. **Right to appeal** a denied request (45-day window). Free twice per 12 months; reasonable fee thereafter.

## Universal opt-out signal

Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-1306(1)(a)(IV); CPA Rules § 5.06. **Required.** Effective July 2024. Controller must recognize signals listed by the AG (currently: Global Privacy Control). Recognition must apply across known users (logged-in profile, not just device).

## Sensitive data

Defined at Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-1303(24). Categories track other states (race/ethnicity, religious beliefs, mental/physical health, sex life/orientation, citizenship, genetic/biometric, precise geolocation, child data).

**Consent required** before processing. CPA Rules detail consent specificity: clear, affirmative, freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous; no dark patterns. CPA Rules § 7.

## Controller duties

Colo. Rev. Stat. § 6-1-1308.
- Privacy notice meeting CPA Rules § 6 specificity (categories, purposes, categories of third parties, sources, retention, opt-out mechanism).
- Data protection assessments for high-risk activities (CPA Rules § 8). Triggering activities include sale, targeted advertising, sensitive data processing, profiling with significant effects.
- Loyalty programs / bona fide loyalty programs receive special treatment under CPA Rules § 6 — a controller must provide a separate notice and obtain consent.
- Reasonable security; data minimization; purpose limitation; non-discrimination; processor contracts.

## Profiling rules

CPA Rules § 9 sets detailed profiling requirements when profiling produces legal or similarly significant effect:
- Right to opt out.
- Right to access info about the logic and consequences.
- Heightened DPA requirements.

## Enforcement

AG and district attorneys. Civil penalty up to **$20,000 per violation**, capped at $500,000 per related series of violations. **Cure period sunset Jan 1, 2025** — no longer mandatory. **No private right of action.**

## CO-specific drafting considerations

1. CPA Rules are highly prescriptive — privacy notices, consent flows, and DPAs must meet specific content requirements. A CA-targeted notice often misses CPA-Rules-specific items (e.g., loyalty program disclosures, profiling notices).
2. UOOM recognition is mandatory. CMPs without GPC ingestion are non-compliant.
3. Sensitive data consent specificity: pre-checked boxes and "agree by use" clauses are facially non-compliant.
4. Loyalty programs require a separate notice and structure under CPA Rules § 6.04 — many programs are silently non-compliant.
