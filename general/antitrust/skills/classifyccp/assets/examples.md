- **Example for "as a defence (not applicable)**:
 - Reference: "66. In any event, Arriva arranged the meeting on 14 March 2000. However, the Director recognised from copies of training manuals and evidence that training had taken place and from documents reporting contacts with competitors that the parties both had genuine compliance systems in place which appeared to be generally followed and adhered to. As a result the penalties would be reduced by 10 per cent.
 ....The Director is normally minded to give a reduction in a penalty when a party has cooperated with his investigation. However, as both Arriva and FirstGroup benefit from the leniency programme and as a condition of being granted leniency they both agreed to co-operate with the Director, he does not consider that there should be an additional reduction in the penalties under this head to reflect that co-operation."
 - Explanation/Note: Penalties should have been reduced by 10% as a mitigating factor because both parties had genuine compliance systems in place that were generally followed. But not applicable here as they benefited from leniency programme.

 - **Example for "as a defence (allowed)**:
- Reference: "we consider that a discount for compliance is appropriate in relation to FPM...a discount of 5% is the right starting point" (Tribunal decision, para 234)
- Explanation/Note: CMA initially denied FPM a compliance discount, finding insufficient evidence of genuine cultural commitment. On appeal, the Tribunal granted a 5% discount, compared to 10% for other parties with more extensive programmes.
