## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Constitutional Identity (Preliminary Title - Articles 1-9)
**Use when**: Understanding the foundational principles and values of the Spanish State

#### Spain as a Social and Democratic State under the Rule of Law (Article 1)
- **Social State**: State has positive obligation to promote social welfare and equality
- **Democratic State**: Popular sovereignty exercised through democratic institutions
- **Rule of Law (Estado de Derecho)**: All powers subject to Constitution and law
- **Parliamentary Monarchy**: Political form is the parliamentary monarchy
- **Superior Values**: Liberty, justice, equality, and political pluralism are superior values of the legal order and social coexistence

#### Indivisible Nation with Autonomous Nationalities (Article 2)
- **Indivisible Unity**: Constitution is based on the indissoluble unity of the Spanish Nation
- **Common Fatherland**: Spain is the common and indivisible fatherland of all Spaniards
- **Right to Autonomy**: Recognizes and guarantees the right to autonomy of the nationalities and regions
- **Solidarity**: Among all Spanish territories

#### Official Language and Capital (Articles 3-4)
- **Castilian**: Official language of the State throughout Spain
- **Regional Languages**: Other languages official in their Autonomous Communities
- **Linguistic Heritage**: Richness of linguistic modalities is cultural heritage to be respected and protected
- **Capital**: Madrid is the capital of the State

#### Political Pluralism and Social Organizations (Articles 5-7)
- **Political Parties**: Express political pluralism, contribute to formation of popular will, instruments for political participation
- **Internal Democracy**: Must have democratic internal structure
- **Trade Unions and Employers Associations**: Contribute to defense and promotion of economic and social interests
- **Right to Found**: Trade unions and professional associations have right to be founded freely

#### Armed Forces and Rule of Law (Articles 8-9)
- **Armed Forces Mission**: Guarantee sovereignty and independence of Spain, defend territorial integrity and constitutional order
- **Subject to Law**: Citizens and public powers are subject to the Constitution and the rest of the legal order
- **Promotion Obligation**: Public powers must promote conditions for real and effective liberty and equality
- **Legal Security**: Public powers must act in accordance with the law, which must be hierarchically ordered and published

## Part 01: Rights and Duties of Citizens (Articles 10-55)

### Spaniards and Foreigners (Articles 11-13)
**Use when**: Analyzing nationality, citizenship, and rights of foreigners
- **Spanish Citizenship**: Defined by law
- **No Deprivation**: No Spaniard of origin may be deprived of nationality
- **Foreigners' Rights**: Enjoy public freedoms guaranteed by Constitution, subject to conditions established by law and treaties
- **Extradition**: Only in compliance with treaty or law
- **Political Asylum**: Granted to foreigners persecuted for defending constitutional values or for political reasons

### Fundamental Rights and Public Freedoms (Articles 14-38)
**Use when**: Analyzing core civil and political rights

#### Equality Before Law (Article 14)
- **Principle**: Spaniards are equal before the law
- **Non-Discrimination**: Cannot be discriminated against for any reason (birth, race, sex, religion, opinion, or any other personal or social condition)

#### Right to Life and Physical Integrity (Article 15)
- **Right to Life**: Everyone has the right to life
- **No Torture**: No one may be subjected to torture or to inhuman or degrading punishment
- **Death Penalty**: Abolished, except as provided by military criminal law in times of war

#### Freedom of Ideology, Religion, and Worship (Article 16)
- **Freedom Guaranteed**: Of ideology, religion, and worship of individuals and communities
- **No State Religion**: No religion shall have a state character
- **Public Manifestation**: Everyone has the right to freely manifest their religion or beliefs in public or in private

#### Right to Liberty and Security (Article 17)
- **Right to Liberty**: Everyone has the right to liberty
- **Due Process**: No one may be deprived of liberty except as provided by law
- **Habeas Corpus**: Organic act shall regulate preventive detention procedure
- **Information Rights**: Detained persons must be informed of reasons and charges

#### Right to Privacy (Article 18)
- **Honor, Privacy, Image**: Right to honor, personal and family privacy, and own image is guaranteed
- **Domicile Inviolability**: Home is inviolable
- **Secrecy of Communications**: Guaranteed for postal, telegraphic, and telephone communications
- **Data Protection**: Law shall limit use of information technology to guarantee privacy

#### Freedom of Residence and Movement (Article 19)
- **Freedom of Residence**: Spaniards have right to freely choose place of residence
- **Freedom of Movement**: Free movement throughout national territory
- **Enter/Leave Spain**: Right to freely enter and leave Spain, subject to public interest limitations

#### Freedom of Expression and Information (Article 20)
- **Freedom of Expression**: Right to freely express and disseminate thoughts, ideas, and opinions
- **Literary and Artistic Freedom**: Right to literary, artistic, scientific, and technical production
- **Right to Information**: Right to freely communicate or receive truthful information
- **Limitations**: By law for respect of rights recognized in Title I, or in state of siege

#### Right to Assembly (Article 21)
- **Peaceful Assembly**: Right to assemble peacefully and without arms
- **No Prior Authorization**: Required for meetings in public places and demonstrations
- **Public Notification**: Prior notification to authorities required

#### Right to Association (Article 22)
- **Right to Associate**: For any lawful purpose
- **Prohibited Associations**: Secret and paramilitary associations
- **Registration Requirement**: For associations pursuing economic and social interests
- **Dissolution**: Only by court decision for criminal offenses

#### Right to Participate in Public Affairs (Article 23)
- **Right to Participate**: Directly or through freely elected representatives
- **Universal Suffrage**: Periodic elections by universal suffrage
- **Access to Public Office**: On equal conditions

#### Right to Petition (Article 29)
- **Individual and Collective**: Right to submit petitions to public authorities

### Rights and Duties of Citizens (Articles 30-38)
**Use when**: Analyzing economic, social, and specific civil rights

#### Civil Duties (Article 30)
- **Defense of Spain**: Right and duty to defend Spain
- **Military Service**: Regulated by law; conscientious objection may be regulated by organic act

#### Right to Education (Article 27)
- **Right to Education**: Everyone has the right to education
- **Compulsory and Free**: Basic education is compulsory and free
- **Public Education**: Public authorities guarantee right to education
- **Private Education**: Respect for parents' right to religious and moral education
- **University Autonomy**: Universities have autonomy within limits of law

#### Right to Work (Article 35)
- **Right and Duty**: All Spaniards have the duty to work and the right to work
- **Free Choice**: Of profession or trade
- **Working Conditions**: Law guarantees right to rest, limits working day, guarantees periodic paid vacations
- **Special Protection**: For minors

#### Right to Property (Article 33)
- **Right**: To private property and inheritance
- **Social Function**: Limited by social function
- **Expropriation**: Only for justified public utility or social interest, with compensation

#### Right to Private Enterprise (Article 38)
- **Freedom**: Within framework of market economy
- **Public Sector**: Law guarantees and protects public sector in economic system
- **Public Planning**: Law may reserve to public sector essential resources or services

#### Right to Found Foundations (Article 34)
- **Right**: To found foundations for lawful ends
- **Protection**: Activities and assets protected by law
- **Registration**: In Registry as determined by law

### Guiding Principles of Social and Economic Policy (Articles 39-52)
**Use when**: Understanding state obligations in social and economic spheres

#### Family Protection (Article 39)
- **Marriage**: Based on full consent of spouses
- **Equality**: Spouses have equal rights and duties
- **State Protection**: Public powers ensure social, economic, and legal protection of the family

#### Children (Article 39)
- **Equal Rights**: Children born within or outside marriage have same rights
- **Protection**: Law provides for protection of minors

#### Public Health (Article 43)
- **Right**: To protection of health
- **State Obligation**: Public authorities organize and foster public health and preventative measures
- **Promotion**: Health education, physical education, and sports

#### Culture and Science (Article 44)
- **Right**: To enjoy culture and its benefits
- **State Obligation**: Promote and foster access to culture
- **Scientific Research**: Promote science and technical research for general interest

#### Environment (Article 45)
- **Right**: To enjoy environment suitable for personal development
- **Public Duty**: Everyone must preserve it
- **State Obligation**: Watch over rational use of natural resources, prevent pollution
- **Criminal Sanctions**: Law regulates use of natural resources

#### Consumer Protection (Article 51)
- **State Obligation**: Guarantee defense of consumers and users
- **Regulation**: Law regulates consumer rights and obligations
- **Education and Information**: Promote consumer education and information

## Part 02: The Crown (Articles 56-65)
**Use when**: Analyzing the role of the monarchy in the Spanish constitutional system

### The King as Head of State (Article 56)
- **Role**: Head of State, symbol of unity and permanence
- **Arbitration**: Arbitrates and moderates regular functioning of institutions
- **Representation**: Highest representative of Spanish State in international relations
- **Functions**: Exercises functions expressly attributed by Constitution and laws

### Inviolability (Article 56.3)
- **Inviolability**: Person of the King is inviolable and cannot be held accountable
- **Counter-signature**: Acts shall always be countersigned
- **Validity**: Without countersignature, acts are not valid (except as provided in Article 65.2)

### Succession to the Throne (Article 57)
- **Hereditary**: Crown is hereditary among successors of Juan Carlos I de Borbon
- **Order**: Regular line and by representation, preference to nearest line
- **Male Preference**: In same line, closest degree preferred; within same degree, male preferred over female; in same sex, elder over younger
- **Exclusion**: Descendants of excluded persons also excluded
- **Regulation**: By organic act

### Proclamation and Oath (Article 61)
- **Oath**: "I swear to faithfully perform my duties, to uphold the Constitution and the laws and to respect the rights of the citizens and of the Autonomous Communities"
- **Prince of Asturias**: On reaching majority, takes oath before King
- **Regent**: Takes oath before Cortes Generales

### Regency (Articles 59-60)
- **When Required**: When King is minor or disabled
- **Order**: Father or mother of King, or nearest relative of legal age in line of succession
- **Regent Oath**: Takes oath provided in Article 61.2
- **Regency Council**: When no person has right to regency, exercised by President of Cortes Generales, President of Constitutional Court, and President of Council of Ministers

### Tutorship (Article 60)
- **Regulation**: By law
- **Incompatibility**: Tutor of King cannot hold regency simultaneously

### Royal Functions (Article 62)
- **Sanction and Promulgation**: Of laws
- **Supreme Commander**: Of Armed Forces
- **Diplomatic**: Accredits ambassadors, receives accreditation
- **Cortes**: Calls for elections, dissolves Cortes Generales
- **Referendum**: Calls referendum under constitutional conditions
- **Government**: Proposes candidate for President of Government, appoints/dismisses him; appoints/dismisses other Ministers on PM proposal
- **Decrees**: Issues decrees agreed upon by Council of Ministers
- **Appointments**: Confers civil and military positions and honorific distinctions

### Counter-signature (Article 64)
- **Requirement**: Acts of King countersigned by President of Government and competent Ministers
- **Validity**: Without countersignature, not valid
- **Exceptions**: Appointment and dismissal of President of Government, dissolution of Cortes Generales (as provided in Constitution)

### House of the King (Article 65)
- **Appointment**: King freely appoints and dismisses members of his House
- **Organization**: King determines organization and operation of his House
- **Funding**: Civil list and finances determined by State Budget

## Part 03: Cortes Generales (Legislative Power) (Articles 66-96)
**Use when**: Analyzing the Spanish Parliament and legislative process

### Definition and Composition (Article 66)
- **Definition**: Cortes Generales represent the Spanish people
- **Composition**: Congress of Deputies + Senate
- **Functions**: Exercise legislative power, approve budget, control action of Government, other functions attributed by Constitution

### Chamber of Deputies (Articles 68-79)
- **Representation**: Each province minimum initial representation; cities of Ceuta and Melilla each elect one deputy
- **Election**: Universal, free, equal, direct, secret suffrage
- **Term**: 4 years, mandated to meet annually
- **Electoral Law**: Regulated by organic act

### Senate (Articles 69-79)
- **Territorial Representation**: Most senators elected by provinces, some by Autonomous Communities
- **Election**: Direct and indirect suffrage as provided by law
- **Minimum**: Each province elects at least 4 senators; island provinces 3 each (Majorca, Gran Canaria, Tenerife) + 1 each (Ibiza-Formentera, Minorca, La Palma, Gomera, Hierro, Lanzarote-Fuerteventura)
- **Autonomous Communities**: Each elects at least one senator and one more for each million inhabitants

### Legislative Procedure (Articles 87-91)
- **Initiative**: Government, Congress, Senate, Autonomous Communities, 500,000 citizens
- **Government Bills**: Deliberated by Council of Ministers, submitted with explanatory memorandum
- **Private Bills**: Submitted to Bureau of one chamber, referred to committee
- **Amendments**: Can be proposed during committee stage and before full chamber
- **Passage**: Bill must be passed by both chambers

### Organic Acts (Article 87.2)
- **Matters**: Development of fundamental rights, approval of Statutes of Autonomy, electoral system, other matters provided by Constitution
- **Procedure**: Special procedure with enhanced majorities

### Decree-Laws and Legislative Delegation (Articles 82-85, 86)
- **Decree-Laws**: Government may issue provisional legislative measures in extraordinary and urgent cases
- **Validation**: Congress must validate within 30 days
- **Delegation**: Cortes may delegate to Government power to issue norms with force of law
- **Limits**: Cannot delegate matters reserved to organic acts

### Budget (Article 134)
- **Annual Budget**: Government prepares State budget
- **Approval**: Cortes approve budget
- **Fiscal Matters**: Only organic act can establish taxes

## Part 04: Government and Public Administration (Articles 97-123)
**Use when**: Analyzing the executive branch

### Government Composition and Functions (Article 97-98)
- **Composition**: President, Vice-Presidents (if any), and Ministers
- **Direction**: Government directs domestic and foreign policy, civil and military administration, defense of State
- **Executive Power**: Exercises executive power and political leadership

### Formation of Government (Articles 99-102)
- **Nominations**: King proposes candidate for President of Government
- **Investiture**: Congress grants confidence by absolute majority
- **Appointment**: King appoints President of Government
- **Ministerial Appointment**: President appoints and dismisses Ministers
- **Oath**: President and Ministers take oath before King
- **Collective Responsibility**: Government is collectively responsible to Congress

### Government Functions (Article 98)
- **Political Leadership**: Directs domestic and foreign policy
- **Administration**: Directs civil and military administration and defense of State
- **Executive Power**: Exercises executive power in accordance with Constitution and laws

### Motion of Censure (Article 113-114)
- **Constructive**: Congress may require Government to resign by adopting motion of censure
- **Majority**: Absolute majority of Congress
- **Candidate**: Motion must propose a candidate for President of Government
- **Effects**: King appoints proposed candidate as President of Government
- **Limitations**: Cannot be voted until 5 days after its presentation; cannot be debated until 2 days after its presentation

### Decree-Laws (Article 86)
- **Extraordinary Measures**: Government may issue provisional legislative measures in cases of extraordinary and urgent need
- **Validation**: Congress must validate within 30 days of issue
- **Effects**: If not validated, decree-laws are repealed

### Legislative Delegation (Articles 82-85)
- **Delegating Act**: Cortes may delegate to Government power to issue norms with force of law
- **Matter**: On specific matters
- **Time**: For determined period
- **Limits**: Cannot delegate matters reserved to organic acts

### Council of Ministers (Article 98)
- **Composition**: President, Vice-Presidents, Ministers
- **Presidency**: President presides
- **Functions**: Deliberates on Government policy, ensures its implementation

## Part 05: Relations between Government and Cortes Generales (Articles 108-116)
**Use when**: Analyzing executive-legislative relations

### Legislative Initiative (Article 87)
- **Government**: Primary source of bills
- **Cortes**: Congress and Senate can propose laws
- **Autonomous Communities**: Can propose laws
- **Citizens**: 500,000 citizens can propose laws

### Decree-Laws (Article 86)
- **Government**: May issue provisional legislative measures in extraordinary and urgent cases
- **Validation**: Congress must validate within 30 days
- **Consequence**: If not validated, decree-laws are repealed

### Legislative Delegation (Articles 82-85)
- **Cortes**: May delegate law-making power to Government
- **Specific**: On specific matters
- **Limited**: For limited time
- **Excluded**: Matters reserved to organic acts cannot be delegated

### Government Oversight (Article 109)
- **Information**: Government must provide information to Cortes on its management
- **Control**: Cortes can require Government presence and information

### Question Time and Interpellation (Article 110)
- **Questions**: Government must answer questions from members of Cortes
- **Interpellation**: Government must explain its policy

## Part 06: Judicial Power (Articles 117-127)
**Use when**: Analyzing the Spanish judiciary

### Judicial Independence (Article 117)
- **Independence**: Judges and magistrates are independent, impartial, and subject only to the rule of law
- **Irremovability**: Cannot be removed, suspended, transferred, or retired except for causes and with guarantees provided by law

### Judicial Unity (Article 123)
- **Supreme Court**: Highest judicial body in all orders, except constitutional guarantees
- **Hierarchy**: Judicial power is exercised by courts and tribunals established by law

### General Council of the Judiciary (Articles 122-124)
- **Composition**: President of Supreme Court (presides) + 20 members appointed by King (12 judges, 8 other legal professionals)
- **Appointment**: 12 judges elected by judges themselves, 8 elected by Congress and Senate
- **Functions**: Governs judiciary, ensures independence
- **Term**: 5 years

### Public Prosecutor's Office (Article 124)
- **Mission**: Promotes justice, defends legality and public interest
- **Organization**: Organic act regulates
- **Autonomy**: Acts with autonomy according to principles of unity of action and hierarchical dependency

### Popular Jury (Article 125)
- **Participation**: Citizens may participate in administration of justice through jury
- **Regulation**: Organic act regulates

### Military Justice (Article 117.4-5)
- **Scope**: Military courts only for military offenses committed by military personnel
- **Peacetime**: Only in state of siege as provided by organic act
- **Guarantees**: Essential guarantees for defense and assistance by lawyer

## Part 07: Economy and Finance (Articles 128-136)
**Use when**: Analyzing economic system and public finance

### Economic System (Article 128)
- **Wealth Subject to General Interest**: All wealth in its different forms is subject to general interest
- **Public Initiative**: Recognized in economic activity
- **Private Initiative**: Free but must respect general interest
- **Public Planning**: Organic act may plan economic activity

### Public Wealth (Article 128.2)
- **National Heritage**: Public wealth, irrespective of ownership, is subject to law

### Public Sector (Article 128.2)
- **Public Sector**: Exists in economic system
- **Protection**: Law guarantees and protects its exercise
- **Public Resources**: Law may reserve to public sector essential resources or services

### Taxation (Article 133)
- **Exclusive Competency**: State has exclusive competency to establish taxes
- **Local Taxes**: Local corporations may impose and collect taxes in accordance with law

### Public Credit (Article 135)
- **State Borrowing**: Only by law
- **Limits**: Law establishes limits on public debt

### Budget (Article 134)
- **Annual Budget**: Government prepares State budget
- **Approval**: Cortes approve budget
- **Execution**: Government executes budget

### Wealth Distribution (Article 131)
- **State Planning**: Organic act may plan economic activity to meet collective needs, balance and harmonize development between parts of territory, and ensure fair distribution of wealth

## Part 08: Territorial Organization of the State (Articles 137-158)
**Use when**: Analyzing Spain's decentralized structure

### Territorial Entities (Article 137)
- **Composition**: Municipalities, provinces, and Autonomous Communities
- **Autonomy**: All enjoy autonomy for management of their respective interests
- **Indivisible Nation**: State is organized territorially into these entities

### Municipalities (Article 140)
- **Legal Personality**: Full legal personality
- **Autonomy**: Manage their own interests
- **Government**: Through their respective bodies

### Provinces (Article 141)
- **Legal Personality**: Full legal personality
- **Composition**: Grouping of municipalities
- **Purpose**: Carrying out activities of State

### Autonomous Communities (Articles 143-158)
- **Right to Autonomy**: Nationalities and regions have right to autonomy
- **Autonomous Communities**: Territorial entities with legislative and executive powers
- **Statutes of Autonomy**: Fundamental institutional norm of each Community
- **Autonomy Process**: Initiative by provincial deputation or inter-island body + 3/4 of municipalities representing majority of population + approval by Cortes

### Statutes of Autonomy (Article 147)
- **Content**: Name, territory, institutions, competencies
- **Approval**: By organic act
- **Reform**: By procedure established in Statute and organic act

### Competencies (Articles 148-149)
- **State Competencies**: Exclusive competencies listed in Article 149
- **Autonomous Community Competencies**: Exclusive competencies in Article 148, plus any not assumed by State
- **Shared Competencies**: State has competencies in matters not attributed to Autonomous Communities
- **Solidarity**: All Spanish territories have solidarity obligations

### Financial Autonomy (Article 156-157)
- **Autonomous Community Revenues**: From own taxes, participation in State revenues, other revenues
- **State Financing**: Compensation fund among Autonomous Communities
- **Local Financing**: Municipalities and provinces have their own revenues

### Conflicts of Competency (Article 161.1.c)
- **Resolution**: Constitutional Court resolves conflicts between State and Autonomous Communities or between Autonomous Communities

## Part 09: Constitutional Court (Articles 159-165)
**Use when**: Analyzing constitutional review in Spain

### Composition (Article 159)
- **12 Members**: 4 appointed by Congress, 4 by Senate, 2 by Government, 2 by General Council of the Judiciary
- **Appointment**: 2/3 majority in each appointing body
- **Term**: 9 years, renewed by thirds every 3 years
- **Requirements**: Magistrates, public prosecutors, professors, lawyers, or civil servants with recognized competence and at least 15 years of professional experience

### Jurisdiction (Articles 161-163)
- **Constitutional Review**: Judges constitutionality of laws, regulations, or acts with force of law
- **Concrete Review**: When raised by judge or court in a specific case
- **Abstract Review**: Of laws and regulations before promulgation
- **Constitutional Complaint**: Protection of fundamental rights against public power violations
- **Competency Conflicts**: Between State and Autonomous Communities or between Autonomous Communities
- **Other Powers**: As provided by Constitution and organic act

### Decisions (Article 164)
- **Force of Law**: Decisions have force of law
- **Binding**: On all public powers and citizens
- **Publication**: In Official State Gazette
- **Effects**: Affect unconstitutional provisions, not necessarily acts based on them

## Part 10: Constitutional Amendment (Articles 166-169)
**Use when**: Understanding how the Spanish Constitution can be changed

### Ordinary Procedure (Article 167)
- **Initiative**: 3/5 majority in each chamber, or 2/3 majority in one chamber and absolute majority in the other
- **Approval**: Absolute majority in both chambers
- **Referendum**: If requested by 1/10 of members of either chamber within 15 days after approval

### Aggravated Procedure (Article 168)
- **Initiative**: 2/3 majority in both chambers
- **Dissolution**: Cortes are immediately dissolved
- **New Chambers**: Newly elected chambers must approve by 2/3 majority
- **Referendum**: Mandatory popular referendum for ratification
- **Scope**: Total revision of Constitution or partial revision of fundamental rights or Crown provisions
