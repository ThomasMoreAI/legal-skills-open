# Patterns - Constitution of Spain

## Parliamentary Monarchy Pattern
**When to use**: Designing constitutional systems that balance ceremonial leadership with democratic governance
**How**: Separate symbolic head of state (monarch) from executive power (government), with monarch's acts requiring counter-signature by elected officials
**Trade-offs**: Provides stability and continuity but requires careful definition of monarch's role; ceremonies vs. substance; but enables non-partisan symbolic leadership
**Examples**:
- King as arbitrator and moderator of institutions (Article 56)
- Counter-signature requirement for all royal acts (Article 64)
- Monarch's inviolability balanced by ministerial responsibility (Article 56.3)

---

## Autonomous Communities Pattern
**When to use**: Designing federal or decentralized systems that recognize regional diversity within national unity
**How**: Guarantee right to autonomy for nationalities and regions while maintaining indissoluble unity of the nation
**Trade-offs**: Balances regional self-governance with national solidarity; requires negotiation of competencies; but enables cultural and linguistic diversity
**Examples**:
- Recognition of autonomous nationalities and regions (Article 2)
- Guarantee of autonomy for all Spanish territories (Article 143)
- Solidarity principle among all territories (Article 2)
- Protection of linguistic heritage (Article 3)

---

## Social and Democratic State Pattern
**When to use**: Establishing constitutional frameworks that promote social welfare alongside political democracy
**How**: Define state as both social (promoting welfare) and democratic (popular sovereignty) under rule of law
**Trade-offs**: Requires active state intervention for social goals; may create tension between individual liberty and collective welfare; but ensures comprehensive citizen well-being
**Examples**:
- Spain as social and democratic state under rule of law (Article 1)
- Superior values: liberty, justice, equality, political pluralism (Article 1)
- Public powers promote conditions for real and effective liberty and equality (Article 9.2)

---

## Fundamental Rights Protection Pattern
**When to use**: Creating comprehensive rights frameworks that protect individuals from state power
**How**: Establish inviolable rights with direct applicability, protected by constitutional court and ordinary judges
**Trade-offs**: Requires robust judicial system; may limit legislative flexibility; but provides strong protection against arbitrary state action
**Examples**:
- Equality before law with non-discrimination (Article 14)
- Right to life and prohibition of torture (Article 15)
- Right to liberty and security with habeas corpus (Article 17)
- Right to privacy and data protection (Article 18)
- Right to freedom of expression and information (Article 20)

---

## Constitutional Court Pattern
**When to use**: Designing judicial review systems to ensure constitutional supremacy
**How**: Create independent constitutional court with power to review laws and protect fundamental rights
**Trade-offs**: May create tension with legislative power; requires careful appointment process; but ensures constitutional interpretation is authoritative
**Examples**:
- Constitutional Court as supreme interpreter of Constitution (Article 159)
- Independence and impartiality of judges (Article 117)
- Judicial power as part of state power (Article 106)

---

## Territorial Autonomy Pattern
**When to use**: Implementing decentralized governance in linguistically and culturally diverse nations
**How**: Devolve competencies to autonomous communities while maintaining national framework for coordination
**Trade-offs**: Requires clear division of competencies; may create coordination challenges; but respects regional identity and promotes efficient local governance
**Examples**:
- Autonomous Communities have own institutions of self-government (Article 147)
- State respects autonomous communities' self-government (Article 149)
- Autonomous Communities can assume competencies in various matters (Article 148)

---

## Rule of Law Pattern
**When to use**: Establishing legal frameworks where all power is subject to law and constitutional limits
**How**: Bind all public powers to constitution and legal hierarchy, with judicial review of administrative acts
**Trade-offs**: May slow decision-making; requires comprehensive legal framework; but prevents arbitrary exercise of power
**Examples**:
- All citizens and public powers subject to Constitution (Article 9.1)
- Legal hierarchy with law as highest norm after Constitution (Article 9.3)
- Legal security through publication of norms (Article 9.3)
- Judicial review of administrative acts (Article 106)

---

## Political Pluralism Pattern
**When to use**: Designing democratic systems that ensure representation of diverse political views
**How**: Guarantee right to form political parties with internal democracy, express pluralism, and participate in political life
**Trade-offs**: May lead to fragmentation; requires regulation to prevent abuse; but ensures diverse representation and prevents monopolization of power
**Examples**:
- Political parties as instruments of political participation (Article 6)
- Requirement for internal democracy in political parties (Article 6)
- Political pluralism as superior value (Article 1)
- Right to form political parties (Article 22)

---

## Constitutional Amendment Pattern
**When to use**: Creating flexible but stable constitutional amendment processes
**How**: Establish multi-stage process with special majorities for different types of reforms
**Trade-offs**: Makes amendment difficult but ensures stability; requires broad consensus for fundamental changes; but allows constitutional evolution
**Examples**:
- Different procedures for partial vs. total revision (Article 167-168)
- Requirement of 2/3 majority in both chambers for organic acts (Article 87.2)
- Referendum requirement for total constitutional revision (Article 168)
- Constitutional reform cannot be initiated in time of war or states of exception (Article 169)

---

## Economic and Social Rights Pattern
**When to use**: Establishing constitutional frameworks for economic justice and social protection
**How**: Guarantee right to work, private property with social function, and public sector in economy
**Trade-offs**: Requires state intervention in economy; may limit pure market freedom; but promotes social justice and prevents exploitation
**Examples**:
- Right to work and free choice of profession (Article 35)
- Private property with social function limitation (Article 33)
- Public sector guaranteed in economic system (Article 38)
- Public planning may reserve essential resources or services (Article 38)
- Right to education, compulsory and free (Article 27)

---

## Judicial Independence Pattern
**When to use**: Designing judicial systems that ensure independence from political pressure
**How**: Establish judges' and magistrates' independence, irremovability, incompatibility, and inamovibility
**Trade-offs**: May reduce accountability; requires careful selection process; but ensures impartial administration of justice
**Examples**:
- Judges and magistrates independent, irremovable, incompatible, inamovible (Article 117)
- Judicial power exercised by judges and magistrates (Article 117.1)
- Constitutional Court independent from other state powers (Article 159)
- Judges appointed by General Council of Judicial Power (Article 122)

---

## Emergency Powers Pattern
**When to use**: Establishing constitutional frameworks for crisis management
**How**: Define states of alarm, exception, and siege with specific powers and limitations
**Trade-offs**: Grants extraordinary powers to executive; may limit individual rights; but provides framework for effective crisis response
**Examples**:
- States of alarm, exception, and siege (Article 116)
- Decree-laws for extraordinary and urgent cases (Article 86)
- Limitations on rights during states of exception must respect Constitution (Article 55)
- Congress cannot be dissolved during states of exception (Article 116.5)
