# Part 08: Territorial Organization of the State
**Articles: 137-158**

## Core Idea
Spain is organized as a decentralized State composed of municipalities, provinces, and Autonomous Communities. This structure recognizes the right to autonomy while maintaining the indissoluble unity of the Nation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Autonomous Communities**: Territorial entities with legislative and executive powers
- **Local Government**: Municipalities and provinces with self-government
- **Cooperative Federalism**: State and Autonomous Communities cooperate in exercise of competencies

## Key Concepts
- **Indivisible Nation**: State is organized territorially in municipalities, provinces, and Autonomous Communities (Art. 137)
- **Right to Autonomy**: All these entities enjoy autonomy for management of their respective interests (Art. 137)
- **Autonomous Communities**: May have their own governments and parliaments (Art. 147)
- **Statutes of Autonomy**: Fundamental institutional norm of each Autonomous Community (Art. 147.2)
- **Competency Distribution**: State has exclusive competencies in some areas, Autonomous Communities in others, with shared competencies (Art. 148-149)
- **Solidarity Principle**: All Spanish territories have solidarity obligations (Art. 138)
- **Local Autonomy**: Municipalities have full legal personality and autonomy (Art. 140)
- **Provinces**: Local entities with legal personality, formed by municipalities (Art. 141)

## Mental Models
- Use **autonomy within unity** — decentralization without separation
- Think of **competency catalogue** — clear distribution prevents conflicts
- Apply **cooperation principle** — State and regions work together

## Anti-patterns
- **Centripetal federalism**: State overreach into autonomous competencies
- **Centrifugal forces**: Autonomous Communities acting beyond their competencies
- **Asymmetric autonomy without justification**: Different treatment requires constitutional basis

## Key Takeaways
1. Spain is a decentralized unitary state, not a federation
2. Autonomous Communities have significant self-government powers
3. Autonomy is exercised through Statutes approved by State
4. Competencies are distributed between State and Communities
5. Municipalities and provinces have local self-government
