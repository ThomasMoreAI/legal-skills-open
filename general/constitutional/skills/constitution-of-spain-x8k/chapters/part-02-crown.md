# Part 02: The Crown
**Articles: 56-65**

## Core Idea
The Crown is the Head of State, personified by the King, who symbolizes the unity and permanence of the State. The King serves as the constitutional arbiter and highest representative of Spain in international relations, while the actual executive power is exercised by the Government. This establishes Spain as a parliamentary monarchy.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The King as Head of State (Article 56)
- **Constitutional Role**: The King is the Head of State, symbol of its unity and permanence
- **Arbitration**: He arbitrates and moderates the regular functioning of the institutions
- **Representation**: He is the highest representative of the Spanish State in international relations
- **Functions**: He exercises the functions expressly attributed to him by the Constitution and the laws

### Inviolability and Immunity (Article 56.3)
- **Inviolability**: The person of the King is inviolable and shall not be held accountable
- **Counter-signature**: His acts shall always be countersigned in the manner established in Article 64
- **Accountability**: Without such countersignature they shall not be valid, except as provided for in Article 65(2)

### Succession (Article 57)
- **Hereditary**: The Crown of Spain is hereditary among the successors of S.M. Juan Carlos I de Borbon
- **Order of Succession**: The order of succession to the throne shall follow the regular line and by representation, preference being given to the nearest over the more remote line
- **Prefer male over female**: In the same line, the closest degree shall be preferred; within the same degree, the male shall be preferred over the female; and in the same sex, the elder over the younger
- **Exclusion**: The descendants of persons who are excluded from succession to the throne shall also be excluded
- **Constitutional Act**: All these questions shall be regulated by an organic act

### Exclusion from Succession (Article 57.2)
- **Exclusion**: Persons who have a constitutional right to the succession who contract a marriage against the express prohibition of the King and of the Cortes Generales shall be excluded from succession to the throne
- **Children**: Their descendants shall also be excluded
- **Consent**: An organic act may establish the cases of ineligibility for and extinction of the rights to succession

### Proclamation and Oath (Article 61)
- **Proclamation**: The King, on being proclaimed before the Cortes Generales, shall take the following oath: "I swear to faithfully perform my duties, to uphold the Constitution and the laws and to respect the rights of the citizens and of the Autonomous Communities"
- **Additional Formula**: He shall also take the oath to faithfully perform his duties before the King, in the case of the Prince of Asturias, and before the Cortes Generales, in the case of the Regent or Regents
- **Prince of Asturias**: The Prince of Asturias, on reaching his majority, and the Regent or Regents, on assuming office, shall take the same oath, as well as that of loyalty to the King

### Regency (Articles 59-60)
- **Regency Provision**: A regency shall be established when the King is a minor or is disabled
- **Order of Regency**: The regency shall be exercised, in the manner provided for by law, by the father or mother of the King, or by the nearest relative of legal age in the line of succession to the throne
- **Regent Oath**: The regent, on assuming office, shall take the oath provided for in Article 61.2
- **Regency Council**: When there are no persons having a right to the regency, this shall be assumed by a Council consisting of the President of the Cortes Generales, the President of the Constitutional Court, and the President of the Council of Ministers

### Tutorship (Article 60)
- **Minor King**: The exercise of the tutorship of the minor King shall be regulated by law
- **Tutor Incompatibility**: The tutor of the King may not at the same time hold the regency

### Royal Functions (Article 62)
- **Sanction and Promulgation**: The King sanctions and promulgates the laws
- **Command of Armed Forces**: He is the supreme commander of the Armed Forces
- **Diplomatic Functions**: He accredits ambassadors and other diplomatic representatives
- **International Representation**: Ambassadors and other diplomatic representatives are accredited to him
- **Calls and Dissolves Cortes**: He calls for elections and dissolves the Cortes Generales
- **Calls Referendum**: He calls for a referendum under the conditions provided for in the Constitution
- **Appoints and Dismisses**: He proposes the candidate for President of the Government and, as the case may be, appoints or dismisses him
- **Appoints Ministers**: He appoints and dismisses the other members of the Government, on the proposal of its President
- **Issues Decrees**: He issues the decrees agreed upon by the Council of Ministers
- **High Military Commands**: He confers civil and military positions and honorific distinctions in accordance with the law

### Counter-signature (Article 64)
- **Requirement**: The acts of the King shall be countersigned by the President of the Government and, as the case may be, by the competent Ministers
- **Validity**: Without such countersignature, they shall not be valid
- **Exception**: This shall not apply to the appointment and dismissal of the President of the Government and to the dissolution of the Cortes Generales, in accordance with the terms provided for in the Constitution

### House of the King (Article 65)
- **Organization**: The King freely appoints and dismisses the members of his House
- **Funding**: The King shall determine the organization and operation of his House
- **State Budget**: Civil list and the finances of his House shall be determined by the State Budget

## Key Concepts
- **Parliamentary Monarchy**: King reigns but does not rule; executive power exercised by Government
- **Constitutional Arbitration**: King arbitrates and moderates functioning of institutions
- **Symbol of Unity**: King represents the unity and permanence of the State
- **Inviolability**: King cannot be held accountable for acts
- **Counter-signature**: All royal acts require ministerial countersignature for validity
- **Hereditary Succession**: Crown passes to descendants of Juan Carlos I de Borbon
- **Regency**: Temporary exercise of royal functions when King cannot perform duties
- **Tutorship**: Guardianship of minor King
- **Royal House**: Personal staff and organization of the King

## Mental Models
- Use **King as constitutional symbol** — represents the State but does not govern
- Think of **counter-signature as democratic check** — ministers are responsible, not the King
- Apply **arbitration role** — King resolves disputes between institutions
- Use **unity representation** — King symbolizes the permanence of the Spanish Nation

## Anti-patterns
- **Personal rule**: King cannot exercise executive power directly
- **Uncountersigned acts**: Royal acts without ministerial signature are invalid
- **Political interference**: King must remain above partisan politics
- **Hereditary absolutism**: Succession is constitutional, not absolute

## Key Takeaways
1. King is Head of State, symbol of unity and permanence
2. King arbitrates and moderates functioning of institutions
3. King exercises functions attributed by Constitution and laws
4. King's person is inviolable; acts require ministerial countersignature
5. Crown is hereditary in the line of Juan Carlos I de Borbon
6. King sanctions laws, commands Armed Forces, accredits diplomats
7. King appoints President of Government and Ministers on PM proposal
8. Regency and tutorship provisions ensure continuity when King cannot serve
9. Counter-signature ensures ministerial responsibility for royal acts
10. King's House is organized and funded by the State
