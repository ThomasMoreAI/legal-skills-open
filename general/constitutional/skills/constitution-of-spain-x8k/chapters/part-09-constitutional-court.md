# Part 09: Constitutional Court
**Articles: 159-165**

## Core Idea
The Constitutional Court is the supreme interpreter of the Constitution, guaranteeing the primacy of the Constitution, the rule of law, and the protection of fundamental rights. It is independent of other constitutional bodies and subject only to the Constitution and its Organic Law.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Constitutional Review**: Power to declare laws, regulations, or acts unconstitutional
- **Rights Protection**: Guarantees fundamental rights and freedoms through constitutional complaint
- **Competency Arbitration**: Resolves conflicts of competency between State and Autonomous Communities or between Autonomous Communities

## Key Concepts
- **Composition**: 12 members — 4 appointed by Congress, 4 by Senate, 2 by Government, 2 by General Council of the Judiciary (Art. 159.1)
- **Term**: 9 years, with rotation every 3 years (Art. 159.2)
- **Jurisdiction**: Entire Spanish territory and all constitutional matters (Art. 161)
- **Jurisdiction Types**: Concrete and abstract constitutional review, constitutional complaint, competency conflicts, and other powers (Art. 161-163)
- **Decisions**: Have force of law and are binding on all public powers (Art. 164.1)
- **Judgment Effects**: Affect unconstitutional provisions, not necessarily the acts based on them (Art. 164.2)

## Mental Models
- Use **Court as Constitution guardian** — final word on constitutional interpretation
- Think of **constitutional complaint as rights shield** — citizens can challenge violations
- Apply **concrete vs abstract review** — different procedures for different cases

## Anti-patterns
- **Politicized appointments**: Selecting justices based on political loyalty rather than competence
- **Ignoring Court rulings**: Public powers must comply with Constitutional Court decisions

## Key Takeaways
1. Constitutional Court is independent and superior in constitutional matters
2. 12 justices with staggered 9-year terms ensure stability and rotation
3. Court has broad jurisdiction over constitutional questions
4. Decisions are final and binding
5. Constitutional complaint protects fundamental rights against public power violations
