# Part 01: Rights and Duties of Citizens
**Articles: 10-55**

## Core Idea
Chapter I of Title I (Articles 10-55) establishes the fundamental rights and duties of Spanish citizens, structured into several sections that protect civil, political, economic, social, and cultural rights. These rights form the core of the constitutional protection of human dignity and are the foundation of the legal order.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Section 1: Spaniards and Foreigners (Articles 11-13)
- **Spanish Citizenship**:Spaniards are those who have such nationality in accordance with the law
- **Dual Nationality**: No Spaniard of origin may be deprived of his nationality
- **Foreigners**: Foreigners in Spain shall enjoy the public freedoms guaranteed by the Constitution, subject to the conditions established by law and international treaties
- **Extradition**: Extradition may be granted only in compliance with a treaty or the law, in accordance with its terms
- **Political Refuge**: Spain grants the right of asylum to foreigners persecuted for defending the values and principles recognized in the Constitution or for political reasons

### Section 2: Fundamental Rights and Public Freedoms (Articles 14-38)

#### Principle of Equality (Article 14)
- **Equality Before Law**: Spaniards are equal before the law and may not be discriminated against for any reason whatsoever with regard to birth, race, sex, religion, opinion, or any other personal or social condition or circumstance

#### Rights Recognized (Article 15)
- **Right to Life**: Everyone has the right to life
- **Physical and Moral Integrity**: No one may be subjected to torture or to inhuman or degrading punishment or treatment
- **Abolition of Death Penalty**: The death penalty is abolished, except as provided for by military criminal law in times of war

#### Freedom of Ideology, Religion, and Worship (Article 16)
- **Freedom of Ideology**: Freedom of ideology, religion, and worship of individuals and communities is guaranteed
- **No State Religion**: No religion shall have a state character
- **Public Manifestation**: Everyone has the right to freely manifest their religion or beliefs in public or in private

#### Right to Liberty and Security (Article 17)
- **Right to Liberty**: Everyone has the right to liberty
- **Due Process**: No one may be deprived of his liberty except in accordance with the provisions of this Article and in the cases and in the manner provided for by the law
- **Habeas Corpus**: An organic act shall regulate the procedure and conditions for preventive detention and the maximum duration thereof
- **Information**: Everyone taken into custody must be informed immediately, in a manner that he can understand, of the reasons for his detention and of the charges against him

#### Right to Privacy (Article 18)
- **Privacy**: The right to honor, to personal and family privacy, and to the own image is guaranteed
- **Domicile Inviolability**: The home is inviolable
- **Secrecy of Communications**: The secrecy of communications is guaranteed, particularly postal, telegraphic, and telephone communications
- **Limitations**: The law shall limit the use of information technology in order to guarantee the honor and personal and family privacy of citizens

#### Right to Free Residence and Movement (Article 19)
- **Freedom of Residence**: Spaniards have the right to freely choose their place of residence and to freely move throughout the national territory
- **Right to Enter and Leave Spain**: Spaniards have the right to freely enter and leave Spain, subject to the limitations that may be established by law for reasons of the public interest

#### Right to Expression and Information (Article 20)
- **Freedom of Expression**: The right to freely express and disseminate thoughts, ideas, and opinions through words, in writing, or by any other means of reproduction is recognized
- **Freedom of the Press**: The right to literary, artistic, scientific, and technical production and creation is recognized
- **Right to Information**: The right to freely communicate or receive truthful information by any means of diffusion whatsoever is recognized
- **Limitations**: These rights may be limited by law for the respect of the rights recognized in this Title, in the case of formal and express declaration of a state of siege

#### Right to Assembly (Article 21)
- **Right to Assemble Peacefully**: The right to assemble peacefully and without arms is recognized
- **No Prior Authorization**: Prior authorization is not required for the exercise of this right
- **Public Places**: In the case of meetings in public places and of public demonstrations, prior notification shall be given to the authorities

#### Right to Association (Article 22)
- **Right to Associate**: The right to associate for any lawful purpose is recognized
- **No Secret Associations**: Secret and paramilitary associations are prohibited
- **Registered Associations**: Associations constituted for the pursuit of purposes relating to the defense and promotion of economic and social interests are required to register for the purposes of public knowledge
- **Unlawful Associations**: Associations are prohibited that pursue purposes or use means typed as criminal offenses
- **Suspension**: Associations may be suspended or dissolved only by a court decision motivated by the breach of the terms of the preceding paragraph

#### Right to Participate in Public Affairs (Article 23)
- **Right to Participate**: Citizens have the right to participate in public affairs, directly or through representatives freely elected in periodic elections by universal suffrage
- **Eligibility**: They also have the right to accede under conditions of equality to the public functions and positions of their country
- **Election Law**: The law shall regulate the causes of ineligibility and incompatibility

#### Right to Petition (Article 29)
- **Individual Petition**: All Spaniards have the individual and collective right to submit petitions to the public authorities
- **Collective Petition**: The law shall regulate the manner in which this right is to be exercised, both individually and collectively

### Section 2: Rights and Duties of Citizens (Articles 30-38)

#### Civil Duties (Article 30)
- **Defense of Spain**: Spaniards have the right and the duty to defend Spain
- **Military Service**: The law shall regulate the military obligations of Spaniards and shall determine, where appropriate, the social benefits that may be established
- **Conscientious Objection**: An organic act may regulate the conscientious objection
- **Jury Service**: An organic act may impose the duty of fulfilling social duties

#### Right to Education (Article 27)
- **Right to Education**: Everyone has the right to education
- **Compulsory and Free**: Basic education is compulsory and free
- **Public Education**: Public authorities guarantee the right of all to education by means of adequate programming of public education
- **Private Education**: Public authorities shall respect the right of parents to ensure the religious and moral education of their children in accordance with their own convictions

#### Right to Work (Article 35)
- **Right to Work**: All Spaniards have the duty to work and the right to work
- **Free Choice of Profession**: The law shall regulate a system of professional training and readjustment and the rules governing labor contracts and the Statute of Workers
- **Working Conditions**: The law shall guarantee the right to rest and shall limit the working day
- **Paid Vacations**: The law shall guarantee periodic paid vacations and special protection for minors

#### Right to Property (Article 33)
- **Right to Private Property**: The right to private property and inheritance is recognized
- **Social Function**: These rights shall be limited by their social function, in accordance with the laws
- **Expropriation**: No one may be deprived of his property and rights except on justified grounds of public utility or social interest
- **Compensation**: In the cases of expropriation, the owner shall always be compensated

#### Right to Private Enterprise (Article 38)
- **Freedom of Enterprise**: Freedom of enterprise is recognized within the framework of the market economy
- **Public Sector**: The law shall guarantee and protect its exercise and the maintenance of a public sector in the economic system
- **Public Planning**: The law may reserve to the public sector essential resources or services, as well as undertakings that should be nationalized or monopolized by the State

#### Right to Foundations (Article 34)
- **Right to Found Foundations**: The right to found foundations for lawful ends is recognized
- **Endowments**: The activities of foundations and the assets allocated to their ends are protected by law
- **Registration**: Foundations shall be entered in the Registry in the manner determined by law

### Section 3: Guiding Principles of Social and Economic Policy (Articles 39-52)

#### Family Protection (Article 39)
- **Marriage**: Marriage is based on the full consent of the spouses
- **Equality**: Spouses have equal rights and duties
- **Family**: The law shall provide for forms of marriage, the age and capacity for marriage, the rights and duties of the spouses, the grounds for dissolution, and their effects
- **Protection**: Public powers shall ensure the social, economic, and legal protection of the family

#### Children (Article 39)
- **Equal Rights**: Children born within or outside marriage have the same rights
- **Protection**: The law shall provide for the protection of minors
- **Adoption**: The law shall regulate the investigation of paternity

#### Public Health (Article 43)
- **Right to Health Protection**: The right to the protection of health is recognized
- **Public Measures**: It is incumbent upon the public authorities to organize and foster public health and preventative measures
- **Health Education**: Public authorities shall promote health education, physical education, and sports

#### Culture and Science (Article 44)
- **Right to Culture**: Everyone has the right to enjoy culture and its benefits
- **Public Promotion**: It is incumbent upon the public authorities to promote and foster access to culture
- **Scientific Research**: Public authorities shall promote science and scientific and technical research for the general interest

#### Environment (Article 45)
- **Right to Environment**: Everyone has the right to enjoy an environment suitable for the development of the person
- **Public Duty**: It is incumbent upon everyone to preserve it
- **Public Measures**: Public authorities shall watch over the rational use of all natural resources with a view to protecting and improving the quality of life
- **Criminal Sanctions**: The law shall regulate the effective use of natural resources to prevent environmental pollution

#### Housing (Article 47)
- **Right to Housing**: All Spaniards have the right to enjoy decent and adequate housing
- **Public Policy**: Public authorities shall promote the necessary conditions and establish appropriate norms to make this right effective
- **Land Use**: Public authorities shall regulate the use of land in accordance with the general interest to prevent speculation

#### Consumer Protection (Article 51)
- **Consumer Rights**: Public authorities shall guarantee the defense of consumers and users
- **Health and Safety**: The law shall regulate consumer rights and obligations
- **Information**: Public authorities shall promote consumer education and information

#### Economic System (Article 128)
- **Social Market Economy**: The entire wealth of the country in its different forms and irrespective of ownership is subject to the general interest
- **Public Initiative**: Public initiative in economic activity is recognized through means of law
- **Private Initiative**: Private economic initiative is free
- **Public Planning**: An organic act may plan economic activity to meet collective needs

## Mental Models
- Use **rights as constitutional guarantees** — rights are not granted by the State but recognized as inherent
- Think of **rights as interconnected** — economic rights support civil rights and vice versa
- Apply **proportionality principle** — limitations on rights must be necessary, suitable, and balanced
- Use **dignity as foundation** — all rights derive from human dignity

## Anti-patterns
- **Absolute rights**: Rights are not absolute; can be limited for legitimate public interests
- **Selective rights application**: All rights must be respected and protected equally
- **Rights without duties**: Rights are accompanied by duties (e.g., defense of Spain)
- **State interference in private matters**: Privacy and family life are protected from state interference

## Key Takeaways
1. Fundamental rights are structured in sections covering different categories
2. Rights include civil, political, economic, social, and cultural dimensions
3. Rights can be limited only by law and for legitimate public interests
4. Some rights have direct effect (self-executing), others require legislation
5. Rights are accompanied by duties (e.g., defense, work, tax payment)
6. Guiding principles of social and economic policy direct state action
7. Human dignity is the foundation of all rights
