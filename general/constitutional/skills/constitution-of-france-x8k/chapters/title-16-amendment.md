# Title XVI: Constitutional Revision
**Article: 89**

## Core Idea
Title XVI establishes the procedure for constitutional revision, requiring both parliamentary approval and popular ratification. The procedure is designed to ensure that constitutional changes receive broad support from both the people's representatives and the people themselves.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Initiative (Article 89)
- **Government Initiative**: Revision of the Constitution may be decided upon by the President of the Republic on a proposal from the Prime Minister
- **Parliamentary Initiative**: As well as by members of Parliament meeting in Congress
- **Congress**: For a bill to revise the Constitution to be adopted, the text must be voted by the National Assembly and the Senate in identical terms. The revision shall then be decided by a vote in Congress

### Parliamentary Approval
- **Identical Text**: The National Assembly and Senate must approve identical text
- **Congress Decision**: Revision shall be decided by a vote in Congress

### Popular Ratification
- **Referendum**: A constitutional bill may not be submitted to referendum by the President of the Republic if it has not been adopted in identical terms by both assemblies within the required time limits
- **Approval**: The constitutional revision bill must be approved by referendum
- **Promulgation**: The President of the Republic may decide to submit to a referendum any Government bill which deals with the organization of the public authorities or which provides for the approval of a treaty which, although not contrary to the Constitution, would have provisions incompatible with it

## Amendment Procedure Steps
```
1. Initiative: Government (President on PM proposal) or Parliament meeting in Congress
2. Parliamentary Approval: National Assembly and Senate approve identical text
3. Congress: Congress (joint session of both assemblies) votes on revision
4. Referendum: Constitutional revision is submitted to popular referendum
5. Promulgation: President promulgates approved revision
```

## Mental Models
- Use **dual approval** — both Parliament and people must consent
- Think of **broad consensus** — requires support from representatives and citizens
- Apply **Congress role** — joint session for constitutional decisions

## Anti-patterns
- **Parliamentary only**: Constitutional revision requires popular referendum
- **Government only**: Initiative can come from Government or Parliament
- **Simple majority**: Requires identical approval by both assemblies

## Key Takeaways
1. Constitutional revision can be initiated by Government or Parliament
2. Both National Assembly and Senate must approve identical text
3. Revision requires approval by Congress and by referendum
4. Process ensures broad consensus for constitutional changes
5. President promulgates approved constitutional revisions

## Connects To
- **All Titles**: Constitutional revision can affect any part of Constitution
- **Title II (President)**: President's role in revision process
- **Title IV (Parliament)**: Parliament's role in revision process
