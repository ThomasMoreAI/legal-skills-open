# Preamble

## Core Idea
The Preamble of the 1958 Constitution incorporates by reference three foundational texts that together form the "constitutional block" (bloc de constitutionnalité): the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 1789, the Preamble of the 1946 Constitution, and the Fundamental Principles Recognized by the Laws of the Republic (PFRLR). These texts establish the core values and principles of the French Republic.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Constitutional Block
- **1958 Constitution**: The current governing text establishing the Fifth Republic
- **1789 Declaration**: Foundational statement of natural, inalienable, and sacred rights of man
- **1946 Preamble**: Adds social rights and principles to the constitutional order
- **PFRLR**: Fundamental principles from republican legislation recognized as having constitutional value

### Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (1789)

#### Article 1
- **Freedom and Equality**: Men are born and remain free and equal in rights. Social distinctions may only be founded upon the common good.
- **When to use**: Foundation for all human rights claims
- **How**: Establishes equality and freedom as natural, inalienable rights

#### Article 2
- **Purpose of Political Association**: The aim of all political association is the conservation of the natural and imprescriptible rights of man. These rights are liberty, property, security, and resistance to oppression.
- **When to use**: Understanding the limits of state power
- **How**: State exists to protect, not violate, fundamental rights

#### Article 3
- **National Sovereignty**: The principle of all sovereignty resides essentially in the Nation. No body nor individual may exercise authority which does not proceed from the Nation.
- **When to use**: Legitimacy of state power
- **How**: All authority must trace back to the Nation

#### Article 4
- **Freedom and its Limits**: Liberty consists in the ability to do anything which does not harm others. The exercise of natural rights has no limits except those which secure to other members of society the enjoyment of the same rights.
- **When to use**: Assessing limitations on rights
- **How**: Rights are limited only by rights of others

#### Article 6
- **Law as General Will**: Law is the expression of the general will. All citizens have a right to concur personally or through representatives in its formation. Law must be the same for all, whether it protects or punishes.
- **When to use**: Legislative process and equality before law
- **How**: Law reflects collective will, not arbitrary power

#### Article 8
- **Legal Guarantees**: The law ought to establish only penalties that are strictly and obviously necessary. No one can be punished except by virtue of a law established and promulgated prior to the offense.
- **When to use**: Criminal law principles
- **How**: Nullum crimen, nulla poena sine lege (no crime, no punishment without law)

#### Article 9
- **Presumption of Innocence**: Every man being presumed innocent until declared guilty, if it is judged indispensable to arrest him, every rigor which may not be necessary to secure his person ought to be severely repressed by law.
- **When to use**: Criminal procedure
- **How**: Fundamental guarantee of fair trial

#### Article 10
- **Freedom of Opinion**: No one ought to be molested for his opinions, even religious ones, provided their manifestation does not trouble the public order established by law.
- **When to use**: Freedom of expression and religion
- **How**: Broad protection with public order limitation

#### Article 11
- **Freedom of Communication**: The free communication of thoughts and opinions is one of the most precious rights of man. Every citizen may therefore speak, write and publish freely.
- **When to use**: Freedom of speech and press
- **How**: Core democratic right

### Preamble of the 1946 Constitution

#### Social Rights
- **Equality of Men and Women**: The law guarantees to women equal rights to those of men in all domains
- **Social Security**: The Nation provides to the individual and the family the conditions necessary to their development
- **Work**: Everyone has the duty to work and the right to obtain employment
- **Trade Unions**: All persons may defend their rights and interests through trade union action
- **Right to Strike**: The right to strike is exercised within the framework of laws which regulate it
- **Health**: The Nation guarantees to all, notably to the child, the mother, and the aged worker, protection of health
- **Education**: The Nation guarantees equal access to education, vocational training, and culture to all children
- **Property**: Every person may become the owner of property of any kind
- **Asylum**: Every person persecuted in the name of his activity in favor of liberty has the right of asylum

## Mental Models
- Use **1789 Declaration as foundation** — establishes liberal rights tradition
- Think of **1946 Preamble as expansion** — adds social rights to liberal rights
- Apply **constitutional block as unity** — all texts must be read together

## Anti-patterns
- **Selective reading**: Ignoring one component of the constitutional block
- **Hierarchical interpretation**: Treating 1958 text as superior to 1789 Declaration
- **Frozen interpretation**: Not adapting constitutional principles to modern conditions

## Key Takeaways
1. The French constitutional order rests on a block of texts, not just the 1958 Constitution
2. The 1789 Declaration establishes fundamental liberal rights
3. The 1946 Preamble adds social rights and equality guarantees
4. Constitutional principles evolve through interpretation
5. The constitutional block provides a rich foundation for rights protection

## Connects To
- **All Titles**: Constitutional block principles guide interpretation of all constitutional provisions
- **Title VII (Constitutional Council)**: Council ensures conformity of laws with constitutional block
- **French legal system**: All legislation must respect constitutional block
