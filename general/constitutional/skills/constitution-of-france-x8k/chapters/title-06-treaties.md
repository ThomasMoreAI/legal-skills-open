# Title VI: International Treaties and Agreements
**Articles: 52-55**

## Core Idea
Title VI establishes the procedure for negotiating and ratifying international treaties, distinguishing between those that require parliamentary approval and those that can be approved by the Government alone. It also addresses the relationship between international law and French law.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Treaty Negotiation and Ratification (Article 52)
- **Presidential Role**: The President of the Republic shall negotiate and ratify treaties
- **Categories Requiring Approval**: Peace treaties, trade treaties, treaties or agreements relative to international organization, those committing the finances of the State, those modifying provisions which are of a legislative nature, those relative to the status of persons, and those involving the ceding, exchanging, or adding of territory, may be ratified or approved only by virtue of an act of Parliament
- **Simple Ratification**: They shall take effect only after they have been ratified or approved
- **Publication**: No treaty shall be ratified or approved if it be not compatible with the provisions of the Constitution

### Authorization for Ratification (Article 53)
- **Parliamentary Authorization**: Treaties which commit the finances of the State shall be submitted to Parliament for the authorization to ratify them
- **Implementation Legislation**: They shall not take effect until the act authorizing their ratification has been passed

### Supremacy of Treaties (Article 55)
- **International Law Supremacy**: Treaties or agreements duly ratified or approved shall, from the moment of their publication, have an authority superior to that of domestic laws, subject, for each agreement or treaty, to its application by the other party
- **Reciprocity**: Agreements or treaties which are contrary to the Constitution may not be ratified

### Emergency Treaty Powers
- **Emergency Measures**: If the Constitutional Council, in the conditions provided for under Article 61 below, has declared that an international commitment contains a stipulation contrary to the Constitution, authorization to ratify or approve the international commitment in question may be given only after the revision of the Constitution

## Mental Models
- Use **dualism with supremacy** — treaties have authority superior to domestic laws
- Think of **parliamentary control** — important treaties require parliamentary approval
- Apply **constitutional conformity** — no treaty contrary to Constitution can be ratified

## Anti-patterns
- **Automatic supremacy**: Treaties only superior to laws if reciprocally applied
- **Constitutional violation**: Treaties contrary to Constitution cannot be ratified
- **Bypassing Parliament**: Treaties committing finances require parliamentary authorization

## Key Takeaways
1. President negotiates and ratifies treaties
2. Important treaties (peace, trade, international organizations, financial commitments, etc.) require parliamentary approval
3. Ratified treaties have authority superior to domestic laws
4. Treaties contrary to Constitution cannot be ratified without constitutional amendment
5. Constitutional Council reviews treaty conformity with Constitution
