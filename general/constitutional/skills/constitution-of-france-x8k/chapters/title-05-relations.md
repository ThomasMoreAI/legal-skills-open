# Title V: Relations between the Government and Parliament
**Articles: 34-51**

## Core Idea
Title V defines the respective domains of law and regulation, establishes the legislative procedure, and sets the conditions under which the Government is responsible to Parliament. This title creates the framework of "rationalized parliamentarism" that distinguishes the Fifth Republic from the Fourth.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Legislative Domain (Article 34)
- **Matters Reserved to Statute**: Parliament determines the rules concerning: civil rights and fundamental guarantees accorded to citizens for the exercise of their public liberties; the obligations imposed upon the person and the property of citizens for the purposes of national defense and the preservation of the environment; nationality, the status and capacity of persons, marriage contracts, inheritance, and gifts; the determination of criminal offenses and the penalties therefor; criminal procedure; the creation of new categories of courts and the status of the judiciary; the status of civil servants; the general electoral system for the election of the President of the Republic, the deputies, and the senators; the free administration of local collectivities, their powers, and their resources
- **Budget**: The finance laws shall determine the resources and obligations of the State under the conditions and with the reservations provided for by an organic act
- **Social Security**: The finance laws shall determine, for a given financial year, the resources and obligations of social security, its operating expenses, and its objectives in terms of economic and social equilibrium, under the conditions and with the reservations provided for by an organic act

### Regulatory Domain (Article 37)
- **Government Regulation**: Matters other than those coming under the domain of statute shall be of a regulatory nature. Decrees taken in the Council of Ministers shall be countersigned by the Prime Minister
- **Autonomous Regulations**: Regulations to which organic acts or ordinary acts have granted independent power to the authorities other than the Prime Minister for the implementation of the said acts may only be issued after consultation with the Council of State

### Organic Laws (Article 46)
- **Procedure**: Organic acts may be amended, under the same conditions, by virtue of an act passed by the Senate and the National Assembly by identical terms
- **Senate Consent**: A joint committee composed of an equal number of members representing each Assembly may be charged with considering the provisions in dispute
- **Final Decision**: If the joint committee cannot propose a common text, or if this text is not adopted within the required time limits by the two Assemblies, the Government may request the National Assembly to give a final decision

### Legislative Procedure
- **Government Bills**: Government bills shall be discussed in the Council of Ministers after deliberation by the Council of State and shall be deposited on the bureau of one of the two Assemblies (Article 39)
- **Private Members' Bills**: Private members' bills shall be deposited on the bureau of the first Assembly by which they were adopted (Article 39)
- **Committee Stage**: Bills and private members' bills, having been deposited, shall be sent for consideration to one of the permanent committees whose number is limited to six in each Assembly (Article 43)
- **Amendments**: The right of amendment is exercised in commission and in public session. After the opening of the debate, the Government may object to any amendment which has not previously been submitted to the committee (Article 44)

### Government Responsibility (Articles 49-50)
- **Confidence Vote**: The Prime Minister, after deliberation by the Council of Ministers, shall commit before the National Assembly the responsibility of the Government on its program or else, if it should so require, on a declaration of general policy (Article 49.1)
- **Motion of Censure**: The National Assembly may bring down the Government by the adoption of a motion of censure. Such a motion shall not be admissible unless it is signed by at least one tenth of the members of the National Assembly. The vote may not take place until forty-eight hours after the motion has been filed (Article 49.2)
- **Trust Vote**: When, as a result of a vote, a finance bill or a social security finance bill has not been adopted within seventy days following its deposit, the provisions of the bill may be put into effect by ordinance (Article 49.3)
- **Automatic Adoption**: Within forty-eight hours after the adoption of the motion of censure, the Prime Minister shall hand to the President of the Republic the resignation of the Government (Article 50)

### Time Limits (Article 45)
- **Shuttle (Navette)**: Every bill or private member's bill shall be examined successively in the two Assemblies of Parliament with a view to the adoption of an identical text
- **45-Day Rule**: Without prejudice to the application of Articles 40 and 41, all amendments which are not accepted or all parts of a bill or private member's bill which have not been adopted by the first Assembly before which it was pending shall be submitted to the second Assembly and discussed for consideration of the text voted by the first Assembly
- **Conference Committee**: The Prime Minister, the President of the National Assembly, and the President of the Senate may, by common agreement, for a limited period and for a single subject matter, create a joint commission composed of an equal number of members from the National Assembly and from the Senate charged with proposing a text on the provisions remaining under discussion

## Mental Models
- Use **domain distinction** — law vs. regulation clearly separated
- Think of **rationalized parliamentarism** — Government controls legislative agenda, Parliament has limited power to amend
- Apply **Government responsibility** — National Assembly can remove Government but with high threshold

## Anti-patterns
- **Parliamentary obstruction**: Government can use Article 49.3 to bypass parliamentary opposition
- **Regulatory overreach**: Government cannot regulate in areas reserved for statute
- **Censure abuse**: Motion of censure requires 1/10 of deputies and 48-hour delay

## Key Takeaways
1. Parliament has exclusive legislative domain (Art. 34) while Government has regulatory domain (Art. 37)
2. Organic laws require special procedure with Senate consent
3. Government controls legislative agenda through committee stage
4. National Assembly can remove Government through motion of censure (49.2)
5. Rationalized parliamentarism limits parliamentary power to prevent instability

## Connects To
- **Title III (Government)**: Government exercises regulatory power
- **Title IV (Parliament)**: Parliament exercises legislative power
- **Title II (President)**: President can use Article 12 to dissolve Assembly
