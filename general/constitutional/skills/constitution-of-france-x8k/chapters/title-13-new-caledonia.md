# Title XIII: New Caledonia
**Articles: 76-77**

## Core Idea
Title XIII establishes a special status for New Caledonia within the French Republic, recognizing its unique situation and providing for a process of gradual devolution of powers. This title reflects France's approach to accommodating territorial diversity within the unitary State.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Special Status (Article 76)
- **Population**: New Caledonia comprises the population living in New Caledonia and the Loyalty Islands
- **Citizenship**: There shall be a New Caledonian citizenship, as provided for by organic act
- **Competencies**: The Republic shall organize, with and for the New Caledonian population, the sharing of sovereign powers with New Caledonia under the conditions provided for by this Title
- **Gradual Devolution**: The law may, in the economic, social, and environmental fields, fix the conditions and limits within which the powers of the State may be transferred to New Caledonia

### Institutional Organization
- **Local Institutions**: The organization, other than the court system, the functioning of the institutions of New Caledonia proper, and the rules governing New Caledonian citizenship shall be fixed and modified by deliberation of the Congress adopted by a majority of three-fifths of its members
- **Irreversibility**: Such modification shall take effect only if approved by a majority of three-fifths of the New Caledonian electors at a referendum

### Final Provisions (Article 77)
- **Referendum**: After twenty years of application of the provisions resulting from the agreements signed on 5 May 1998 and approved by the New Caledonian people on 8 November 1998, the Congress of New Caledonia may decide to consult the New Caledonian electors on a new accord which it shall propose for the organization of the public powers in New Caledonia
- **International Personality**: The new accord shall be submitted to the French people for approval. If approved, the President of the Republic shall sign the organic act or acts for its implementation

## Mental Models
- Use **special status as recognition** — New Caledonia's unique identity within Republic
- Think of **gradual devolution** — powers transferred progressively
- Apply **New Caledonian citizenship** — distinct from French citizenship

## Anti-patterns
- **Independence**: Special status is within French Republic, not separation
- **Unilateral devolution**: Power transfers require agreement and legal process
- **Ignoring local will**: Modifications require New Caledonian approval

## Key Takeaways
1. New Caledonia has special status within French Republic
2. New Caledonian citizenship is distinct from French citizenship
3. Powers are shared between Republic and New Caledonia
4. Gradual devolution of powers in economic, social, environmental fields
5. Institutional organization can be modified by Congress with popular approval

## Connects To
- **Title XII (Territorial Collectivities)**: New Caledonia is a special territorial collectivity
- **Title I (Sovereignty)**: Sovereignty is shared between Republic and New Caledonia
- **Constitution**: Special status respects principle of territorial integrity
