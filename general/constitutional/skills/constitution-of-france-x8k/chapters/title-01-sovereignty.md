# Title I: Of Sovereignty
**Articles: 2-4**

## Core Idea
Title I establishes the foundational principles of the French Republic: its nature as an indivisible, secular, democratic, and social Republic, and the principle of national sovereignty. These articles define France's constitutional identity and the source of all state power.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Article 1: Nature of the Republic
- **Indivisible Republic**: France is an indivisible Republic (Art. 1)
- **Secular Republic**: France is a secular (laïque) Republic
- **Democratic Republic**: France is a democratic Republic
- **Social Republic**: France is a social Republic
  - When to use: Understanding French constitutional identity
  - How: These four adjectives define the nature and values of the French State

### Republican Values
- **Equality Before Law**: The Republic shall ensure the equality of all citizens before the law without distinction of origin, race, or religion (Art. 1)
- **Respect for All Beliefs**: The Republic shall respect all beliefs (Art. 1)
- **Motto**: The motto of the Republic is "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity" (Art. 2)
- **National Emblems**: The national emblem is the blue, white, and red tricolor flag. The national anthem is La Marseillaise. The principle of the Republic is: Government of the people, by the people, and for the people. (Art. 2)

### National Sovereignty (Article 3)
- **Source of Sovereignty**: National sovereignty belongs to the people who shall exercise it through their representatives and by means of referendum (Art. 3.1)
- **Voting Rights**: All male and female French citizens, who have attained their majority and are in possession of their civil and political rights, may vote as provided for by law (Art. 3.2)
- **Universal Suffrage**: Voting may be either direct or indirect as provided for by the Constitution and it shall always be by universal, equal, and secret ballot (Art. 3.3)
  - When to use: Understanding democratic participation
  - How: Sovereignty exercised through representative democracy and direct democracy

### National Languages (Article 2)
- **French Language**: The language of the Republic is French
- **Regional Languages**: Regional languages belong to the heritage of France

## Key Concepts
- **Indivisibility**: The Republic cannot be divided; France is one and united
- **Secularism (Laïcité)**: State neutrality in religious matters; religion is private, not public
- **Democracy**: Power derived from the people through elections
- **Social State**: State has positive obligations to promote social welfare
- **Equality**: Formal equality before law, with special measures for substantive equality
- **Universal Suffrage**: All adult citizens have right to vote
- **Representative Democracy**: Sovereignty exercised through elected representatives
- **Direct Democracy**: Sovereignty also exercised through referendum

## Mental Models
- Use **four adjectives as interpretive lens** — indivisible, secular, democratic, social define the Republic
- Think of **sovereignty as national, not popular** — belongs to the Nation, not directly to individuals
- Apply **secularism as neutrality** — State does not recognize or fund any religion
- Use **social Republic principle** — State must actively promote social justice

## Anti-patterns
- **Communitarianism**: Recognizing group rights based on origin, race, or religion violates equality
- **State religion**: Any establishment of official religion violates secularism
- **Exclusion from suffrage**: Limiting voting rights without constitutional basis violates universal suffrage
- **Divisibility**: Any suggestion that the Republic can be divided violates indivisibility

## Key Takeaways
1. France is an indivisible, secular, democratic, and social Republic
2. National sovereignty belongs to the people, exercised through representatives and referendum
3. Equality before law is a constitutional guarantee
4. The Republic respects all beliefs but does not recognize any religion
5. Universal, equal, and secret suffrage is the foundation of democracy

## Connects To
- **Preamble**: Constitutional block establishes the values that Title I implements
- **Title II (President)**: President represents the Republic defined in Title I
- **Title IV (Parliament)**: Parliament exercises sovereignty on behalf of the people
- **All constitutional rights**: Rights are guaranteed within the framework of the Republic
