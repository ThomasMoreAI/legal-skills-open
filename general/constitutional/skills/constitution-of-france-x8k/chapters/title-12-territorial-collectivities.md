# Title XII: Territorial Collectivities
**Articles: 72-75**

## Core Idea
Title XII establishes the organization of the French Republic into territorial collectivities with self-government. France is a unitary but decentralized State, with local authorities exercising powers devolved from the central State.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Principle of Free Administration (Article 72)
- **Free Administration**: The territorial collectivities of the Republic shall be communes, departments, regions, special collectivities, and collectivities with special status
- **Self-Government**: These collectivities shall freely administer themselves by means of elected councils and under the conditions provided for by the law
- **Devolution**: In the collectivities, the representative of the State, representing each of the members of the Government, shall be responsible for national interests, administrative control, and for ensuring compliance with the law

### Local Powers (Article 72-2)
- **Devolution**: The territorial collectivities shall have the power to make decisions in all matters in which their competence is established
- **Conditions**: Within the conditions provided for by the Constitution and the organic acts, and save where the law or the Constitution has made provision for the State in a specific field, the territorial collectivities shall have the power to make decisions in all matters in which their competence is established

### Experimental Powers (Article 72-4)
- **Derogation**: Any territorial collectivity may, with regard to the exercise of its powers, derogate, for a limited purpose and for a limited duration, from the provisions of a law or regulation which regulate the exercise of its powers
- **Authorization**: Derogations may not be authorized when the constitutional principles or powers concerning the territory, the liberty, the property, or the equality before the law are called into question

### Financial Autonomy (Article 72-2)
- **Own Resources**: The territorial collectivities may be granted enhanced powers as provided for by an act or, as the case may be, by the Constitution. An act may provide for the devolution to the territorial collectivities of the resources necessary for the exercise of their powers
- **Compensation**: Any transfer of powers between territorial collectivities shall be accompanied by the transfer of the corresponding resources

### Specific Collectivities
- **Overseas**: The legislator may, by statute, as the case may be, create territorial collectivities in the overseas territories governed by Article 73, which may be substituted for a department and a region, or create a collectivity with special status within the Republic
- **Special Status**: The rules applicable to the special status collectivities shall be fixed by an organic act, it being specified which of the rules applicable to overseas territories are applicable to them

## Mental Models
- Use **free administration principle** — local authorities govern themselves freely
- Think of **devolution** — State transfers powers to territorial collectivities
- Apply **financial autonomy** — resources follow competencies
- Use **experimental powers** — allows for local innovation

## Anti-patterns
- **Centralization**: State cannot interfere in local matters without legal basis
- **Unfunded mandates**: Transfer of powers requires transfer of resources
- **Unlimited derogation**: Experimental powers cannot violate constitutional principles

## Key Takeaways
1. France has territorial collectivities: communes, departments, regions, special collectivities
2. Collectivities freely administer themselves through elected councils
3. State representative ensures compliance with national interests and law
4. Collectivities have own resources and can receive devolved powers
5. Experimental powers allow for local innovation within constitutional limits

## Connects To
- **Title I (Sovereignty)**: Territorial collectivities are part of indivisible Republic
- **Title XV (EU)**: Collectivities participate in EU matters
- **Constitution**: Decentralization implements principle of local self-government
