# Title II: The President of the Republic
**Articles: 5-19**

## Core Idea
The President of the Republic is the Head of State, elected by universal direct suffrage, who embodies national unity and ensures respect for the Constitution. The 1958 Constitution created a strong presidency as a response to the weaknesses of the Fourth Republic, establishing France's characteristic semi-presidential system.

## Frameworks Introduived

### Presidential Election (Article 6)
- **Universal Direct Suffrage**: The President of the Republic shall be elected by universal direct suffrage (since 1962 constitutional amendment)
- **Majority System**: Absolute majority required; if not obtained on first ballot, second ballot held with only top two candidates
- **Eligibility**: Any French citizen aged 23 or over, enjoying civil and political rights
- **Term**: 5 years (since 2000, previously 7 years), renewable once

### Presidential Powers and Duties (Article 5)
- **Role**: The President of the Republic shall ensure due respect for the Constitution. He shall ensure, by his arbitration, the proper functioning of the public authorities and the continuity of the State.
- **Guarantee of National Independence**: He shall be the guarantor of national independence, territorial integrity, and due respect for treaties.
  - When to use: Understanding the President's constitutional role
  - How: President as arbiter, not ruler; ensures constitutional order

### Executive Powers
- **Head of Executive**: The President of the Republic shall be the Head of the executive power (Art. 20)
- **Chair of Council of Ministers**: The President of the Republic shall preside over the Council of Ministers (Art. 9)
- **Appointment Power**: The President of the Republic shall appoint the Prime Minister. He shall terminate the functions of the Prime Minister when the latter tenders the resignation of the Government. On the proposal of the Prime Minister, he shall appoint the other members of the Government and terminate their functions (Art. 8)
- **Cabinet**: The President of the Republic shall have his own Cabinet

### Legislative Powers
- **Dissolution**: The President of the Republic may dissolve the National Assembly (Art. 12)
- **Referendum**: The President of the Republic may, on a proposal from the Government or on a joint motion of the two Assemblies, submit to a referendum any Government bill which deals with the organization of the public authorities or which provides for the approval of a treaty (Art. 11)
- **Message to Parliament**: The President of the Republic may send messages to the two Assemblies (Art. 18)
- **Promulgation**: The President of the Republic shall promulgate the statutes within fifteen days following the transmission to the Government of the definitive adoption of a bill (Art. 10)

### Exceptional Powers (Article 16)
- **Emergency Powers**: When the institutions of the Republic, the independence of the Nation, the integrity of its territory, or the fulfillment of its international commitments are under serious and immediate threat, and when the regular operation of the constitutional public authorities is interrupted, the President of the Republic shall take the measures demanded by these circumstances
- **Conditions**: After formal consultation with the Prime Minister and the Presidents of the Assemblies
- **Information**: The Nation shall be informed of these measures by a message
- **Time Limit**: These measures must be dictated by the desire to provide as quickly as possible the constitutional public authorities with the means to accomplish their mission
- **Parliamentary Oversight**: The Constitutional Council shall be consulted with respect to such measures. Parliament shall meet as of right
- **Termination**: The application of these measures shall cease when the conditions which gave rise to them no longer obtain

### Diplomatic and Military Powers
- **Treaty Negotiation**: The President of the Republic shall negotiate and ratify treaties (Art. 52)
- **Ambassadors**: The President of the Republic shall accredit ambassadors and envoys extraordinary to foreign powers; foreign ambassadors and envoys extraordinary shall be accredited to him (Art. 14)
- **Command of Armed Forces**: The President of the Republic shall be the Head of the Armed Forces. He shall preside over the higher national defense councils and committees (Art. 15)
- **Nuclear Deterrence**: The President is the sole authority for nuclear weapons use

### Other Powers
- **Pardon**: The President of the Republic shall have the right to pardon individual offenders (Art. 17)
- **Amnesty**: Collective pardons (amnesties) require Parliamentary approval
- **Honorary Powers**: The President of the Republic shall confer the dignities, offices, and honors of the State

### Presidential Acts and Counter-signature (Article 19)
- **Acts of the President**: The acts of the President of the Republic other than those provided for under Articles 8 (1st paragraph), 11, 12, 16, 18, 54, 56, and 61 shall be countersigned by the Prime Minister and, where required, by the Ministers responsible
- **Counter-signature Requirement**: Ensures Ministerial responsibility for presidential acts

### Presidential Immunity (Article 67)
- **Inviolability**: The President of the Republic may not be required to testify before any French jurisdictional authority. No investigative, judicial, or administrative proceedings or actions may be undertaking against him
- **Limited to Office**: This immunity applies only for acts related to official functions, not private acts
- **After Term**: The above provisions shall not prevent actions or proceedings from being undertaken against the President of the Republic before the High Court of Justice as provided in Article 68

## Mental Models
- Use **President as arbiter** — ensures constitutional functioning and continuity
- Think of **semi-presidentialism as balance** — strong President but Government responsible to Parliament
- Apply **Article 16 as exceptional** — emergency powers for extraordinary circumstances only
- Use **counter-signature as check** — Ministers share responsibility for presidential acts

## Anti-patterns
- **President as ruler**: President exceeding arbitration role violates Article 5
- **Routine Article 16**: Using emergency powers for non-extraordinary situations
- **Uncountersigned acts**: Presidential acts without Ministerial countersignature are invalid
- **Absolute immunity**: Immunity does not apply to private acts or after term

## Key Takeaways
1. President is Head of State, elected by universal direct suffrage
2. President ensures respect for Constitution and proper functioning of institutions
3. President has significant powers but most require counter-signature
4. Article 16 provides exceptional emergency powers with safeguards
5. President has immunity during term but can be held accountable after

## Connects To
- **Title I (Sovereignty)**: President represents the Nation and ensures its sovereignty
