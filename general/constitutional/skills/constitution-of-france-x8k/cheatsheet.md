# Cheatsheet - Constitution of the French Fifth Republic (1958)

## 📋 Quick Reference: Constitutional Structure

### The Constitutional Block
```
1958 Constitution
├── Incorporates by reference:
│   ├── 1789 Declaration of Rights of Man and Citizen (Liberal rights)
│   ├── 1946 Preamble (Social rights)
│   └── PFRLR - Fundamental Principles Recognized by Laws of the Republic
└── Forms unified constitutional foundation
```

### Republican Principles (Article 2)
| Principle | Meaning | Application |
|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Indivisible** | Cannot be divided | One unified Republic |
| **Secular (Laïque)** | State neutrality in religion | No state religion, respects all beliefs |
| **Democratic** | Power from the people | Universal suffrage, representative government |
| **Social** | State welfare obligations | Social security, right to work, education |

### National Symbols (Article 2)
- **Motto**: "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity"
- **Flag**: Blue, white, and red tricolor
- **Anthem**: La Marseillaise
- **Language**: French (official), regional languages part of heritage

## 🏛️ Institutional Framework

### Executive Branch: Dual Structure

#### The President of the Republic (Title II)
```
Election: Universal direct suffrage, 5-year term (quinquennat), renewable once
Majority: Absolute majority (1st ballot), or simple majority (2nd ballot with top 2)
Eligibility: French citizen, age 23+, civil & political rights
```

**Powers:**
- Head of State, ensures respect for Constitution (Art. 5)
- Guarantees national independence, territorial integrity (Art. 5)
- Head of Armed Forces, nuclear weapons authority (Art. 15)
- Appoints Prime Minister and Ministers (Art. 8)
- Presides over Council of Ministers (Art. 9)
- May dissolve National Assembly (Art. 12)
- May submit bills to referendum (Art. 11)
- Negotiates and ratifies treaties (Art. 52)
- Accredits ambassadors (Art. 14)
- Right to pardon (Art. 17)
- Emergency powers under Article 16

**Limits:**
- Most acts require Prime Minister counter-signature (Art. 19)
- Inviolability during term for official acts (Art. 67)
- Cannot be required to testify (Art. 67)
- After term, can be tried by High Court of Justice (Art. 68)

#### The Government (Title III)
```
Composition: Prime Minister + Ministers (collective body)
Appointment: President appoints PM; PM proposes other Ministers
Responsibility: Collectively responsible to Parliament (Art. 20)
```

**Prime Minister Powers:**
- Directs Government action (Art. 21)
- Ensures execution of laws (Art. 21)
- Has regulatory power (Art. 21)
- Appoints to civil and military offices (Art. 21)
- May delegate powers to Ministers (Art. 21)

---

### Legislative Branch: Asymmetrical Bicameralism

#### Parliament (Title IV)
```
Composition:
├── National Assembly: 577 deputies
│   ├── Election: Direct universal suffrage, majority two-round
│   ├── Term: 5 years (unless dissolved)
│   └── Powers: Primacy in legislative matters, can remove Government
└── Senate: 348 senators
    ├── Election: Indirect suffrage by electoral college
    ├── Term: 6 years (renewed by halves every 3 years)
    └── Powers: Advisory, delaying role
```

**Parliamentary Functions:**
- Exercises legislative power (Art. 24)
- Controls Government action (Art. 24)
- Evaluates public policies (Art. 24)

**Parliamentary Immunity:**
- Cannot be prosecuted for opinions/votes in exercise of functions (Art. 26)
- Cannot be arrested without Assembly authorization (except flagrante delicto) (Art. 27)
- Detention/prosecution suspended if Assembly demands by majority (Art. 27)

## ⚖️ Legislative Process (Title V)

### Domain of Law vs. Regulation

| **Domain of Law (Article 34)** | **Domain of Regulation (Article 37)** |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Civil rights & fundamental guarantees | All matters not in legislative domain |
| Criminal law & procedure | Implemented by Government decrees |
| Electoral system | |
| Taxation | |
| Social security financing | |
| National defense obligations | |
| Status of persons (nationality, marriage) | |
| Civil servant status | |
| Judiciary organization | |
| Territorial collectivities | |

### Legislative Procedure
```
1. Initiative: Government, Parliament members
2. Committee Stage: Examined by one of 6 permanent committees per Assembly
3. Amendment: Can be proposed in committee and public session
4. Shuttle (Navette): Goes back and forth between assemblies
5. Conference Committee: Can be created for disputed provisions (limited time, single subject)
6. Final Decision: National Assembly can give final decision on Government request
```

### Organic Laws (Special Procedure)
- **Matters**: Electoral system, Senate powers, Constitutional Council, judiciary status, territorial collectivities
- **Procedure**: Must be approved by both assemblies in identical terms
- **Dispute Resolution**: Joint committee, then National Assembly final decision

### Government Responsibility Mechanisms
| Mechanism | Threshold | Effect |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Motion of Censure** | 1/10 of deputies to sign, 48-hour delay, absolute majority to pass | Brings down Government |
| **Article 49.3** | None (Government decision) | Bill adopted unless motion of censure passed within 24 hours |
| **Confidence Vote** | Simple majority | Government program or general policy approved |

## 🌍 International Relations (Title VI)

### Treaty Categories

| **Category** | **Approval Required** | **Examples** |
|-------------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Parliamentary Approval** | Yes (Act of Parliament) | Peace treaties, trade treaties, international organization treaties, treaties committing state finances, modifying legislative provisions, status of persons, territorial changes |
| **Simple Ratification** | No (President alone) | Other international agreements |

### International Law Hierarchy
```
Constitution (Supreme)
↑
Treaties (duly ratified) - Authority superior to domestic laws (Art. 55)
↑
Domestic Laws
↑
Regulations
```

**Conditions for Treaty Supremacy:**
- Duly ratified or approved
- Published
- Subject to reciprocity (other party must also apply)

**Constitutional Limit:** No treaty contrary to Constitution may be ratified (Art. 54). If Constitutional Council declares incompatibility, constitutional revision required.

## ⚖️ Constitutional Review & Justice

### Constitutional Council (Title VII)
```
Composition: 9 members (3 by President, 3 by Assembly President, 3 by Senate President) + former Presidents as life members
Term: 9 years, non-renewable, renewed by 1/3 every 3 years
```

**Jurisdiction:**
- Abstract review of organic acts and parliamentary rules before implementation
- Concrete review of laws referred by President, PM, or Assembly Presidents
- 60-day referral review
- Electoral supervision (presidential, parliamentary elections, referendums)
- Reviews President's Article 16 emergency measures

**Decision Characteristics:**
- Final and not subject to appeal
- Binding on all public authorities and jurisdictional authorities
- Unconstitutional provisions cannot be promulgated or applied

---

### High Court of Justice (Title IX)
```
Composition: Parliament members meeting together, elects its own president
Jurisdiction: Only court that can try President for official acts
Standard: Grave and characterized violation of duties
Procedure: Two-thirds majority of Parliament required
```

### Court of Justice of the Republic (Title X)
```
Composition: 2/3 parliamentary members, 1/3 Supreme Judicial Court members
Jurisdiction: Government members for acts in official capacity
Indictment: Requires vote of both Assemblies in identical terms or High Court decision
Immunity: Protection from coercive measures without court authorization
```

---

## 🏛️ Judiciary (Title VIII)

### Judicial Independence Framework
```
Guarantor: President of Republic (Art. 64)
Supervision: Superior Council of the Judiciary (CSM)
Protection: Magistrates of the seat are irremovable (Art. 64)
```

**Superior Council of the Judiciary (CSM):**
- **Composition**: Chaired by President, includes Minister of Justice, judges from various courts, public prosecutors, advocates, notable persons
- **Appointment**: 3 members each by President, Assembly President, Senate President
- **Functions**: Ensures judicial independence, opinions on appointments, disciplinary matters
- **Term**: 4 years, non-renewable

---

## 🗺️ Territorial Organization

### Territorial Collectivities (Title XII)
```
Types:
├── Communes (municipalities)
├── Departments
├── Regions
├── Special collectivities
└── Collectivities with special status
```

**Principles:**
- Free administration through elected councils (Art. 72)
- Devolution of powers from State (Art. 72)
- State representative ensures compliance with national interests and law (Art. 72)
- Financial autonomy: resources follow competencies (Art. 72-2)
- Experimental powers: can derogate from laws/regulations for limited purpose and duration (Art. 72-4)

### New Caledonia (Title XIII)
- **Status**: Special territorial collectivity within Republic
- **Citizenship**: New Caledonian citizenship distinct from French citizenship
- **Sovereignty**: Shared between Republic and New Caledonia
- **Devolution**: Gradual transfer of powers in economic, social, environmental fields
- **Modification**: Requires 3/5 majority in Congress + popular approval in referendum

## 💼 Economic & Social Institutions

### Economic and Social Council (Title XI)
```
Composition: Representatives of economic and social categories
Appointment: By organic act
Functions: Advisory body
Mandatory Consultation: Government must consult on economic/social/environmental plans/programs
Reporting: Annual report to President, PM, and Assembly Presidents
```

---

## 🇪🇺 European Union Integration (Title XV)

### EU Participation Framework
- **Membership**: Republic participates in European Communities and EU per Maastricht Treaty (Art. 88-1)
- **Elections**: Law may provide for universal direct suffrage for European Parliament representatives
- **Subsidiarity**: Draft EU legislative acts may be submitted to French Parliament or Senate (Art. 88-3)
- **Parliamentary Resolutions**: Each Assembly may adopt motivated resolutions on draft EU acts
- **Accession Treaties**: Government bill authorizing ratification submitted to referendum by President (Art. 88-5)
- **Constitutional Incompatibility**: Treaties with provisions incompatible with Constitution require constitutional revision

## 📝 Constitutional Amendment (Title XVI)

### Amendment Procedure (Article 89)
```
1. Initiative: President on PM proposal OR Parliament meeting in Congress
2. Parliamentary Approval: National Assembly AND Senate must approve IDENTICAL text
3. Congress: Congress (joint session) votes on revision
4. Referendum: Constitutional revision submitted to popular referendum
5. Promulgation: President promulgates approved revision
```

**Characteristics:**
- No eternity clauses (except implicitly through difficult process)
- Requires broad consensus (both chambers + popular approval)
- Both Government and Parliament can initiate
- Process ensures democratic legitimacy

## 🎯 Core Rights & Principles Summary

### From 1789 Declaration (Incorporated by Reference)
- Freedom and equality (Article 1)
- Purpose of political association: conservation of natural rights (Article 2)
- National sovereignty resides in the Nation (Article 3)
- Liberty consists in ability to do anything not harming others (Article 4)
- Law expresses general will (Article 6)
- Presumption of innocence (Article 9)
- Freedom of opinion (Article 10)
- Freedom of communication (Article 11)

### From 1946 Preamble (Incorporated by Reference)
- Equality of men and women
- Social security rights
- Right to work and obtain employment
- Trade union rights
- Right to strike (within legal framework)
- Health protection
- Equal access to education, vocational training, culture
- Asylum for persons persecuted for liberty

### From 1958 Constitution
- Republican form: indivisible, secular, democratic, social
- Equality before law without distinction
- Universal, equal, secret suffrage
- Presidential system with dual executive
- Asymmetrical bicameralism
- Rationalized parliamentarism
- Constitutional review
- Territorial decentralization
- Judicial independence

## 📊 Comparison Tables

### French vs. Italian vs. Spanish Constitutional Systems

| Feature | France (1958) | Italy (1948) | Spain (1978) |
|---------|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Executive System** | Semi-presidential | Parliamentary | Parliamentary (with constitutional monarchy) |
| **Head of State** | President (direct election) | President (parliamentary election) | King (hereditary) |
| **Head of Government** | Prime Minister | Prime Minister | President of Government |
| **Legislature** | Bicameral (asymmetrical) | Bicameral (perfect) | Bicameral (asymmetrical) |
| **Lower House** | National Assembly (577) | Chamber of Deputies (400) | Congress of Deputies (350) |
| **Upper House** | Senate (348, indirect) | Senate (~200, mixed) | Senate (~266, regional) |
| **Constitutional Court** | Constitutional Council (9 members) | Constitutional Court (15 judges) | Constitutional Court (12 judges) |
| **Amendment Process** | Parliament + Referendum | Parliament (special) + optional Referendum | Parliament (3/5) + optional Referendum |
| **Rigid Constitution?** | Yes (but no eternity clause) | Yes (republican form unamendable) | Yes (some protected provisions) |
| **Federal/Unitary** | Unitary with decentralization | Unitary with regional autonomy | Unitary with autonomous communities |

### French Presidential Powers: Checks and Balances

| Power | Check/Balance |
|-------|---------------|
| Appoints Prime Minister | Must have parliamentary confidence |
| Dissolves National Assembly | Limited by timing (not in last 6 months, not during war) |
| Article 16 emergency powers | Consultation required, Constitutional Council review, Parliament meets as of right |
| Treaty ratification | Parliamentary approval required for important treaties |
| Promulgates laws | 15-day limit, Constitutional Council review possible |
| Council of Ministers presidency | Government collectively responsible to Parliament |
| Most presidential acts | Require Prime Minister counter-signature |

## 🎯 Key Takeaways for Quick Reference

### 10 Most Important Constitutional Principles
1. **Constitutional Block**: 1958 Constitution + 1789 Declaration + 1946 Preamble + PFRLR
2. **Republican Identity**: Indivisible, secular, democratic, social
3. **Sovereignty**: Belongs to the people, exercised through representatives and referendum
4. **Semi-Presidentialism**: Strong President + Government responsible to Parliament
5. **Rationalized Parliamentarism**: Limited parliamentary power to remove governments
6. **Constitutional Review**: Constitutional Council ensures conformity with Constitution
7. **Judicial Independence**: President is guarantor, CSM ensures autonomy
8. **Territorial Decentralization**: Free administration of territorial collectivities
9. **Treaty Supremacy**: Duly ratified treaties superior to domestic laws (with reciprocity)
10. **Amendment Process**: Requires parliamentary consensus + popular referendum

### Quick Decision Tree: Which Institution?
```
Is it about...
├── Constitutional interpretation? → Constitutional Council
├── Presidential accountability? → High Court of Justice
├── Ministerial accountability? → Court of Justice of the Republic
├── Legislative matters? → Parliament (National Assembly primacy)
├── Executive leadership? → President (with PM counter-signature)
├── Day-to-day governance? → Government (Prime Minister + Ministers)
├── Judicial matters? → Independent judiciary (with CSM oversight)
├── Local matters? → Territorial collectivities (with State oversight)
└── EU matters? → Parliament + Government (with treaty procedures)
```
