## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Constitutional Block (Preamble)
**Use when**: Understanding the foundation of French constitutional law and rights
- **Core Idea**: The 1958 Constitution incorporates by reference three foundational texts: the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 1789, the Preamble of the 1946 Constitution, and the Fundamental Principles Recognized by the Laws of the Republic (PFRLR)
- **1789 Declaration**: Establishes natural, inalienable, and sacred rights of man: liberty, property, security, and resistance to oppression
- **1946 Preamble**: Adds social rights and principles: equality of men and women, social security, right to work, trade union rights, health protection, education access
- **PFRLR**: Fundamental principles from republican legislation with constitutional value
- **Application**: All constitutional interpretation must consider the constitutional block as a unified whole

### Republican Principles (Title I - Articles 2-4)
**Use when**: Analyzing French constitutional identity and fundamental values
- **Core Idea**: France is an **indivisible, secular (laïque), democratic, and social Republic**
- **Republic Nature**: Indivisible (cannot be divided), Secular (state neutrality in religious matters), Democratic (power from the people), Social (state has positive obligations for social welfare)
- **Equality Before Law**: The Republic shall ensure the equality of all citizens before the law without distinction of origin, race, or religion
- **Respect for All Beliefs**: The Republic shall respect all beliefs
- **National Motto**: "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity"
- **National Symbols**: Tricolor flag, La Marseillaise anthem, principle: "Government of the people, by the people, and for the people"

### National Sovereignty (Article 3)
**Use when**: Understanding the source of state power in France
- **Core Idea**: National sovereignty belongs to the people who shall exercise it through their representatives and by means of referendum
- **Universal Suffrage**: All French citizens (men and women) who have attained majority and possess civil and political rights may vote
- **Voting Methods**: Direct or indirect suffrage as provided by Constitution, always by universal, equal, and secret ballot
- **French Language**: The language of the Republic is French; regional languages belong to the heritage of France

### The Semi-Presidential System (Titles II-III)
**Use when**: Analyzing executive power distribution in France
- **Core Idea**: France operates a **semi-presidential system** with a strong President and a Government responsible to Parliament
- **President as Arbiter**: Ensures due respect for the Constitution, by arbitration ensures proper functioning of public authorities and continuity of the State (Art. 5)
- **Dual Executive**: President (Head of State) + Government headed by Prime Minister (Head of Government)
- **Balance of Power**: President presides over Council of Ministers, but Government is collectively responsible to Parliament

### Presidential Powers and Limits (Title II - Articles 5-19)
**Use when**: Analyzing the role and authority of the French President
- **Election**: Universal direct suffrage, absolute majority required (2-ballot system), 5-year term (quinquennat), renewable once
- **Constitutional Guarantee**: President is guarantor of national independence, territorial integrity, and due respect for treaties
- **Executive Leadership**: Head of executive power, presides over Council of Ministers, appoints Prime Minister and Ministers
- **Legislative Influence**: May dissolve National Assembly, submit bills to referendum, send messages to Parliament, promulgate laws
- **Exceptional Powers (Article 16)**: In cases of serious and immediate threat, President may take extraordinary measures after consulting Prime Minister and Assembly Presidents
- **Counter-signature**: Most presidential acts require Prime Minister counter-signature (Art. 19), ensuring Ministerial responsibility
- **Immunities**: President enjoys inviolability during term for official acts, can be tried after term only by High Court of Justice

### Government Structure and Responsibility (Title III - Articles 20-23)
**Use when**: Analyzing the French Government's composition and accountability
- **Core Idea**: The Government determines and conducts the policy of the Nation under the direction of the Prime Minister
- **Composition**: Prime Minister + other Ministers as a collective body
- **Prime Minister's Role**: Directs Government action, ensures execution of laws, has regulatory power, appoints to civil and military offices
- **Council of Ministers**: Presided over by President of the Republic, agenda determined by President
- **Collective Responsibility**: Government is collectively responsible to Parliament under conditions in Articles 49-50

### Bicameral Parliament (Title IV - Articles 24-33)
**Use when**: Understanding the French legislative system
- **Core Idea**: Parliament exercises legislative power and controls Government action through two assemblies
- **National Assembly**: 577 deputies, direct universal suffrage, majority two-round system, 5-year term (unless dissolved)
- **Senate**: 348 senators, indirect suffrage by electoral college, 6-year term (renewed by halves every 3 years)
- **Asymmetrical Bicameralism**: National Assembly has primacy — can remove Government through motion of censure, Senate cannot
- **Functions**: Legislative power, Government control, public policy evaluation
- **Parliamentary Immunity**: Members cannot be prosecuted for opinions or votes cast in exercise of functions; protection from arrest without Assembly authorization

### Rationalized Parliamentarism (Title V - Articles 34-51)
**Use when**: Analyzing the legislative process and Government-Parliament relations
- **Legislative Domain (Article 34)**: Matters reserved to statute: civil rights, fundamental guarantees, criminal law, electoral system, social security finances, etc.
- **Regulatory Domain (Article 37)**: Matters not in legislative domain are regulatory; Government issues decrees and orders
- **Organic Laws**: Special category requiring identical approval by both assemblies or joint committee
- **Government Responsibility**: National Assembly can bring down Government through motion of censure (requires 1/10 of deputies to sign, 48-hour delay, absolute majority to pass)
- **Legislative Procedure**: Bills examined by committees, amendments can be proposed, Government controls much of agenda through committee stage
- **Shuttle (Navette)**: Bills go back and forth between assemblies until identical text approved

### Treaty Powers and International Law (Title VI - Articles 52-55)
**Use when**: Analyzing France's international relations and treaty law
- **Presidential Role**: President negotiates and ratifies treaties
- **Parliamentary Approval Required**: For peace treaties, trade treaties, treaties relative to international organizations, those committing state finances, those modifying legislative provisions, those relative to status of persons, those involving territorial changes
- **Supremacy of Treaties (Article 55)**: Duly ratified treaties have authority superior to domestic laws, subject to reciprocity
- **Constitutional Conformity**: No treaty contrary to Constitution may be ratified; if Constitutional Council declares incompatibility, constitutional revision required

### Constitutional Review (Title VII - Articles 56-63)
**Use when**: Understanding constitutional interpretation and protection in France
- **Composition**: 9 members (3 appointed by President, 3 by National Assembly President, 3 by Senate President), plus former Presidents as life members
- **Jurisdiction**: Reviews conformity of organic acts and parliamentary rules with Constitution; reviews laws referred by President, PM, or Assembly Presidents; examines 60-day referrals
- **Electoral Supervision**: Supervises presidential elections, parliamentary elections, and referendums; proclaims results
- **Decision Authority**: Decisions are final, binding on all public authorities and jurisdictional authorities
- **Emergency Review**: Reviews President's Article 16 emergency measures when referred by Assembly Presidents or 60 deputies/senators

### Judicial Independence (Title VIII - Articles 64-66)
**Use when**: Analyzing the French judiciary and rule of law
- **Presidential Role**: President is guarantor of judicial independence
- **Superior Council of the Judiciary (CSM)**: Body presided by President, includes Minister of Justice and members from Court of Cassation, Courts of Appeal, Tribunal des Conflits, public prosecutors, advocates, and notable persons
- **Appointment**: President, National Assembly President, and Senate President each appoint CSM members
- **Judicial Guarantees**: Magistrates of the seat are irremovable; prosecutors cannot be disciplined without CSM opinion during term

### Presidential Accountability (Title IX - Articles 67-68)
**Use when**: Analyzing mechanisms for holding the President accountable
- **High Court of Justice**: Special court composed of Parliament members meeting together, elects its own president
- **Jurisdiction**: Only court that can try President for acts committed in exercise of official functions
- **Removal Standard**: President may be removed only for grave and characterized violation of duties
- **Procedure**: Requires two-thirds majority of Parliament for conviction

### Ministerial Responsibility (Title X - Articles 68-1 to 68-3)
**Use when**: Analyzing criminal responsibility of Government members
- **Court of Justice of the Republic**: Special court with jurisdiction over Government members for acts in official capacity
- **Composition**: Two-thirds parliamentary members, one-third Supreme Judicial Court members
- **Indictment**: Requires vote of both Assemblies in identical terms or High Court of Justice decision
- **Immunity**: Members enjoy protection from coercive measures during investigation without court authorization

### Economic and Social Council (Title XI - Articles 69-71)
**Use when**: Understanding advisory mechanisms in French governance
- **Composition**: Representatives of economic and social categories, as determined by organic act
- **Functions**: Advisory body that may be consulted by Government and Assembly Presidents on legislative, ordinance, or regulatory bills and economic/social/environmental problems
- **Annual Report**: Submits annual report on activities to President, PM, and Assembly Presidents
- **Mandatory Consultation**: Government must consult Council on economic, social, or environmental plans/programs

### Territorial Decentralization (Title XII - Articles 72-75)
**Use when**: Analyzing local government and decentralization in France
- **Territorial Collectivities**: Communes, departments, regions, special collectivities, collectivities with special status
- **Free Administration**: Collectivities freely administer themselves through elected councils under conditions provided by law
- **State Representative**: Represents State interests, ensures compliance with national interests, administrative control, and law
- **Devolution**: Collectivities have power to make decisions in matters of their competence
- **Experimental Powers**: Can derogate from laws/regulations for limited purpose and duration, except where constitutional principles are affected
- **Financial Autonomy**: Transfers of powers must be accompanied by transfer of corresponding resources

### New Caledonia Special Status (Title XIII - Articles 76-77)
**Use when**: Analyzing France's approach to territorial diversity
- **New Caledonian Citizenship**: Special citizenship distinct from French citizenship
- **Shared Sovereignty**: Republic shares sovereign powers with New Caledonia
- **Gradual Devolution**: Powers in economic, social, and environmental fields may be transferred to New Caledonia
- **Institutional Modification**: Requires three-fifths majority in Congress and popular approval in referendum
- **Future Consultation**: After 20 years (from 1998 agreements), Congress may consult New Caledonian electors on new organizational accord

### Association Agreements (Title XIV - Article 88)
**Use when**: Understanding France's international association mechanisms
- **Community Organization**: France may accept organizations with decision-making powers for common objectives and coordination of economic/social policies
- **Internal Application**: Community decisions apply in internal law of member states
- **Parliamentary Authorization**: French participation requires parliamentary act

### European Union Integration (Title XV - Articles 88-1 to 88-5)
**Use when**: Analyzing France's participation in European integration
- **EU Membership**: Republic participates in European Communities and European Union per Maastricht Treaty
- **Direct Elections**: Law may provide for universal direct suffrage for European Parliament representatives
- **Subsidiarity**: Draft European legislative acts may be submitted to French Parliament or Senate; each Assembly may adopt resolutions
- **Accession Treaties**: Require referendum for approval
- **Constitutional Incompatibility**: Treaties with provisions incompatible with Constitution require constitutional revision

### Constitutional Amendment (Title XVI - Article 89)
**Use when**: Understanding how the French Constitution can be changed
- **Initiative**: President on PM proposal, or Parliament meeting in Congress
- **Parliamentary Approval**: Both National Assembly and Senate must approve identical text
- **Popular Ratification**: Constitutional revision requires referendum approval
- **Procedure**: Government or parliamentary initiative → identical approval by both chambers → Congress vote → popular referendum → promulgation
- **No Special Protection**: Unlike some constitutions, French Constitution does not have eternity clauses (except implicitly through difficult amendment process)
