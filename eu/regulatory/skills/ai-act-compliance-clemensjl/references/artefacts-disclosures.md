# Artefact templates: disclosures, documentation, oversight, logging

Companion to `artefacts-records.md`, which holds the inventory entry and the classification record. Same rules: every generated artefact carries `<!-- DRAFT — not legally cleared -->` as its first line until sign-off is confirmed, and unknown values stay as `[[MISSING: …]]` in the artefact and in the report back to the user.

## 1. Art 50 user-facing disclosures

Substance and placement per `transparency-art50.md`. Publish in the language of the interface; the explanation around them stays in English.

**Chat or assistant, first turn — Art 50(1).** Persistent, not dismissible into invisibility:

```
You are chatting with an AI assistant, not a person.
It can be wrong. [[Ask for a human]] at any time.
```

Placement: in the message thread as the first element and in the composer header, present before the user's first input. Not in a tooltip, not behind a "learn more", not only in the terms.

**Voice channel, opening line — Art 50(1):**

```
This call is answered by an automated AI assistant.
Say "agent" at any time to reach a person.
```

Spoken before the first substantive exchange, in the language of the call.

**Agent acting on someone's behalf — Art 50(1), Guidelines C(2026) 5054 final point 31.** Discloses both artificial nature and principal:

```
I am an AI assistant acting on behalf of [[principal name]].
I can [[permitted actions]]. I cannot [[excluded actions]].
```

**Generated image, video or audio — Art 50(2), provider side.** Not user-facing text; a technical specification:

```yaml
marking:
  layer_1_metadata:
    applies_to: [image, video, audio, containerised text]
    field: [[metadata field carrying the AI-generated flag]]
    signed: true                # digitally signed, tamper-evident
    timestamped: true
    key_management: [[where signing keys live and who controls them]]
    standard_or_best_practice: [[MISSING: named standard]]
  layer_2_watermark:
    applies_to: [image, video, audio, free-form text > 200 tokens]
    method: [[model-level | post-hoc]]
    implementation: [[MISSING: library or service and version]]
  optional_fingerprint_or_log: [[none | describe — never sufficient alone]]
detection:
  mechanism: [[endpoint, tool or API through which markings can be checked]]
  audience: [[public | verified expert users for free-form text]]
  result_states: [[indicates whether based on metadata, watermark, forensic]]
  interoperability_route: [[industry-standard access method | public signpost |
                            consortium solution | other]]
  interoperability_due: 2027-02-02
out_of_scope_outputs:
  - [[source code and configuration — Guidelines point 68]]
  - [[UI labels, alt-text, image captions, short sequences]]
  - [[machine-to-machine output not perceived by humans]]
exceptions_relied_on:
  - [[assistive standard editing — Art 50(2), reasoning]]
  - [[no substantial alteration of input data or its semantics — reasoning]]
```

**Deepfake label, deployer side — Art 50(4) first subparagraph.** Visual, using the EU icon from Annex 1 of the Code of Practice or an equivalent:

- Main element: capitalised **AI**, letters of equal vertical dimension, proportions preserved on resize.
- Second layer, encouraged: `generated` or `modified`, and what was modified.
- Placement: embedded in the content or an equivalent overlay, top-right or another position free of intervening elements, visible long enough to be noticed, at the latest at first exposure.
- Accessibility: screen-reader detectable, sufficient contrast, alternative cue where the visual is unavailable.

Audio-only, spoken at the beginning of the deep fake:

```
The following audio was generated by artificial intelligence.
```

**Published text on matters of public interest — Art 50(4) second subparagraph.** Above the fold, before the body text:

```
This text was generated by an AI system and published without human
editorial review.
```

If the human-review exception is relied on instead, record the natural or legal person holding editorial responsibility in the classification record; do not publish an unsupported claim of editorial control.

**Emotion recognition or biometric categorisation — Art 50(3), deployer side.** Before exposure:

```
This [[space | service | session]] uses a system that [[recognises emotions |
categorises people by biometric data]] for the purpose of [[purpose]].
[[Link to the privacy information and to how to object.]]
```

Check Art 5(1)(f) and (g) first: in a workplace or education institution, emotion inference is prohibited outright unless for medical or safety reasons.

## 3. Model card / technical documentation skeleton, aligned to Annex IV

Headings follow Annex IV so that the model card and the technical file are the same document, not two. Providers that are SMEs or SMCs may use the Commission's simplified form under Art 11(1) as amended — record which was used.

```markdown
<!-- DRAFT — not legally cleared -->
# Technical documentation — [[system]] · version [[…]] · [[date]]
Annex IV to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Simplified SME/SMC form used: [[yes/no]]

## 1. General description — Annex IV(1)
(a) Intended purpose; provider name; version and relation to previous versions
(b) Interaction with hardware or other software, including other AI systems
(c) Software and firmware versions; version update requirements
(d) Forms in which it is placed on the market: [[embedded | download | API]]
(e) Hardware it is intended to run on
(f) Photographs or illustrations, where a component of a product
(g)+(h) Deployer user interface; instructions for use

## 2. Elements and development process — Annex IV(2)
(a) Methods and steps, including use of pre-trained systems or third-party tools,
    and how they were used, integrated or modified
(b) Design specifications: general logic and algorithms; key design choices with
    rationale and assumptions, including as to the persons or groups the system is
    intended for; main classification choices; what it optimises for; relevance of
    parameters; expected output and output quality; trade-offs made to meet
    Chapter III Section 2
(c) System architecture; computational resources used to develop, train, test, validate
(d) Data: datasheets for training methodologies and techniques and for the training
    sets — general description, provenance, scope, main characteristics, how obtained
    and selected, labelling procedures, cleaning methodologies
(e) Assessment of the human oversight measures under Art 14, including technical
    measures to facilitate interpretation of outputs under Art 13(3)(d)
(f) Pre-determined changes and the technical solutions ensuring continuous compliance
(g) Validation and testing: procedures, data and its characteristics; metrics for
    accuracy, robustness and other requirements; potentially discriminatory impacts;
    **test logs and test reports, dated and signed by the responsible persons**
(h) Cybersecurity measures

## 3. Monitoring, functioning and control — Annex IV(3)
Capabilities and limitations in performance; degrees of accuracy for specific persons
or groups and overall expected accuracy; foreseeable unintended outcomes and sources
of risk to health, safety, fundamental rights and discrimination; human oversight
measures; input data specifications

## 5. Risk management system under Art 9 — Annex IV(5)
## 6. Relevant changes through the lifecycle — Annex IV(6)
## 7. Harmonised standards applied, with OJ references; where none, the solutions adopted — Annex IV(7)
## 8. Copy of the EU declaration of conformity under Art 47 — Annex IV(8)
## 9. Post-market monitoring system under Art 72, including the plan — Annex IV(9)
```

For a **GPAI model** rather than a system, use Annex XI instead: general description including tasks, acceptable use policies, release date and distribution methods, architecture and number of parameters, input and output modalities, licence; then design specifications and training process, data used for training with type, provenance and curation, **training compute in FLOP**, training time, and known or estimated energy consumption. Systemic-risk models add evaluation strategies and results, adversarial testing, and system architecture.

## 5. Log retention specification

Reconciles three regimes that pull in different directions: the AI Act floor, GDPR storage limitation, and sectoral law.

```yaml

role: [[provider | deployer]]

purpose_of_logging:              # Art 12(2)
  - identify situations that may cause a risk under Art 79(1) or a substantial modification
  - facilitate post-market monitoring under Art 72
  - enable deployer monitoring under Art 26(5)

events:
  - name: [[inference_request]]
    fields: [[timestamp, system version, model version, input reference,
              output reference, confidence, latency, actor]]
    personal_data: [[yes/no — categories]]
    retention: [[e.g. 6 months]]
    basis_for_period: [[Art 19 (provider) / Art 26(6) (deployer) floor of at least
                        six months, plus [[sectoral rule]], minus GDPR Art 5(1)(e)]]
  - name: [[human_override]]
    fields: [[timestamp, actor, original output, action, reason]]
    retention: [[…]]
  - name: [[incident]]
    fields: [[…]]
    retention: [[…]]

  applicable: [[yes/no]]

    - period of each use: start date and time, end date and time
```
