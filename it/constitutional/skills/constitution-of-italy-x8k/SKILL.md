## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Republican Foundation (Article 1)
**Use when**: Understanding the fundamental nature of the Italian State
- **Core Idea**: **Italy is a democratic Republic, founded on labor**
- **Sovereignty**: Belongs to the people, who exercise it in the forms and within the limits of the Constitution
- **Labor as Foundation**: Not just an economic activity, but the very foundation of the Republic
- **Republican Form**: Cannot be amended (Article 139) - eternal clause
- **Implications**: All institutions derive from popular sovereignty; labor has constitutional primacy

### Fundamental Principles (Articles 2-12)
**Use when**: Analyzing the constitutional values and foundational commitments of the Italian Republic

#### Human Dignity and Rights (Article 2)
- **Inviolable Rights**: Republic recognizes and guarantees inviolable rights of the person, both as individual and in social groups
- **Duty of Solidarity**: Requires fulfillment of unalienable duties of political, economic, and social solidarity
- **Foundation**: Human dignity is the foundation of all constitutional rights

#### Equality (Article 3)
**Use when**: Analyzing equal protection and anti-discrimination principles
- **Formal Equality**: All citizens have equal social dignity and are equal before the law without distinction of sex, race, language, religion, political opinions, personal and social conditions
- **Substantive Equality**: It is the Republic's duty to remove economic and social obstacles that limit freedom and equality
- **Implementation**: Active state role in promoting real, not just formal, equality

#### Republican Values (Articles 4-12)
- **Right to Work**: Republic recognizes right to work and promotes conditions for its exercise (Art. 4)
- **Unity and Indivisibility**: Republic is one and indivisible (Art. 5)
- **Protection of Minorities**: Republic protects linguistic minorities with special measures (Art. 6)
- **Decentralization**: Republic is composed of Municipalities, Provinces, Metropolitan Cities, Regions (Art. 114)
- **International Law**: Italy agrees to limitations of sovereignty necessary for a system of peace and justice among nations (Art. 11)
- **Rejection of War**: Italy repudiates war as an instrument of offense against the freedom of other peoples (Art. 11)

## Part I: Rights and Duties of Citizens (Articles 13-54)

### Civil Relations Framework (Title I - Articles 13-28)
**Use when**: Analyzing personal freedoms and limitations on state power

#### Personal Liberty (Article 13)
- **Inviolability**: Personal liberty is inviolable
- **Restrictions**: No form of detention, inspection, or personal search allowed except by reasoned act of judicial authority
- **Limits**: Restrictions must respect human dignity and cannot involve physical or moral violence

#### Domicile Inviolability (Article 14)
- **Protection**: Home is inviolable
- **Exceptions**: Searches and seizures require judicial warrant under conditions established by law

#### Freedom of Movement (Article 16)
- **Right**: Free circulation and residence throughout national territory
- **Limitations**: Limitations may be established by law for reasons of health or security

#### Freedom of Correspondence (Article 15)
- **Secrecy**: Secrecy of letters and other communications is inviolable
- **Limitations**: Can be restricted only by judicial decision with guaranteed minimum safeguards

#### Freedom of Thought and Expression (Article 21)
- **Right**: Everyone has the right to freely manifest their thought in speech, writing, or other means of diffusion
- **Press Freedom**: Journalistic activities cannot be subject to authorization or censorship
- **Limitations**: Seizure may be ordered only by judicial authority for specific offenses

#### Freedom of Religion (Articles 19-20)
- **Right**: Everyone has the right to freely profess their religious belief in any form, individually or with others
- **Organization**: Everyone has the right to organize religious associations, provided they do not have a political character
- **State-Religion Relations**: Catholic Church and other religious denominations are equally free before the law
- **No State Religion**: No state church or religion

#### Freedom of Assembly (Article 17)
- **Right**: Citizens have the right to assemble peacefully and unarmed
- **Limitations**: Meetings in public places require advance notice to authorities
- **Prohibition**: Meetings may be prohibited only for proven reasons of security or public safety

#### Freedom of Association (Article 18)
- **Right**: Citizens have the right to associate freely for purposes not prohibited by criminal law
- **Secret Associations**: Secret associations and associations with military character are prohibited

#### Family Rights (Articles 29-31)
- **Family Recognition**: Republic recognizes the rights of the family as a natural society founded on marriage
- **Marriage Equality**: Spouses are morally and legally equal
- **Parental Rights and Duties**: Parents have right and duty to maintain, instruct, and educate their children
- **Children's Rights**: Children born out of wedlock have legal and social protection equal to legitimate children

#### Health (Article 32)
- **Right**: Republic protects health as a fundamental right of the individual and in the interest of the community
- **Public Health**: Mandatory medical treatment cannot be imposed except as provided by law
- **No Health Obstacle**: No one may be deprived of medical treatment due to poverty

### Ethico-Social Relations (Title II - Articles 29-34)
**Use when**: Analyzing family, health, education, and cultural rights

#### Education (Articles 33-34)
- **Right to Education**: School is open to all
- **State and Private Schools**: State establishes free schools of all levels; private individuals and bodies have right to establish schools without financial burden
- **Equality**: State schools are open to all without discrimination; private schools must offer education equivalent to state schools
- **Autonomy**: Universities and academies have right to establish their own regulations within limits of law

#### Art and Science (Article 9, 33)
- **Promotion**: Republic promotes development of culture and scientific and technical research
- **Academic Freedom**: Art and science are free and teaching is free

---

### Economic Relations (Title III - Articles 35-47)
**Use when**: Analyzing labor rights, property, economic organization

#### Right to Work (Article 4, 35)
- **Constitutional Right**: Republic recognizes right to work and promotes conditions for its exercise
- **Remuneration**: Worker has right to remuneration proportionate to quality and quantity of work and sufficient to ensure free and dignified existence
- **Fair Wages**: Minimum wage guaranteed by law

#### Working Conditions (Article 36)
- **Working Hours**: Maximum daily working hours established by law
- **Rest**: Right to weekly rest and annual paid holidays, cannot be waived
- **Special Protections**: Special protections for women and minors established by law

#### Trade Union Freedom (Article 39)
- **Right**: Trade union organization is free
- **Conditions**: Only conditions are registration at local or central offices, according to regulations established by law
- **Representation**: Trade unions may represent members collectively in relations with employers
- **Strike**: Right to strike is exercised within limits established by law

#### Private Property (Article 42)
- **Right**: Private property is recognized and guaranteed by law
- **Social Function**: Property has a social function and can be expropriated for general interest with compensation
- **Public and Private**: Public and private economic initiative are guaranteed
- **Limitations**: Private economic initiative cannot be conducted against common utility or in a way that damages security, freedom, or human dignity

#### Taxation (Article 53)
- **Obligation**: Everyone must contribute to public expenses according to their ability
- **Progressivity**: Tax system must be progressive

---

### Political Relations (Title IV - Articles 48-54)
**Use when**: Analyzing democratic participation and political rights

#### Voting Rights (Article 48)
- **Universal Suffrage**: All citizens, men and women, who have attained majority have right to vote
- **Equality**: Vote is personal and equal, free, and secret
- **Eligibility**: All citizens of either sex are eligible for public office according to law

#### Equal Access to Public Office (Article 51)
- **Right**: All citizens of either sex can access public offices and elective positions on equal conditions
- **Implementation**: Law must promote equal access for all citizens

#### Political Parties (Article 49)
- **Right**: Citizens have right to freely associate in parties to contribute to determination of national policy by democratic means
- **Regulation**: Method of formation and activities of parties are regulated by law
- **Democracy**: Parties must have democratic internal organization

#### Referendum (Article 75)
- **Popular Participation**: People may be called to decide on abrogation of total or partial laws
- **Initiative**: Abrogative referendum can be requested by 500,000 voters or 5 Regional Councils

## Part II: Organization of the Republic (Articles 55-139)

### Parliament (Title I - Articles 55-82)
**Use when**: Analyzing the Italian legislative system and representation

#### Bicameral System
- **Perfect Bicameralism**: Both chambers (Chamber of Deputies and Senate) have identical powers and functions
- **Equality**: Every law must be approved by both chambers in identical terms
- **Representation**: Chamber represents the Nation; Senate represents the Nation and territorial collectivities

#### Chamber of Deputies
- **Composition**: 400 members (reduced from 630 in 2020 constitutional reform)
- **Election**: Direct and universal suffrage, proportional system
- **Eligibility**: Voters aged 18+; candidates aged 25+
- **Term**: 5 years

#### Senate of the Republic
- **Composition**: 200 elected members (reduced from 315+ in 2020 reform), plus life senators
- **Election**: Regional basis with voters aged 25+
- **Life Senators**: Former Presidents of Republic + up to 5 citizens appointed by President for outstanding merits
- **Term**: 5 years

#### Parliamentary Functions
- **Legislative Power**: Exercised collectively by both chambers
- **Political Direction**: Parliament guides and controls Government policy
- **Representative Mandate**: Members represent the Nation, not just their constituency

#### Legislative Process
- **Initiative**: Government, any member of Parliament, Regional Councils, CNEL (National Council for Economy and Labor), or 50,000 voters
- **Procedure**: Bill examined by committees, then by full chamber, with amendments
- **Decree-Laws**: Government can issue provisional measures with force of law, must be converted by Parliament within 60 days
- **Legislative Delegation**: Parliament can delegate to Government power to issue decrees with force of law, within limits and for limited time

#### Government Oversight
- **Question Time**: Government must answer questions from Parliament members
- **Interpellations**: Government must explain its policy or conduct
- **Motion of No Confidence**: Government can be removed by motion signed by at least one-tenth of members and including a new Prime Minister designate (constructive no confidence)
- **Investiture Vote**: Government needs confidence of both chambers; Prime Minister is appointed by President and must obtain confidence vote

### President of the Republic (Title II - Articles 83-91)
**Use when**: Analyzing the role and powers of the Italian Head of State

#### Election
- **Electoral College**: Parliament in joint session + 3 representatives from each Region (except Aosta Valley with 1)
- **Majority**: 2/3 majority on first three ballots, absolute majority from fourth ballot, relative majority after seventh ballot
- **Term**: 7 years, cannot be immediately re-elected

#### Powers and Duties
- **Representative**: Represents national unity, can send messages to chambers, authorizes presentation of Government bills
- **Executive**: Appoints Prime Minister, on PM proposal appoints and dismisses Ministers, promulgates laws, commands armed forces
- **Legislative**: Can dissolve one or both chambers (except in last 6 months of term or during war), calls elections
- **Judicial**: President of Supreme Council of the Judiciary (CSM), can grant pardons and commute sentences
- **Emergency**: Can adopt extraordinary measures in cases of necessity and urgency, subject to Government responsibility

#### Checks and Balances
- **Counter-signature**: All presidential acts, except those specifically exempted, require counter-signature by proponent Minister
- **Impeachment**: Can be impeached by Parliament for high treason or attack on Constitution, tried by Constitutional Court with special composition
- **Responsibility**: President is not responsible for acts performed in exercise of functions, except for high treason or attack on Constitution

### Government (Title III - Articles 92-100)
**Use when**: Analyzing the Italian executive branch

#### Composition
- **Council of Ministers**: Collective body composed of Prime Minister and Ministers
- **Prime Minister**: President of Council of Ministers, directs and is responsible for general Government policy
- **Ministers**: Head ministries, are members of Council of Ministers

#### Formation
- **Appointment**: President appoints Prime Minister; on PM proposal, President appoints Ministers
- **Investiture**: Government must obtain confidence of both chambers within 10 days of formation
- **Oath**: Prime Minister and Ministers take oath before President and, within one day, before Parliament

#### Functioning
- **Collective Responsibility**: Government is collectively responsible to Parliament
- **Individual Responsibility**: Each Minister is individually responsible for their department
- **Political Direction**: Government directs national policy in domestic and foreign affairs
- **Administrative Direction**: Government directs public administration
- **Military Command**: Government has command of armed forces through Prime Minister as President of Council

#### Government-Parliament Relations
- **Confidence Requirement**: Government needs and must maintain confidence of both chambers
- **Motion of No Confidence**: Can be brought by motion signed by at least one-tenth of members of either chamber; must include name of new Prime Minister; approved by absolute majority
- **Resignation**: Prime Minister can resign; Government resigns when loses confidence vote or Prime Minister resigns

### Judiciary (Title IV - Articles 101-113)
**Use when**: Analyzing the Italian judicial system and constitutional guarantees

#### Judicial Independence
- **Independence and Impartiality**: Judges are subject only to the law
- **Irremovability**: Judges cannot be removed from office

#### Judicial Organization
- **Ordinary Judiciary**: Judges for civil and criminal matters, organized hierarchically with Supreme Court of Cassation at top
- **Administrative Judiciary**: Specialized courts (TAR, Council of State) for disputes between citizens and public administration
- **Accounting Judiciary**: Court of Accounts for public finance matters
- **Military Judiciary**: Military courts only in times of war
- **Constitutional Court**: Special court outside ordinary judiciary for constitutional matters

#### Self-Governance
- **Supreme Council of the Judiciary (CSM)**: Governing body of ordinary judiciary
- **Composition**: Elected judges and members from Parliament and law professors
- **Disciplinary Powers**: CSM has authority over judges' careers, assignments, and discipline

---

### Constitutional Court (Title VI - Articles 134-137)
**Use when**: Analyzing constitutional review and guarantees in Italy

#### Composition
- **15 Judges**: 5 appointed by President, 5 by Parliament (3 by Chamber, 2 by Senate), 5 by supreme ordinary and administrative courts
- **Term**: 9 years, judges cannot be re-appointed
- **Incompatibilities**: Office incompatible with membership in Parliament or Regional Council

#### Jurisdiction
- **Constitutional Review**: Judges constitutionality of laws and acts with force of law (State and Regions)
- **Conflict Resolution**: Resolves conflicts of attribution between powers of State and between State and Regions
- **Presidential Accountability**: Judges accusations against President of Republic for high treason or attack on Constitution
- **Access**: Can be accessed by State, Regions, or any person claiming violation of constitutional rights

---

### Regions, Provinces, Municipalities (Title V - Articles 114-133)
**Use when**: Analyzing Italian federalism and territorial autonomy

#### Territorial Autonomy
- **Republic Composition**: Republic is composed of Municipalities, Provinces, Metropolitan Cities, Regions, and State
- **Autonomy Recognition**: All are autonomous entities with their own statutes, powers, and functions

#### Regions
- **Status**: 20 Regions, 5 with special statutes (Sicily, Sardinia, Trentino-Alto Adige, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Valle d'Aosta)
- **Statutes**: Each Region has its own Statute as fundamental norm
- **Government**: Each Region has Regional Council (legislative), Giunta Regionale (executive), and President
- **Competencies**: Regions have legislative power in matters not reserved to State; concurrent legislation in some areas; administrative functions

#### Local Governments
- **Municipalities**: Fundamental local entities with elected mayor and council, responsible for local services
- **Provinces**: Intermediate level between Regions and Municipalities, with coordinating functions
- **Metropolitan Cities**: Special local entities for large urban areas

#### Subsidiarity Principle
- **Administrative Functions**: Functions attributed to Municipalities unless attributed to Provinces or Regions to ensure exercise close to citizens
- **State Subsidiarity**: State has subsidiary role, intervening only when local entities cannot adequately exercise functions

#### State-Regions Relations
- **Cooperation**: State and Regions cooperate in matters of common interest
- **Coordination**: Special bodies coordinate between State and Regions
- **Conflict Resolution**: Constitutional Court resolves conflicts of attribution

---

### Constitutional Guarantees (Title VI - Articles 134-139)
**Use when**: Analyzing the protection and amendment of the Constitution

#### Amendment Procedure
- **Initiative**: Members of Parliament, Government, Regional Councils, or 500,000 voters
- **Procedure**: Each chamber approves text twice with interval of at least 3 months between readings
- **Majority**: Second approval requires absolute majority in each chamber
- **Referendum**: If requested by one-fifth of members of either chamber, 500,000 voters, or 5 Regional Councils, constitutional law submitted to popular referendum
- **Limitations**: No amendment can alter the Republican form of the State (Article 139 - eternity clause)
