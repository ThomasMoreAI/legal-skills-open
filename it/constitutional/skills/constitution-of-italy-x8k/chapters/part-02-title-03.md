# Part II, Title III: Government
**Articles: 92-100**

## Core Idea
The Government of the Republic, composed of the President of the Council of Ministers (Prime Minister) and the Ministers, is the executive body that directs the general policy of the country. It must have the confidence of both parliamentary chambers and is collectively responsible to them.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Government Composition
- **Council of Ministers**: Collective body composed of Prime Minister and Ministers (Art. 92)
- **Prime Minister**: President of the Council of Ministers, directs and is responsible for general government policy
- **Ministers**: Head ministries, are members of Council of Ministers

### Government Formation
- **Appointment**: President of Republic appoints Prime Minister and, on Prime Minister's proposal, appoints Ministers (Art. 92-93)
- **Investiture**: Government must obtain confidence of both chambers within 10 days of formation (Art. 94)
- **Oath**: Prime Minister and Ministers take oath before President of Republic and, within one day, before Parliament

### Government Functioning
- **Collective Responsibility**: Government is collectively responsible to Parliament
- **Individual Responsibility**: Each Minister is individually responsible for their department
- **Presidential Coordination**: Prime Minister ensures unity of political and administrative direction, promoting and coordinating activity of Ministers
- **Ministerial Meetings**: Council of Ministers meets under chairmanship of Prime Minister

## Key Concepts
- **Political Direction**: Government directs national policy in domestic and foreign affairs
- **Administrative Direction**: Government directs public administration
- **Military Command**: Government has command of armed forces (through Prime Minister as President of Council)
- **Regulatory Power**: Government can issue regulations for execution of laws
- **Delegated Legislation**: Can issue legislative decrees when authorized by Parliament (Art. 76-77)

### Government-Parliament Relations
- **Confidence Requirement**: Government needs and must maintain confidence of both chambers
- **Motion of No Confidence**: Can be brought by motion signed by at least one-tenth of members of either chamber; must include name of new Prime Minister; approved by absolute majority (Art. 94)
- **Resignation**: Prime Minister can resign; Government resigns when loses confidence vote or Prime Minister resigns
- **Information**: Government must inform Parliament on its policy and be available for questions

## Mental Models
- Use **collective responsibility** — Government succeeds or fails together
- Think of **Prime Minister as primus inter pares** — first among equals but with coordinating role
- Apply **confidence relationship** — Government and Parliament in continuous dialogue

## Anti-patterns
- **Minority government without program**: Government needs clear parliamentary support
- **Individual minister defiance**: Ministers cannot act against Government policy
- **Ignoring Parliament**: Government must maintain Parliament's confidence

## Key Takeaways
1. Government is collective executive body directed by Prime Minister
2. Must have and maintain confidence of both parliamentary chambers
3. Prime Minister coordinates and directs Government policy
4. Government can be removed by constructive motion of no confidence
5. Government exercises both political direction and administrative execution

## Connects To
- **Title I (Parliament)**: Parliament grants and can withdraw confidence
- **Title II (President)**: President appoints Government
- **Fundamental Principles**: Government implements popular sovereignty
