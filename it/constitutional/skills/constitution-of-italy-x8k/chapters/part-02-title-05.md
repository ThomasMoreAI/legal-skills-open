# Part II, Title V: Regions, Provinces, Municipalities
**Articles: 114-133**

## Core Idea
The Italian Republic is organized in a decentralized manner, with Regions, Provinces, and Municipalities recognized as autonomous entities with their own statutes, powers, and elected governments. This system, established to give effect to the principles of local autonomy and subsidiarity, creates a multi-level governance structure.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Territorial Autonomy
- **Republic Composition**: Republic is composed of Municipalities, Provinces, Metropolitan Cities, Regions, and State (Art. 114)
- **Autonomy Recognition**: Municipalities, Provinces, Metropolitan Cities, and Regions are autonomous entities with their own statutes, powers, and functions according to constitutional principles (Art. 115)

### Regions
- **Status**: 20 Regions, 5 with special statutes (Sicily, Sardinia, Trentino-Alto Adige, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Valle d'Aosta)
- **Statutes**: Each Region has its own Statute as fundamental norm, approved by Regional Council with special procedures
- **Government**: Each Region has a Regional Council (legislative), a Giunta Regionale (executive), and a President
- **Competencies**: Regions have legislative power in matters not reserved to State; concurrent legislation in some areas; administrative functions

### Local Governments
- **Municipalities**: Fundamental local entities with elected mayor and council, responsible for local services
- **Provinces**: Intermediate level between Regions and Municipalities, with coordinating functions
- **Metropolitan Cities**: Special local entities for large urban areas

### Subsidiarity Principle
- **Administrative Functions**: Functions are attributed to Municipalities unless attributed to Provinces or Regions to ensure exercise close to citizens or to guarantee unitary determination
- **State Subsidiarity**: State has subsidiary role, intervening only when local entities cannot adequately exercise functions

## Key Concepts
- **Concurrent Legislation**: In matters of concurrent legislation, Regions exercise legislative power within principles established by State laws
- **Residual Competencies**: Regions have legislative power in all matters not expressly reserved to State
- **State Supervision**: State can supervise regional and local acts for compliance with Constitution, laws, and national interest
- **Financial Autonomy**: Regions and local entities have own revenues and financial autonomy
- **Equal Treatment**: All Regions have equal status, though special statute Regions have additional powers

### State-Regions Relations
- **Cooperation**: State and Regions cooperate in matters of common interest
- **Coordination**: Special bodies coordinate between State and Regions
- **Conflict Resolution**: Constitutional Court resolves conflicts of attribution between State and Regions

### Special Statute Regions
- **Enhanced Autonomy**: Five Regions with special statutes have broader legislative and administrative competencies
- **Historical Reasons**: Special status based on historical, geographical, or linguistic factors
- **Financial Powers**: Special Regions often have enhanced financial powers

## Mental Models
- Use **subsidiarity principle** — decisions should be made at level closest to citizens
- Think of **cooperative federalism** — State and Regions work together in shared areas
- Apply **asymmetry acceptance** — special statute Regions have different competencies

## Anti-patterns
- **State centralization**: State overreach into regional competencies violates autonomy
- **Regional secession**: Regional autonomy cannot become independence (Republic is one and indivisible)
- **Discrimination between Regions**: All Regions must be treated equally except where Constitution provides otherwise

## Key Takeaways
1. Italy has a multi-level system with State, Regions, Provinces, Municipalities
2. Regions have significant legislative and administrative autonomy
3. Subsidiarity principle guides distribution of functions
4. Special statute Regions have enhanced powers based on historical factors
5. Constitutional Court resolves State-Region competency conflicts

## Connects To
- **Fundamental Principles**: Regional autonomy implements principle of local self-government
- **Part I**: Regions protect fundamental rights within their competencies
- **Title VI**: Constitutional Court resolves competency disputes
