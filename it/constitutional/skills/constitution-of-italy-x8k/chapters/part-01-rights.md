# Part I: Rights and Duties of Citizens
**Articles: 13-54**

## Core Idea
Part I establishes the catalog of inviolable rights and duties that define the status of citizens in the Italian Republic. These provisions create a comprehensive framework for personal freedom, social participation, economic activity, and political engagement, all grounded in human dignity and equality.

## Key Titles
- **Title I: Civil Relations** (Art. 13-28) — Personal liberty, privacy, religion, family, health
- **Title II: Ethico-Social Relations** (Art. 29-34) — Family, health, education, culture
- **Title III: Economic Relations** (Art. 35-47) — Work, property, enterprise, taxation
- **Title IV: Political Relations** (Art. 48-54) — Voting, eligibility, parties, referendum

## Frameworks Introduced

### Inviolable Rights
- **Human Dignity**: The Republic recognizes and guarantees the inviolable rights of the person, both as an individual and in social groups (Art. 2)
  - When to use: Any constitutional rights interpretation
  - How: All rights find their foundation in human dignity

### Personal Liberty Framework
- **Personal Liberty**: Personal liberty is inviolable (Art. 13). No form of detention, inspection, or personal search is allowed except by reasoned act of judicial authority and only in cases and forms provided by law.
- **Limits**: Restrictions must respect human dignity and cannot involve physical or moral violence.

### Equality Framework
- **Formal Equality**: All citizens have equal social dignity and are equal before the law (Art. 3.1)
- **Substantive Equality**: It is the Republic's duty to remove economic and social obstacles that limit freedom and equality (Art. 3.2)

## Key Concepts by Title

### Title I: Civil Relations (Art. 13-28)
- **Personal Liberty** (Art. 13): Inviolable; restrictions only by judicial authority
- **Domicile Inviolability** (Art. 14): Home is inviolable; searches and seizures require judicial warrant
- **Freedom of Movement** (Art. 16): Free circulation and residence throughout national territory
- **Freedom of Correspondence** (Art. 15): Secrecy of letters and other communications
- **Freedom of Thought** (Art. 21): Free expression of thought in speech, writing, or other means
- **Freedom of Religion** (Art. 19-20): Right to freely profess religion in any form
- **Freedom of Assembly** (Art. 17): Right to assemble peacefully and unarmed
- **Freedom of Association** (Art. 18): Right to associate freely for lawful purposes

### Title II: Ethico-Social Relations (Art. 29-34)
- **Family** (Art. 29-31): Natural society founded on marriage; moral and legal equality of spouses
- **Health** (Art. 32): Republic protects health as fundamental right of the individual and collective interest
- **Education** (Art. 33-34): School is open to all; state and private schools with equal rights
- **Art and Science** (Art. 9, 33): Republic promotes development of culture and scientific research

### Title III: Economic Relations (Art. 35-47)
- **Right to Work** (Art. 4, 35): Republic recognizes right to work and promotes conditions for its exercise
- **Fair Remuneration** (Art. 36): Worker has right to remuneration proportionate to work quality/quantity and sufficient for free and dignified existence
- **Working Hours** (Art. 36): Maximum daily working hours established by law
- **Rest and Holidays** (Art. 36): Right to weekly rest and annual paid holidays
- **Trade Union Freedom** (Art. 39): Right to form unions and join them freely
- **Right to Strike** (Art. 40): Right to strike within limits established by law
- **Private Property** (Art. 42): Recognized and guaranteed, with social function
- **Private Economic Initiative** (Art. 41): Free but cannot be conducted against common utility or in way that damages security, freedom, or human dignity
- **Taxation** (Art. 53): Everyone must contribute to public expenses according to ability

### Title IV: Political Relations (Art. 48-54)
- **Voting Right** (Art. 48): All citizens, men and women, who have attained majority have right to vote
- **Eligibility** (Art. 51): All citizens of either sex are eligible for public office according to law
- **Equal Access to Public Office** (Art. 51): Everyone can access public offices and elective positions on equal conditions
- **Political Parties** (Art. 49): Right of citizens to associate freely in parties to contribute to determination of national policy
- **Referendum** (Art. 75): People may be called to decide on abrogration of total or partial laws

## Mental Models
- Use **dignity as interpretive key** — all rights must be interpreted to protect human dignity
- Think of **rights as interconnected** — economic rights support personal rights and vice versa
- Apply **proportionality** — limitations on rights must be necessary, suitable, and balanced

## Anti-patterns
- **Absolute property rights**: Ignoring the social function of property (Art. 42.2)
- **Unlimited economic freedom**: Failing to consider common utility (Art. 41)
- **Discrimination in access to offices**: Violates equal access principle (Art. 51)

## Key Takeaways
1. Rights are inviolable and grounded in human dignity
2. Rights have both negative (protection from State) and positive (State obligation) dimensions
3. Economic rights are as important as civil and political rights
4. Rights can be limited but only within constitutional bounds
5. Duties accompany rights — solidarity is a constitutional value

## Connects To
- **Fundamental Principles**: Rights implement the values of democracy, equality, and solidarity
- **Part II, Title IV**: Judiciary protects rights from violations
- **Part II, Title VI**: Constitutional Court ensures rights protection
