# Fundamental Principles
**Articles: 1-12**

## Core Idea
The Italian Republic is founded on labor, democracy, and the sovereign will of the people. These principles establish Italy as a democratic State based on work, with popular sovereignty, equality, and the inviolable rights of the person at its core.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Republican Form
- **Italy is a Republic**: The State is a democratic Republic founded on labor (Art. 1)
  - When to use: Understanding the nature of the Italian State
  - How: All institutions and powers derive from this foundation
- **Sovereignty belongs to the people**: Sovereignty belongs to the people, who exercise it in the forms and within the limits of the Constitution (Art. 1)

### Republican Values
- **Labor as foundation**: Labor is not only a right but the very foundation of the Republic (Art. 1, 4)
- **Solidarity**: Republic requires fulfillment of unalienable duties of political, economic, and social solidarity (Art. 2)
- **Equality**: All citizens have equal social dignity and are equal before the law without distinction of sex, race, language, religion, political opinions, personal and social conditions (Art. 3)

### Limits on Sovereignty
- **Republican form cannot be amended**: No constitutional amendment may alter the Republican form of the State (Art. 139)

## Key Concepts
- **Democratic Republic**: A State where power derives from the people and is exercised through democratic institutions
- **Founded on Labor**: The Constitution explicitly bases the Republic on labor, not just abstract principles
- **Popular Sovereignty**: People are the source of all state authority, exercised through constitutional mechanisms
- **Substantive Equality**: The Republic must remove economic and social obstacles that limit freedom and equality (Art. 3.2)
- **Unity and Indivisibility**: The Republic is one and indivisible (Art. 5)

## Mental Models
- Use **labor as constitutional value** — work is not just an economic activity but a founding principle
- Think of **equality as both formal and substantive** — requires both equal treatment and active removal of obstacles
- Apply **republican principle** — no one holds power by hereditary right; all authority is derived from the people

## Anti-patterns
- **Amending republican form**: Any attempt to restore monarchy or other non-republican forms violates Art. 139
- **Ignoring substantive equality**: Formal equality without addressing economic/social obstacles fails Art. 3.2
- **Disregarding labor foundation**: Policies that devalue work undermine the constitutional foundation

## Key Takeaways
1. Italy is a democratic Republic founded on labor
2. Sovereignty belongs to the people, exercised through the Constitution
3. Republican form is immutable and cannot be amended
4. Equality requires both equal treatment and active promotion of equal opportunities
5. Solidarity is a constitutional duty, not just a moral principle

## Connects To
- **All Parts**: The fundamental principles guide interpretation of the entire Constitution
- **Part I**: Rights and duties implement the values of democracy, equality, and solidarity
- **Part II**: Institutions are structured to give effect to popular sovereignty
