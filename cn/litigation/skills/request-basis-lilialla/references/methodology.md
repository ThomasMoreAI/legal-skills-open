# Methodology

## Study Version

In exam/Gutachtenstil analysis, facts are given. The task is to find candidate request bases and test them through elements, defenses, extinguishment, and exercisability.

## Practice Version

In litigation, facts are not given. Request-basis thinking helps identify legally relevant assertions, proof targets, burden allocation, and evidence gaps. Analyze party statements first, then contested legally relevant facts, then evidence.

## Core Question

Who may demand what from whom on what basis?

With cause of action:

Who may demand what from whom, under what cause of action and legal/normative basis?

With evidence:

What element facts are needed, who bears the burden, and what evidence supports or fails to support each element?

## Concept Guard

Before using the phrase request basis, separate it from adjacent concepts:

- Cause of action: classification/index.
- Claim: requested result.
- Article: possible norm, but may be auxiliary/defense/referral/definition.
- Legal relationship: background.
- Evidence: proof material.

If the analysis cannot state claimant, respondent, request content, normative/legal-transaction basis, elements, and defenses, call it a candidate path rather than a located request basis.

## Three Layers / Four Steps

Every detailed request basis must pass four gates:

| Gate | Chinese Label | What To Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right arises | 请求权是否产生 | positive elements; legal transaction/statutory trigger; right-not-arisen defenses |
| Right not extinguished | 请求权是否未消灭 | performance, setoff, release, settlement, merger, rescission consequences |
| Right exercisable | 请求权是否可行使 | limitation, simultaneous performance, prior performance, insecurity defense, agreed conditions |
| Defense response | 抗辩/反抗辩 | counter-defenses, re-counter-defenses, waiver, interruption/suspension, defense unavailable |

Do not treat positive elements as the whole analysis. In practice mode, connect each gate to evidence, burden, dispute, proof gap, and verification needs.

## Recursive Norm Decomposition

When a gate contains an abstract legal concept, unpack it rather than naming it:

- main norm: the rule or legal transaction that directly supports the claim;
- auxiliary norm: a rule defining an element or legal effect of the main norm;
- lower auxiliary norm: a rule defining a concept inside an auxiliary norm;
- defense norm: a rule that blocks the main norm or one auxiliary norm;
- counter-defense norm: a rule that defeats the defense.

Use this recursively only as far as the facts require. Example pattern: contract claim -> contract formation -> offer/acceptance -> offer definition -> specific content and binding intent -> effect of public expression.

## Typical Outer Order

1. Contract.
2. Contract-like.
3. Negotiorum gestio.
4. Property/possession.
5. Unjust enrichment.
6. Tort.

This is a starting order, not a mechanical rule.
