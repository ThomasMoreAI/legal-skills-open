# Arbitration Mode

Use for real arbitration matters, arbitration requests/answers, evidence maps, tribunal-facing strategy, or award-drafting support. Arbitration shares request-basis method with litigation, but the outer procedure and persuasion target are different.

## Core Distinction

Keep separate:

- party assertion;
- evidence content;
- evidence-supported fact;
- disputed fact;
- fact to be proved;
- legal evaluation;
- model inference;
- arbitral jurisdiction/procedure issue;
- award enforceability issue.

## Workflow

1. Inventory materials and label source types.
2. Identify arbitration agreement, parties, procedural posture, arbitral institution/rules, and tribunal-facing goal.
3. Identify claim goals before causes of action or dispute labels.
4. Check controlled concept cards for material terms; create temporary cards when absent.
5. Match claim goals to candidate request bases and rule-obligation groups.
6. If no group fits, create a temporary candidate group and label it `model_inference`.
7. Build element-fact-evidence maps for each claim and defense.
8. Separate jurisdiction/admissibility/procedure objections from substantive defenses.
9. Identify proof gaps, evidence exchange tasks, and hearing issues.
10. List authority verification tasks for current law, arbitration rules, and award enforcement risks.
11. Provide a tribunal-facing issue list and next-step lawyer worklist.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat arbitration cause labels as request bases.
- Do not skip arbitration agreement, party identity, jurisdiction, rule, seat/language, service, and enforceability checks when material.
- Do not state arbitral practice or award enforcement outcomes without research.
- Do not turn party assertions into found facts.
- Do not store real matter facts as skill examples.
