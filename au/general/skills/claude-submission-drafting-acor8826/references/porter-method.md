# The Porter Method — the canon

**Read in full before writing.** Chester Porter's persuasion doctrines as nine operational rules. They bind every agent; where a local brief is silent, the canon governs. Rendered in the skill's own words, not quotations. Porter — "the Smiling Funnel-Web" — persuaded by appearing fair and reasonable while leading the reader, fact by fact, to the only conclusion left standing. The genial surface was the mechanism.

1. **Know the reader.** Persuasion is about the audience, not the writer. What to open on, what to concede, the register, what to leave out — all decided by who reads and what they already believe. Fix the reader first (Step 1); never write to a generic audience.

2. **Credibility is spent once.** The instant the reader catches one exaggeration, one unsupported "fact", or one hollow concession, they discount everything else — permanently. Guard credibility above effect; it *is* the effect.

3. **Understatement beats overstatement.** Adjectives, intensifiers, and superlatives signal the facts aren't carrying the weight, and invite resistance. State things slightly under their strength and let the reader supply the force. Strip intensifiers on sight.

4. **Fact persuades; opinion provokes.** An undisputed fact the reader cannot argue with and didn't have pushed on them outperforms any loud claim. Convert opinion to the fact beneath it ("he is dishonest" → the specific verifiable conduct) or cut it.

5. **Build the funnel — lead, don't bludgeon.** Never announce the conclusion and push; lay undisputed facts in an order such that the reader *reaches* it and feels it is their own. Open wide on agreed ground; make each step unarguable; narrow to the one conclusion; state it quietly; stop. A conclusion the reader reached, they defend; one you forced, they resist.

6. **Concede frankly and early.** Deal with your weaknesses openly, before the reader (or opponent) deploys them. A frank early concession defuses the point, denies the opponent the "exposure", and buys credibility for what you hold. But concede only what is real and load-bearing — an ornamental concession the reader sees through is worse than the objection.

7. **Courtesy disarms; aggression arms.** A courteous, reasonable, faintly humble register lowers defences; sarcasm and triumph raise them and make the reader hunt for reasons to disagree. Rewrite accusations as observations, demands as requests, rebuttals as queries. The harder the point, the gentler the delivery.

8. **Plain language, or no persuasion.** A reader who must work to follow you is being lost. Remove jargon (or define once); keep sentences short; prefer the concrete word. Complexity is a leak, not sophistication.

9. **Know when to stop.** End at the conclusion. Don't re-argue, don't add a flourish, don't overstay. The quietest last line is the most persuasive. Brevity is itself credible and courteous — it signals you trust the facts and respect the reader's time. Cut every line that doesn't earn its place.

**Rule 2 overrides everything, including persuasive effect.** It is enforced at synthesis (an unsourced load-bearing fact may not be asserted) and at the gate (the honesty guard).
