# Statutory Declarations

Use this reference when the document is a statutory declaration, or when the
matter needs a choice between a declaration, an affidavit and an unsworn
statement. It supplies no current law, form or witnessing rule. The applicable
declarations Act, its regulations and the prescribed or approved form control.

## Choose The Right Instrument First

| Instrument | Made under | Typical use | Sworn or affirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statutory declaration | A Commonwealth, state or territory declarations or oaths Act | Evidence of a fact to a department, agency, insurer, bank, registry or private party outside a proceeding | Declared before an authorised witness |
| Affidavit | Court or tribunal rules and the forum's form | Evidence filed in a proceeding | Sworn or affirmed before an authorised person |
| Witness statement | A rule, practice note or direction permitting statements instead of affidavits | Evidence in a proceeding where the forum takes statements | Signed, often with a truth statement rather than an oath |
| Unsworn statement or letter | Nothing | Background, instructions or correspondence | Not applicable |

Do not use a declaration where the forum requires an affidavit, and do not
convert an affidavit into a declaration because a witness is easier to find.
Where a proceeding is on foot, ask the forum skill or the current rules which
instrument the filing accepts.

## Pick The Right Regime

Where the declaration is required for a statutory or Commonwealth purpose, the
regime follows the recipient and the law under which the declaration is
required, not the declarant's home state. Where a private recipient asks for a
declaration and names no statutory basis, follow the regime the recipient
nominates, or otherwise the declarant's own jurisdiction: a declaration valid
where it was made is commonly accepted, and state and territory oaths
legislation may recognise a declaration made in another Australian
jurisdiction. Record and verify:

- which declarations or oaths Act applies, and its current form or approved
  wording;
- whether a Commonwealth agency, a state or territory body, or a private
  recipient is asking, and whether the requesting instrument names a regime;
- the prescribed declaration wording, which differs between jurisdictions and
  must be reproduced exactly;
- who may witness under that regime, and any occupation, registration or
  jurisdiction limit on the witness;
- whether electronic signing, audio-visual witnessing or a digital declaration
  is permitted for that regime and that recipient; and
- the version or effective date of the form, and the date checked.

A declaration made under the wrong Act, or witnessed by a person that Act does
not authorise, can be worthless to the recipient even though the facts are true.
Where the regime is unclear, draft an internal working version, state the open
question and stop before execution.

## Shape

Keep the structure the applicable form gives. Where the form leaves the body to
the declarant, use this order:

1. Prescribed heading and the Act named by the form.
2. The declarant's full name, address and occupation as the form requires.
3. Numbered paragraphs, one proposition each, in first person.
4. Identification of any attached document by the label the form uses, with the
   attachment marked as the regime requires.
5. The prescribed declaration clause, reproduced word for word.
6. Signature, place and date, and the witness block with the witness's name,
   qualification or authorising occupation, and any registration number the form
   asks for.

Do not paraphrase, modernise or tidy the prescribed declaration clause. Do not
move it, split it or add words to it.

## Content Discipline

- State facts the declarant can properly give, with dates, amounts and document
  anchors.
- Separate personal knowledge from information and belief, and name the source
  of any belief where the regime or recipient expects it.
- Describe events in lay terms. A declaration is not a place for a diagnosis, a
  valuation or an expert opinion unless the declarant is qualified to give it and
  the recipient accepts it in that form.
- Keep argument, characterisation and complaint out. A declaration that argues
  invites the recipient to weigh advocacy rather than accept a fact.
- Do not declare a legal conclusion the declarant cannot know, for example that
  a contract was void, that conduct was unlawful, or that another person
  intended something.
- Where a fact is genuinely unknown, say so plainly or leave it out. Do not
  smooth a gap with a confident sentence.

## False Declaration Exposure

Making a false declaration is a criminal offence under every Australian
declarations regime, and the offence attaches to the declarant. That does not
immunise the drafter: a person who knowingly drafts or procures a false
declaration can face accessorial liability and professional discipline. Both
points change the drafting duty:

- never draft around a fact the declarant has told you is uncertain;
- never draft a paragraph the declarant has not confirmed in their own words;
- never combine two declarants' knowledge into one person's declaration; and
- escalate rather than resolve a conflict between the instructions and a source
  document.

Where the declarant is under pressure, is self-represented, or is declaring
about a disputed event, keep the wording conservative and flag the paragraphs
that need qualified review.

## Common Failures

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Prescribed wording paraphrased | Restore the exact form wording |
| Wrong jurisdiction's form used | Re-verify the regime against the requesting instrument |
| Witness not authorised under that Act | Check the authorised-witness list for the regime before execution |
| Attachment mentioned but not marked or identified | Use the regime's attachment or annexure convention and match the labels |
| Belief stated as knowledge | Re-classify and name the source |
| Argument or accusation inside the body | Move it to correspondence or submissions |
| Undated, unsigned or unwitnessed page in the executed copy | Re-check the rendered document page by page |

## Completion Gate

Confirm the regime and form against the current official source, render the
document, check the declaration clause character by character against the form,
confirm the witness is authorised for that regime, and keep execution as a
separately authorised act by the declarant. Signing, witnessing and lodging are
not drafting steps.
