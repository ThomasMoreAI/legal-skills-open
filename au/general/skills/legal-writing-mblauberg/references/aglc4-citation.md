# AGLC4 Citation

Use this reference when a document needs Australian Guide to Legal Citation,
fourth edition, formatting: academic writing, articles, theses, advices,
research memoranda and any document whose instructions call for AGLC. Load
`source-boundary-and-citations.md` first for whether a source may be cited at
all; this file covers only how a verified citation is presented. The Guide
itself controls, and its rules are checked against the published edition, not
recalled from another document's formatting.

## When AGLC Applies

| Document | Citation style |
|---|---|
| Filed submissions, affidavits, orders | The forum's own practice, which is usually not AGLC |
| Correspondence | Plain identification: title, date, pinpoint |
| Academic articles, theses, book chapters | AGLC |
| Advices, research memoranda, internal notes | AGLC where the recipient expects it; otherwise plain identification |

Do not convert a forum's citation practice into AGLC because AGLC is tidier, and
do not convert AGLC into loose prose citation while condensing.

## Core Patterns

AGLC citations sit in footnotes, not in body text. Every footnote ends with a
full stop.

- **Reported case**: case name in italics, then the year, volume, report series
  abbreviation and starting page, then the pinpoint. Round brackets around the
  year where the report series is numbered by volume; square brackets where the
  year identifies the volume.
- **Unreported case with a medium neutral citation**: case name in italics, year
  in square brackets, court identifier, judgment number, then a paragraph
  pinpoint in square brackets.
- **Legislation**: the Act's short title and year in italics, then the
  jurisdiction abbreviation in round brackets, then the pinpoint. Jurisdiction
  abbreviations are `Cth`, `NSW`, `Vic`, `Qld`, `SA`, `WA`, `Tas`, `NT` and
  `ACT`.
- **Delegated legislation**: the same shape as an Act, with the instrument's own
  title.
- **Book**: author, title in italics, then publisher, edition and year in round
  brackets, then the pinpoint.
- **Journal article**: author, article title in single quotation marks, year,
  volume and issue, journal title in italics, starting page, then the pinpoint.
- **Online material**: author, title, publication or site, date, and the address,
  with a retrieval date where the material may change.

## Pinpoints

- Reported judgments: page numbers.
- Medium neutral citations: paragraph numbers in square brackets.
- Legislation: `s` and `ss` for sections, and the Guide's abbreviations for
  subsections, parts, divisions, schedules, regulations and rules.
- Give the pinpoint every time a proposition is attributed to a source. A
  citation without a pinpoint is an invitation to check nothing.
- Attribute a judgment's proposition to the judge who wrote it: judge names take
  `J`, `JJ` for two or more, and the office abbreviation for a Chief Justice or
  President.

## Repeat References

- `Ibid` refers to the immediately preceding footnote, with a new pinpoint where
  the pinpoint changes.
- A later reference to a source already cited uses the short form the Guide
  prescribes, with the footnote number of the first full citation.
- Do not mix short-form conventions from other guides into an AGLC document.
- Renumbering footnotes breaks every back-reference. Re-check them after any
  cut, insertion or reordering.

## Quotations

- Use single quotation marks for a short quotation, with double marks only for a
  quotation inside a quotation.
- Set a long quotation as an indented block without quotation marks, following
  the Guide's length threshold.
- Reproduce the source exactly. Mark an omission with an ellipsis, an
  interpolation with square brackets, an original error with `[sic]`, and added
  emphasis with a note that the emphasis is added.
- Never adjust a quotation's spelling, capitalisation or punctuation to match
  house style. House style yields to the quoted source.

## Common Failures

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Square and round brackets around the year swapped | Check whether the year or the volume identifies the volume |
| Report series abbreviation guessed | Verify against the Guide's list and the report itself |
| Missing pinpoint | Add the page or paragraph, or drop the attribution |
| Medium neutral citation cited as if reported | Use the authorised report where one exists |
| Case name not italicised, or `v` punctuated | Italicise the case name and write `v` with no full stop |
| Statute cited without a jurisdiction | Add the bracketed jurisdiction |
| Short-form back-reference pointing at a renumbered footnote | Re-check after renumbering |
| Citation copied from a secondary source | Verify against the primary source before citing |

## Integrity

AGLC formatting makes a citation look verified. It does not verify it. Every
authority still needs the source-integrity rules in
`source-boundary-and-citations.md`: no authority that has not been located and
pinpoint-checked against a primary source, no invented pinpoint or quotation,
and no holding stated above its true altitude. A neatly formatted fabrication is
a worse failure than an untidy real citation.
