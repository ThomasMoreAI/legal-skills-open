# Demands And Pre-Action Correspondence

Use this reference for letters of demand, pre-action letters, responses to them,
and offers made before or during a proceeding. Load
`legal-correspondence-style.md` for shape, register and the send scrub; this
file covers what a demand must establish and the traps that follow it. It
supplies no current law, protocol or limitation period.

## Before Drafting

A demand is a document the other side may put before a court. Verify first:

- the claimant's identity and standing to demand;
- the recipient's correct legal identity, including the company or trustee name
  and entity number, and whether a director, guarantor or agent is being
  addressed personally;
- the factual basis, with dates, amounts and source anchors;
- the legal basis: the contract clause, statutory provision, invoice, order or
  duty relied on;
- whether a limitation period is close, and whether the demand affects it;
- whether a mandated or expected pre-action step applies, for example a genuine
  steps requirement, a court or tribunal pre-action protocol, an industry or
  scheme process, or a statutory notice with prescribed content and timing; and
- the user's instruction on what may be demanded, conceded, offered or
  threatened.

Where a statutory notice regime applies, the notice's prescribed content and
service method control. A general letter of demand cannot substitute for a
prescribed notice, and a prescribed notice should not be diluted with extra
demands.

## Structure Of A Demand

1. Identify the parties and the matter in the first line.
2. State what is required and by when, before the history.
3. Set out the facts as a short dated sequence, each anchored to a document.
4. State the legal basis in one or two sentences, naming the clause or provision.
5. State the amount or act demanded, with a calculation where money is claimed.
6. Give a deadline that is specific, reasonable and dated.
7. State what will happen if the deadline passes, limited to steps the client is
   instructed and entitled to take.
8. Say how to respond, and to whom.
9. Attach only the documents the recipient needs to check the claim.

A demand that buries the request under history reads as a complaint. Put the
number, the date and the required act in the first paragraph.

## Language Limits

- Do not threaten a step the client cannot take, is not instructed to take, or
  has no proper basis to take.
- Do not threaten criminal, regulatory or immigration consequences to obtain a
  civil advantage.
- Do not misstate the legal effect of the demand, the consequences of ignoring
  it, or the recipient's rights. Statements to a consumer or small business
  about debt recovery are regulated conduct as well as advocacy.
- Do not use US formulas. Write an Australian demand in Australian terms.
- Take extra care where the recipient is self-represented, vulnerable, a minor,
  or affected by a safety issue. Where family violence or protected contact
  details are involved, load `family-violence-and-redaction.md` before drafting.
- Keep the tone firm and factual. Insult, sarcasm and escalation cost the client
  credibility when the letter is annexed.

## Privilege, Offers And Labels

The label does not control the effect; the content and the occasion do. Use each
deliberately and only on instruction.

| Wording | What it is for | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| Open letter | A communication either side may show the court for any purpose | Nothing is protected |
| `Without prejudice` | A genuine attempt to settle a real dispute, protected from use on the merits | Does not protect a letter that makes no offer, and does not hide a threat or a misstatement |
| `Without prejudice save as to costs` | A settlement offer that may be shown on costs after judgment, the Calderbank pattern | Does not by itself carry any rule-based costs consequence |
| Formal offer under the forum's rules | The costs consequences the rules attach | Does not work unless the rule's form, content and timing are met |
| Non-admission wording | Preserves the client's position while responding | Does not neutralise an admission of fact made elsewhere in the same letter |
| Reservation of rights | Preserves a right the client actually holds | Does not create a right, and reads as filler when used by habit |

A settlement offer should state the offer, what it settles, what each party must
do, the time it stays open, and whether it includes costs and interest. An offer
that leaves the release terms unstated is an invitation to argue later.

## Responding To A Demand

Decide the posture before drafting: deny, admit in part, seek particulars, make
a counter-offer, or acknowledge and seek time. Then:

- answer each demanded item, so no item is treated as unanswered;
- correct factual errors with anchors rather than adjectives;
- do not admit a fact, a debt, service or liability unless instructed;
- do not adopt the sender's characterisation by repeating it without qualifying
  it; and
- where more time or information is needed, send a short holding response that
  names a date, rather than silence.

Silence can carry consequences under a protocol, a contract's notice clause or a
statutory notice regime. Check before advising that no response is needed.

## Completion Gate

Verify the recipient's legal identity, the amount, the deadline and the legal
basis against sources. Confirm the client's instruction for every threat, offer,
concession and reservation. Run the send scrub in
`legal-correspondence-style.md`. Sending remains a separately authorised act.
