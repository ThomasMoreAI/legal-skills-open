# AGLC4 foreign domestic sources

Use this reference with `general-rules.md` for AGLC4 chapters 15–26. Prefer the jurisdiction-specific rule. Use chapter 26 only when AGLC4 does not provide one.

## Contents

1. Canada and China
2. France and Germany
3. Hong Kong, Malaysia, and Singapore
4. New Zealand and South Africa
5. United Kingdom
6. United States
7. Other jurisdictions and translations

## Canada — chapter 15

- Cases generally follow Australian case form. Prefer an official federal report, then a semi-official provincial report, then an unofficial report such as `DLR`; add the court when not apparent.
- Statute: `Title, Statute Volume and Jurisdiction Year, c Chapter, Pinpoint.` Example structure: `Title, RSC 1985, c X-1, s 4`.
- Federal constitutional statutes use the fixed forms in rule 15.3.1; provincial and territorial constitutions use the statute form.
- Revised federal regulation: `Title, CRC, c Chapter, Pinpoint.`
- Unrevised federal regulation: `Title, SOR/Year-Regulation Number, Pinpoint.`
- Provincial and territorial regulations use the jurisdiction's official regulation abbreviation and numbering under rule 15.4.3. Verify uncommon forms against a complete AGLC4 copy.

## China — chapter 16

- Preserve Chinese characters as the source presents them. Do not transliterate Chinese characters into pinyin merely for convenience.
- Do not italicise Chinese characters. Put a title that would otherwise be italicised between guillemets: `« »`.
- Put the English translation in non-italic square brackets immediately after the Chinese element.
- Reported case: `«Case Name» [English Translation] [Year] Issue Report Series [English Translation] Starting Page, Pinpoint.`
- Unreported case: identify translated case name, court, case number, full date, and pinpoint.
- Legislative act: `«Title» [English Translation] (People's Republic of China) Promulgating Body, Order or Instrument Number, Full Date, Pinpoint.`
- Chinese secondary source: follow its ordinary AGLC4 source type, preserve surname/given-name order as rule 16.4 requires, and give translations.

## France and Germany — chapters 17–18

### France

- Case: use the conventional short case name if any, court abbreviation or full court, chamber where relevant, full date, official identifier/report, and pinpoint.
- Individual legislation: `Title (France) Law or Decree Number, Full Date, Pinpoint.`
- Code: `Code Title (France) Pinpoint.` A date/version may be added when it matters.
- Constitution: use the fixed title and full date under rule 17.2.3, then pinpoint.

### Germany

- Case: identify court abbreviation, full date, docket number, report series and starting page where available, and pinpoint.
- Individual law: `Title (Germany) Full Date, Official Gazette Citation, Pinpoint.`
- Code: `Code Title (Germany) Pinpoint.`
- Constitution: use the fixed Basic Law title and pinpoint under rule 18.2.3.

Provide English translations in square brackets under chapter 26 when the source is not in English.

## Hong Kong, Malaysia, Singapore — chapters 19, 20, 22

- Hong Kong cases generally use chapter 2, preferring authorised `HKCFAR`/`HKLRD` forms as applicable. Legislation uses the official title and chapter number: `Title (Hong Kong) cap Number, Pinpoint`; cite the Basic Law under rule 19.2.2.
- Malaysian cases use the applicable report series, year/volume, starting page, court if useful, and pinpoint. Legislation uses title, act number where supplied, jurisdiction if needed, and pinpoint; cite the Federal Constitution under rule 20.2.2.
- Singapore cases generally use chapter 2 and the reissued Singapore Law Reports where available. Unreported cases use the court's neutral citation or rule 22.1.3. Legislation uses official title, chapter number where required, revised-edition year if relevant, and pinpoint; constitutional documents follow rule 22.2.2.

## New Zealand and South Africa — chapters 21 and 23

### New Zealand

- Cases generally follow chapter 2. Prefer official or authorised reports and then the court's neutral citation.
- Māori Land Court or Māori Appellate Court: retain the court, district or minute-book identifier, full date, and page/minute pinpoint required by rule 21.1.4.
- Waitangi Tribunal: cite the report as an institutional report with claim identifier and date under rule 21.1.5.
- Statute: `Title Year (NZ) Pinpoint.` Delegated legislation uses its official title, statutory-regulation or legislative-instrument identifier, year, and pinpoint.

### South Africa

- Case: `Case Name Year (Volume) Report Series Starting Page (Court) Pinpoint` in the order required by rule 23.1. Use the report's division/court identifier.
- Legislation: use official title, act number and year, jurisdiction if needed, and pinpoint under rule 23.2.
- Constitution and Truth and Reconciliation Commission material have specialised fixed forms in rules 23.2.2 and 23.3.

## United Kingdom — chapter 24

- Modern reported case: follow chapter 2 with the UK report hierarchy. Prefer the authorised `AC`, `QB`, `Ch`, or `Fam` report where available over `WLR` or `All ER`.
- Neutral-citation case: `Case Name [Year] Court Identifier Judgment Number, [Pinpoint]`; add a reported citation when the judgment has been reported in an authorised series according to rule 24.1.2.
- Nominate report: include the nominate-report citation and its required parallel `ER` citation under rule 24.1.3.
- Scottish and unreported decisions follow rules 24.1.4–24.1.5. Add the judicial officer under rule 24.1.6 when relevant.
- Statute: `Title Year (UK) Pinpoint.` Older statutes may require regnal year and chapter: verify rule 24.2.3.
- Delegated legislation: `Title Year, SI Year/Number, Pinpoint.`
- Parliamentary debate: identify `HC Deb` or `HL Deb`, full date, volume, column pinpoint, and speaker if needed.
- Command paper: `Author, Title (Command Paper Series Number, Year) Pinpoint.`
- Parliamentary paper: `Author, Title (House Paper No Number, Session Year) Pinpoint.`

For UK materials outside chapter 24, AGLC4 points to the latest OSCOLA, but remain consistent with AGLC4's general rules in an AGLC4 document.

## United States — chapter 25

### Cases

`Case Name, Volume Report Series Starting Page, Pinpoint (Jurisdiction and Court, Year).`

- Put a non-italic comma after the case name.
- Prefer `US` for Supreme Court, `F` series for federal appellate courts, `F Supp` for federal district courts, and the appropriate regional or official state reporter.
- Do not add a court name for a US Supreme Court decision.
- Federal appellate identifier: `1st Cir`, `2nd Cir`, `DC Cir`, or `Fed Cir`.
- Federal district: `D State`, `ND State`, `SD State`, `ED State`, `WD State`, or `MD State` as applicable.
- For a state case, include state and lower-court identifier unless apparent from the reporter; omit the court for the state's highest court.
- Unreported: `Case Name (Court, Docket No, Full Date) slip op Pinpoint.`
- Put a judge after the pinpoint and before the court/year parenthesis.

### Legislation and constitutions

- Code: `Statute Title, Title/Volume Code Abbreviation § Pinpoint (Publisher, Year).` Use `§` or `§§`; add publisher only for an unofficial code.
- Session law: identify statute title, public/private law or chapter number, original pinpoint where required, volume/year, session-law abbreviation, starting page, pinpoint, and year under rule 25.3.
- Constitution: `US Constitution art/amend Pinpoint` or the state constitution form under rule 25.4.
- Federal regulation: use the `CFR` title/section/year form or `Federal Register` form under rule 25.5.1.
- Congressional debate, bill, resolution, report, hearing, and other material use the specialised forms in rules 25.6–25.8.

## Other jurisdictions and translations — chapter 26

### Author's translation

- Put the translation directly after the foreign element in non-italic square brackets.
- Add `[tr author]` or `[tr Translator Name]` at the end, unless a global translation statement covers the document.
- Do not italicise non-Latin characters unless that writing system uses an equivalent convention.

### Published translation

Cite the primary source first, then put the published translation in square brackets. Prefix the translator with `tr`. Match corresponding pinpoints where available. Always disclose translation information when relying on a translation.

### General foreign decision

For a common-law jurisdiction, adapt chapter 2. Otherwise use, as available:

`Case Name, Court or Body and Jurisdiction, Case Number, Full Date reported in Report Details, Pinpoint.`

### General foreign legislation

For a common-law jurisdiction, adapt chapter 3 and write the jurisdiction in full. Otherwise use:

`Title of Foreign Law [Translation] (Jurisdiction) Promulgating Body, Law Number, Full Date, Official Publication, Pinpoint.`

For non-English secondary material, apply the ordinary source-type rule and put translations immediately after translated elements.
