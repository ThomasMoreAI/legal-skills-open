# AGLC4 general rules

Read this reference for every citation task. Rule numbers refer to AGLC4 chapter 1.

## Footnotes and placement — rules 1.1–1.3

- Footnote propositions, direct quotations, material relied on, and source-retrieval details. Give the first citation in full.
- Put a footnote number after sentence punctuation. Place it immediately after the relevant text only when that avoids ambiguity.
- Separate multiple sources in one footnote with semicolons, not `and`.
- When an introductory signal changes, start a new sentence within the footnote.
- End every footnote with a full stop or other appropriate closing punctuation.
- Put a pinpoint immediately after its citation. Do not prefix pages with `p`, `pp`, `pg`, or `at`.
- Put paragraph pinpoints in separate square brackets: `[14]`, `[14]–[18]`. Use `para` only for source types that require it, including legislation and some international documents.
- If both page and paragraph are useful, use `page [paragraph]`: `127 [36]`.
- Separate multiple pinpoints with commas. Use a non-spaced en dash for a span and shorten the ending number except for paragraph spans: `431–2`, `[57]–[63]`, `312–13 [5]–[11]`.
- Cite a footnote within a source as `page n footnote-number` and a span as `nn`: `46 n 7`, `46 nn 7–8`.

Use a phrase such as `quoting`, `quoted in`, `citing`, `cited in`, `discussing`, or `discussed in` when the relationship between two sources matters. Precede the phrase with a comma.

## Introductory signals — rule 1.2

| Signal | Use |
|---|---|
| no signal | the source is quoted or directly supports the proposition |
| `See` | qualified support |
| `See, eg,` | one of several supporting authorities |
| `See also` | additional or general support |
| `See especially` | strongest of several supporting authorities |
| `See generally` | background material |
| `Cf` | useful contrast |
| `But see` | partial disagreement |

Do not use a signal mechanically. Match it to the relationship between the proposition and source.

## Subsequent references — rule 1.4

### Cross-references

- Cases and legislation: `Short Title (n Footnote Number) Pinpoint`.
- Most secondary sources: `Author Surname (n Footnote Number) Pinpoint`.
- Several works by the same author: `Author Surname, Short Title (n Footnote Number) Pinpoint`.
- Authors with the same surname: use the authors' full names as shown on the sources.
- No author or a body author better identified by title: `Short Title (n Footnote Number) Pinpoint`.
- Keep the short title in the same style as the full title: italics for a book, single quotation marks for an article.

Introduce a short title after the complete first citation, after pinpoints and parenthetical clauses: `(‘Short Title’)`. Use a popular or first-party name for cases unless the Crown is first, in which case the second party is generally clearer.

### `Ibid`

Use `Ibid` only for the sole source in the immediately preceding footnote.

- Same source and same pinpoint: `Ibid.`
- Same source and a different pinpoint: `Ibid 24.`
- Preceding footnote has multiple sources: use `(n X)`, not `Ibid`.
- Preceding footnote has a pinpoint but the new citation refers to the source generally: use `(n X)`, not `Ibid`.
- Within the same footnote, use `at` for a later pinpoint to the immediately preceding source; do not use `ibid`.

Recheck every `Ibid` and `(n X)` after inserting, deleting, or reordering footnotes.

## Quotations — rule 1.5

- Use single quotation marks for a quotation of three lines or fewer.
- Set a quotation of four lines or more as an indented, smaller-font block without quotation marks. A legislative or treaty extract may use block form regardless of length.
- Preserve the source wording. Put alterations and insertions in square brackets.
- Indicate omissions with a spaced ellipsis: ` … `. Do not use a leading ellipsis.
- Alter the first letter's case with square brackets where needed.
- Put `[sic]` after a significant source error, not a spelling variant or unusual capitalisation.
- Use double quotation marks inside a short single-quoted passage. In a block quote, use single quotation marks for an internal quotation.
- Put the source footnote immediately after the quotation or the proposition it supports.
- Add `(emphasis added)` or `(emphasis in original)` after the pinpoint when relevant.

## Typography, punctuation, and language — rules 1.6–1.12

- Use Australian English and the source's official spelling unless a quotation rule permits alteration.
- Omit full stops in abbreviations. Keep full stops that form part of an identifier such as a UN document number.
- Use single quotation marks. Put punctuation outside the closing quotation mark unless it forms part of the quoted material.
- Italicise case names, legislation, treaty titles, books, journals, and other source titles only as their source-specific rule requires.
- Do not italicise report-series abbreviations, party `v`, citation punctuation, or translations in square brackets.
- Use an en dash for number and date spans and to join treaty parties; use an em dash for a break in a sentence.
- Use numerals for numbers above nine and for footnotes, pages, sections, parts, divisions, percentages, measurements, dates, and times. Spell zero to nine in ordinary prose subject to AGLC4 exceptions.
- Write full dates as `1 January 2026`. Use `10:30 am` for time, and `00:14:20` for a recording pinpoint where hours are relevant.
- Follow the target document's approved heading style. AGLC4 rule 1.12 is suggestive, not mandatory.

## Bibliographies — rule 1.13

Include every source relied on, not only sources cited in the text or footnotes. A conventional structure is:

1. Articles/Books/Reports
2. Cases
3. Legislation
4. Treaties
5. Other

Keep each citation in its source-specific AGLC4 form, with these changes:

- invert only the first author's first name and surname and separate them with a comma;
- do not invert later authors;
- omit the final full stop; and
- alphabetise by first author's surname, then first name, then later authors, then title. For a body author, sort by the first significant word of the body name. With no author, sort by the first significant word of the title. Ignore initial `The` when sorting.
