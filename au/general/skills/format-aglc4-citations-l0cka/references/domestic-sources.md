## Australian cases — chapter 2

### Source preference

Use the first available version in this order:

1. authorised report;
2. generalist unauthorised report;
3. subject-specific unauthorised report;
4. court-issued medium-neutral citation; or
5. unreported decision without a medium-neutral citation.

Do not give parallel citations to Australian cases. If the relevant passage is absent from a partial report, cite the unreported judgment that contains it.

### Party names — rule 2.1

- Italicise the case name, not `v` or citation details.
- Use the parties as they appear on the first page, but omit given names and initials of individuals, post-nominals, most corporate-status indicators, punctuation in abbreviations, and additional parties.
- Cite only the first plaintiff/appellant and first defendant/respondent. Do not add `& Anor` or `& Ors`.
- Keep `[No 2]` and equivalent proceeding identifiers where needed.
- Use `R` for the Crown in criminal matters. Follow the chapter's specific treatment for Commonwealth, state, government, Attorney-General, DPP, `Re`, `ex parte`, `ex rel`, and admiralty names.
- For a subsequent reference, use the popular case name or a clear first-party short title. If the Crown is first, generally use the second party.

### Reported decisions — rule 2.2

Template:

`Case Name (Year) Volume Report Series Starting Page, Pinpoint (Judge).`

or, where the year identifies the volume:

`Case Name [Year] Volume Report Series Starting Page, Pinpoint (Judge).`

Rules:

- Use round brackets when the reports are organised by volume and the year is not essential to locating the volume. Use square brackets when the year identifies the volume.
- Use the AGLC4 report-series abbreviation. If the report is not listed in appendix A, write its name in full and not in italics.
- Repeat the starting page when it is also the pinpoint.
- Use pages for paginated reports. Add paragraph numbers after pages where useful: `215 [48]`.
- Put the judge or judges after the pinpoint in parentheses. Add the court only when it is material and not apparent from context or report series.

### Medium-neutral decisions — rule 2.3.1

Template:

`Case Name [Year] Court Identifier Judgment Number, [Pinpoint] (Judge).`

Use only a medium-neutral identifier allocated by the court. Use paragraph pinpoints. If a reported version is available, use it according to the preference hierarchy.

### Unreported decisions without a medium-neutral citation — rule 2.3.2

Template:

`Case Name (Court, Judge or Judges, Full Date) Pinpoint.`

Use page pinpoints to the delivered judgment; add square-bracket paragraphs if present.

### Related court materials — rules 2.3.3–2.8

- Proceeding: `Case Name (Court, Proceeding Number, commenced Full Date).`
- Separate court order: `Order of Judge in Case Name (Court, Proceeding Number, Full Date).`
- Administrative decision: follow the applicable reported or unreported case form, adapting the decision-maker and identifier.
- Arbitration: identify the parties, phase or award, tribunal or administering body, case number, full date, and pinpoint as the source permits.
- Transcript: `Transcript of Proceedings, Case Name (Court, Proceeding Number, Full Date) Pinpoint.`
- Submission: identify the party, document type, case name, court, proceeding number, full date, and pinpoint.

### Judicial officers and case history — rules 2.4–2.5

- Put the judicial officer after the pinpoint: `(Kiefel CJ)`, `(Gageler and Gordon JJ)`.
- Identify agreement or dissent only where relevant. Distinguish a joint judgment from separate judgments.
- Add case history only when it matters to the proposition. Cite each decision in its own correct form and describe the procedural relationship clearly.

## Australian legislative materials — chapter 3

### Statutes — rule 3.1

Template:

`Title Year (Jurisdiction) Pinpoint.`

Italicise the title and year, but not the jurisdiction or pinpoint. Use the short title enacted by Parliament and the original enactment year. Cite the principal Act as amended unless the amending Act itself matters.

Jurisdictions: `Cth`, `ACT`, `NSW`, `NT`, `Qld`, `SA`, `Tas`, `Vic`, `WA`.

Common pinpoints:

| Division | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| article | `art` | `arts` |
| chapter | `ch` | `chs` |
| clause | `cl` | `cls` |
| division | `div` | `divs` |
| paragraph | `para` | `paras` |
| part | `pt` | `pts` |
| schedule | `sch` | `schs` |
| section | `s` | `ss` |
| subsection | `sub-s` | `sub-ss` |

Put a space between designation and number but none inside the section hierarchy: `s 14(1)(a)(i)`. Cite the highest applicable level: `s 31(1)`, not `sub-s 31(1)`. Do not insert commas inside one hierarchical pinpoint: `pt III div 2`.

### Other legislative sources

- Bill: `Title Year (Jurisdiction).` Keep `Bill` in the official title; add a pinpoint under the statute rules where available.
- Multiple Australian statutes or bills: order Commonwealth, states alphabetically, then territories alphabetically unless context calls for another logical order.
- Delegated legislation: `Title Year (Jurisdiction) Pinpoint.` Use the instrument's official title and year; include a gazette or identifying number where the instrument requires it.
- Constitution: `Australian Constitution s 51(xxix).` State constitutions use the statute form.
- Explanatory material: `Explanatory Memorandum, Bill Title Year (Jurisdiction) Pinpoint.` Adapt `Statement of Compatibility`, `Explanatory Statement`, or `Explanatory Note` to the source.
- Legislative history: cite the Act or provision being enacted, amended, repealed, or inserted and identify the responsible amending legislation when that history matters.
- Gazette: `Jurisdiction, Gazette Name, Number, Full Date, Pinpoint.`
- Government instrument or ruling: include issuing body, instrument type and identifier, title if any, full date, and pinpoint.
- Non-government delegated rules: cite the issuing entity, title, version or date, and pinpoint.
- Court practice direction or note: include court, document type and number, title, full date, and pinpoint.

### Subsequent references

Introduce a clear short title if useful: `Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 (Cth) (‘ADJR Act’).` Then use `ADJR Act (n 12) s 5(2).` Use `Ibid` only under rule 1.4.3.
