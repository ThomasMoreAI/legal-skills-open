## Elements shared by secondary sources — chapter 4

### Authors and editors

- Reproduce names as shown on the title page, without full stops or spaces between initials, post-nominals, or ordinary honorifics. Keep `Sir`, `Dame`, and peerage titles if shown.
- Two or three authors: list all and join the final two with `and`.
- More than three: first author followed by `et al`.
- Editor with no author: add `(ed)` or `(eds)` after the name.
- Body author: use the body's name. Add an abbreviated jurisdiction to a government department if otherwise unclear. Omit initial `The` and corporate-status terms.
- Subdivision or individual writing for a body: `Individual or Subdivision, Body`.
- No prominently credited author: start with the title. Do not infer an author from copyright or hosting metadata.

### Titles, URLs, and archives

- Reproduce the source title, but normalise AGLC4 punctuation and capitalisation. Separate a title and first subtitle with a colon.
- Enclose article-like titles in single quotation marks. Italicise book, journal, website, film, and other container titles only where the source-specific rule requires it.
- Add a URL only when it aids retrieval. Enclose it in point brackets and place it after the pinpoint but before a short title: `<https://example.test>`.
- Do not add an access date.
- Use a stable archived link as `, archived at <https://perma.cc/...>` after the live URL, or alone if appropriate.

## Journal articles — chapter 5

Template:

`Author, ‘Article Title’ (Year) Volume(Issue) Journal Starting Page, Pinpoint.`

For a journal organised by year:

`Author, ‘Article Title’ [Year] (Issue) Journal Starting Page, Pinpoint.`

Rules:

- Use round brackets around the year for volume-organised journals; square brackets when the year locates the volume.
- Include both volume and issue where available: `42(3)`. For a year-organised journal: `[2024] (2)`.
- Write the journal title in full and italics. Omit initial `The` and a non-essential subtitle.
- Do not put punctuation between journal title and starting page. Precede the pinpoint with a comma.
- Online-only journal article: use the same form where volume, issue, and article metadata exist; otherwise adapt the internet-material rule.
- Forthcoming or advance article: replace missing final publication details with the source's forthcoming or advance status, then update when the final version exists.

## Books and chapters — chapter 6

### Book

`Author, Book Title (Publisher, Edition, Year) Pinpoint.`

- Italicise the book title.
- Omit corporate-status terms and unnecessary geographic designations from the publisher. Use the first-listed publisher or imprint.
- Include an edition only after the first: `2nd ed`; use `rev ed` where the source identifies an unnumbered revised edition.
- Use the publication year of the edition cited.
- Multi-volume work: add `vol X` before the pinpoint where required.

### Chapter in an edited book

`Chapter Author, ‘Chapter Title’ in Editor (ed), Book Title (Publisher, Edition, Year) Starting Page, Pinpoint.`

For several editors, use `(eds)`. A book with both an author and editor, or a translation, must identify each role in the order required by rules 6.6–6.7. For an audiobook, identify the author, title, narrator where prominent, publisher or platform details, year, and time pinpoint.

## Reports and research material — rules 7.1–7.2

### Report or similar document

`Author, Title (Document Type Document Number, Full Date) Pinpoint.`

Use the specific type shown by the source: `Report`, `Final Report`, `Issues Paper`, `Discussion Paper`, `Catalogue`, or equivalent. For committees, use `Committee, Legislature` as author. Do not invent an author for a royal commission report without one prominently stated.

Examples of adaptations:

- law reform body: include `Report No`, `Discussion Paper No`, or `Issues Paper No`;
- ABS: use `Australian Bureau of Statistics` and `Catalogue No`;
- parliamentary paper or digest: identify committee or department, Parliament, document type and number, full date, and pinpoint;
- research or working paper: `Author, ‘Title’ (Series or Document Type No Number, Institution, Full Date) Pinpoint`;
- thesis: `Author, ‘Title’ (Degree Thesis, University, Year)`;
- conference paper: `Author, ‘Title’ (Conference Paper, Forum, Full Date)`.

## Government, parliamentary, and institutional sources

- Speech: `Author, ‘Title’ (Speech or Named Lecture, Forum or Place, Full Date) Pinpoint.`
- Media release: `Author, ‘Title’ (Release Type Document Number, Body, Full Date) Pinpoint.` Omit the body when it is also the author.
- Parliamentary debate: `Jurisdiction, Parliamentary Debates, Chamber, Full Date, Pinpoint (Speaker).`
- Submission: `Author, Submission No Number to Body, Inquiry Name (Full Date) Pinpoint.`
- Committee evidence: identify the witness, committee, legislature, hearing date, and transcript pinpoint.
- Constitutional convention debate: identify convention, date, and pinpoint.
- Dictionary: `Dictionary Title (Edition, Year) ‘Entry’.`
- Legal encyclopedia: identify title, publisher or platform, edition or update date, volume, title number or subject, paragraph pinpoint, and author where credited.
- Looseleaf service: identify author or service, title, publisher, service number or update date where relevant, and paragraph pinpoint.
- IP material: identify jurisdiction or issuing body, right type and number, owner or applicant, title or mark, status/date, and relevant pinpoint.
- Corporate constitutive document: `Entity, Document Title (Full Date) Pinpoint.`

## News, correspondence, interviews, and media

- Print newspaper: `Author, ‘Article Title’, Newspaper (Place, Full Date) Starting Page, Pinpoint.`
- Electronic newspaper: use the newspaper form and add the URL; omit a page when none exists.
- Periodical, newsletter, or magazine: adapt the journal or newspaper form to its issue metadata.
- Written correspondence: `Correspondence from Author to Recipient, Full Date, Pinpoint.` Add the communication type where needed.
- Interview: `Interview with Person, Role or Affiliation (Interviewer, Place or Medium, Full Date).`
- Film or audiovisual work: `Title (Production Company or Studio, Year) Time Pinpoint.` Add director or episode details where the source type requires them.
- Television episode: `‘Episode Title’, Series Title (Production Company, Full Date) Time Pinpoint.`
- Podcast or radio: `‘Episode Title’, Series Title (Producer or Station, Full Date) Time Pinpoint.` Use the date at the place of origin, not a device-adjusted Australian date.

## Internet and social media — rules 7.15–7.16

### Web material

`Author, ‘Document Title’, Website Title (Document Type, Full Date) Pinpoint <URL>.`

- Use only when no more specific AGLC4 rule or published version applies.
- Omit the author if none is shown or if author and website title are identical.
- Use `Web Page`, `Blog Post`, `Forum Post`, or the source's clearer type.
- Prefer last-updated date; otherwise use creation date or the date precision shown. Omit a date that is not available.
- Use square-bracket paragraph pinpoints if the page supplies them.

### Social media and uploaded video

`Username (Real Name), ‘Title’ (Platform, Full Date, Time and Zone) Time Pinpoint <URL>.`

Keep `@` for a Twitter/X handle. Omit a nonexistent title. Include the time and zone when needed to distinguish posts. Add an archive for deleted or unstable content.

## Subsequent references

Use `Surname (n X) Pinpoint`. If the same author has several works, add the title or introduced short title. Keep `Ibid` subject to rule 1.4.3.
