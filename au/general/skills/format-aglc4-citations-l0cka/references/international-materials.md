## Treaties — chapter 8

### Open multilateral treaty

`Treaty Title, opened for signature Full Date, Volume Treaty Series Starting Page (entered into force Full Date) Pinpoint.`

### Closed multilateral, bilateral, or trilateral treaty

`Treaty Title, Parties joined by en dashes, signed Full Date, Volume Treaty Series Starting Page (entered into force Full Date) Pinpoint.`

Rules:

- Italicise the treaty title. Omit procedural wording that is not part of its substantive title.
- Omit party names for an open multilateral treaty with more than three signatories.
- If bilateral or trilateral parties do not appear in the title, add their conventional short names separated by non-spaced en dashes.
- Use an official treaty series, preferably `UNTS` for UN-registered treaties; use another authoritative national or historical series when appropriate.
- If not in force, replace the parenthetical entry-into-force element with `(not yet in force)` where the chapter requires it.
- Pinpoints commonly use `art`, `arts`, `pt`, `annex`, or another internal designation.
- Memorandum of understanding: identify title, parties, signature date, series or retrieval information, status if relevant, and pinpoint.

## United Nations — chapter 9

### Charter

`Charter of the United Nations art 51.`

### Official document

Use the elements that appear, in this order:

`Author, Title, Resolution or Decision Number, Official Records, Committee, Session, Meeting, Agenda Item, Supplement, UN Doc Document Number (Full Date) Annex Pinpoint.`

If the UN document number makes the item readily retrievable through an official UN database, official-record, committee, session, meeting, agenda, and supplement elements may be omitted.

- Italicise a document title.
- Keep full stops inside a UN document number.
- Use the official organ/type abbreviation: for example `GA Res`, `SC Res`, or the appropriate decision form.
- Put the full date in parentheses after the document number.
- UN pinpoints generally use `para` or `paras`, not square brackets.
- Treaty-body communication decision: identify committee, parties, communication number, document number, date, and pinpoint.
- Submission to a treaty body: identify author, document description, communication or review, document number if any, date, and pinpoint.
- Yearbook: cite the relevant UN yearbook title, year/volume, publication details, starting page, and pinpoint.
- Use the chapter 9 short-reference form for repeatedly cited UN documents; do not improvise an ambiguous acronym.

## ICJ and PCIJ — chapter 10

### Basic document

- `Statute of the International Court of Justice art X.`
- `International Court of Justice, Rules of Court (adopted Full Date) Pinpoint.`

### Reported decision

`Case Name (Parties v Parties or Advisory Opinion) (Phase) [Year] ICJ Rep Starting Page, Pinpoint (Judge).`

For PCIJ, use its report series and series letter/number.

- Omit introductory `Case concerning`, terminal `Case`, and similar procedural wording from the case name.
- Italicise the case name, parties, and phase; keep citation details roman.
- Use `(Advisory Opinion)` instead of parties.
- Normalise phases such as `(Judgment)`, `(Preliminary Objections)`, `(Jurisdiction)`, or `(Provisional Measures)`.
- For pleadings or unreported material, identify document type, case, parties, phase, court, case number or date, retrieval details, and pinpoint under rules 10.3–10.4.

## International arbitral and tribunal decisions — chapter 11

### State–state reported decision

`Case Name (Parties v Parties) (Phase) (Year) Volume Report Series Starting Page, Pinpoint.`

### Unreported or individual–state decision

Identify, as available:

`Case Name (Parties) (Phase) (Tribunal or Administering Body, Case Number, Full Date) Pinpoint.`

Use the official report or administering institution's decision where available. Distinguish state–state from investor/individual–state matters, and distinguish award, jurisdiction, merits, annulment, and procedural phases.

## International criminal tribunals and courts — chapter 12

- Constitutive instrument: cite as a treaty under chapter 8 unless rule 12.1 supplies a specialised form.
- Rules: `Court or Tribunal, Rules Title (adopted or amended Full Date) Pinpoint.`
- Case: `Prosecutor v Accused (Phase) (Court, Chamber, Case No, Full Date) Pinpoint (Judge).`
- Use paragraph pinpoints in square brackets unless the source-specific rule calls for another form.
- A reported case may use the official report series under rule 12.3.
- Keep chamber, case number, and phase because they often distinguish documents in the same prosecution.

## International economic materials — chapter 13

### WTO and GATT documents

Identify the issuing body or dispute parties, italicised document title, decision type, document number, full date, adoption status/date where relevant, and pinpoint.

Typical structure:

`Body or Dispute Name, Title, Document Type, WTO Doc Document Number (Full Date, adopted Full Date) Pinpoint.`

- Distinguish panel, Appellate Body, arbitration, waiver, ministerial, committee, and secretariat documents.
- Use `WTO Doc` or `GATT Doc` with the exact identifier.
- Add adoption information where the rule requires it because legal status may turn on adoption.
- For investment and trade treaties, use chapter 8. For investor–state arbitration, use chapter 11.

## Supranational materials — chapter 14

### European Union

- Official Journal: `Document Title [Year] OJ Series Issue/Starting Page, Pinpoint.`
- Electronic C-series item: use `OJ C Issue E/Starting Page`.
- Pre-1974 material may require a parallel English `OJ Spec Ed` citation.
- EU constitutive treaty: use chapter 8, substituting the Official Journal citation where required and introducing the chapter's current short title.
- EU court decision: identify parties or proceeding name, case number, phase if any, year, report series (`ECR` or current electronic citation as applicable), starting page or identifier, and pinpoint.

### Council of Europe and human-rights bodies

- Basic instrument: use the treaty form.
- European Court of Human Rights: identify case name, application number, phase, full date, report series or database identifier, and pinpoint under rule 14.3.2.
- European Commission of Human Rights: use its decision/report form under rule 14.3.3.

### Other supranational bodies

For decisions, advisory opinions, rules, pleadings, and parliamentary or council documents, include the issuing body, title or parties, document type, case/document number, date, report or official publication, and pinpoint in the order used by rules 14.4–14.5.

## Subsequent references

Introduce a short title for a long treaty, international case, or institutional document. Use the source-specific subsequent-reference rules in chapters 8.8, 9.5, 10.5, 11.3, 12.4, 13.4, and 14.6; use `Ibid` only where rule 1.4.3 permits.
