# Consumer guarantees, returns and warranties

The Australian Consumer Law is Schedule 2 to the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), applied as a law of each state and territory. Enforced by the ACCC and the state and territory fair trading agencies. Status as at 2026-08-05.

## There is no cooling-off period for online purchases

Australian law gives no change-of-mind right for a purchase made online, by phone, or in a shop. Cooling-off in the ACL attaches to **unsolicited consumer agreements** — the door-to-door and telemarketing regime in Part 3-2 Division 2 — where the consumer may terminate within **10 business days from and including the day after** the agreement was signed or received (ACL ss 76, 82). That period extends to **3 months** where the seller broke the rules: calling or visiting outside permitted hours, failing to identify themselves and their business, omitting required information or documents from the agreement, or supplying during the cooling-off period.

Separate cooling-off regimes exist outside the ACL for specific products — residential property in most states, some financial products and insurance under the Corporations Act. Never generalise from them.

So: a change-of-mind returns policy is a commercial decision. If the business offers one, it is drafted as a voluntary policy that sits **on top of** the consumer guarantees, and never described as a right the law requires.

## Consumer guarantees — Part 3-2 Division 1

A person is a "consumer" (ACL s 3) if the goods or services cost **$100,000 or less** — the threshold rose from $40,000 for supplies from 1 July 2021 — or are of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use or consumption, or are a vehicle or trailer used mainly to transport goods on public roads. Goods acquired for resupply or for use up in a production process are excluded. Many business-to-business purchases under $100,000 therefore carry the guarantees.

**Goods must be:** of acceptable quality (safe, durable, free from defects, acceptable in appearance and finish, and fit for all purposes for which goods of that kind are commonly supplied); fit for any disclosed purpose; matching their description; matching any sample or demonstration model; supplied with clear title, undisturbed possession and free from undisclosed securities; and supported by spare parts and repair facilities for a reasonable period.

**Services must be:** supplied with due care and skill; fit for any particular purpose made known; and supplied within a reasonable time where no time is agreed.

**The guarantees cannot be excluded, restricted or modified (ACL s 64).** Any term purporting to do so is void. For goods and services not of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic or household use, liability may be limited to repair, replacement, resupply or the cost of doing so, where that limitation is fair and reasonable (ACL s 64A) — this is the only liability cap that works against the guarantees, and it never works against consumer-grade goods.

**Duration.** There is no fixed period. Acceptable quality lasts as long as is reasonable for goods of that kind, price, and any statement made about them. A $3,000 appliance is expected to last far longer than a manufacturer's 12-month warranty. Never write "the guarantee period is 12 months".

## Major and minor failure — who chooses

| | Major failure | Failure that is not major |
|---|---|---|
| Goods | The **consumer** chooses: a refund, or an identical replacement, or keeping the goods and claiming compensation for the drop in value | The **business** chooses: repair, replacement or refund, within a reasonable time |
| Services | The **consumer** chooses: cancel the contract and get a refund for the unused portion, or keep the contract and claim compensation for the reduced value | The **business** must fix the problem within a reasonable time; if it does not, the consumer may cancel and get a refund for the unused portion |

A failure is major where a reasonable consumer would not have bought the goods had they known about it; the goods are significantly different from the description, sample or demonstration model; the goods are substantially unfit for their normal purpose and cannot easily be made fit within a reasonable time; the goods do not do what the consumer told the supplier they needed and cannot easily be made to; or the goods are unsafe. **Multiple minor failures can together amount to a major failure.**

In every case the consumer is also entitled to compensation for any other reasonably foreseeable loss or damage caused by the failure.

The consumer's rights run against the **supplier** — the business that sold the item. Sending the customer to the manufacturer instead of dealing with the claim is itself a misrepresentation of their rights.

## What a returns policy must never say

Each of these purports to exclude the guarantees (s 64) and misrepresents the existence or effect of a guarantee, right or remedy contrary to ACL s 29, a civil penalty provision:

- "No refunds" / "No returns"
- "Exchange or store credit only"
- "No returns on sale items" (change-of-mind can be excluded on sale items; a faulty sale item cannot)
- "Returns accepted within 14 days only" applied to faulty goods
- "Original packaging required" applied to faulty goods
- "Once opened, no returns" applied to faulty goods
- "We are not responsible for goods once they leave our premises" — the guarantee of delivery in acceptable condition sits with the supplier
- "Please contact the manufacturer"
- "Warranty void if the product has been used"

Signs and policies may lawfully limit **change-of-mind** returns, as long as it is clear that the limits apply only to change of mind and not to the consumer guarantees.

## Warranties against defects — Competition and Consumer Regulations 2010 reg 90

A warranty against defects is any representation that a business will repair, replace, resupply or compensate if the goods or services are defective. Manufacturer warranties, extended warranties and "12-month guarantee" claims are all warranties against defects.

The document must be transparent — reasonably plain language, legible, clearly presented — and must concisely state what the consumer must do to claim, and what the business must do to honour it. It must also state:

- the business's name, business address, telephone number and email address
- what the consumer must do to claim, and the claim procedure
- who bears the expense of claiming, and how the consumer is reimbursed
- the period or periods within which a defect must appear
- a statement that the benefits under the warranty are in addition to other rights and remedies of the consumer under a law

**And the prescribed mandatory text, reproduced exactly.** Three versions. Source: ACCC, "Warranties"; the services and combined versions apply to supplies from 9 June 2019.

**Goods:**

> Our goods come with guarantees that cannot be excluded under the Australian Consumer Law. You are entitled to a replacement or refund for a major failure and compensation for any other reasonably foreseeable loss or damage. You are also entitled to have the goods repaired or replaced if the goods fail to be of acceptable quality and the failure does not amount to a major failure.

**Services:**

> Our services come with guarantees that cannot be excluded under the Australian Consumer Law. For major failures with the service, you are entitled: to cancel your service contract with us; and to a refund for the unused portion, or to compensation for its reduced value. You are also entitled to be compensated for any other reasonably foreseeable loss or damage. If the failure does not amount to a major failure, you are entitled to have problems with the service rectified in a reasonable time and, if this is not done, to cancel your contract and obtain a refund for the unused portion of the contract.

**Goods and services together:**

> Our goods and services come with guarantees that cannot be excluded under the Australian Consumer Law. For major failures with the service, you are entitled: to cancel your service contract with us; and to a refund for the unused portion, or to compensation for its reduced value. You are also entitled to choose a refund or replacement for major failures with goods. If a failure with the goods or a service does not amount to a major failure, you are entitled to have the failure rectified in a reasonable time. If this is not done you are entitled to a refund for the goods and to cancel the contract for the service and obtain a refund of any unused portion. You are also entitled to be compensated for any other reasonably foreseeable loss or damage from a failure in the goods or service.

Additional explanatory information may be added, but nothing may limit or contradict the mandatory text. Paraphrasing, shortening or reformatting the mandatory text into bullet points is the contravention.

## Checkpoints

- [ ] No "no refunds", "store credit only", "sale items final" or equivalent anywhere on the site, in emails, or on physical signage
- [ ] Guarantee rights stated before, not after, the voluntary change-of-mind policy
- [ ] The voluntary policy explicitly labelled as voluntary and confined to change of mind
- [ ] Major-failure remedy choice sits with the consumer, and the text says so
- [ ] No stated expiry on the consumer guarantees
- [ ] Customers are not redirected to the manufacturer for guarantee claims
- [ ] Any warranty against defects carries the reg 90 mandatory text verbatim, in the right version
