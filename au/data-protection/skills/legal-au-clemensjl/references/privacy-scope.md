# Who the Privacy Act binds

Decide this before writing a privacy policy. Getting it wrong in either direction is expensive: an exempt business ships obligations it does not have, or a covered business ships nothing at all. Status as at 2026-08-05.

## APP entity

The Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) binds "APP entities" — Australian Government agencies and "organisations". An organisation is an individual, body corporate, partnership, unincorporated association or trust that is not a small business operator, a registered political party, an agency, a state or territory authority, or a prescribed instrumentality.

Everything in the APPs flows from that status. There is no size threshold for the number of records, no headcount trigger, and no registration step.

## The small business exemption — Privacy Act s 6D

A small business operator is an entity whose **annual turnover for the previous financial year is $3,000,000 or less** (s 6D). Turnover for this purpose means all income from all sources; it excludes assets held, capital gains and proceeds of capital sales. New businesses that have not completed a financial year are treated as small business operators until turnover exceeds the threshold.

The exemption is the default position for most Australian small websites. It is also the most over-claimed provision in Australian privacy practice, because the carve-outs are broad.

**A business is covered regardless of turnover if it:**

- provides a health service and holds health information other than in an employee record
- discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or advantage
- provides a benefit, service or advantage in order to collect personal information about another individual from someone else
- is a Commonwealth contracted service provider
- operates a residential tenancy database
- is a credit reporting body
- is a reporting entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006
- is an employee association registered under the Fair Work (Registered Organisations) Act 2009
- conducts a protected action ballot
- is a telecommunications service provider subject to the data retention obligations in Part 5-1A of the Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979
- is accredited under the Consumer Data Right
- is a related body corporate of an entity that is covered
- is prescribed by the Privacy Regulation 2013
- has opted in to coverage (s 6EA)

Source: OAIC, "Small business", oaic.gov.au. The trading-in-personal-information carve-outs are the ones that catch ordinary businesses — data-sharing arrangements with marketing partners, lead generation, list swaps, and "free tool in exchange for contact details" funnels all need to be tested against them.

**Exempt from the Privacy Act is not exempt from everything else.** The Spam Act 2003, the Do Not Call Register Act 2006, the Australian Consumer Law, the Online Safety Act 2021 and the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 apply irrespective of turnover. So does the statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy.

## Other exemptions that get over-claimed

- **Employee records.** An organisation's handling of an employee record in relation to a current or former employment relationship is exempt. It does not extend to job applicants, to contractors, to prospective employees, or to health information held outside an employee record. Recruitment data is fully covered.
- **Registered political parties**, and acts of political representatives in connection with an election, are outside the Act.
- **Journalism.** Media organisations are exempt for acts in the course of journalism where the organisation is publicly committed to published privacy standards.
- **Related bodies corporate** may share personal information between themselves as if they were one entity for collection purposes — but only where each is an APP entity, and the sharing still has to be consistent with the collection notice given.

None of these exemptions removes the ACL, the Spam Act or the statutory tort.

## Decision order

1. Is the entity an agency? Then the APPs apply, with the agency-specific variants.
2. Is annual turnover above $3,000,000? Then it is an organisation and the APPs apply in full.
3. At or under $3,000,000: test every s 6D carve-out. One hit means the APPs apply in full.
4. No carve-out: has it opted in under s 6EA? If yes, the APPs apply in full.
5. Otherwise the Privacy Act does not apply — write the policy as a voluntary statement of practice, and move straight to the Spam Act, ACL, Online Safety Act and accessibility obligations, which do apply.

## Opting in

An entity that would otherwise be exempt may opt in under s 6EA by giving the Commissioner a written notice, after which it is treated as an organisation. Practical reasons to opt in: enterprise customers who require it contractually, tender eligibility, and the ability to state Privacy Act compliance truthfully in a policy. Once opted in, the whole Act applies — including the NDB scheme.

**Do not state Privacy Act compliance in a policy for an entity that is exempt and has not opted in.** A false statement of compliance is a misleading representation under ACL s 18. Write instead that the business handles personal information in line with the APPs as a matter of policy, if that is true.

## Reach beyond Australia

The Act applies to an organisation with an Australian link — including a body corporate incorporated in Australia and, for foreign entities, carrying on business in Australia. The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 removed the additional requirement that the information be collected or held in Australia, so a foreign entity carrying on business in Australia is caught by reference to the business connection alone. ## The reverse trap: the GDPR reaching Australia

An Australian business is caught by Regulation (EU) 2016/679 Art 3(2) if it offers goods or services to individuals in the EU or monitors their behaviour in the EU. Indicators: EU-country shipping options, prices in euros, EU language versions, EU-targeted advertising, or analytics that profile EU visitors. Where Art 3(2) applies, the GDPR obligations sit **on top of** the APPs, not instead of them: lawful basis, Art 13/14 information, Art 15–22 rights including erasure, an Art 27 EU representative, and 72-hour breach notification under Art 33.

The UK GDPR and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 raise the equivalent question for the UK; the California Consumer Privacy Act does for California residents where the thresholds are met. [[UNVERIFIED: current UK and California thresholds were not checked in this session]]

Do not merge the two regimes into one document by default. Either write an Australian policy and a separate EU-facing annex, or write one policy with a clearly delimited "If you are in the European Economic Area" section.

## Related reforms still in flight

- **Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024** — first tranche. Statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy in effect from 10 June 2025. Doxxing offences inserted into the Criminal Code. APP 1.7–1.9 automated decision-making transparency commences 10 December 2026. The Children's Online Privacy Code must be registered by 10 December 2026; OAIC ran the exposure draft consultation from 31 March to 5 June 2026.
- **Second tranche** — the more structural changes (a fair and reasonable test, a direct right of action, a right to erasure, and review of the small business and employee records exemptions) were announced but are not law. ## Checkpoints

- [ ] Turnover figure obtained and recorded, with the financial year it relates to
- [ ] Every s 6D carve-out tested individually and the answer written down
- [ ] Data-sharing, lead-gen and list arrangements tested against the trading-in-personal-information carve-outs
- [ ] Related bodies corporate checked, not just the trading entity
- [ ] Opt-in status confirmed before any statement of Privacy Act compliance
- [ ] No claim of Privacy Act compliance by an exempt, non-opted-in entity
- [ ] EU/UK/California exposure assessed and documented, with the trigger identified
- [ ] Nothing drafted against a reform that is not yet in force
