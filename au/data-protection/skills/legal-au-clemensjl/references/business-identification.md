# Business identification

Australia has no imprint law. There is no statutory obligation equivalent to § 5 ECG, § 5 TMG or the EU e-Commerce Directive requiring a website to carry a legal notice page. The obligations that do exist are narrower and attach to documents and transactions, not to web pages as such. Status as at 2026-08-05.

Consequence: a template that ports an "Impressum" or "Legal Notice" page into an Australian site invents obligations. The real risks are the reverse — a customer who cannot identify or contact the trader, which is misleading conduct territory under ACL s 18, and Spam Act s 17 sender identification.

## ABN

The Australian Business Number is issued under the A New Tax System (Australian Business Number) Act 1999 (Cth). There is no general statutory duty to publish an ABN on a website.

Where an ABN matters:

- **Tax invoices.** A tax invoice for a taxable supply must show the supplier's identity or ABN, among other required particulars, under the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999. Without a valid tax invoice a GST-registered customer cannot claim an input tax credit. [[UNVERIFIED: the section reference (GST Act s 29-70) and the $82.50 GST-inclusive threshold below which a tax invoice is not required were not confirmed against legislation.gov.au in this session]]
- **No-ABN withholding.** A business that receives an invoice from a supplier who does not quote an ABN must generally withhold from the payment at the top rate and remit it to the ATO. [[UNVERIFIED: the current withholding rate was not confirmed in this session]]
- **Verification.** Customers and other businesses check ABNs on ABN Lookup (abr.business.gov.au). Publishing the ABN on the website is therefore a practical trust and verification measure, and it is also what makes the Spam Act s 17 sender identification unambiguous.

Recommendation: publish the ABN. Not because a statute names websites, but because the alternative is an unidentifiable trader.

## Companies — ACN and Corporations Act s 153

A company registered under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) must set out its name on all its public documents and negotiable instruments, and its ACN must appear with the name (s 153). ASIC's guidance lists the documents where the name and ACN are required:

- documents lodged with ASIC
- documents required to be signed, issued or published under the Corporations Act
- documents signed or issued by the company in a transaction
- invoices and statements of account, receipts, and orders for goods and services
- business letterhead, official company notices, letters of credit
- cheques, promissory notes and bills of exchange

The name and ACN must appear on the first page where required, must be legible, and it must be clear which company they belong to. A common seal, if used, must carry the company's name, the words "Australian Company Number" and the ACN.

Where the company's ABN incorporates its ACN — which is the usual case, the ABN being the ACN with a two-digit prefix — ASIC accepts the ABN in place of the ACN provided it is used in the same places and the ACN is identifiable within it.

A website page is not itself listed by ASIC. Order confirmations, invoices and receipts generated by the site plainly are. Build the identifiers into the transactional document templates, not only into a footer.

## Registered business names

A name used in business that is not the entity's own legal name must be registered on the Business Names Register administered by ASIC under the Business Names Registration Act 2011 (Cth). Registration does not create rights in the name; it only permits its use and links it to an ABN. Trade mark protection is separate, under the Trade Marks Act 1995, administered by IP Australia.

Where a registered business name is used publicly, the underlying legal entity should be identifiable: "Widget World is a registered business name of Widget Holdings Pty Ltd ACN [[ACN]]". A customer who cannot work out who they contracted with has a misleading conduct argument.

## What actually belongs where

| Surface | Identifiers |
|---|---|
| Website footer or contact page | Legal entity name, registered business name if different, ABN, ACN for companies, street address (or at minimum a service address), email, phone |
| Order confirmation, invoice, receipt, statement | Full company name and ACN or ABN on the first page; the tax invoice particulars where GST applies |
| Marketing email and SMS | Legal entity name, ABN, and a contact route valid for at least 30 days (Spam Act s 17) |
| Contracts and terms of service | Full legal entity name and ABN/ACN of the contracting entity — not the brand |
| Licensed trades and professions | Licence number and issuing regulator, per the relevant state or territory licensing law |

A PO box is acceptable for correspondence but does not by itself identify where the business operates. Sole traders operating from home face a real disclosure decision; that is a business decision (registered office service, coworking address), not a drafting problem. Raise it rather than working around it.

## Checkpoints

- [ ] Legal entity name exactly as registered, taken from ASIC or ABN Lookup rather than from memory
- [ ] ABN present and correct; ACN present for companies
- [ ] Registered business name registered, current, and linked to the correct ABN
- [ ] Contracting entity in the terms of service is the same entity that issues the invoices
- [ ] Company name and ACN or ABN appear on the first page of invoices, receipts, order confirmations and statements
- [ ] Tax invoice particulars correct where GST applies
- [ ] Marketing messages carry entity name, ABN and a valid contact route
- [ ] Address is a real geographic address or a deliberate, disclosed service address
- [ ] Licence numbers and regulators shown where the trade or profession is licensed
- [ ] No imported "Impressum", "Legal Notice per § 5 TMG", or EU-style legal disclosure page
