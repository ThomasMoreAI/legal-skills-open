# Statutory privacy tort method

## Authority and operation

Verify the point-in-time Privacy Act text. Schedule 2 commenced on 10 June
2025 (verified 26 August 2026). Section 94A and Schedule 2 clauses 6(2)–(3) require the cause of action to
be read separately from most of the Act. Do not make APP-entity coverage a
gateway.

## Element prompts

Against the current statutory text, test:

1. an intrusion upon the plaintiff's seclusion, misuse of information relating
   to the plaintiff, or both;
2. a reasonable expectation of privacy in all the circumstances;
3. intentional or reckless conduct;
4. seriousness;
5. whether the public interest in privacy outweighs countervailing public
   interests identified by the statute; and
6. all application, procedural and burden provisions.

The tort can be actionable without proof of damage. Verify rather than assume
the current defences, exemptions, remedies and limitation periods.

## AI and technology triggers

Always consider the screen for covert or persistent surveillance, recording,
facial or biometric analysis, location tracking, large-scale scraping,
profiling or inference, deepfakes, disclosure or publication, intimate or
health information, and a design choice plausibly showing intent or
recklessness. A trigger means investigate; it does not establish an element.

Do not use case law, tribunal decisions, overseas analogies or case-derived
tests. Identify interpretive uncertainty for the supervising lawyer.
