# Insolvency Proceeding Types Across Jurisdictions

Reference guide for interpreting insolvency and distress-related proceedings in Poland, Austria, and Spain. Each section covers the legal framework, proceeding types, stages, and what they mean for creditors and credit risk assessment.

---

## Poland

Polish insolvency law is governed by two main statutes: Prawo upadlosciowe (Bankruptcy Law) and Prawo restrukturyzacyjne (Restructuring Law). The KRZ (Krajowy Rejestr Zadluzonych) and MSiG (Monitor Sadowy i Gospodarczy) are the primary publication channels.

### Bankruptcy Proceedings (Upadlosc)

**Upadlosc likwidacyjna** (Liquidation bankruptcy)
- The company ceases operations and its assets are sold to satisfy creditors.
- Triggered when the debtor is insolvent (unable to meet obligations for 3+ months or liabilities exceed assets).
- A trustee (syndyk) is appointed to manage the estate.
- Creditor recovery rates for unsecured claims: typically 5-15%.
- KRZ status: "W toku" during proceedings, "Zakonczono" when finalized.
- **Credit risk implication:** Critical. No recovery of trade receivables beyond the distribution plan.

**Upadlosc konsumencka** (Consumer bankruptcy)
- Applies to natural persons (individuals), not companies.
- Relevant when assessing sole proprietors or personal guarantors.
- Simplified proceedings, shorter timelines.
- **Credit risk implication:** Relevant if the debtor is a sole trader or the personal guarantor of a corporate obligation.

### Restructuring Proceedings (Restrukturyzacja)

Poland offers four types of restructuring, ordered from least to most court-supervised:

**1. Postepowanie o zatwierdzenie ukladu** (Arrangement approval proceedings)
- Lightest form. The debtor negotiates directly with creditors.
- No court supervision during negotiation. Court only approves the final arrangement.
- Debtor retains full management control.
- **Credit risk implication:** High. Company is in difficulty but functional. Monitor closely.

**2. Przyspieszone postepowanie ukladowe** (Accelerated arrangement proceedings)
- Court-supervised but fast-tracked. Creditor list is not disputed.
- A supervisor (nadzorca sadowy) is appointed but the debtor stays in control.
- Typical duration: 2-4 months.
- **Credit risk implication:** High. More formal than type 1. Company acknowledged it needs court involvement.

**3. Postepowanie ukladowe** (Arrangement proceedings)
- Standard court-supervised restructuring. Creditor claims can be disputed.
- A supervisor is appointed. Debtor retains management but with oversight.
- Typical duration: 6-12 months.
- **Credit risk implication:** High to Critical. Longer duration suggests deeper problems.

**4. Postepowanie sanacyjne** (Remedial proceedings)
- Most intensive. The debtor may lose management control to a court-appointed administrator (zarzadca).
- Allows termination of unfavorable contracts and workforce reductions.
- Provides the strongest protections against enforcement actions.
- **Credit risk implication:** Critical. The company's situation is severe enough to warrant full court takeover.

### KRZ Proceeding Stages

| Status | Polish | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| In progress | W toku | Active proceeding. Company is currently subject to insolvency or restructuring. |
| Concluded | Zakonczono | Proceeding finished. Outcome may be successful restructuring or completed liquidation. |
| Discontinued | Umorzono | Proceeding dropped. Possible reasons: insufficient assets, debtor recovered, procedural defect. |
| Arrangement approved | Uklad zatwierdzony | Restructuring plan accepted by creditors and approved by court. Company continues under plan terms. |
| Arrangement rejected | Uklad odrzucony | Creditors or court rejected the plan. Proceeding may convert to bankruptcy. |

### MSiG Announcement Types (Credit Risk Relevant)

| Announcement | Polish Term | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Restructuring application | Wniosek o otwarcie restrukturyzacji | High |
| Restructuring opened | Otwarcie postepowania restrukturyzacyjnego | High |
| Bankruptcy declared | Ogloszenie upadlosci | Critical |
| Creditor list published | Lista wierzytelnosci | Critical |
| Distribution plan | Plan podzialu | Critical |
| Proceedings concluded | Zakonczenie postepowania | Check outcome |
| Call to creditors | Wezwanie wierzycieli | High - you may need to file a claim |
| Change of address | Zmiana adresu | Low (but note if to virtual office) |
| Board member change | Zmiana w skladzie zarzadu | Low-Medium (context dependent) |

## Austria

Austrian insolvency law is governed by the Insolvenzordnung (IO). All insolvency publications are made through the Ediktsdatei, the federal edict database maintained by the Ministry of Justice.

### Insolvency Proceeding Types

**Konkursverfahren** (Bankruptcy proceedings)
- Full liquidation of the debtor's assets.
- A Masseverwalter (insolvency administrator) is appointed to manage the estate.
- Creditors file claims within a court-set deadline (Anmeldefrist).
- Assets are sold and proceeds distributed according to statutory priority.
- **Credit risk implication:** Critical. Recovery depends on priority class and asset pool.

**Sanierungsverfahren mit Eigenverwaltung** (Restructuring with self-administration)
- The debtor proposes a restructuring plan (Sanierungsplan) and retains management.
- Must offer creditors at least 30% of their claims, payable within 2 years.
- A Sanierungsverwalter (restructuring supervisor) oversees the process.
- If creditors approve the plan, the company continues operating.
- **Credit risk implication:** High. Viable companies can emerge successfully, but the 30% minimum means significant haircuts for unsecured creditors.

**Sanierungsverfahren ohne Eigenverwaltung** (Restructuring without self-administration)
- Same as above, but the debtor loses management control.
- An Insolvenzverwalter takes over operations.
- Indicates the court determined the debtor cannot be trusted to self-manage.
- **Credit risk implication:** Critical. More severe than self-administered restructuring.

**Schuldenregulierungsverfahren** (Debt regulation proceedings)
- Applies only to natural persons (individuals) and unincorporated businesses.
- Provides a path to discharge of debts for individuals.
- Not applicable to GmbH, AG, or other corporate forms.
- **Credit risk implication:** Relevant for sole proprietors and personal guarantors.

### Ediktsdatei Entry Structure

Each Ediktsdatei entry contains:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Aktenzeichen | Case number (format: XXX S YYY/ZZ where XXX is the court code) |
| Gericht | Responsible court (e.g., Handelsgericht Wien, Landesgericht Linz) |
| Schuldner | Debtor name and address |
| Verfahrensart | Proceeding type (Konkurs, Sanierung mit/ohne Eigenverwaltung) |
| Insolvenzverwalter | Appointed administrator - name, address, contact |
| Anmeldefrist | Deadline for creditors to file claims |
| Berichtstagsatzung | Reporting hearing date |
| Prufungstagsatzung | Claim examination hearing date |

**Reading tip:** If you see an entry with a Sanierungsverfahren that later has a follow-up entry converting to Konkursverfahren, the restructuring attempt failed and the company is now being liquidated.

## Spain

Spanish commercial registry announcements are published through BORME (Boletin Oficial del Registro Mercantil). BORME does not exclusively cover insolvency - it publishes all corporate acts. The skill focuses on acts that signal financial distress.

### BORME Section A - Distress-Signaling Acts

**Concurso de acreedores** (Creditor proceedings / insolvency)
- Spanish equivalent of formal insolvency proceedings.
- Since the 2022 reform (Ley Concursal), divided into pre-insolvency (comunicacion del art. 583) and full proceedings.
- A Administrador concursal (insolvency administrator) is appointed.
- **Credit risk implication:** Critical. Formal insolvency.

**Disolucion** (Dissolution)
- The company is being wound up and will cease to exist.
- May be voluntary (shareholder decision) or forced (court order, statutory cause).
- If followed by "Liquidacion," assets are being distributed.
- **Credit risk implication:** Critical. Company is terminating.

**Reduccion de capital** (Capital reduction)
- Shareholders reduce the company's share capital.
- Can be routine (returning excess capital) or distress-driven (offsetting accumulated losses).
- Distress signal when combined with other negative indicators.
- **Credit risk implication:** Medium in isolation. High when combined with Cese or declining financials.

**Cese** (Cessation / officer removal)
- A director, administrator, or officer has been removed.
- Check whether a corresponding **Nombramiento** (appointment) follows.
- Cese without Nombramiento means the position is vacant - a governance gap.
- **Credit risk implication:** Low-Medium in isolation. High if multiple Cese entries without replacements.

**Revocacion de poderes** (Revocation of powers of attorney)
- Powers previously granted to an individual are revoked.
- May indicate internal conflict, loss of trust, or governance restructuring.
- **Credit risk implication:** Low-Medium. Notable when combined with Cese.

### BORME Progression Patterns

Healthy company pattern:
```
Nombramiento (appointment) -> Ampliacion de capital (capital increase) -> Nombramiento
```

Distress pattern:
```
Cese (removal) -> Cese (removal) -> Reduccion de capital -> Disolucion
```

Insolvency pattern:
```
Cese -> Concurso de acreedores -> Nombramiento de administrador concursal -> Disolucion
```

**Timing matters:** Look at the date gaps between entries.
- Multiple Cese entries within 2-3 months = exodus pattern (High risk)
- Reduccion de capital followed by Disolucion within 6 months = terminal pattern (Critical)
- Concurso de acreedores entry = immediate Critical classification

### BORME Section B vs Section A

- **Section A** contains corporate acts (appointments, removals, capital changes, dissolutions). This is where distress signals appear.
- **Section B** contains general announcements and court notices. Less useful for credit risk but may contain supplementary information about proceedings.

The `regdata/borme-corporate-acts-scraper` extracts Section A data, which is the relevant section for credit risk assessment.

## Cross-Jurisdiction Comparison

| Concept | Poland | Austria | Spain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidation bankruptcy | Upadlosc likwidacyjna | Konkursverfahren | Concurso + Liquidacion |
| Restructuring (debtor in control) | Postepowanie o zatwierdzenie ukladu | Sanierungsverfahren mit Eigenverwaltung | Pre-concurso (art. 583) |
| Restructuring (court-controlled) | Postepowanie sanacyjne | Sanierungsverfahren ohne Eigenverwaltung | Concurso de acreedores |
| Publication channel | KRZ + MSiG | Ediktsdatei | BORME |
| Individual insolvency | Upadlosc konsumencka | Schuldenregulierungsverfahren | Concurso (persona fisica) |
| Minimum creditor recovery (restructuring) | Per arrangement terms | 30% within 2 years | Per convenio terms |
| Typical unsecured recovery (liquidation) | 5-15% | 5-20% | 5-15% |
