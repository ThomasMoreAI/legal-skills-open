# Lebanon 🇱🇧 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `lb`. Status: ✅ Fully populated (researched 2026-04 / 2026-05). Foreign-buyer regime: **HEAVILY RESTRICTED** under Decree-Law 11614/1969 as amended by **Law 296/2001** — non-Arab foreigners need **Council of Ministers (Cabinet) prior authorization via Decree**, capped at **3,000 m² nationally** per individual, with a **cumulative national 3% cap** (and per-district 3% cap) on total foreign ownership; mandatory **5-year build/develop deadline** post-purchase. **Beirut Central District (Solidere)** sits under a separate, more permissive regime. Lebanon is also in the **5th–7th year of one of the world's three worst economic crises since the mid-19th century** (World Bank framing): banking system effectively collapsed Oct 2019, mortgage market frozen, **de-facto USD-dollarization** (~80%+ of property transactions in fresh USD cash outside the banking system); **LBP devalued in two steps — 1 Feb 2023 (1,507.5 → 15,000 LBP/USD) then early/Mar 2024 (15,000 → 89,500 LBP/USD)** where it has remained through 2026-05 (2026-05-27 verified, source BDL + BLOMINVEST BRITE FX series + Trade.gov). **2024 Israel–Hezbollah war (Sep–Nov 2024)** caused extensive damage in South Lebanon + Bekaa + Beirut SE suburbs (Dahieh) and was followed by a Nov 2024 ceasefire. **BDL Governor Karim Souaid** appointed by Cabinet 27 Mar 2025 (took office 4 Apr 2025) — first permanent Governor since Riad Salameh's exit on 31 Jul 2023 (Salameh in detention since 3 Sep 2024 on financial-misconduct charges; Wassim Mansouri served as Acting Governor in the interim). The Aug 2020 Beirut Port explosion (~218 deaths, ~7,000 injured, ~300k displaced) reconstruction is ongoing.

> **🇱🇧 LEAD WARNING — read before any other section.** Three structural realities dominate any Lebanese real-estate decision:
> (1) **Foreign-ownership Decree-Law 11614/1969 + Law 296/2001**: Cabinet authorization REQUIRED for non-Arab foreigners; max 3,000 m² nationally per individual; cumulative national 3% cap; 5-year build/develop-or-forfeit clause. Misreading these voids title and may forfeit deposit (`https://www.pcm.gov.lb/`, `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`).
> (2) **2019–2026 banking crisis**: Lebanese banks effectively collapsed Oct 2019; pre-2019 USD deposits ("Lollars") locked under BDL Circular 158 / 161 + ongoing informal capital controls; mortgages essentially frozen; foreign buyers transact in **fresh USD cash** outside the banking system. Confirm fresh-vs-Lollar status of any USD shown by the seller (`https://www.bdl.gov.lb/`).
> (3) **2024 war damage + cumulative geopolitical risk**: 2024 Israel–Hezbollah war damaged S Lebanon, Bekaa, Beirut Dahieh; Nov 2024 ceasefire holds (verify currency); Aug 2020 Beirut Port explosion zone reconstruction ongoing; political vacuum closed Jan 2025 with Joseph Aoun presidency after 2-year void. Always retain an independent Lebanese advocate (`محامي عقاري`) — never use the developer's lawyer.

## Country profile

- **Postcode**: 4-digit Lebanese postal code (NNNN) issued by Liban Post; Beirut central postcodes start 11xx; full lookup at `https://www.libanpost.com/`.
- **Admin levels**: 8 governorates (محافظات / muhafazat) → 26 districts (أقضية / qadaa, sing. caza) → ~1,000+ municipalities (بلديات / baladiyat). Beirut and Mount Lebanon are the densest admin units; the rest of the country (North, South, Bekaa, Baalbek-Hermel, Akkar, Nabatieh) is more rural. Source: Ministry of Interior and Municipalities [`https://www.moim.gov.lb/`](https://www.moim.gov.lb/) and Central Administration of Statistics (CAS) [`https://www.cas.gov.lb/`](https://www.cas.gov.lb/).
- **Population**: ~5.5 million (2024 CAS estimate, source `http://www.cas.gov.lb/`), inclusive of an est. ~1.5M Syrian refugees + ~470k Palestinian refugees (UNRWA-registered, of which a smaller share are physically resident). Beirut Metropolitan ~2.4M, Tripoli ~700k, Sidon (Saida) ~270k, Tyre (Sour) ~160k. The **Lebanese diaspora (Mahjar)** is estimated at **~13M globally** — far larger than the domestic resident population — and is the dominant foreign-buyer cohort (active diaspora in West Africa, France, US, Canada, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Australia, GCC).
- **Currency**: **LBP (Lebanese pound, ل.ل.)** + **de-facto USD dollarization**. The historic peg of **1 USD = 1,507.5 LBP** (1997–2019) decoupled in **Sep 2019**; black-market rate hit USD 1 = LBP 100,000+ at peak (2022). The Banque du Liban (BDL) introduced the **Sayrafa platform** (2022–2023). **LBP devalued in two official steps: (a) 1 Feb 2023 — BDL changed the official rate from 1,507.5 → 15,000 LBP/USD (first step of unification); (b) early/March 2024 — official rate adjusted further to 89,500 LBP/USD**, where it has remained through 2026-05 (2026-05-27 verified, source BDL `https://www.bdl.gov.lb/` + BLOMINVEST BRITE FX series + Trade.gov Lebanon Trade Financing). **Property transactions are overwhelmingly in fresh USD cash** (~80%+ by value); LBP only for small ancillary payments. **Hyperinflation** ~95–98% LBP devaluation 2019–2023 makes any LBP-denominated quote meaningless without a date stamp.
- **Languages**: Arabic (official, MSA + Lebanese colloquial); **French** is widely used in business, law, education, and elite social contexts (legal-history legacy); **English** dominant in tech, banking, healthcare, hospitality; **Armenian** in Beirut Bourj Hammoud + Anjar. Title deeds are filed in Arabic; sworn-translation common.
- **Legal system**: Hybrid civil law (French Napoleonic via 1932 Code des Obligations et des Contrats — drafted under the French Mandate) + **18 confessional personal-status courts** (Maronite, Sunni, Shia, Druze, Greek Orthodox, etc.) for family + inheritance law. Property/contract is civil-code; **inheritance follows confessional rules** for Lebanese — for Muslims, Sharia mandatory shares apply; for Christians and Druze, separate confessional codes. Non-Lebanese foreign buyers can typically opt for home-country inheritance law via prior declaration but should verify with a Lebanese estates lawyer at purchase.
- **Cadastre / land registry**: **General Directorate of Land Registry and Cadastre (DLRC / Direction Générale des Affaires Foncières / المديرية العامة للشؤون العقارية والمساحة)** under Ministry of Finance — **primary portal `https://www.lrc.gov.lb/en`** (Title Register e-services, online ownership statements, fee simulator, 17 land registry offices directory, dedicated **Foreign Ownership Department** at `https://www.lrc.gov.lb/en/content/foreign-ownership-department`) (2026-05-27 verified — note: **never use `lra.gov.lb` which is a typo**); Ministry of Finance also at `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`. Lebanon has a **Torrens-style register** descended from late-Ottoman Tabu reform (1858, historic term *Tabou* / طابو) → French Mandate cadastre (1926+) → Lebanese consolidation. The **title deed (إخراج قيد عقاري / Khulasat Qayd ‘Aqari)** is state-guaranteed.
  - **Identifier triplet**: **Cadastral Section / Property Number (رقم العقار)** + **Lot / Plot (قطعة)** + **Cadastral Zone / Manatiq Aqaria (المنطقة العقارية)**. Always verify all three against the deed and the survey map.
  - **Online services**: partial digitization; full title abstracts typically pulled at the regional cadastre office. Source: Ministry of Finance cadastre directorate `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`.
- **Major reforms / regulatory landscape** (verify date-stamps in `shared/regulatory-watch.md`):
  - **Decree-Law 11614/1969** — base foreign-ownership regime; multiple subsequent amendments.
  - **Law 296/2001** — most consequential amendment: **3,000 m² national cap** per non-Arab foreign individual; cumulative 3% national + per-district caps; 5-year build/develop deadline; Cabinet-by-Decree authorization mandatory.
  - **Banking crisis 2019–2026** — BDL Circulars 151 / 158 / 161 + ongoing informal capital controls; "Lollar" haircuts; mortgage market essentially frozen; no formal IMF program agreed as of 2026-05.
  - **LBP two-step devaluation 2023–2024**: 1 Feb 2023 (1,507.5 → 15,000 LBP/USD), then early/Mar 2024 (15,000 → 89,500 LBP/USD); Sayrafa platform 2022–2023 transition (2026-05-27 verified, source BDL + BLOMINVEST BRITE).
  - **BDL Governor transition 2023–2025**: Riad Salameh's 30-yr tenure ended 31 Jul 2023 (in detention since 3 Sep 2024 on financial-misconduct charges); Wassim Mansouri served as Acting Governor; **Karim Souaid appointed Governor 27 Mar 2025** by Cabinet (17 of 24 ministerial votes), officially took office 4 Apr 2025 — first permanent Governor since Salameh, reform-mandated on AML + anti-terror finance + currency stabilization (2026-05-27 verified, source The National 27 Mar 2025 + UPI 4 Apr 2025 + Al Jazeera 30 Mar 2025).
  - **VAT 11%** since 2018 (raised from 10%).
  - **Beirut Port explosion Aug 2020** + reconstruction frameworks (verify at Council for Development and Reconstruction `https://www.cdr.gov.lb/`).
  - **2024 Israel–Hezbollah war (Sep–Nov 2024) + Nov 2024 ceasefire** — extensive damage S Lebanon + Bekaa + Beirut SE suburbs (Dahieh); reconstruction frameworks in flux 2025–2026.
  - **Joseph Aoun presidency (Jan 2025)** — ended ~2-year political vacuum; Nawaf Salam government (formed Feb 2025) negotiating reform agenda; verify currency at Presidency `https://www.presidency.gov.lb/` and PCM `https://www.pcm.gov.lb/`.
- **Foreign-buyer regime**: HEAVILY RESTRICTED — see `## Foreign buyer eligibility` below.

## 🇱🇧 LEAD SECTION: Foreign buyer eligibility

**The single most consequential section of this playbook for non-Lebanese buyers.** Misreading these limits voids title and may forfeit the deposit. Cabinet decisions evolve — verify against the General Directorate of Cadastre + Council of Ministers (PCM) in writing for your specific nationality before any contract.

### Decree-Law 11614/1969 + Law 296/2001 — core regime

Under **Decree-Law 11614/1969** as amended by **Law 296/2001**, real estate acquisition by **non-Arab foreigners** requires **prior Council of Ministers (Cabinet) authorization issued via Decree**. Without the Decree, the transaction is null, the title cannot be registered at the cadastre, and the deposit is at risk. Source: Council of Ministers (PCM) `https://www.pcm.gov.lb/`; Ministry of Finance cadastre directorate `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`.

| Limit | Threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Maximum aggregate area per non-Arab foreign individual nationally** | **3,000 m²** | Across all Lebanese territory; combined plot + building footprint. Family-aggregated in some readings — verify with cadastre directorate |
| **Cumulative national cap (all foreign owners aggregated)** | **3% of total Lebanese territory** | Once reached, further foreign acquisitions paused pending Cabinet review |
| **Per-district (caza/qadaa) cap** | **3% of district area** typical | Geographic dispersion constraint; some districts already near cap |
| **Build / develop deadline** | **5 years** from registration | Foreigner must build or develop the property within 5 yrs or face forced sale / confiscation procedures (verify currency of enforcement) |
| **Beirut Central District (Solidere zone)** | **More permissive separate regime** | Foreign ownership allowed more freely under Solidere's master-plan framework; verify with Solidere SAL `https://www.solidere.com/` and cadastre directorate |
| **Arab nationals (Arab League members)** | **Largely treated equivalent to Lebanese** | Subject to bilateral arrangements + reciprocity; some restrictions still apply, e.g., border zones |
| **Border zones + military / strategic zones** | **Forbidden** to non-Lebanese without special-case Cabinet approval | Title void if attempted |

(Source: PCM `https://www.pcm.gov.lb/`; Ministry of Finance `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`; verify current numerical thresholds and procedural details with the cadastre directorate before contracting — Cabinet decisions adjust these.)

### The Cabinet authorization process (non-Arab foreign buyer)

1. **Pre-contract due diligence**: title abstract from cadastre directorate; confirm property is outside forbidden zones; verify 3% per-district cap is not breached.
2. **Application to Cabinet**: filed via the cadastre directorate; supporting documents include passport copy, source-of-funds (AML), property identification triplet, intended use declaration, build-plan undertaking.
3. **Inter-ministerial review**: Ministry of Finance + Ministry of Interior + Ministry of Defense (security clearance) + cadastre directorate — typically **6–18 months** in practice (longer during political vacuums).
4. **Cabinet Decree issued**: published in the Official Gazette (Al-Jarida Al-Rasmiyya `https://www.pcm.gov.lb/Pages/RecentActivity.aspx`).
5. **Registration at regional cadastre**: Decree presented + transaction registered + 5-year build/develop clock starts.

**Practical timing**: factor **9–24 months** from offer to clean title for non-Arab foreign buyers; conditional sale contracts (with cancellation/refund clauses if Cabinet authorization is denied) are standard practice. Always retain an independent Lebanese advocate to draft the conditional clauses.

### Beirut Central District (Solidere) — separate regime

The **Beirut Central District (BCD)**, administered by **Solidere SAL** (Société Libanaise pour le Développement et la Reconstruction du Centre-Ville de Beyrouth), operates under a **separate, more permissive foreign-ownership regime** following the post-civil-war 1994 master-plan framework. Source: `https://www.solidere.com/`.

- **Foreign ownership freely allowed** within the Solidere master-plan area (subject to standard AML + transaction documentation).
- **Premium pricing**: BCD has historically traded at significant premium vs. comparable Beirut neighbourhoods.
- **Aug 2020 Port explosion impact**: BCD properties (esp. eastern edge near the port) sustained material damage in Aug 2020; reconstruction status varies per building.
- **Trap**: a Beirut listing tagged "Solidere area / freehold for foreigners" must be verified to lie WITHIN the Solidere zone master-plan boundaries — not merely "near" it. Confirm with Solidere SAL + cadastre directorate.

### Visa / residency via property

**Lebanon does NOT operate a golden-visa-by-real-estate program** comparable to Portugal, Greece, or UAE. Property-based residency pathways are limited and discretionary:

| Path | Threshold | Outcome | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Investor Residency Permit** | **≥ USD 50k** investment in a Lebanese business / commercial activity | 1-year renewable residency | Issued by General Security (Sûreté Générale `https://www.general-security.gov.lb/`); not automatic; AML + business plan required |
| **Property ownership-based residency** | **≥ USD 200k** property typical (informal threshold; verify with General Security) | Can support residency application | NOT automatic; discretionary; not a defined CBI/RBI track |
| **Lebanese descent (Mahjar diaspora)** | Proof of Lebanese ancestry (paternal line typically; some maternal recognition reforms) | Streamlined naturalization track for descendants of Lebanese emigrants | Processes through the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Emigrants** — the ministry publishes at `mfa.gov.lb`, which did not answer any route on 2026-09-18, so confirm the current procedure with a Lebanese consulate. Diaspora-vs-domestic population ratio: **data not publicly available from a primary source in this row** — for the domestic denominator use the Central Administration of Statistics [`https://www.cas.gov.lb/`](https://www.cas.gov.lb/) |
| **Naturalization (general)** | 5+ years continuous residence + Arabic + good conduct + waiver of original citizenship in some readings | Lebanese citizenship | Rarely granted to non-Arab non-diaspora applicants; **dual citizenship allowed since 2007** for Lebanese nationals |

**Headline check — Lebanese passport global mobility (as of 2026)**:
- Visa-free / visa-on-arrival access: ~**40 destinations** (verify current at Henley Passport Index `https://www.henleyglobal.com/passport-index`).
- Among the **weaker passports globally** in mobility terms — well below EU/EEA/Caribbean CBI passports.
- **Implication**: Lebanon does not operate as a global-mobility CBI play. The diaspora-naturalization track is the dominant inbound foreign-citizenship pathway and is driven by ancestry, not investment.

### Mortgage / financing — POST-2019 BANKING CRISIS REALITY

**The Lebanese mortgage market is essentially frozen.** Key context:

- **Pre-2019**: robust mortgage market via Bank Audi `https://www.bankaudigroup.com/group/audi-research`, BLOM Bank `https://www.blombank.com/`, Byblos Bank `https://www.byblosbank.com/`, BLC Bank, Bank of Beirut, Fransabank, BBAC, etc., LTV up to 70–80%, tenures 15–25 years, USD-denominated common.
- **Oct 2019 onward**: banking system effectively collapsed; depositor "Lollar" haircuts (pre-2019 USD deposits redeemable at heavily discounted exchange rates under BDL Circulars 151 / 158 / 161); informal capital controls; bank balance sheets impaired; new lending essentially halted.
- **2026-05 reality**: new mortgages from Lebanese banks remain extremely scarce; where available, limited to fresh-USD deposits + heavily collateralized borrowers + short tenures + high effective rates.
- **Foreign buyers** typically pay **CASH in fresh USD** (banknotes or international wire to a non-Lebanese escrow account) — Lebanese-bank financing is essentially unavailable to non-residents.
- **Practical workaround**: home-country mortgage against home-country collateral, then deploy fresh USD cash in Lebanon.

Source: Banque du Liban (BDL) `https://www.bdl.gov.lb/`; Association of Banks in Lebanon (ABL) `https://www.abl.org.lb/`; verify current circulars and any reform measures at the time of transaction.

### Buyer due diligence — minimum checklist

1. **Cadastre directorate title abstract (إخراج قيد عقاري + كشف عقاري)** — verify ownership chain, encumbrances (`الرهونات` mortgages, `الحجوزات` attachments, easements). Pull at the regional cadastre office. Source: `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`.
2. **Cabinet authorization Decree (non-Arab foreigners)** — copy of the published Decree (Official Gazette) authorizing the specific transaction. Without this, title cannot be registered.
3. **Plot identifier triplet match** — verify Property Number + Lot + Cadastral Zone against deed and survey map.
4. **Survey extract (مخطط مساحي)** — confirm boundaries; commission a licensed surveyor for plot/villa purchases.
5. **Building permit + occupancy certificate (رخصة بناء + شهادة إشغال)** — issued by the local municipality (Beirut Municipality `https://www.beirutmunicipality.gov.lb/` for Beirut; relevant baladiya elsewhere).
6. **War-damage assessment** — for properties in S Lebanon, Bekaa, Beirut Dahieh + immediate Beirut Port surroundings + any 2024-war-affected zone, commission an independent structural + remediation assessment. Verify any post-2024 reconstruction permits.
7. **Real estate tax clearance (مخالصة ضريبة الأملاك المبنية)** — no outstanding annual property tax. Issued by municipality + Ministry of Finance.
8. **Utility clearances** — Électricité du Liban (EDL `https://www.edl.gov.lb/` — chronic supply issues; private generators standard), water (regional water establishments), telecom.
9. **HOA / building committee balance** — for apartments under condominium regime (`الملكية المشتركة`).
10. **Source-of-funds documentation** — under AML; foreign buyer pre-prepares bank statements + source-of-wealth letter; **verify USD is "fresh" not "Lollar"** if any inflow touches the Lebanese banking system.
11. **Independent Lebanese advocate (محامي مستقل)** — never use the seller's or developer's lawyer; standard fee 1–3% of price; verify membership at Beirut Bar Association `https://www.bba.org.lb/` or Tripoli/North Bar.
12. **For Solidere zone — Solidere SAL master-plan compliance + reconstruction status post-Aug 2020** (especially eastern BCD edge near the port).

### Confidence

**HIGH** for the legal framework (Decree-Law 11614/1969, Law 296/2001, banking-crisis BDL circulars — all primary government sources cross-verified). **MEDIUM** for current procedural timelines + per-district 3% cap utilization (Cabinet decision rhythm shifts; some districts may be near or at cap and the public ledger is not always current in English). **MEDIUM** for post-2024-war reconstruction status per parcel — site visit + local engineer mandatory for any property in affected zones.

### Compute

1. Listing price/m² = listing USD ÷ built-up m² (`المساحة المبنية`). **Clarify built-up vs. net usable** — Lebanese listings often advertise built-up incl. shared walls; net usable can be 10–25% smaller.
2. **All quotes in USD**: post-2019 crisis, LBP quotes are date-sensitive — convert any LBP figure at the BDL official rate (`https://www.bdl.gov.lb/`) AND note the date stamp.
3. Compare against the asking-side band table above for the relevant market.
4. **Trap — fresh USD vs. Lollar**: a "USD 200,000" asking price may be intended for fresh-USD payment, but in resale contexts a seller may attempt to receive Lollars (locked deposits) which are heavily discounted vs. fresh — **always confirm currency-class in writing** before signing.
5. **Trap — 2024 war damage discount**: properties in S Lebanon, Bekaa, Beirut Dahieh may be listed at headline pre-war prices but require material war-damage remediation. Demand a post-2024 structural assessment.
6. **Trap — Aug 2020 Port explosion damage**: Beirut neighbourhoods within ~3 km of the port (Mar Mikhael, Gemmayzeh, Karantina, eastern Achrafieh) sustained varied damage; verify reconstruction status + permits post-Aug 2020.
7. **Trap — common-area allocation**: Lebanese condos allocate land share by built area; affects re-sale value if the building is later redeveloped. Verify in deed.

**MEDIUM** — asking-price listings are abundant on OLX + RealEstate.com.lb. **Transaction-verified price data is not publicly available in Lebanon**. ## Section: `--traffic`

### Primary sources

- **Ministry of Public Works and Transport (MoPWT) — Directorate General of Land and Maritime Transport** — [`http://www.transportation.gov.lb/ar/home`](http://www.transportation.gov.lb/ar/home) — land- and maritime-transport administration (licensing, seafarer certificates, administrative transactions). ⚠️ **Traffic-flow data: not publicly available** — this DG does not publish road-network or traffic-count data, and the Directorate General of Roads and Buildings has no reachable site (checked 2026-09-18); verify road data directly with MoPWT.
- **Beirut Municipality (بلدية بيروت)** — urban arterial counts: **data not publicly available — verify with the Municipality of Beirut via the Ministry of Interior and Municipalities** [`https://www.moim.gov.lb/`](https://www.moim.gov.lb/); the municipality's own domains (`beirutmunicipality.gov.lb`, `baladiyabeirut.gov.lb`) no longer resolve and `beirut.gov.lb` answered no route on 2026-09-18.
- **Internal Security Forces (ISF) Traffic Department** — `https://www.isf.gov.lb/` — accident statistics; not granular AADT.
- **Council for Development and Reconstruction (CDR)** — `https://www.cdr.gov.lb/` — historic transport masterplan studies; recent capacity limited.
- **OpenStreetMap (OSM)** — fallback for road class.

### Key term

**No standardized AADT-equivalent published per-segment** comparable to FR TMJA, DE DTV, UK AADT. Lebanese traffic data is mostly published as **annual aggregate flows on strategic corridors** plus **vehicle-registration density per mohafaza** (a poor proxy). Public statistics capacity is materially weaker post-2019 crisis.

### Coarse fallback

OSM `highway` class (Lebanon OSM coverage is good in Beirut + Mount Lebanon, decent on coastal highway, thinner in Bekaa + Akkar):
- `motorway` = strategic highway (e.g., parts of Beirut–Tripoli coastal highway, Beirut–Damascus highway)
- `trunk` = main inter-city
- `primary` = governorate main road
- `secondary` = governorate secondary
- `tertiary` = local arterial
- `residential` = neighbourhood (شارع داخلي)

### Verdict bands (estimated, calibrated to qualitative noise impact)

- 🟢 < 1,000 v/d (rural Akkar, Bekaa village road)
- 🟡 1,000–10,000 v/d (governorate secondary, urban side street)
- 🟠 10,000–50,000 v/d (urban arterial, Beirut neighbourhood approaches)
- 🔴 > 50,000 v/d (Beirut Ring Road / Charles Helou Avenue / Corniche al-Mazraa / Damascus Road approaches / coastal highway segments north-south of Beirut)

### Beirut-specific notes

- **Beirut has notoriously congested traffic** — narrow streets, hilly topography, generator noise (private diesel generators run 8–12 hours daily during rolling EDL outages — see `--mains`), motorbike density, weak traffic enforcement.
- **Coastal highway Beirut–Jounieh** + **Beirut–Damascus highway approaches** + **Charles Helou Avenue** + **Corniche al-Mazraa** + **Boulevard Sami Solh** are among the busiest arterials.
- **Generator noise is the under-acknowledged second source of "traffic-equivalent" noise** — almost every Beirut residential building runs a private diesel generator during EDL outages. Verify generator location + noise distance + scheduling at property visit.
- **Friday + Sunday** are quieter days (weekend pattern Sat–Sun in Lebanon, with Friday partly relaxed in Muslim-majority districts) — sample drives on these days under-state weekday peak noise.
- **Air quality**: Beirut PM2.5 elevated during traffic + generator-runtime peaks + winter heating; **WHO Global Air Quality Database** `https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/themes/air-pollution` annual mean often above WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³.

### Confidence

**LOW** — per-segment traffic counts comparable to EU/UK datasets are **not publicly available** for Lebanon. Use OSM road class + governorate vehicle density + on-visit verification (incl. generator-noise audit) as coarse proxy. **Sat–Sun visits will under-state.**

## Section: `--tax`

### Annual property tax — Built Property Tax (ضريبة الأملاك المبنية / Mahriyet al-Mil'k al-Mabani)

Administered jointly by **municipality + Ministry of Finance** under successive Built Property Tax laws (most recent significant reform **2018–2019** modified rates + bands). Source: Ministry of Finance `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`; Beirut Municipality `https://www.beirutmunicipality.gov.lb/` for Beirut; relevant baladiya elsewhere.

**Computed**: Tax assessed on **annual rental value** (`القيمة الإيجارية السنوية`) of the property — the committee-assessed rentable value, NOT actual rent received. Assessment incl. location, finish, age, services.

| Item | Value (2026 reference; verify per-municipality and current annual rate band) |
|---|---|
| **Rate** | **Sliding 4–14% of annual rental value** (post-2018–19 reform), depending on bracket |
| **Brackets** | Progressive — lower brackets ~4–6%, upper brackets ~10–14% (verify current at MoF) |
| **Owner-occupied primary residence relief** | Partial deduction available; verify currency at MoF |
| **Re-valuation cycle** | Periodic municipality + MoF joint committee; cadence variable |
| **Vocational License Fee (الرسم البلدي)** | Separate municipal fee for commercial use of premises |

### Example calculation (residential, Beirut Achrafieh apartment)

A 150 m² apartment in Achrafieh, committee-assessed annual rental value ~USD 12,000:
- At ~8% mid-bracket → **~USD 960/year**

For a 250 m² villa in Broummana (Mount Lebanon) at ~USD 18,000 assessed annual rental value:
- At ~10% bracket → **~USD 1,800/year**

**→ Annual property tax in Lebanon is moderate** — somewhat higher than Jordan/Egypt municipal rates but lower than EU residential property tax peaks. The bigger fiscal drag is at transaction (below).

### Transaction taxes (one-time at purchase)

| Item | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration Fee (رسم التسجيل العقاري)** | **5.8% of declared value** | Raised from 5% in 2003; paid by buyer at the cadastre directorate. Source: Ministry of Finance `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`. |
| **Notary fees** | ~**0.6–1%** typical | Notarization of the sale contract; sliding scale |
| **Stamp duty** | **0.4%** | Standard transaction stamp duty |
| **Lawyer fees (independent)** | **1–3%** of price typical | Foreign buyer should always retain independent counsel |
| **Brokerage** | **2–5%** typical (often split, sometimes seller-paid only) | Negotiable |
| **Cabinet authorization fees (non-Arab foreigners)** | Variable nominal + processing fees | Specific to the Decree-issuance process |
| **AML / KYC processing** | nominal flat fees | Bank-side and cadastre-side |

**Total foreign-buyer transaction cost**: est. **~7–10% of price** (or **~9–13%** if Cabinet authorization route + full lawyer involvement) — broadly comparable to Jordan and Egypt at the higher end.

### Capital gains tax (on sale)

- **CGT on real-estate sales by individuals**: **10% of net gain** under Income Tax Law (verify current at MoF). Primary-residence exemption variable — verify currency.
- **Corporate sellers**: subject to corporate income tax on RE gains (typically 17% for trading entities — verify at MoF).
- **Practical reality**: declared sale prices understated → reported "gain" often modest → effective CGT often well below headline 10%. But: enforcement could rise under any future IMF program.

### VAT / General Sales Tax

- **Standard VAT rate**: **11%** (raised from 10% in 2018). Source: Ministry of Finance `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`.
- **First-sale residential by developer**: **0% (out of scope for residential rental supply)** typical; verify at MoF.
- **Resale residential**: **0% VAT** (out of scope; Registration Fee applies instead).
- **Commercial property new development**: 11% VAT may apply.

### Income tax on rental (foreigner / non-resident)

- **Non-resident landlords** taxed under Lebanese Income Tax Law. Rental income from Lebanese real estate is Lebanon-sourced → taxable in Lebanon regardless of landlord residency.
- **Progressive PIT brackets (2026 reference)**: ~**4–21%** across bands (Lebanon's PIT was raised in 2017–2019 reforms). Source: Ministry of Finance `https://www.finance.gov.lb/`.
- **Withholding** on gross rental paid to non-resident landlords: verify current rate at MoF.
- **Maintenance/expense allowance**: deduction available; verify percentage.

### Future risk

- Any future IMF program (negotiations stalled as of 2026-05) may drive tax-base expansion + enforcement tightening.
- Built Property Tax committee re-valuations may catch up to post-2019 USD-denominated rental realities.
- VAT band adjustments possible.

### Confidence

**MEDIUM** for the legal framework (Built Property Tax, Registration Fee 5.8%, VAT 11%, CGT 10% — primary government sources). **MEDIUM** for current numerical thresholds — Lebanese tax rates have been adjusted multiple times in 2017–2019 reforms and the most current figure should be reconfirmed with MoF at transaction time, especially given fiscal-pressure context.

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term residential

Lebanese residential rental is dual-tier — a legacy from the civil-war-era frozen-rent regime + modern free-market leases:

| Tier | Regulation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Old Rent Law (الإيجار القديم) — pre-1992 contracts** | Frozen rents under historic rent-control regime | Notoriously low (some USD 50–500/year for Beirut apartments). **2014 Rent Law reform** initiated phased liberalization but implementation has been uneven; Constitutional Council interventions + amendments. **Avoid buying any property with old-rent tenants in place** — ejection is legally + politically fraught |
| **New Rent Law — post-1992 contracts** | Free-market under successive Code des Obligations + 2014 reform | Standard fixed-term lease; most foreign-buyer-relevant tier |

Source: Ministry of Justice `https://www.justice.gov.lb/`; Bar Association references.

### Short-term rentals (Airbnb / Booking)

**Status**: legal but undergoverned, especially post-2019 crisis (enforcement has weakened with state capacity).

- **Tourism licensing**: short-term rentals operated commercially require licensing under the **Ministry of Tourism** (`http://www.mot.gov.lb/`). In practice, most informal Airbnb operations in Beirut + Mount Lebanon + Byblos + Faraya operate **without** tourism license — regulatory grey zone with weak enforcement post-2019.
- **Beirut Municipality** + governorate municipalities have municipal-level rules — verify per address.
- **HOA / building bylaws** in modern compounds (Solidere residences, gated developments) often **ban or restrict** short-let — read the bylaws before underwriting any STR yield.
- **Dollarization advantage**: STR receipts overwhelmingly in fresh USD (foreign tourists + diaspora) — **the cleanest USD income stream available in Lebanon**, given banking-system risk.

### Strategic notes

- **Yields**: Beirut rental yields gross ~5–10% on USD-denominated leases (foreign embassies, INGOs, multinationals, diaspora returnees) — relatively high but reflect macro risk. Mount Lebanon premium ski-towns (Faraya, Faqra) + Byblos + Jounieh have seasonal dynamics.
- **Diaspora demand**: ~13M global Lebanese diaspora drives sustained Beirut + Mount Lebanon demand; summer season (Jun–Sep) sees diaspora returnees inflate STR + furnished-rental demand materially.
- **USD-denominated leases**: large diplomatic + INGO + multinational tenant pool pays in fresh USD — the only structurally FX-stable income for foreign owners.
- **2024 war demand pattern**: south Lebanon + Bekaa + Dahieh saw mass internal displacement Sep–Nov 2024; demand re-allocation to Mount Lebanon + Beirut central + diaspora-second-home areas. Post-Nov 2024 ceasefire return migration is partial; verify per district.
- **University rental (American University of Beirut, Lebanese American University, Université Saint-Joseph, Notre Dame, Beirut Arab University)**: student rental in Hamra, Achrafieh, Sin el-Fil — relatively stable demand in USD.

### Confidence

**MEDIUM** — legal framework is mature (post-2014 reform); STR regulation is documented but enforcement very weak post-2019; per-parcel STR feasibility depends on HOA bylaws + municipal rules. **USD-denominated lease pool is the structural advantage for foreign buyers.**

### Geopolitical / security risk — CRITICAL CONTEXT

This is the **dominant risk dimension** for Lebanese real estate in 2026.

- **2024 Israel–Hezbollah war (Sep–Nov 2024)**: extensive damage in **South Lebanon** (Tyre, Bint Jbeil, Marjayoun caza), **Bekaa** (Baalbek, Hermel), and **Beirut SE suburbs (Dahieh — Ghobeiry, Haret Hreik, Bourj el-Brajneh)**. Nov 2024 ceasefire has held (verify currency at the time of transaction); reconstruction frameworks in flux 2025–2026. **Avoid any property in war-affected zones without an independent post-2024 structural + remediation assessment.**
- **Aug 2020 Beirut Port explosion**: ~218 deaths, ~7,000 injured, ~300,000 displaced; ammonium nitrate detonation; damage radius ~3+ km (Mar Mikhael, Gemmayzeh, Karantina, eastern Achrafieh, parts of BCD/Solidere). Reconstruction ongoing 2025–2026; verify per parcel.
- **2006 Israel–Hezbollah war**: prior major conflict; reconstruction context still relevant for older damage patterns.
- **Civil war (1975–1990)**: structural damage legacy in older buildings; bullet damage + structural compromise in some pre-1990 stock.
- **Political stability**: **Joseph Aoun presidency (Jan 2025)** ended 2-year political vacuum; Nawaf Salam government (formed Feb 2025) is negotiating reform agenda; verify currency at Presidency `https://www.presidency.gov.lb/`. Parliamentary deadlock + sectarian power-sharing (Taif Accord 1989) create chronic governance risk.
- **Crime in Lebanon**: low-to-moderate; petty theft + armed robbery + vehicle crime moderate; security forces stretched post-2019 crisis; **private security + door-staff arrangements common** in upscale Beirut + Mount Lebanon residences. Source: ISF `https://www.isf.gov.lb/`.

### Economic crisis — 2019–2026 ongoing

- **World Bank framing**: Lebanon's 2019–onward economic crisis has been ranked among the **three worst economic crises globally since the mid-19th century** (Spring 2021 Lebanon Economic Monitor). Ongoing through 2026.
- **Banking crisis**: Oct 2019 → Lollar haircuts → BDL Circulars 158/161 + ongoing informal capital controls.
- **Hyperinflation**: ~95–98% LBP devaluation 2019–2023; CPI peaked ~200%/year.
- **State capacity erosion**: public services (electricity — see `--mains`, water, healthcare, education) materially weakened; private substitutes (generators, water tankers, private hospitals + schools) have absorbed significant household budget.

### Flood risk

- **Litani River + Awali River basins**: periodic flooding during winter storm events (Dec–Mar); Litani River Authority `https://www.litani.gov.lb/` monitors. Bekaa Valley flat-land developments + south Lebanon coastal alluvial zones most exposed.
- **Beirut urban drainage**: hilly topography channels heavy rainfall; older drainage infra strained in major winter storms; periodic urban flooding in low-lying districts (Bourj Hammoud, parts of southern Beirut).
- **Coastal storm surge**: Mediterranean winter storms occasionally affect coastal road + low-lying coastal properties; modest exposure overall.

### Seismicity

- **Yammouneh Fault (eastern Lebanon, splay of Dead Sea Transform)** + offshore Mt. Lebanon thrust faults — Lebanon sits in a moderate-seismic zone within the broader Dead Sea Transform fault system. Source: CNRS Lebanon `https://www.cnrs.edu.lb/` + regional seismic monitoring networks.
- **Historical events**:
  - **551 AD Beirut earthquake + tsunami** — historic catastrophic event; baseline reference.
  - **1956 Chim earthquake** (M 5.5) — Mount Lebanon damage; 20th-century reference.
  - **2023 Türkiye–Syria earthquake (Feb 2023, M 7.8)** — felt strongly in Lebanon; minor damage reported; underscored regional fault-system connectivity.
  - **Smaller M 4–5 events** periodic.
- **Building codes**: Lebanese seismic code provisions exist but enforcement uneven historically. Modern Beirut + Mount Lebanon construction (post-2000) generally better-engineered. **Pre-1975 stock + civil-war-damaged stock** warrants structural review by a licensed engineer.

### Climate risks (rising)

- **Drought + water stress**: Lebanon is increasingly water-stressed; Litani basin overexploitation; aridification of Bekaa agricultural zones (LARI studies). NDC commitments under Paris Agreement (verify current at Ministry of Environment `https://www.moe.gov.lb/`).
- **Heat extremes**: Beirut + Bekaa summer >35°C events increasing; mountain resorts (Faraya, Faqra) provide vertical thermal escape.
- **Sea-level rise + coastal erosion**: Mediterranean coast modest SLR exposure (~30–50 cm by 2050 RCP4.5); sand-mining + coastal-development impacts compound erosion in some zones.
- **Air pollution**: Beirut PM2.5 elevated due to traffic + private generator runtime + winter heating + occasional waste-burning; WHO Global Air Quality Database benchmarks.
- **Snow + winter storms (mountain resorts)**: Faraya/Mzaar/Faqra ski-zones have winter storm exposure; seasonal access risk.

## Caveats unique to LB (HEAVY — read all)

- **Decree-Law 11614/1969 + Law 296/2001 cap structure**: 3,000 m² national per non-Arab foreigner, cumulative national 3% cap, per-district 3% cap, mandatory Cabinet Decree, 5-year build/develop deadline. These caps are not negotiable and the deposit is at risk if a transaction proceeds without authorization.
- **Cabinet authorization timelines 6–18 months** typical (longer during political vacuums) — build conditional clauses into the contract.
- **Banking crisis 2019–2026**: pre-2019 USD deposits ("Lollars") are NOT equivalent to fresh USD; haircuts apply; verify currency-class of any USD shown by counterparty in writing.
- **Mortgage market essentially frozen** — foreign buyers transact in fresh USD cash; arrange home-country financing if needed.
- **De-facto USD dollarization** — ~80%+ of transactions in fresh USD; LBP for small ancillary only; LBP figures require date stamps given hyperinflation history.
- **2024 Israel–Hezbollah war damage**: S Lebanon, Bekaa, Beirut Dahieh — independent post-2024 structural assessment mandatory before any purchase in affected zones.
- **Aug 2020 Beirut Port explosion**: ~3 km radius (Mar Mikhael, Gemmayzeh, Karantina, eastern Achrafieh, parts of BCD) — verify reconstruction status + permits per parcel.
- **5-year build/develop clause** for foreign buyers: forfeiture risk if undeveloped.
- **5.8% Registration Fee + 0.4% stamp + ~1% notary + 1–3% lawyer + 2–5% brokerage** = ~7–13% transaction friction.
- **10% CGT on net gain** at exit — moderate; primary-residence relief variable.
- **Solidere BCD separate regime** — more permissive but verify per-parcel master-plan compliance.
- **Confessional inheritance regime**: 18 personal-status courts; foreign buyers may opt for home-country law via prior declaration — verify with estates lawyer.
- **Inheritance + civil-war + sectarian dynamics**: chain-of-title can be fragmented across heirs scattered globally; 13M-strong diaspora makes consents complex; expect long lead times on heir-traced sales.
- **Diaspora-driven demand (~13M global)**: structurally supports Beirut + Mount Lebanon + Byblos + diaspora-second-home districts; offsets domestic crisis pressure but does not eliminate macro risk.
- **EDL grid only 3–12 hours/day** typical — private generator subscription is a mandatory recurring cost (USD 80–250+/month Beirut).
- **Water rationing** scheduled supply 1–4 days/week typical — rooftop tank capacity + pump quality matter.
- **Generator noise + air quality**: chronic Beirut ambient noise + diesel-particulate exposure during outages — consider this in any "quiet street" assessment.
- **Pre-1975 + civil-war-damaged building stock**: structural review by OEA-licensed engineer for any older property.
- **Permit irregularities**: a meaningful share of older stock has informal/legalization issues — insist on building permit + occupancy certificate.
- **AML / source-of-funds**: Lebanon is FATF-assessed; foreign buyers pre-prepare bank statements + source-of-wealth letter; cash transactions above defined thresholds reportable.
- **Political vacuum risk**: chronic governance dysfunction can pause Cabinet decisions (incl. foreign-buyer Decrees) for months — Joseph Aoun presidency Jan 2025 closed the most recent 2-year vacuum but the structural risk remains.
- **No IMF program (as of 2026-05)** — fiscal stabilization path uncertain; future tax + enforcement tightening possible.
- **Old Rent Law tenants**: avoid buying property with pre-1992 frozen-rent tenants in place — eviction is legally + politically fraught.
- **STR regulation grey zone**: Ministry of Tourism licensing required for commercial STR; many informal Airbnbs unlicensed; HOA bylaws often dispositive; enforcement very weak post-2019.
