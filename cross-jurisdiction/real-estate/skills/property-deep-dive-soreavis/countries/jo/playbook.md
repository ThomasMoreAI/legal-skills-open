# Jordan 🇯🇴 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `jo`. Status: ✅ Fully populated (researched 2026-04 / 2026-05). Foreign-buyer regime: **RECIPROCITY-BASED** — non-Jordanians can own only if their home country grants reciprocal rights to Jordanians; geographic + size restrictions apply; Cabinet approval required above threshold areas. Among MENA's higher transaction-tax regimes (~10–12% all-in) but **0% capital gains tax on individual real-estate sales** (uniquely favourable on exit). CBI Jordan exists from 2018 but the Jordanian passport's mobility profile (~50 visa-free) limits CBI demand vs. EU/Caribbean alternatives. JOD pegged to USD since 1995 → no FX risk for USD-denominated buyers.

> **🇯🇴 LEAD WARNING — read before any other section.** The reciprocity list maintained by the **Department of Lands and Survey (DLS / دائرة الأراضي والمساحة)** under the Council of Ministers is **dynamic and not always published in English**. Confirm reciprocity for your nationality with DLS in writing before any deposit (`https://www.dls.gov.jo/`). Foreigners are **categorically barred** from owning land in border zones (5–15 km belts depending on category) and military zones; **Aqaba Special Economic Zone (ASEZA)** sits under a separate framework requiring its own approval. Property between **5 and 30 dunums (5,000–30,000 m²)** requires **Minister of Finance approval** on DLS Director's recommendation; **above 30 dunums (30,000 m²)** requires explicit **Council of Ministers approval** with timelines that can extend to 6–12 months (2026-05-27 verified, source Al Tamimi & Co + US State Department 2024 Investment Climate Statement). Always retain an independent Jordanian advocate (`محامي`) — never use the developer's lawyer. **2 July 2025 CBI overhaul** abolished all passive paths (incl. JOD 750k bond + JOD 1M deposit) and replaced them with 8 active-business pathways — see `## CBI Jordan (post-2-Jul-2025 reform)` below.

## Country profile

- **Postcode**: 5-digit numeric (NNNNN). Per-city ranges carried in the corpus and **now unsourced** (`est.` — no reachable source states them as at 2026-09-18): Amman 11118–11953, Zarqa 13110–13219, Irbid 21110–21199, Aqaba 77110 — verify at a Jordan Post counter or against the UPU Postal Addressing Systems sheet for Jordan (https://www.address.post/PAS). ⚠️ Jordan Post's own site `https://www.jordanpost.com.jo/` is unusable as a citation: TLS certificate expired 2026-04-22 and the host serves a reCAPTCHA bot wall (verified 2026-09-18) — a certificate renewal would restore it with no URL change, so re-check in ~90 days.
- **Admin levels**: 12 governorates (محافظات / muhafazat) → 52 districts (ألوية / liwa') → 89 sub-districts (أقضية / aqdiya) → ~100+ municipalities (بلديات / baladiyat). Greater Amman Municipality (GAM / أمانة عمان الكبرى) is a special municipality covering ~1,680 km². Source: Ministry of Local Administration `https://www.mola.gov.jo/` and DOS Department of Statistics `https://dosweb.dos.gov.jo/`.
- **Population**: ~11.4 million (2024 DOS estimate, source `https://dosweb.dos.gov.jo/`). Urban-concentrated: Amman Metro ~4.5M, Zarqa Governorate ~1.5M, Irbid Governorate ~1.9M, Balqa ~0.55M, Aqaba ~0.20M. Significant non-Jordanian population (Syrian + Iraqi + Palestinian + Egyptian + GCC residents) — DOS counts ~30%+ of total population as non-citizens (mixed methodology; verify at `https://dosweb.dos.gov.jo/population/population-2/`).
- **Currency**: **JOD (Jordanian dinar, د.أ.)** — pegged to USD at **1 JOD = 1.41044 USD (i.e., 1 USD ≈ 0.7090 JOD)** since October 1995, anchored by the Central Bank of Jordan (CBJ). The peg has held continuously through 30+ years and multiple regional shocks. Source: CBJ `https://www.cbj.gov.jo/Pages/viewpage.aspx?pageID=87` (exchange rates page; verify current rate). **No FX risk** for USD-denominated transactions; minimal capital controls; CBJ requires resident-account documentation but JOD ↔ USD conversion is unrestricted at banks.
- **Languages**: Arabic (official, MSA + Levantine Jordanian dialect). English **widely used in business, tourism, medical, university, government secondary correspondence** — title documents are nonetheless filed in Arabic at DLS and require sworn translation for non-Arabic-reading buyers. French + German are minority languages in educated diaspora.
- **Legal system**: Hybrid — civil law (Ottoman Mejelle origins → French-Egyptian-Jordanian Civil Code 1976) for contract/property + Sharia courts for personal status (family, inheritance for Muslims) + Tribal customary law in some rural Bedouin contexts. **Inheritance for Muslims follows Sharia mandatory shares** at Jordanian-titled real estate; non-Muslim foreigners may opt for home-country law via prior declaration but should verify with an estates lawyer at purchase.
- **Cadastre / land registry**: **Department of Lands and Survey (DLS / دائرة الأراضي والمساحة)** under Ministry of Finance — `https://www.dls.gov.jo/`. The Jordanian cadastre is a **Torrens-style register (Tabu / السند الطابو)** descended from late-Ottoman Tabu reform (1858) → British Mandate refinements → 1953 Land Settlement Law and successors. The **title deed (سند تسجيل / sanad tasjil)** is state-guaranteed; coverage is **near-complete in urban areas** and high but uneven in rural/Bedia.
  - **Identifier triplet**: Plot Number (`رقم القطعة`) + Basin Number (`رقم الحوض`) + Sub-Basin / Quarter (`الحي` or sometimes `محلة`). Always verify all three against the deed.
  - **Online services**: DLS portal offers partial digitization (deed search, registration appointment booking); full title abstracts still typically pulled at the regional Tabu office. Source: `https://www.dls.gov.jo/AR/Pages/eservices.aspx`.
- **Major reforms / regulatory landscape** (verify date-stamps in `shared/regulatory-watch.md`):
  - **Ownership of Real Estate Law No. 13 of 2019** (قانون ملكية العقار رقم 13 لسنة 2019) — unifying statute that superseded 14 prior laws + 19 bylaws (incl. residual Ottoman Land Code 1858, Lands and Water Settlement Law 40/1952) into one code governing all real-estate ownership + transactions; foreign-ownership + reciprocity provisions regulated under this law plus Cabinet decisions issued thereunder; reciprocity-based with size + zone restrictions in dunums (1 dunum = 1,000 m²). Source: DLS `https://www.dls.gov.jo/` + Al Tamimi law update (2026-05-27 verified).
  - **Property Sales Tax (post-2019 reform)** — **9% total = 5% registration fee (رسوم تسجيل) + 4% sale tax (ضريبة بيع العقار)**; historically split between buyer and seller in varying ratios pre-2019. Verify current at Income & Sales Tax Department (ISTD) `https://www.istd.gov.jo/` (2026-05-27 verified, source Lloyds Bank Trade + Homes Jordan).
  - **CBI Jordan — Cabinet overhaul 2 July 2025** — abolished all passive investment paths (JOD 750k zero-coupon bond, JOD 1M bank deposit, passive real-estate CBI); replaced with **8 active-business pathways** from JOD 350k (existing project outside Amman) to JOD 3M (specialised sectors); annual cap **500 approvals**; **real-estate residency permits (JOD 200k / 100k) separated from citizenship** (no longer lead to citizenship under any timeline). Source: Ministry of Investment `https://www.moin.gov.jo/` + IMI Daily (2026-05-27 verified) — see `## CBI Jordan (post-2-Jul-2025 reform)` below for full table.
  - **Apartment first-150-m² exemption** (periodically renewed Cabinet ordinance) — first 150 m² from a housing-company developer = 0% registration fee + 0% sale tax; above 150 m² = 3% (1.5% for first-time buyers); resale apartments = 3% up to 150 m² + 6% above (4.5% first-time); Musaqafat 50% discount for 3 yrs for first-time buyers. Verify currency at ISTD + DLS (2026-05-27 verified, source Amaken Jordan + Homes Jordan + Lexis Middle East).
  - **Real Estate Brokers Law 2024** — licensing + transparency requirements for brokers. Source: Ministry of Public Works and Housing `https://www.mpwh.gov.jo/` and ISTD secondary.
  - **Vision Jordan 2025 / Economic Modernisation Vision 2033** — economic plan; real-estate ancillary effects. Source: Prime Ministry `https://www.pm.gov.jo/`.
  - **Crown Prince Real Estate Initiative** — subsidised housing for young Jordanian families (Jordanian-only; not foreign-buyer relevant directly but affects supply pipeline). Source: Royal Hashemite Court `https://rhc.jo/` and JLGC `https://www.jlgc.com/`.
  - **East Amman Development Plan + Greater Amman Master Plan 2025** — zoning + density framework. Source: GAM `https://www.amman.jo/`.
  - **Aqaba Special Economic Zone Law No. 32 of 2000 (as amended)** (قانون منطقة العقبة الاقتصادية الخاصة رقم 32 لسنة 2000) — 47 articles establishing ASEZA (`https://www.aseza.jo/`), 5% flat corporate income tax (vs. mainland 20%), customs exemptions, and separate land-ownership approval channel for foreign buyers in ASEZ (2026-05-27 verified, source InforMEA legislation record + ASEZ portal).
- **Foreign-buyer regime**: PARTIALLY RESTRICTED — see `## Foreign buyer eligibility` below.

## 🇯🇴 LEAD SECTION: Foreign buyer eligibility

**The single most consequential section of this playbook for non-Jordanian buyers.** Misreading these limits voids title and the deposit may not be recoverable. Cabinet decisions evolve — verify against DLS in writing for your specific nationality before any contract.

### Reciprocity-based ownership rule

Under the **Ownership of Real Estate Law No. 13 of 2019** (قانون ملكية العقار رقم 13 لسنة 2019) and Cabinet decisions issued thereunder regarding foreign ownership, a non-Jordanian individual or entity may acquire real estate in Jordan **only if their home country grants reciprocal acquisition rights to Jordanian nationals**. The reciprocity list is maintained jointly by DLS + Council of Ministers and is **dynamic — countries are added or removed by Cabinet decision without broad public notification** (2026-05-27 verified, source Al Tamimi & Co + DLS).

**Categories typically observed** (verify currency for your nationality with DLS in writing before contracting; status as of 2026-04 research window):

| Category | Treatment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **GCC nationals** (Saudi, UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman) | ≈ Jordanian treatment under GCC-Jordan agreement | Most permissive class; verify per emirate-specific edge cases |
| **Iraqi nationals** | Special bilateral arrangements | Large Iraqi diaspora; treatment evolves with bilateral agreements |
| **Palestinian-Jordanian dual nationals** | Jordanian (most have full citizenship) | Legacy of 1948/1967 — most West Bank-origin Palestinians hold Jordanian passports |
| **Syrian nationals** | Special arrangements + restrictions | Refugee context affects practical access |
| **Reciprocating Western states** (US, UK, most EU, Canada, Australia, NZ, Japan, etc.) | ✅ Allowed subject to size + zone limits | The dominant Western foreign-buyer pathway |
| **Other** | Case-by-case Cabinet approval | Always verify |

(Source: DLS `https://www.dls.gov.jo/`; Ministry of Foreign Affairs `https://www.mfa.gov.jo/`; verify per-nationality at the time of transaction — **the list changes by Cabinet decision**.)

### Size + zone limits for non-Jordanian buyers

Under the same Cabinet framework (Decisions 2019 onward; verify current):

| Category | Threshold (statutory unit = dunum; 1 dunum = 1,000 m²) | Approval needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Single dwelling unit (apartment/villa) for personal use** | typically **1 unit, ≤5 dunums (5,000 m²) combined plot** | Streamlined: DLS regional director |
| **5–30 dunums (5,000–30,000 m²)** | Typical large-villa / multi-unit threshold | **Minister of Finance approval** (on DLS Director's recommendation) — intermediate tier (2026-05-27 verified, source Al Tamimi & US State Dept 2024) |
| **Above 30 dunums (30,000 m²)** | Estate / development scale tier | **Council of Ministers approval** (timelines 6–12 months, sometimes longer) |
| **Lease over 10 dunums (10,000 m²) for term >3 years** | Long-term lease above small-plot threshold | **Minister of Interior permit** required (Real Estate Ownership Law 13/2019) |
| **Commercial / income-producing property** | Subject to separate quotas + approvals | Often through Ministry of Investment licence channels |
| **Agricultural land** | Generally **prohibited** to non-Jordanians except via Cabinet special-case approval | Prefer not to attempt |
| **Border zones (5–15 km belts depending on category)** | **Forbidden** to non-Jordanians without Cabinet special-case approval | Effectively unavailable for residential foreign buyer |
| **Military / strategic zones** | **Forbidden** | Title void if attempted |
| **Aqaba Special Economic Zone (ASEZ)** | Separate ASEZA approval framework | See ASEZA section below |

(Source: DLS Cabinet decision summaries `https://www.dls.gov.jo/AR/Pages/Default.aspx`; verify current numerical thresholds before contracting — they have been adjusted in past Cabinet decisions.)

### Key procedural quirks

1. **Reciprocity certificate**: DLS will, on application, issue a written confirmation of reciprocity for a specific nationality. **Always insist on this in writing** before a deposit moves — verbal assurances from a broker or developer are not protective.
2. **Holding period**: a foreign buyer may face a minimum holding period before resale (historically referenced; verify current requirement at DLS — has been adjusted by Cabinet decision).
3. **Beneficial-ownership scrutiny**: corporate ownership structures (LLC, offshore SPV) are scrutinised; Jordan is FATF-compliant and applies AML to real-estate transactions above defined thresholds — source-of-funds documentation required for non-residents.
4. **Approvals processing**: even within-quota transactions take 4–12 weeks at DLS for foreign buyers; Cabinet-approval cases far longer. Build this into the contract's longstop date.

### Aqaba Special Economic Zone (ASEZ) — separate framework

- **ASEZA** governs the Aqaba Special Economic Zone (port + Red Sea coast). Source: `https://www.aseza.jo/`.
- **Tax incentives**: 5% corporate income tax (vs. mainland 20% for most companies), customs exemptions, no GST on certain goods.
- **Land ownership for foreigners**: separate ASEZA approval channel; freehold and long-leasehold both available subject to project-fit + sectoral priority. Foreign developers active in Aqaba (Saraya Aqaba, Ayla Oasis, Marsa Zayed plans) sell to foreign buyers but **always verify ASEZA-issued title approval, not a generic DLS extract alone**.
- **Trap**: an Aqaba listing tagged "freehold for foreigners" may be reliant on a developer's master-licence — confirm individual deed registration at ASEZA + DLS.

### CBI Jordan (post-2-Jul-2025 reform)

Cabinet decision-based pathway. Source: Ministry of Investment `https://www.moin.gov.jo/` (formerly Jordan Investment Commission); IMI Daily + Liberty Mundo + CitizenX + Roya News + UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor measure 5144 (2026-05-27 verified). Verify current thresholds with Ministry of Investment + Ministry of Interior.

**On 2 July 2025 the Cabinet completely overhauled the CBI programme** — the most significant change since the 2018 launch. **ALL passive paths were abolished** (bank deposits, government zero-coupon bonds incl. the JOD 750k bond, passive real-estate CBI). They were replaced by **8 active-business pathways** all requiring active economic participation. Annual cap: **500 approvals/year** (vs. historical <100/year since 2018).

| Path (post-2-Jul-2025) | Threshold (JOD) | Outcome | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Existing project outside Amman** | **350,000** (~USD 493k) | Jordanian citizenship | Active business required |
| **Specialised sectors** (pharmaceuticals, logistics, etc.) | up to **3,000,000** | Citizenship | Sector-specific qualifying activity |
| **Stock market investment** | **1,000,000** with 3-year profit/withdrawal lockup | Citizenship | Locked equity exposure |
| **Employment-based** (novel pathway) | No specific investment amount — **150 Jordanians in Amman OR 100 outside Amman** must be hired | Citizenship | Employment-creation track |
| **Other paths** (4 remaining tiers in the 8-tier framework) | Verify per-pathway thresholds | Citizenship | Confirm at Ministry of Investment |
| **Real-estate residency permits (separate from citizenship)** | JOD 200k / JOD 100k tiers survive | **Residency only** (5-year renewable) — **no longer leads to citizenship under any timeline** | Property must be purchased from a developer, not from an existing owner |

**🔴 The pre-2-Jul-2025 thresholds (JOD 750k zero-coupon bond, JOD 1M bank deposit, JOD 1M private investment, JOD 250k SME) are no longer available.** Any guidance citing those numbers as currently active is out of date.

**Headline check — Jordanian passport global mobility (as of 2026)**:

- Visa-free / visa-on-arrival access: ~**50 destinations** (verify current at Henley Passport Index `https://www.henleyglobal.com/passport-index` and ArtonCapital — figures shift annually).
- Among the **weaker passports globally** (well below EU / EEA / Caribbean CBI passports). For perspective: Maltese citizenship offers ~180+ visa-free; Caribbean CBIs ~140–150; Jordanian ~50.
- **Implication**: Jordan CBI is rarely an attractive **global mobility** play. It is more frequently chosen by:
  - **Diaspora reconnection** — second/third-generation Jordanian-origin individuals
  - **Regional business** — entrepreneurs operating in Iraq, Palestinian Territories, GCC where a Jordanian passport carries practical value
  - **Family reunification** — spouses / children of Jordanian citizens
- For pure visa-free access to the West, EU CBI/RBI pathways or Caribbean CBI deliver materially better mobility per dollar.

**Verification path before relying on CBI thresholds**:
1. Ministry of Investment `https://www.moin.gov.jo/` for current Cabinet decisions
2. Ministry of Interior `https://www.moi.gov.jo/` for naturalisation file processing
3. Use a Jordanian advocate licensed with the Jordanian Bar Association `https://www.jba.org.jo/` — not a developer's in-house lawyer

### Investor residence permits (no citizenship)

If full citizenship is not the goal, Jordan offers investor residence permits with materially lower thresholds (verify with Ministry of Interior):

| Path | Threshold | Outcome | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real estate in Greater Amman** | **≥ JOD 200,000** (~USD 282k) | 5-year renewable residence | Plus CV + bank statements + clean record |
| **Real estate outside Greater Amman** | **≥ JOD 100,000** (~USD 141k) | 5-year renewable residence | Lower threshold to encourage decentralisation |
| **Bank deposit (Bona Fide Investor)** | **≥ JOD 50,000** in Jordanian bank, ≥1 year | Renewable residence | Most flexible; lowest threshold |
| **Special Investor (Crown) Permit** | Case-by-case high-net-worth | Bespoke | Discretionary |

Source: Ministry of Interior `https://www.moi.gov.jo/`; verify current at Investment Commission portal.

### Mortgage / financing for non-residents

- **Bank of Jordan limits** (CBJ regulated; verify per bank):
  - **Resident Jordanians**: typically **75–80% LTV**, tenures up to **25–30 years**, JOD or USD denominated
  - **Non-resident foreign buyers**: typically **50–65% LTV**, tenures **15–20 years** maximum, often JOD-denominated only
  - **Rates (2026 reference window)**: ~**6–10% JOD** floating; ~**5–8% USD** where offered (limited availability for non-residents). Verify with the lender. Source: CBJ supervisory data `https://www.cbj.gov.jo/`.
- **Major lenders**: Housing Bank for Trade and Finance (HBTF — `https://www.hbtf.com/`) is the largest mortgage lender; Arab Bank `https://www.arabbank.com.jo/`; Jordan Kuwait Bank `https://www.jkb.com/`; Cairo Amman Bank `https://www.cab.jo/`; Jordan Ahli Bank `https://www.ahli.com/`.
- **Documentation**: source-of-funds (AML), home-country credit history (where available), proof of income, residence-permit copy if held.
- **JLGC Jordan Loan Guarantee Corporation** — `https://www.jlgc.com/` — Housing Loan Guarantee Programme for low-income Jordanians; **NOT available to non-residents**.

### Buyer due diligence — minimum checklist

1. **DLS title abstract (سند تسجيل + كشف)** — verify ownership, encumbrances (`الرهونات` / mortgages, `الحجوزات` / attachments, easements). Pull in person at the regional Tabu office or via authorised digital request. Source: `https://www.dls.gov.jo/`.
2. **Reciprocity confirmation** — written DLS confirmation for the buyer's nationality.
3. **Plot identifier triplet match** — verify Plot Number + Basin Number + Sub-Basin against deed and against the survey map.
4. **Survey extract (مخطط مساحي)** — confirm boundaries; commission a licensed surveyor (`مساح مرخص`) for plot/villa purchases or any boundary doubt.
5. **Building permit + occupancy certificate (رخصة بناء + شهادة إشغال)** — issued by the local municipality (GAM in Amman; relevant baladiya elsewhere). Confirm structure was legally built and inhabited.
6. **Real estate tax clearance (مخالصة ضريبة الأبنية والأراضي)** — no outstanding annual property tax. Issued by GAM/baladiya.
7. **Utility clearances** — electricity (NEPCO/JEPCO/IDECO/EDCO, `https://www.nepco.com.jo/`), water (WAJ/Miyahuna, `https://www.miyahuna.com.jo/`), gas where mains LPG distribution exists, telecom.
8. **HOA / building committee balance** — for apartments under condominium regime, request statement of arrears + reserve fund.
9. **Source-of-funds documentation** — under AML; foreign buyer pre-prepares bank statements + source-of-wealth letter.
10. **Independent Jordanian advocate (محامي مستقل)** — never use the seller's or developer's lawyer; standard fee 1–3% of price.
11. **For ASEZ purchases — ASEZA-issued individual approval letter**, not a generic DLS extract.
12. **For pre-2010 Amman buildings — seismic + structural review** by a licensed engineer; building stock dates to 1927+ Jericho earthquake era pre-code.

### Confidence

**HIGH** for legal framework (Cabinet decision basis, DLS structure, mortgage LTV limits — published in Reshmi al-Mamlaka and on `https://www.pm.gov.jo/`). **MEDIUM** for current numerical thresholds (Cabinet decisions adjust these without consistent English publication). **MEDIUM** for CBI economics — the headline JOD figures are stable, but Jordanian passport mobility shifts with bilateral agreements.

### Compute

1. Listing price/m² = listing JOD ÷ built-up m² (`المساحة المبنية`). **Clarify built-up vs. net usable** — Jordanian listings often advertise built-up incl. shared walls; net usable can be 10–20% smaller.
2. Convert to USD at the **CBJ peg rate** (`https://www.cbj.gov.jo/`) — pegged at 1 JOD = 1.41 USD; the rate has been stable since 1995 and has no parallel-market premium.
3. Compare against the asking-side band table above for the relevant market.
4. **Trap — gross floor area inflation**: developers may quote "shaqqa shamil" (apartment incl. shared services share) instead of net useable. Always ask for the deed-registered area (`المساحة في السند`).
5. **Trap — common-area allocation**: Jordanian condos allocate land share by built area; the allocated land share affects re-sale value if the building is later redeveloped. Verify in the deed.
6. **Trap — ASEZ pricing premium**: Aqaba branded developments command 50–100% premiums over comparable mainland — not all justified by infrastructure; resale liquidity weaker than Amman.

### Coarse fallback

OSM `highway` class (Jordan OSM coverage is good in Amman + Aqaba + main inter-city, thinner in Bedia + south):
- `motorway` = strategic highway (e.g., Amman Ring Road segments, parts of Desert Highway)
- `trunk` = main inter-city (Desert Highway, King's Highway, Dead Sea Highway)
- `primary` = governorate main road
- `secondary` = governorate secondary
- `tertiary` = local arterial
- `residential` = neighbourhood (شارع / شارع داخلي)

### Verdict bands (estimated, calibrated to qualitative noise impact)

- 🟢 < 1,000 v/d (rural Bedia, secondary village road)
- 🟡 1,000–10,000 v/d (governorate secondary, urban side street)
- 🟠 10,000–50,000 v/d (urban arterial, Amman Circle approaches)
- 🔴 > 50,000 v/d (Amman Ring Road segments, Mecca Street, Zahran St, Airport Highway approaches)

### Amman-specific notes

- **Amman Ring Road** + **Airport Highway** + **Zahran Street** + **Mecca Street** + **Queen Rania Street** — among the busiest urban arterials; consistently noisy. Peak hours Sun–Thu 7–10am, 1–4pm, 7–10pm.
- **Friday quiet day**: Friday is the weekend's first day; traffic notably lighter on Fridays vs. weekdays — a sample drive on Friday will under-state peak noise.
- **Traffic congestion vs noise**: Amman has notable hilly topography with ring-road segments at higher elevation than residential neighbourhoods — noise propagation can be amplified at certain elevations.
- **Air quality**: Amman PM2.5 + dust events (haboob spring sandstorms) are recurrent — health exposure layered on top of vehicular noise/particulates. WHO Global Air Quality Database `https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/themes/air-pollution`.

**LOW** — per-segment traffic counts comparable to EU/UK datasets are **not publicly available** for Jordan. Use OSM road class + governorate vehicle density as coarse proxy; for high-stakes purchases, a site visit during peak hours (Sun–Thu) is the only reliable signal. **Friday visits will under-state.**

## Section: `--tax`

### Annual property tax — Building & Land Tax (ضريبة الأبنية والأراضي)

Administered at **municipal level** (GAM in Amman; relevant baladiya elsewhere) under the Building & Land Tax Law (Law 11/1954 as amended) plus annual municipal ordinances. Source: GAM tax page `https://media.ammancity.gov.jo/En/List/Electronic_services` (search "ضريبة الأبنية والأراضي"); verify per-municipality.

**Computed**: `annual tax = (built-up area in m²) × (zone-classified rate JOD/m²)` for buildings; `(plot area in m²) × (zone-classified rate JOD/m²)` for vacant land.

| Item | Value (2026 reference, GAM zones; verify with `https://www.amman.jo/`) |
|---|---|
| **Residential rate range** (built-up) | ~**JOD 0.5–3.0/m²/year** depending on zone classification (A/B/C/D) and property age |
| **Commercial rate range** | Higher; ~JOD 2–8/m²/year typical |
| **Vacant land rate** | Lower JOD/m²; varies by zone |
| **Re-valuation / re-zoning cycle** | Periodic GAM ordinance updates; typically every 3–5 years per zone |
| **Vocational License Fee (رخصة مهن)** | Separate; commercial-only — annual fee for trade activity at the address |

### Example calculation (residential, Amman 7th Circle, finished apartment)

A 150 m² apartment in 7th Circle Amman, GAM Zone B, residential rate ~JOD 1.5/m²/year:

- 150 × 1.5 = **JOD 225/year (~USD 317/year)**

For a 250 m² villa in Abdoun (Zone A) at ~JOD 2.5/m²/year:

- 250 × 2.5 = **JOD 625/year (~USD 881/year)**

**→ Annual property tax in Jordan is low** by international comparison — comparable to Italian IMU on a small holiday flat. The bigger fiscal drag is at transaction (below).

### Transaction taxes (one-time at purchase)

| Item | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Property Sales Tax (ضريبة المبيعات العقارية)** | **9%** of declared transaction value | Unified rate since 2019 reform (combining historical 4% buyer + 5% seller). Payable at DLS at registration. Source: ISTD `https://www.istd.gov.jo/`. |
| **DLS registration fees (رسوم تسجيل)** | ~**0.4–1%** depending on case | Tiered fee schedule; verify at DLS |
| **Notarial / Survey / Service fees** | ~**0.5–1%** | Includes deed preparation, notary, survey verification |
| **Lawyer fees (independent)** | **1–3%** of price typical | Foreign buyer should always retain independent counsel |
| **Brokerage** | **2–5%** typical (often split, sometimes seller-paid only) | Real Estate Brokers Law 2024 introduced licensing |
| **AML / KYC processing** | nominal flat fees | Bank-side and DLS-side |

**Total foreign-buyer transaction cost**: est. **~10–13% of price** depending on lawyer rate + whether brokerage is split. Among the higher transaction-tax regimes in MENA — comparable to Belgium / UK / Italy at the higher end.

### Capital gains tax (on sale)

- **Jordan does NOT levy capital gains tax on real estate sales by individuals** (Income Tax Law 38/2018 as amended). Source: ISTD `https://www.istd.gov.jo/`.
- **Corporate sellers** are subject to corporate income tax on RE gains (typically 20% — verify rate at ISTD).
- **Implication for foreign buyers**: a 0% individual CGT exit is uniquely favourable in MENA — it offsets the ~10–13% entry friction over a multi-year hold.

### VAT / General Sales Tax (GST)

- **Standard GST rate**: **16%** on most goods + services (Jordanian GST Law). Source: ISTD `https://www.istd.gov.jo/`.
- **First-sale residential by developer**: **5% reduced rate** under recent reforms (verify currency at ISTD).
- **Resale residential**: **0% GST** (out of scope; Property Sales Tax applies instead).
- **Commercial property new development**: 16% GST may apply to first sale.

### Income tax on rental (foreigner / non-resident)

- **Non-resident landlords** taxed under Jordanian Income Tax Law 38/2018 + amendments. Rental income from Jordanian real estate is Jordan-sourced → taxable in Jordan regardless of landlord residency.
- **Withholding**: **10% withholding tax** typical on gross rental paid to non-resident landlord (verify current at ISTD).
- **Resident progressive PIT brackets**: **5–30%** across bands. Source: ISTD `https://www.istd.gov.jo/Pages/Income-Tax.aspx`.
- **Maintenance/expense allowance**: deduction available; verify percentage at ISTD.

### Future risk

- ISTD has periodically discussed expanding the property tax base + adjusting GST treatment. Verify at the time of transaction.
- Recurring fiscal-pressure context — IMF program engagements may drive future adjustments.

### Confidence

**MEDIUM-HIGH** for the legal framework (Building & Land Tax Law, Income Tax Law 38/2018, 9% Property Sales Tax — all primary government sources). **MEDIUM** for exact rate values per municipality — GAM zone rates vary and have been adjusted multiple times.

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term residential

Jordanian residential rental is governed by the **Owners and Tenants Law** (recent significant reforms; older "frozen-rent" Law 11/1994 has been substantially eroded by reform). Modern leases are predominantly **fixed-term contracts** (1+1 years typical) with negotiated rents.

| Tier | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Old frozen-rent contracts** (pre-1994) | Diminishing inventory; some residual cases. **Avoid buying property with such tenants in place** — eviction is legally + politically fraught |
| **Modern fixed-term lease** | Standard market; freely-set rent; Owners and Tenants Law amendments improve landlord position vs. earlier framework |

### Short-term rentals (Airbnb / Booking)

**Status**: regulated but enforcement uneven.

- **Tourism licensing**: short-term rentals operated commercially require licensing under the **Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities (MoTA)** + Jordan Hotels Association (`https://johotels.org/` — live, verified 2026-09-18). Source: MoTA `https://www.mota.gov.jo/`.
- **Practical situation in Amman**: many informal Airbnb operations (especially in Abdoun + Sweifieh + Jabal Amman + 7th Circle) operate without licence — regulatory grey zone with periodic enforcement waves.
- **Aqaba + Dead Sea**: STR demand is real but seasonality is sharp; resort compounds may forbid sub-letting in HOA bylaws.
- **HOA bylaws** in modern Amman compounds (Abdali residences, Marina apartments, etc.) often **ban or restrict** short-let — read the bylaws before underwriting any STR yield.

### Strategic notes

- **Yields**: Amman rental yields historically ~5–8% gross in mid-tier districts; lower (3–5%) in Abdoun + Sweifieh; higher (8–12%) in East Amman secondary stock with higher void/management risk.
- **Diaspora demand**: large Iraqi + Syrian + GCC professional populations drive sustained Amman demand; medical-tourism + higher-education catchment in Amman supports furnished-rental segment.
- **University rental (Irbid)**: Yarmouk + JUST catchment for student rental; lower per-m² but high occupancy rates.
- **USD-denominated leases**: large diplomatic + INGO + multinational tenant pool pays in USD-equivalent JOD — provides FX-stable income for foreign owners.

### Confidence

**MEDIUM** — legal framework is mature (Owners and Tenants Law); STR regulation is documented but enforcement uneven; per-parcel STR feasibility depends on HOA bylaws + MoTA licensing.

### Flood risk

- **Wadi flash floods**: Jordan's wadi systems (Wadi Mujib, Wadi Hasa, Wadi Zarqa, etc.) are subject to **flash floods** during winter storms (Dec–Mar) — rainfall is concentrated and the topography channelises runoff. The **2018 Dead Sea school bus tragedy** (Wadi al-Maeen, ~21 fatalities) underscored the risk; subsequent monitoring + warning improvements have been deployed by Civil Defence.
- **Amman urban drainage**: Amman's hilly topography means valleys (Wadi Saqra etc.) drain heavy rainfall through built-up areas; older drainage infra strained in 2018 + 2019 storms. East Amman lower-elevation neighbourhoods + Jordan Valley + wadi outlets are most exposed.
- **Aqaba**: rare flash floods from Eastern Desert wadis during winter storms; coastal flooding minimal (Red Sea has no significant tides + no SLR-driven exposure on Aqaba's compact coast yet).
- **Dead Sea + Jordan Valley**: agricultural inundation risk during wadi events; resort developments in wadi outlets exposed.

### Seismicity

- **Dead Sea Transform fault (DST)** — Jordan's eastern flank along the Dead Sea Rift (Jordan Valley → Dead Sea → Wadi Araba → Gulf of Aqaba) is a left-lateral transform fault zone with **moderate seismic potential**. Source: MEMR — Jordan Seismological Observatory `https://www.memr.gov.jo/Ar/List/%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%B5%D8%AF_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B2%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B2%D9%84`.
- **Historical events**:
  - **1927 Jericho Earthquake** (M 6.2) — major regional event; baseline reference.
  - **1995 Nuweiba (Gulf of Aqaba)** (M 7.2) — felt strongly in Aqaba; structural damage to older buildings.
  - **Smaller M 4–5 events** periodic along the rift.
- **Building codes**: **Jordanian Building Code 2018** (and predecessors) incorporate seismic provisions; modern code-compliant construction in Amman + Aqaba should be acceptable. **Pre-1980 stock** (parts of Jabal Amman, Jabal Hussein, older central districts) may not meet current standards — commission a structural review.

### Climate risks (rising)

- **Drought + extreme water scarcity**: Jordan is among the **most water-poor countries globally** — annual renewable water resources ~**~80–100 m³/capita/year** (well below 500 m³ "absolute scarcity" threshold). Source: Ministry of Water and Irrigation `https://www.mwi.gov.jo/`. Mitigation infra: **Disi Water Conveyance Project** (fossil aquifer pipeline from southern Disi to Amman, operational since 2013); **Aqaba-Amman Water Desalination + Conveyance Project** (planned). Property pricing in some areas reflects water-access reliability — verify per address with Miyahuna (Amman) or local water authority.
- **Heat extremes**: Amman summer days >35°C are increasing; Jordan Valley + Aqaba sees regular >40°C summer events. IPCC AR6 projects **+1.5–3°C** by 2050 vs. 1981–2010 baseline (depending on SSP); **+2.5–5°C** by 2100.
- **Sandstorms / haboobs**: regular Spring (Mar–May) — visibility, air quality, agricultural impact, occasional structural damage to lightweight construction.
- **Air pollution**: Amman PM2.5 elevated during dust events + winter heating + traffic; less severe than Cairo but materially above WHO guidelines on bad days.
- **Sea-level rise**: limited exposure (only Aqaba's compact coastline is affected; Aqaba elevation buffer is good for most developments — verify per-parcel for direct waterfront).
- **Dead Sea declining** (~1m/year sea-surface drop, varies) → progressive shoreline retreat, sinkhole formation, infrastructure stress on Dead Sea resort properties; **Red Sea–Dead Sea project** discussed but stalled.

### Build-era hazards

| Era | Hazards |
|---|---|
| **Pre-1948** (Hashemite-era + older) | Beautiful but often unmaintained; stone construction with weak mortar; lead pipes in some Jabal Amman + Jabal Hussein townhouses; weak seismic capacity |
| **1948–1980** (Hashemite expansion + post-war refugee influx) | Reinforced concrete dominant; mixed quality; pre-modern seismic code |
| **1980–2000** (post-peace expansion) | Mixed — luxury (Abdoun, Sweifieh extensions) and informal/illegal construction (East Amman, Zarqa) |

**Informal construction (مباني عشوائية)**: a meaningful share of East Amman + Zarqa + some peri-urban areas is informal/illegal. **Ad hoc legalisation programs** have been deployed periodically — buyers should insist on a **valid building permit + occupancy certificate** for any older urban property.

- Lead pipes in pre-1980 Amman + Zarqa older buildings possible; replace at renovation.

### Verification

### Water rationing reality

This is a **distinctive Jordanian quirk**: even properties on mains receive water for limited windows (often 1–3 days per week). Almost every Jordanian residential building has rooftop water tanks (`خزانات` / khazanat) sized for the gap. Capacity, pump quality, and tank cleanliness materially affect liveability — **verify at property visit**.

## Caveats unique to JO (HEAVY — read all)

- **Reciprocity list dynamic + opaque**: DLS-maintained list shifts by Cabinet decision; **always confirm in writing for your specific nationality before deposit**.
- **Foreign-ownership size cap + zone restrictions**: 5,000 m² threshold for streamlined; >5,000 m² needs Cabinet approval (slow); border + military zones forbidden.
- **9% Property Sales Tax + ~1–3% other** = ~**10–13% transaction cost** — among MENA's highest entry friction.
- **Offset by 0% individual CGT exit** — uniquely favourable on resale.
- **JOD-USD peg since 1995** — no FX risk for USD buyers; CBJ has held the peg through 30+ years of regional shocks; verify peg is intact at the time of transaction (rare but not impossible to be broken).
- **Water rationing reality**: scheduled mains supply 1–3 days/week typical; rooftop tank + pump capacity is a critical liveability diligence item.
- **CBI Jordan passport mobility ~50 visa-free destinations** — well below EU/Caribbean CBI alternatives; choose Jordan CBI for diaspora reconnection or regional business utility, NOT pure global mobility.
- **Aqaba ASEZ separate framework**: ASEZA approval distinct from DLS; verify **per-unit approval letter**, not generic developer master-licence claim.
- **Pre-1980 building stock seismic concerns**: Dead Sea Transform fault is moderate seismic; pre-modern code-era stock in Jabal Amman + Jabal Hussein + similar warrants structural review.
- **Wadi flash flood risk**: 2018 Dead Sea school bus tragedy reference; verify any property near wadi outlets, esp. winter (Dec–Mar).
- **Refugee + diaspora dynamics drive Amman demand**: Iraqi 2003+, Syrian 2011+, Gaza 2023+ inflows have produced demand spikes followed by stabilisation; model regional sensitivity.
- **AML / source-of-funds**: Jordan is FATF-compliant; foreign buyers pre-prepare bank statements + wealth letter; cash transactions above defined thresholds reportable.
- **Inheritance — Sharia mandatory shares for Muslims**; non-Muslim foreign buyers may opt for home-country law via prior declaration — verify with an estates lawyer at purchase.
- **STR regulation grey zone**: MoTA licensing required for commercial STR; many informal Airbnbs unlicensed; HOA bylaws often dispositive; enforcement waves periodic.
- **Plot identifier triplet**: always verify Plot + Basin + Sub-Basin numbers against deed AND survey map — discrepancies are not uncommon.
- **GST 16% standard / 5% on first-sale residential / 0% on resale** — verify which applies in any new-build purchase.
- **Drought + water scarcity macro risk**: Jordan is among the world's most water-poor; long-horizon Disi aquifer depletion + Red-Dead project status uncertainty are systemic risks affecting valuation.

## Quirks to know

- **Reciprocity list opacity**: dynamic; written DLS confirmation per nationality before any deposit.
- **Plot identifier triplet** (Plot + Basin + Sub-Basin): always verify all three.
- **Water rationing scheduled supply 1–3 days/week** in much of Amman + governorates; rooftop tanks are universal — capacity + pump quality matter for liveability.
- **JOD-USD peg since 1995**: no FX risk for USD buyers; verify peg integrity at transaction.
- **0% individual CGT** is a globally rare exit-side feature in a high-entry-tax market.
- **9% Property Sales Tax** is unified since 2019 reform (was 4% buyer + 5% seller separately).
- **Friday is weekend day 1** (not Saturday): traffic + business-hours pattern. Sun–Thu working week.
- **Building & Land Tax** is municipal-level + zone-classified — verify on the seller's last bill, not generic rate tables.
- **ASEZ Aqaba separate approval**: ASEZA framework distinct from mainland DLS — verify per-unit, not master-licence.
- **Crown Prince Real Estate Initiative**: Jordanian-only subsidised housing — affects supply pipeline, not foreign-buyer access directly.
- **Vision Jordan 2025 / Economic Modernisation Vision 2033**: zoning + density + transport plans evolve under these frameworks; medium-term valuation drift.
- **GAM Greater Amman Master Plan 2025**: zoning revisions affect property classification + tax bands.
- **Off-plan delivery risk**: better-developer track record at major Amman compounds (Abdali, Eagle Hills) + Aqaba (Saraya, Ayla); smaller developers carry delivery risk.
- **Refugee + diaspora demand pulses** drive periodic Amman price spikes (2003, 2011, 2023+).
- **Dead Sea declining ~1m/year**: progressive shoreline retreat, sinkhole risk on some Dead Sea resort properties — verify per parcel.
