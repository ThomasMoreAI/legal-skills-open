# Caribbean Netherlands — Bonaire, Saba, Sint Eustatius (bq) 🇧🇶

ISO2: `bq`. Status: ✅ Researched 2026-05.

> ⚠️ **Scope clarification (CRITICAL)**: `bq` covers the **three special municipalities** of the Netherlands proper — Bonaire (BO), Sint Eustatius (SE/"Statia"), Saba (SA) — collectively the **BES islands** since the 10-10-10 dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles (10 October 2010). They are **`bijzondere gemeenten`** (special municipalities) of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, NOT constituent countries. Aruba (`aw`), Curaçao (`cw`), Sint Maarten (`sx`) are separate constituent countries with their own ISO codes + playbooks.

## Country profile

- **Constitutional status**: **`bijzondere gemeenten` (special municipalities)** of the Netherlands, established by the **Wet openbare lichamen Bonaire, Sint Eustatius en Saba (WolBES)** + **Wet financiën BES (FinBES)**, in force from **10 October 2010** following dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles. NOT constituent countries — direct integration into the Netherlands central administration via the **Rijksdienst Caribisch Nederland (RCN)** (`https://english.rijksdienstcn.com/`) headquartered in Kralendijk (Bonaire) with offices on each island
- **Population** (CBS 1 Jan 2024 reference): **Bonaire ~24,000+**, **Saba ~1,900**, **Sint Eustatius ~3,200** — total BES ~29,000–30,000 (verify current at `https://www.cbs.nl/` *Caribisch Nederland in cijfers*)
- **Surface**: Bonaire **288 km²** (largest), Sint Eustatius **21 km²**, Saba **13 km²** (smallest) — total ~322 km²
- **Currency**: **USD (United States Dollar)** — UNIQUE among Dutch Kingdom jurisdictions. Transitioned from **Netherlands Antillean Guilder (ANG / NAf)** to USD on **1 January 2011** under **Wet geldstelsel BES** following the 10-10-10 reform. The other constituent countries (Aruba: AWG; Curaçao + Sint Maarten: Caribbean guilder XCG, replacing ANG 31 Mar 2025) all use guilders pegged to USD. **EUR is NOT legal tender in BES.** Plus side: zero FX risk for USD-funded buyers; minus side: EUR-area buyers carry full EUR/USD exposure on every payment cycle
- **Languages**: official **Dutch** (Nederlands) under WolBES; recognised local languages per island:
  - **Bonaire**: **Papiamentu** (everyday lingua franca, Iberian-creole; cf. Aruba/Curaçao) + Dutch + English + Spanish (de facto)
  - **Saba** and **Sint Eustatius**: **English** primary everyday language (historic BES-English creole roots), Dutch as the administrative language
  - Notarial deeds, government statutes, and tax declarations are drafted in **Dutch**; English translations are courtesy. Sworn translator strongly advisable for non-Dutch-speaking buyers
- **Legal system**: **Dutch civil law** applies via island-specific code: **`Burgerlijk Wetboek BES`** (BES Civil Code) — adapted from the Netherlands Antilles civil code, evolving toward alignment with the European-Netherlands Burgerlijk Wetboek (Books 1–10). Real-estate transfers governed by **BW BES Book 5 (property) + Book 7 (contracts)**; notarial regime under **Wet op het notarisambt BES**
- **Tax authority**: **`Belastingdienst Caribisch Nederland (B/CN)`** — single tax authority for all three islands, separate from Dutch mainland Belastingdienst. Primary site: `https://www.belastingdienst-cn.nl/`. English summary via RCN at `https://english.rijksdienstcn.com/other-services/tax-administration`
- **Cadastre / land registry**: **`Kadaster BES`** (Bureau Kadaster BES on Bonaire, branches on Saba + Sint Eustatius) — parcel records (`perceel`), title registration (`eigendomsbewijs`), mortgages (`hypotheken`). Web portal limited; in-person/mailed requests typical. The Netherlands **Kadaster** (mainland) coordinates BES cadastre under bilateral arrangement
- **EU/Schengen status**: **NOT in the EU**, classified as **`Land en Gebied Overzee (LGO) / Overseas Country and Territory (OCT)`** under TFEU Annex II — the BES islands followed Aruba/Curaçao/Sint Maarten into OCT status. **NOT in Schengen.** Schengen visas do NOT grant entry to BES; separate **Caribbean visa (Visum Caribische Delen)** regime via Dutch consulates abroad. NL nationals + EU nationals enter visa-free; non-EU/non-NL nationals subject to Caribbean visa
- **Foreign-buyer regime**: **OPEN — no nationality-based restriction on residential real-estate acquisition** under BW BES + national WolBES framework. Restrictions are zoning / planning / coastal-protection / conservation-area driven (Bonaire's **STINAPA**-managed marine + terrestrial parks; Saba **National Land** + park zones; Sint Eustatius **National Parks Foundation** managed areas). Verify zoning per parcel with the **Ruimtelijke Ontwikkeling** office of the relevant island government (Openbaar Lichaam Bonaire / Saba / Sint Eustatius)
- **Identifier**: Kadastrale aanduiding per island — `BO00 sectie X nr.` (Bonaire), `SE00 sectie X nr.` (Sint Eustatius), `SA00 sectie X nr.` (Saba)

Use 12-month rolling averages, never a single comparable.

### Compute

1. USD/m² = price / **built/`bebouwd` area** per Kadaster title (note: BES titles sometimes only carry `perceeloppervlakte` (lot area), not built area → verify built m² via building permit or independent surveyor)
2. BES-specific premium drivers: **direct waterfront** (very scarce — Bonaire Belnem strip; Saba sea-cliff parcels), **Sabadeco / Belnem zoning** (premium gated/managed), **post-2015 hurricane-resilient build** (post-Irma 2017 updated codes)
3. Discount drivers: **hurricane-exposed orientation** (Saba/Statia in main Atlantic hurricane belt), **older wooden Saba-cottage stock without modern upgrades**, **non-Kadaster-registered "long lease" (`erfpacht`)** title vs full freehold, **conservation-zone overlay restricting build/expand**

### Key terms

- `Eigendom` = freehold / full ownership
- `Erfpacht` = long lease (typical 30/60-year island-government leases on some Bonaire + Statia parcels — verify expiry date in title!)
- `Perceel` = parcel/plot
- `Bebouwd` = built / built-up
- `Kavel` = building plot (often unimproved land)

## Section: `--tax`

> **HIGH-confidence section**: `Belastingdienst Caribisch Nederland` is a clean primary source with published rates per island per category.

### Annual property tax — `vastgoedbelasting`

Foundational law: **Belastingwet BES (Wet Belasting BES, Hoofdstuk VI Vastgoedbelasting)**. Authoritative tariff page: `https://www.belastingdienst-cn.nl/onderwerpen/vastgoedbelasting/tarief-vastgoedbelasting` (2026 data, source Belastingdienst CN).

- **Computation**: tax = `17.5% × (4% × waarde onroerend goed)` = **`0.7%` effective national base rate** on assessed property value
- **`Opcenten` (island surcharge)**:
  - **Bonaire**: **30% surcharge** in force since 1 January 2020 (under *Eilandsverordening opcenten vastgoedbelasting Bonaire*) → **effective rate 0.91%** of assessed value
  - **Saba + Sint Eustatius**: **NO opcenten** → effective rate stays at **0.7%**
- **Exemptions** (national, BES-wide):
  - **`Eigen woning` (primary owner-occupied residence)**: **FULLY EXEMPT** — primary residence used by the natural-person owner is out of scope of vastgoedbelasting (most owner-occupiers therefore pay no annual property tax)
  - **Religious / charitable / public-utility / educational buildings**: exempt
  - **Undeveloped land** owned by BES residents: exempt
- **`Second home / non-resident-held residential`** (THIS IS THE TYPICAL FOREIGN-BUYER CASE):
  - **De facto ~USD 70,000 exemption** (est. — verify the current heffingsvrije voet at `Belastingdienst CN`) applies to the assessed value of a residential property that is NOT the owner's primary residence → only the value exceeding USD 70,000 is taxed at 0.91% (Bonaire) / 0.7% (Saba/Statia)
  - Worked example (Bonaire, 2026): assessed value USD 400,000 → taxable base USD 330,000 → annual vastgoedbelasting USD 330,000 × 0.91% = **~USD 3,003/yr**
  - Same property on Saba/Statia: USD 330,000 × 0.7% = **~USD 2,310/yr**
- **Hotels owned by non-natural persons**: base rate **11% from 1 Jan 2025** (was 10% through 2024) → effective `0.44%` of value (BES-wide; Bonaire-surcharge applies on top for that island per `Belastingdienst CN`)
- **Time-sharing**: fixed return allocated time-proportionally with utilization + vacancy factors per Belastingdienst CN guidance
- **Investment facility**: value-increases from construction/renovation can defer for 10 years; application due by 31 December of the year following the works (verify per parcel)

### `2026–2030 valuation cycle` (CRITICAL — current cycle)

- Belastingdienst CN reassesses BES real estate **every 5 years**. Reference / valuation date for the new cycle is **1 January 2026** (2026 data, source Belastingdienst CN announcement)
- Independent recognised appraisers conduct visual observations from public roads + supplement with Kadaster, building-permit, and other public data; **no entry to homes or grounds** is requested
- The 2026 reassessed values will drive vastgoedbelasting bills through tax year 2030; buyers purchasing in 2026–2030 should request the **most recent `Beschikking vastgoedbelasting`** (the formal assessment) from the seller and verify it reflects the current cycle
- If a property is reassessed during the cycle (e.g. major renovation), the buyer can be hit with a step-change in annual tax — make this an explicit pre-deed question

### Transaction tax — `overdrachtsbelasting`

Foundational law: **Belastingwet BES, Hoofdstuk VII Overdrachtsbelasting**. Tariff: `https://www.belastingdienst-cn.nl/onderwerpen/overdrachtsbelasting`.

- **Rate**: **`5%` of the economic market value** (`waarde in het economisch verkeer`) — NOT of the stated contract price if that is artificially low
- **Taxpayer**: the **`verkrijger`** (buyer/acquirer) — natural or legal person
- **Mechanism**: the **`notaris`** (notary) declares and pays the tax to Belastingdienst CN as part of the deed-registration process
- **Base**: economic value at transfer — economic-value rule prevents structuring the deed at a below-market price to reduce the tax
- (Verify exemption schedule for intra-family transfers / corporate restructurings at `https://www.belastingdienst-cn.nl/onderwerpen/overdrachtsbelasting/vrijstelling-overdrachtsbelasting`)

### Rental income tax

- **Resident landlords**: rental income falls under **inkomstenbelasting BES** progressive scale (verify current bracket schedule at Belastingdienst CN — historically 30.4% effective top combined with AOV/AWW + ZV social premiums on the BES wage-tax stack)
- **Non-resident landlords**: BES-source rental income taxed in BES; double-tax-treaty coverage limited — BES has limited DTA network independent of Netherlands DTAs (some Netherlands DTAs apply to BES under post-2010 amendments — verify per home country at `https://www.belastingdienst-cn.nl/`)
- **US persons** (significant share of Bonaire-buyer profile): US worldwide-taxation reach + FATCA disclosure; no specific BES-US DTA applicable; rental income reported on US Form 1040 Schedule E + foreign-tax-credit for BES tax paid (verify with US-cross-border advisor)

### Total transaction cost (buyer side, est.)

- **Resale, typical Bonaire transaction**: ~**`6–9%` all-in** = 5% overdrachtsbelasting + 1–2% notaris + 0.5–1.5% kadaster registration + ancillary (broker if applicable typically 3–5% seller-side or split)
- **New-build first sale by a developer**: similar — overdrachtsbelasting applies to the transfer (verify whether `algemene bestedingsbelasting (ABB)` may apply at first sale by a developer — BES has ABB instead of EU-VAT; verify if your transaction triggers ABB at `https://www.belastingdienst-cn.nl/onderwerpen/abb-algemene-bestedingsbelasting-particulieren`)

⚠️ Algemene bestedingsbelasting (ABB) is the BES VAT-equivalent: **6% Bonaire, 8% Saba, 6% Sint Eustatius** (verify current — Belastingdienst CN). It typically does NOT apply to private residential resale but **can apply to first sale by a developer-builder** depending on classification.

## Section: `--rental`

### Short-let regime (Bonaire dive-expat dominant)

- **Bonaire short-let** is a substantial market — diver tourism, expat seasonal residents, US/NL second-home owners renting nightly via Airbnb/VRBO
- **Permit regime**: short-let accommodation governed by **island-government tourism regulations** (Openbaar Lichaam Bonaire `Directie Toerisme` + relevant zoning under `Ruimtelijk Ontwikkelingsplan Bonaire (ROB)`) — a short-let licence/registration is required for nightly rental; verify exact current process with Openbaar Lichaam Bonaire
- **Tourist tax**: **`Bonaire Visitor Entry Tax (VET)`** — **USD 75 per person per visit**, in force **1 July 2022**, which **replaced the island's former 'room tax' and rental 'car tax'** that accommodations and providers used to collect. From **1 July 2024** the paid tax is **valid for one month** (a re-entry inside that window is not re-charged), a **reduced USD 10** rate applies to island children not living on Bonaire, and Bonairian students studying full-time abroad are exempt. VET is paid by the *visitor* on arrival, not by the property owner (2026 data, source Openbaar Lichaam Bonaire tourism-tax portal; 2026-09-18 verified)
- **Saba / Statia**: short-let market is micro-scale; permits via the respective Openbaar Lichaam; far fewer Airbnb listings than Bonaire

### Long-let

- BES tenancies governed by **BW BES Book 7 (huurrecht)**; long-let leases (~12 months) common; deposit (`waarborgsom`) typically 1–2 months
- Rent regulation lighter than EU-NL; no statutory rent-cap regime; market-rate

### Yields (est.)

- **Bonaire long-term gross yield mid-tier apt 2026**: ≈ **4–6%** (USD-denominated; verify per-parcel)
- **Bonaire short-let (compliant, well-located, dive-season)**: gross yield potentially higher with strong seasonality — Dec–Apr peak, Sep–Oct low; thin transactional cap-rate data → wide CI
- **Saba / Statia**: too thin for a reliable yield band — case-by-case

### Hurricane risk (per island)

- **Saba + Sint Eustatius**: **HIGH** — sit in the **central Atlantic hurricane belt** (~17°N latitude); historic major impacts include **Hurricane Irma (Sept 2017, Cat 5)** which devastated Sint Maarten and damaged Statia/Saba materially, and **Hurricane Luis (1995)**. Building codes were updated post-Irma; hurricane shutters + reinforced concrete construction increasingly standard. **Hurricane insurance is a major recurring cost**
- **Bonaire**: **LOW–MODERATE** — at ~12°N latitude, **south of the main Atlantic hurricane corridor** (the Curaçao/Aruba/Bonaire "ABC" islands are in the "hurricane belt fringe"). Direct hits are rare but **Hurricane Lenny (1999)** and **Hurricane Omar (2008)** brought significant impacts; **Hurricane Felix (2007)** generated heavy surf damage; tropical storms passing south of the main track can still produce flooding + surge. Climate-change projections increase intensity (not necessarily frequency) of southern-Caribbean storms
- **Insurance correlate**: Saba/Statia hurricane-rider premiums substantially higher than Bonaire's; market underwriters have tightened terms post-Irma

### Volcanic risk

- **Saba — `Mount Scenery`**: dormant **stratovolcano** 887m (the **highest point in the entire Kingdom of the Netherlands**); last eruption ~1640s (uncertain); classified as **potentially active**; Smithsonian GVP tracks. Civil-protection plans maintained by RCN + Saba island government
- **Sint Eustatius — `The Quill`**: dormant stratovolcano ~600m; last known eruption ~AD 250 (radiocarbon-dated tephra layers); classified as **potentially active**. The Quill caldera dominates Statia's south end; the National Park overlay restricts most development on the volcano flanks
- **Bonaire**: **not volcanically active** in the modern era; the island is a non-volcanic uplifted reef-platform formation (limestone-dominant); volcanic risk effectively zero
- Buyer guidance: Saba + Statia parcels on the volcano flanks should be evaluated with the latest **`Volcanic Hazard Map`** (Saba Conservation Foundation / STENAPA + RCN); **lava-flow + lahar + pyroclastic-flow exposure** zoned out of high-risk areas by national park status

### Seismic risk

- **MINOR–MODERATE** BES-wide. The northeastern Caribbean (Saba/Statia) sits on the **subduction margin** of the North-American plate beneath the Caribbean plate; minor felt-tremors regular; historic major event **1843 Guadeloupe earthquake** felt across the NE Caribbean. Bonaire (southern Caribbean) sees lower regional seismicity. Modern build codes incorporate seismic detailing aligned with Caribbean engineering practice

### Sea-level rise + erosion

- **Bonaire coastal erosion**: significant — STINAPA + Wageningen UR studies document accelerating coastal-zone retreat on E/SE windward coast; W/leeward (Kralendijk strip + Belnem) less acute but trending. Climate-change projections (KNMI'23 BES scenarios — verify at `https://www.knmi.nl/`) flag **rising-sea-level + reef-decline + extreme-rainfall** compound risks
- **Saba / Statia** cliff-coast morphology: less low-lying vulnerability than Bonaire, but cliff-edge parcels carry erosion + landslide concerns

### Build-era hazards

| Era | Hazards |
|---|---|
| **Pre-1950 colonial stock** | Cottage / wooden Saba-cottage style; lead paint; obsolete electrical; minimal hurricane resilience |
| **1950–1990** | Concrete-block + cement masonry; asbestos-cement (Eternit-class) widespread on roofing; pre-modern hurricane detailing; single-pane glass |
| **1990–2010** | Improving hurricane envelope; asbestos phase-out; gradual code modernisation |
| **2010+ (post-10-10-10, post-Irma 2017 codes)** | Modern hurricane shutters / impact glass standard; reinforced concrete frame; STINAPA / island-zoning compliance for new coastal builds |

## Section: `--mains`

### Sources + operators (per island)

- **Bonaire**: **`WEB Bonaire NV`** (Water- en Energiebedrijf Bonaire) — `https://www.webbonaire.com/` — integrated water + electricity utility; majority island-government-owned. Sewer is municipal (Openbaar Lichaam Bonaire) where mains exist; many parcels rely on **septic tanks** outside Kralendijk + Belnem
- **Saba**: **`Saba Electric Company NV (SEC)`** — `https://www.sabaelectric.com/` — electricity; water typically via **`cistern`** (rainwater collection — standard Saba practice — mandatory by topography; mains water network limited / nonexistent on most parcels) + small public supply in The Bottom/Windwardside
- **Sint Eustatius**: **`STUCO`** (St. Eustatius Utility Company NV) — `https://www.stucoeux.com/` — electricity + water; expanded mains water network post-2010 reform
- **Telecom**: **`Digicel`** + **`Flow / Liberty Latin America`** mobile; FTTH selectively in Kralendijk (Bonaire); satellite + LTE/5G fixed-wireless common elsewhere

### Verification

- **Bonaire Kralendijk + Belnem + Sabadeco**: WEB mains water + electricity universal; sewer often septic except newer subdivisions
- **Rural Bonaire (Lagun, Rincon, Washikemba)**: cistern + septic + LPG common; verify per parcel
- **Saba**: cistern is the norm; SEC electricity reliable in populated areas; LPG cooking standard
- **Statia**: STUCO mains in Oranjestad + Concordia; rural parcels mixed

## Section: `--crime`

- Primary source: **`Korps Politie Caribisch Nederland (KPCN)`** — `https://politiecn.com/` — single police service covering all three BES islands; annual statistical summaries
- BES is broadly low-crime relative to wider Caribbean; **Bonaire** dominates absolute incident counts (population weight); **Saba + Statia** very low absolute numbers (statistical noise dominates)
- Tourist-area opportunistic theft (Kralendijk waterfront, dive-site car break-ins) is the main everyday concern; violent crime rare
- ⚠️ Small absolute numbers → 5-year averages essential; YoY swings dominated by noise

## Section: `--climate`

- **Köppen-Geiger**: **Aw (tropical savanna)** for Bonaire (low rainfall, ~500mm/yr, semi-arid by Caribbean standards); **Am (tropical monsoon)** for Saba + Statia (higher rainfall, mountain orographic)
- Reference baseline: **KNMI Climate Atlas + KNMI'23 Caribbean scenarios** — `https://www.knmi.nl/`; supplementary CMCC / IPCC AR6 regional projections
- **Key projections (BES, KNMI'23 + IPCC AR6)**:
  - Sea-level rise: **+0.3–0.8 m by 2100** (intermediate/high SSP scenarios) — material for low-lying Bonaire coastal parcels (Belnem, Kralendijk)
  - Tropical-cyclone intensity: **increasing major-hurricane frequency** in northern Caribbean → Saba/Statia exposure trend up
  - Coral-reef decline: Bonaire's signature dive industry directly exposed to bleaching events (2023 mass-bleaching event documented by STINAPA)
  - Rainfall: variability increasing — drought + heavy-rain dichotomy
- **Acute-event flags**:
  - 🟠 Bonaire coastal parcel <2 m above MSL — sea-level rise + storm-surge compound
  - 🟠 Saba/Statia cliff-edge parcel — erosion + landslide post-rainfall
  - 🟡 mid-elevation residential — generally resilient with modern build

### Climate verdict (typical Bonaire mid-coast apt)

🟡 **Watch** — sea-level + reef-decline + cooling-load growth; insurance trajectory upward; flash-flood + surge for low-lying coastal parcels. Saba/Statia hurricane-belt exposure is the dominant risk driver.

## Section: `--currency`

### Status

- **USD (United States Dollar)** is sole legal tender since **1 January 2011** under **`Wet geldstelsel BES`**
- Transition: replaced **Netherlands Antillean Guilder (ANG / NAf)** at fixed rate **NAf 1.79 = USD 1** following the 10-10-10 dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles
- **EUR is NOT legal tender in BES** — and neither is the new **Caribbean Guilder (XCG)** which replaced ANG on Curaçao + Sint Maarten 31 March 2025 (BES are NOT in XCG zone)
- USD circulating notes + coins are US Federal Reserve issuance; the central bank with prudential oversight for BES-licensed banks is **`De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB)`** — `https://www.dnb.nl/` — operating under the Caribbean-specific framework

### What this means for buyers

- **USD-funded buyers (US persons, US-asset holders)**: **zero FX risk** for purchase, ongoing tax, utilities, mortgage
- **EUR-funded buyers (NL mainland buyers, EU buyers)**: full EUR/USD exposure on **every** payment cycle — material consideration; **a 10% EUR/USD swing changes annual carrying cost by 10%**. Forward-hedging available via Dutch mainland banks for the deal but ongoing payment cycles harder to hedge
- **GBP / CHF / other-currency buyers**: layered FX (home → USD)
- **AML**: BES applies a Dutch-derived AML framework via DNB + BCN; USD cash transactions reporting thresholds aligned

## Section: `--visa`

- **Caribbean visa (Visum Caribische Delen)** regime — separate from Schengen
- Nationals of EU/EEA, NL, US, UK, CA, AU + most OECD countries enter visa-free up to 180 days
- Long-stay (residence) requires **`vergunning tot tijdelijk verblijf (VTV)`** via **`Immigratie- en Naturalisatiedienst Caribisch Nederland`** — `https://english.rijksdienstcn.com/immigration-naturalisation`
- **No formal golden-visa / CBI programme** for BES — pathways: family reunification, work permit + employer sponsorship, retiree / financially-independent route (income-based; verify quantum at RCN/IND-CN), or full naturalisation (5 years' continuous residence + Dutch-language test toward NL citizenship)
- **Property purchase alone does NOT grant residence rights**

## Section: `--insurance`

- Mortgage-lender mandatory: **fire / multi-peril** (`opstalverzekering`) on dwelling structure
- **Hurricane / windstorm rider**: critical on Saba/Statia; significant premium loading (USD 1,500–6,000+/yr range Saba/Statia residential vs USD 500–1,800/yr Bonaire range — verify per parcel + sum-insured)
- **Flood**: rider; coastal Bonaire exposure
- Major underwriters: **ENNIA Caribe Schade**, **Guardian Group**, **Nagico** — local Dutch-Caribbean insurers; some NL-mainland insurer participation via local agency
- **No BES catastrophic-risk pool** equivalent to French CCR — coverage via private market

## Section: `--notary`

- **`Notaris`** (civil-law notary) mandatory for all RE transfers under **`Wet op het notarisambt BES`**
- Notaris drafts the **`akte van levering`** (deed of transfer), verifies identity, confirms title via Kadaster BES, withholds + remits **`overdrachtsbelasting`** to Belastingdienst CN, files the deed at Kadaster BES
- Standard timeline (Bonaire resale, typical): D0 *koopovereenkomst* (preliminary sales contract) → D14 buyer deposit ~10% in notaris escrow → D14–D45 financing + Kadaster check → D45–D75 *akte van levering* signing + balance + kadaster registration
- **Foreign buyers**: no nationality bar; standard AML / KYC / source-of-funds review; apostilled passport for non-NL nationals; sworn translator advisable for non-Dutch speakers (closing docs in Dutch)
- **Notary fee**: typically `1–2% of price`; **Kadaster registration fee**: small fixed component + value-based component
- **Total buyer closing cost (BES typical)**: ~**`6–9%` of price** all-in (5% overdrachtsbelasting + 1–2% notaris + kadaster + ancillary)

### BES distinctive position

- **Only USD-denominated jurisdiction in the Dutch Kingdom** → unique value for US-asset holders avoiding FX exposure
- **Eigen-woning exemption** + USD 70k second-home threshold → effective vastgoedbelasting bill on a typical USD 400k second home is **~USD 2,300–3,000/yr** depending on island, low by international standards
- **Bonaire** = the practical market; Saba/Statia are micro-markets
