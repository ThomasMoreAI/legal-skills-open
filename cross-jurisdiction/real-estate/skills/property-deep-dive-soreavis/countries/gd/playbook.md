# Grenada 🇬🇩 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `gd`. Status: ✅ Fully populated (researched 2026-05-28).

> **Critical framing — read first**: Grenada is an **English common-law island state** ("the Isle of Spice") in the south-eastern Caribbean — the southern end of the Windward Islands chain, just north of Trinidad — comprising **Grenada (main island), Carriacou and Petite Martinique** (~344 km²). The currency **XCD (Eastern Caribbean Dollar) is pegged XCD 2.70 = USD 1.00 since 1976** — a hard regional peg shared by the 8-member ECCU and administered by the [Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB)](https://www.eccb-centralbank.org/). **Foreign buyers CAN own freehold**, but a non-citizen must obtain an **Alien Landholding Licence (ALHL) ≈ 10% of property value** (≈ 3 months) before completing — **UNLESS** buying **CBI-approved real estate** (the ALHL is waived). On top of the ALHL, a non-citizen **purchaser** pays **Property Transfer Tax (PTT) of 10%**, so a **non-CBI foreign buyer faces a ~22%+ government-plus-fee acquisition stack** (10% PTT + ~10% ALHL + ~2% legal + 15% VAT-on-fees). **Citizenship-by-Investment (CBI) EXISTS** and is the central hook: real-estate qualifying-share minimum **USD 270,000** (+ a USD 50,000 government fee), full-unit minimum **USD 350,000**, NTF donation route **USD 235,000**, all with a **mandatory 5-year hold** — **effective 1 July 2024** (raised from USD 220,000 under the 2024 OECS price-floor harmonisation; **guaranteed buy-backs, discounts and rebates are now PROHIBITED**). Grenada's marquee differentiator is that it is the **only Caribbean CBI state with a US E-2 Treaty-Investor relationship** (treaty in force since 3 March 1989) — **BUT** the **AMIGOS Act (Dec 2022, US FY23 NDAA) requires 3 years' Grenada residence** before a CBI-naturalised citizen can use the E-2, which kills the "instant E-2" sales pitch. **Hurricane risk is the defining structural risk**: **Hurricane Ivan (7 Sep 2004)** caused damage ≈ **200% of GDP** and hit **~89–90% of homes**; pre-2005 building stock is a red flag and **OECS Building Code 2015** compliance is the diligence bar. Title is held under a **deeds-registration** (NOT Torrens) system at the **Deeds & Land Registry of the Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court**; final appeal is to the **UK Privy Council (JCPC)**. **Confirm** any current CBI threshold, PTT/ALHL rate, GRENLEC tariff, and E-2/AMIGOS requirement at primary sources **before any binding step**. Authoritative primary sources: **[Government of Grenada](https://www.gov.gd)**, **[Investment Migration Agency (IMA) Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/citizenship-by-investment/)**, **[Inland Revenue Division (IRD)](https://ird.gd)**, **[Laws of Grenada](https://www.laws.gov.gd)**, **[eLandRegistry](https://elandregistry.gov.gd)**, **[GRENLEC](https://grenlec.com/)**, **[PURC](https://purc.gd)**, **[US Dept of State — E-2 treaty list](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/treaty.html)**.

## Country profile

- **Geography**: Tri-island sovereign state — **Grenada** (main island), **Carriacou** and **Petite Martinique** — **~344 km²** at the southern end of the Windward Islands (Lesser Antilles), ~150 km north of Trinidad. Capital **St. George's** (St. George parish, south-west coast — one of the Caribbean's most picturesque harbours, the Carenage). Six parishes: **St. George** (capital + south-coast property corridor), **St. David**, **St. Andrew**, **St. Patrick**, **St. Mark**, **St. John** + the Carriacou & Petite Martinique dependency.
- **Status**: Independent sovereign state (independence from UK, **7 February 1974**). Constitutional monarchy in the Commonwealth realm — King Charles III head of state, represented by a Governor-General; Westminster parliamentary system.
- **Population**: **~117,000** (≈117,300, World Bank-derived est. 2024/25 — verify at the [Grenada Central Statistical Office](https://stats.gov.gd/), last census 2022). Population near-flat; small ageing demographic with significant diaspora (UK, US, Canada, Trinidad).
- **GDP per capita**: **~USD 12,500 nominal** (2025, World Bank — verify at [IMF DataMapper Grenada](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/profile/GRD)). **Upper-middle-income** economy. Tourism + agriculture (nutmeg/mace — world's historic #2 nutmeg exporter) + CBI inflows + education (St. George's University) dominate.
- **Currency**: **Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD / EC$)** — issued by the [Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB)](https://www.eccb-centralbank.org/), **pegged XCD 2.70 = USD 1.00 since 1976** (hard peg, shared by the 8-member OECS/ECCU). **USD widely accepted** in tourism + real estate; **property prices commonly quoted in USD**, particularly CBI-eligible villas (which cluster at/above the USD 270k qualifying-share / 350k full-unit thresholds by design).
- **Languages**: **English (official, sole)** — all contracts, deeds, statutes in English. Grenadian Creole English + a French-lexicon patois are everyday vernaculars, but legal/professional life is standard English.
- **Legal system**: **English common law** + Grenadian statutes. Superior court is the resident **High Court** division of the **Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court (ECSC)**; appeals to the itinerant ECSC Court of Appeal; **final appeal to the Judicial Committee of the UK Privy Council (JCPC, London)** — Grenada has **NOT** acceded to the Caribbean Court of Justice (CCJ) appellate jurisdiction (rejected at a 2018 referendum; verified, audit 2026-05-28). **Conveyancing**: a **deeds-registration / abstract-of-title** system (NOT a Torrens registered-title system) under the **Deeds and Land Registry Act, Cap. 79**.
- **Admin levels**: **6 parishes** + Carriacou & Petite Martinique dependency. Local-government structure is centralised; parishes used mainly for ecclesiastical, electoral and address-identification purposes.
- **Cadastre / land registry**:
  - **[Deeds and Land Registry — Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court (Grenada)](https://www.gov.gd)** — recording office for deeds of conveyance, mortgages, and title chains under Cap. 79.
  - **[eLandRegistry](https://elandregistry.gov.gd)** — Grenada Land Agency's official online system for searching records registered at the Deeds & Land Registry (`.gov.gd` domain).
  - **[Inland Revenue Division (IRD)](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-tax)** — operates a property-valuation service; valuation references inform PTT + property-tax base but are not market value.
- **Identifier**: **Parish + survey-plan reference + lot/parcel number** recorded at the Deeds & Land Registry. **No universal national parcel ID** equivalent to a French numéro de parcelle; "family land" (old undivided inherited holdings) is a recurring Caribbean title complication.
- **Title concept**: **Freehold (fee simple)** dominant. Title held under the **deeds-recording** model (Cap. 79) — **chain-of-deeds diligence is essential**; statutory-declaration / adverse-possession ("squatter") claims exist, and title reform is widely described as "long overdue."
- **Recent reforms (12-month watchlist)**:
  - **CBI real-estate minimum raised USD 220,000 → USD 270,000 (qualifying share)**, effective **1 July 2024** under the Citizenship by Investment (Amendment) Regulations 2024 + the 2024 OECS CBI Memorandum of Agreement price-floor harmonisation (VERIFIED — IMA Grenada Circular No. 3 of 2024 + UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor measure #5026, audit 2026-05-28). Full-unit USD 350,000 unchanged; **guaranteed buy-backs / discounts / rebates PROHIBITED**.
  - **2019 conveyancing reform**: September 2019 amendment to the Conveyancing and Law of Property Act **reduced the good-title search period from 60 → 30 years**; original title deeds now held in the Supreme Court Registry.
  - **AMIGOS Act (Dec 2022, US Pub. L. 117-263, FY23 NDAA)**: imposes a **3-year treaty-country residence requirement** on CBI-naturalised citizens (Grenada + Turkey explicitly named) before E-2 eligibility (VERIFIED, audit 2026-05-28).
  - **PURC cost-of-service / tariff study** (RfP issued Feb 2026) — GRENLEC electricity tariffs may change; verify current schedule at GRENLEC before relying.
  - **Hurricane Beryl (1–2 July 2024)** devastated **Carriacou & Petite Martinique** (the dependency islands); main-island Grenada was materially less affected — verify Beryl specifics for any Carriacou/Petite Martinique listing at [NaDMA](https://www.gov.gd/resident/nadma) / NOW Grenada.

## 🇬🇩 Foreign buyer eligibility (READ FIRST)

This section determines whether a transaction is even legally possible — and what process applies. Grenada permits foreign freehold ownership, but the **gating mechanism is the Alien Landholding Licence (ALHL)** for non-CBI buyers, and the **central hook is Citizenship-by-Investment (CBI)** which waives the ALHL on approved property.

### Legal basis

- **Deeds and Land Registry Act, Cap. 79** ([laws.gov.gd](https://www.laws.gov.gd/index.php/chapters/d-76-84a/133-chapter-79-deeds-and-land-registry-act)) — deeds-recording + title chain.
- **Conveyancing and Law of Property Act** (as amended Sept 2019 — 30-year search period).
- **Alien Landholding Regulation Act** (Laws of Grenada — *verify exact chapter at [laws.gov.gd](https://www.laws.gov.gd)*) — non-citizen landholding licence regime.
- **Property Transfer Tax Act, No. 37 of 1998** — transaction tax (administered by [IRD](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-transfer-tax)).
- **Citizenship by Investment Act 2013 (as amended)** + **Citizenship by Investment (Amendment) Regulations 2024** — administered by the [Investment Migration Agency (IMA) Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/citizenship-by-investment/).

### Buyer category matrix

| Buyer category | Right |
|---|---|
| **Grenadian citizen** | Full freehold; standard conveyancing; no ALHL |
| **Non-citizen foreign buyer (NON-CBI)** | **Full freehold**, but must obtain an **Alien Landholding Licence (ALHL ≈ 10% of value, ~3 months)** before title transfer completes; **PLUS 10% PTT (purchaser)** — see `--tax` |
| **CBI applicant buying APPROVED real estate** | Full freehold; **ALHL WAIVED** (key cost saving — the ~10% is not payable); property must be a government-approved CBI project; **5-year hold** before resale |
| **CBI applicant — non-approved property** | Falls under the standard non-citizen ALHL + PTT regime (CBI status alone does not waive the ALHL unless the property is an approved CBI project) |

(Source: multiple Grenada conveyancing firms — C21 Grenada, CARICOM Attorneys, Forrester Legal Chambers — *secondary tier*; cross-checked [IMA Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/citizenship-by-investment/) + [IRD](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-transfer-tax) primary; *verify exact ALHL statutory rate + chapter at [laws.gov.gd](https://www.laws.gov.gd) and with a Grenada-admitted attorney before contract*)

### Citizenship-by-Investment (CBI) via real estate

| Route | Threshold (VERIFIED) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Real-estate qualifying-share** | **USD 270,000** + **USD 50,000 government fee** | Effective **1 July 2024** (up from USD 220,000); 5-year hold |
| **Real-estate full-unit** | **USD 350,000** | Unchanged in the 2024 reform; 5-year hold |
| **NTF donation** (National Transformation Fund) | **USD 235,000** (single applicant) | Family-size bands differ — *verify exact bands at IMA Grenada* |
| **Enterprise route** | **USD 1.5M** single investor OR **USD 350,000 per investor** in a JV totalling ≥ USD 5M | Less common |

(Source: [IMA Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/citizenship-by-investment/) + Circular No. 3 of 2024 + UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor measure #5026 — VERIFIED primary/authoritative, audit 2026-05-28. **Do NOT cite the old USD 220,000 figure as current.** **Guaranteed buy-backs / discounts / rebates PROHIBITED** by the 2024 amendment — treat any promised fixed CBI rental return or guaranteed resale with suspicion; it may breach the regulations.)

### US E-2 Treaty-Investor access — the marquee differentiator (with a load-bearing caveat)

- **Grenada holds a US E-2 Treaty of Commerce & Navigation, in force since 3 March 1989** — and is the **ONLY Caribbean CBI country whose citizens can use the US E-2 nonimmigrant treaty-investor visa** (St. Kitts & Nevis, Dominica, Antigua & Barbuda, St. Lucia have NO E-2 treaty). This is the marquee CBI differentiator. (VERIFIED against US State Dept treaty-country listing, audit 2026-05-28.)
- **⚠️ CRITICAL CAVEAT — the E-2 is NOT instant**: the **AMIGOS Act** (enacted **December 2022** as part of US Pub. L. 117-263, the FY23 NDAA) requires a person who acquired E-2 treaty nationality **through a Citizenship-by-Investment programme** (Grenada + Turkey explicitly named) to have **resided in the treaty country for at least 3 continuous years** before applying for the E-2 visa. → This **defeats the "buy Grenada CBI → instant US E-2" marketing pitch.** (VERIFIED — multiple US immigration-counsel + US Embassy summaries; statutory basis is the AMIGOS Act folded into FY23 NDAA, audit 2026-05-28.)
- **Practical sequence**: CBI grant → **genuine 3-year residence in Grenada** → then an E-2 application with a qualifying, active US business investment. It is **NOT a passive shortcut**. The E-2 itself is also a nonimmigrant treaty-investor visa (not a green card / not citizenship).

### Compliance / AML

- **CBI due diligence**: the IMA runs multi-tier background checks; source-of-funds documentation is mandatory.
- **Real-estate attorneys**: regulated; KYC + source-of-funds chain required for any purchase.
- **CRS + FATCA**: tax-residency self-certification at Grenadian bank-account opening.
- **OECS / ECCB AML framework** applies region-wide; beneficial-ownership disclosure for corporate-held real estate.

### Verification path (before any reservation)

1. **Confirm route**: CBI-approved project (ALHL waived) vs ordinary purchase (ALHL ≈ 10% + PTT 10% applies). This single decision drives the whole cost stack.
2. **Engage a Grenada-admitted attorney**: conveyancing must be prepared + executed by an attorney-at-law (no DIY conveyancing).
3. **Pull the title chain**: 30-year good-title search (post-2019 reform) via the Deeds & Land Registry / [eLandRegistry](https://elandregistry.gov.gd); check for "family land," adverse-possession / statutory-declaration claims, boundary issues.
4. **For non-CBI buyers**: budget the ALHL application (~3 months; police certificate, 2 character references, banker's reference) + the 10% purchaser PTT + legal fees + 15% VAT-on-fees.
5. **For CBI buyers**: confirm the property is a government-approved CBI project and understand the 5-year hold (and that any "guaranteed buy-back" is now prohibited).
6. **Structural diligence**: confirm OECS Building Code 2015 compliance / hurricane retrofit (see `--risks`); 3+ catastrophe-insurance quotes.

### Compute

1. **USD/m² = price ÷ built-area m²** (listings often quote **square feet** — convert: 1 m² = 10.764 sqft).
2. Compare to **≥ 3 active listings** on the same parish + community + waterfront status (community + sea-view drive price far more than parish average).
3. **Trap — tax base**: PTT (purchaser 10%) and ALHL (~10%) are both assessed on **market value** — an inflated listing inflates the acquisition tax stack. Commission an **independent valuation** (IRD valuation service exists) before relying on any listing price.
4. **Trap — area definitions**: built-up area vs gross (incl. covered patios/verandahs) vs lot area. Listings often quote the largest. Confirm with the survey plan.
5. **Trap — title-system status**: deeds-recording (not Torrens) — a clean 30-year chain matters more than a headline price; "family land" + boundary disputes can sink a deal.
6. **Trap — Beryl 2024 (Carriacou / Petite Martinique)**: any listing on the dependency islands should be checked for July-2024 hurricane damage + rebuild status.

### Key terms

- **Deed of Conveyance** — instrument transferring title (English-style)
- **Abstract of Title** — chain of conveyances at the Deeds & Land Registry (deeds-recording system)
- **Alien Landholding Licence (ALHL)** — non-citizen licence to hold land (~10% of value)
- **Property Transfer Tax (PTT)** — one-time transfer tax (purchaser 10% / vendor 15% non-citizen; citizen 5%)
- **Family land** — old undivided inherited holding (common Caribbean title complication)
- **CBI-approved project** — government-designated real-estate development qualifying for citizenship-by-investment (ALHL waived; 5-year hold)

## Section: `--tax`

> **Headline**: Grenada has **NO capital gains tax** and **NO inheritance/estate tax** generally; the real-estate-specific burden is concentrated **at acquisition** for foreign buyers. A non-citizen **purchaser pays Property Transfer Tax of 10%** (Act No. 37/1998), **separate from and on top of** the **~10% Alien Landholding Licence** — so a **non-CBI foreign buyer faces a ~22%+ acquisition stack** (10% PTT + ~10% ALHL + ~2% legal + 15% VAT-on-fees). **Annual property tax is low** by international standards (~0.2% land / 0.3% building, residential). The **CBI route waives the ALHL** — materially cheaper on entry for approved-project buyers.

### Property Transfer Tax (PTT) — one-time at transfer

Governed by the **Property Transfer Tax Act, No. 37 of 1998**. Administered by the [IRD](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-transfer-tax).

**Land transfers:**

| Party | Rate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Citizen vendor** | **5%** | market value |
| **Non-citizen VENDOR** | **15%** | consideration or market value (whichever higher) |
| **Non-citizen PURCHASER** | **10%** | consideration or market value (whichever higher) |

**Other (non-land) property — non-citizens**: vendor 10%, purchaser 10%.

- **CRITICAL for foreign buyers**: a non-citizen buying land pays a **10% PTT (purchaser side)** — this is SEPARATE from, and ON TOP OF, the ~10% ALHL fee. A non-CBI foreign buyer therefore faces roughly **~20% in government acquisition charges (10% PTT + ~10% ALHL)** plus ~2% legal + 15% VAT-on-fees ≈ **~22%+ of value** in transaction cost.
- **Thresholds (XCD)**: deed-of-gift tax applies only to value > **XCD 150,000**; other transfers > **XCD 20,000**.
- **Full exemptions**: Crown, statutory boards, charities, mortgage-related, trustee-to-trustee.

(Source: [IRD primary page](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-transfer-tax), retrieved 2026-05-28; statute Act No. 37/1998 — *re-verify current rates at IRD before advising; IRD pages are the authority*.)

### Annual property tax

Administered by the [IRD](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-tax).

| Class | Land | Building |
|---|---|---|
| **Residential** | **0.2%** | **0.3%** |
| **Commercial** | **0.5%** | **0.3%** |
| **Agricultural** | **0.0%** | **0.0%** |

- **Homestead exemption**: XCD 100,000 deducted from the building value of an owner-occupied property (one property only).
- **Assessed at market value** (location, land price, size, building condition).
- **Payment**: demand notices issued 1 Jan annually. **5% early-payment discount** if 50% paid by 31 Mar and remainder by 30 Jun.
- **Late penalty**: 20% fine after 29 Aug if unpaid; 1.5%/month interest thereafter.
- **Low by international standards** — e.g. a USD 270k CBI villa ≈ market value ~XCD 729k; at residential 0.3% building + 0.2% land the annual bill lands in the low-hundreds-USD/yr range — **COMPUTE per the actual land/building split; do not assert a flat figure**.
- Payable to the Government of Grenada at the IRD, Young Street, St. George's, or District Revenue Offices per parish.

(Source: [IRD Property Tax page](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-tax), retrieved 2026-05-28 — *verify current rates + thresholds at IRD before relying*.)

### Alien Landholding Licence fee

- **~10% of property value** (purchase price less value of contents/furniture). **Waived for CBI-approved real estate.** (See Foreign buyer eligibility + `--finance`/conveyancing.)

### VAT + other

- **VAT (15%)** applies to professional services associated with the transaction (legal, agency) — added to the fee. *Verify whether VAT applies to short-stay tourist-accommodation rental above the registration threshold at IRD — flagged for re-verify.*

### Capital gains / inheritance / wealth

- **NO capital gains tax** in Grenada (no separate CGT regime on property gains for individuals — *verify current treatment at IRD*).
- **NO general inheritance / estate tax** (deed-of-gift PTT applies above XCD 150,000).
- **NO wealth tax**.

⚠️ Verification: request the seller's last **property-tax receipt**; confirm current **PTT (purchaser 10% / vendor 15% non-citizen), ALHL rate, and annual property-tax bands** at [IRD](https://ird.gd) + attorney before contract.

## Section: `--work=<profession>`

### Reality

Grenada is a **small, tourism-and-education-anchored economy**; local employment for non-citizens requires a **work permit** (CBI citizenship removes this requirement). The property thesis is mostly **second-home / CBI-investor / SGU-linked / retirement** rather than local-salary-driven.

### Economy + employers

- **Tourism** (hotels, villas, dive operators, cruise calls at St. George's).
- **Education** — **St. George's University (SGU)** in True Blue / Lance aux Épines: a large US-oriented medical (+ veterinary) school, a **major economic anchor and the primary rental-demand driver** on the southern peninsula.
- **Agriculture** — nutmeg/mace ("Isle of Spice"), cocoa, other spices.
- **CBI inflows** — a significant fiscal + construction-sector driver.

### Visa routes for working

| Route | Eligibility | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Work Permit** | Employer-sponsored; non-citizen | Government of Grenada (Labour / Immigration) |
| **CBI citizenship** | USD 270k RE share / 350k unit / 235k NTF donation | [IMA Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/) — removes work-permit requirement |
| **CARICOM Skills Certificate** | CARICOM citizen + recognised skill | Free movement + work within CARICOM |

### Hurricane Ivan (7 September 2004) — the baseline catastrophe

- **Category 3 at landfall** — the single most defining event in Grenada's modern property history.
- **~89–90% of homes damaged; ~30% destroyed**; >14,000 homes impacted; **>80% of all building structures damaged**; 73 of 75 public schools damaged. 39 fatalities.
- **Damage ≈ USD 1.1 billion ≈ ~200% of GDP** (housing sector alone = 38% of GDP; tourism 29%). The economy contracted ~3% in 2004 vs +4.7% projected.
- **Implication**: Grenada sits at the **southern edge of the hurricane belt** — historically hit less frequently than the northern Antilles, but Ivan proved it is **NOT immune**, and older housing stock was catastrophically vulnerable. **Any pre-2005 building should be assumed to predate modern wind-resistant detailing unless retrofitted.**

(Source: World Bank "A Nation Rebuilding: Hurricane Ivan" report; ReliefWeb / OECS assessment — *authoritative secondary*.)

### Hurricane Beryl (1–2 July 2024) — the dependency islands

- **Beryl devastated Carriacou & Petite Martinique** (the dependency islands), with extensive housing destruction; **main-island Grenada was materially less affected**.
- **Diligence**: any listing on **Carriacou / Petite Martinique** must be checked for July-2024 damage + rebuild status — *verify Beryl specifics at [NaDMA](https://www.gov.gd/resident/nadma) / NOW Grenada* (flagged).

### Building code

- **OECS Building Code (2015 edition + updates)** governs hurricane + seismic design — roof hurricane straps, window/door wind-load design, reinforcement anchorage. Grenada is a participating OECS state.
- Grenada also runs a **Climate-Resilient Buildings** programme ([climateresilience.gov.gd](https://climateresilience.gov.gd/)) under ICCAS.
- **Diligence step**: confirm the property was built to / retrofitted to **OECS Building Code 2015** standards (roof straps, shutters, reinforced openings). **Pre-Ivan (pre-2005) stock is the red flag.**

### Insurance — catastrophe cover essential + costly

- **Catastrophe (hurricane/wind) insurance is essential and a material recurring cost**; premiums reflect the catastrophe exposure.
- Grenada participates in **CCRIF SPC** (Caribbean Catastrophe Risk Insurance Facility) at **sovereign** level — this is parametric sovereign cover, **NOT homeowner cover**.
- **Homeowner premium as % of value**: *data not available in a single public source — obtain 3+ quotes from a Grenada insurer and mark for local quote* (flagged for re-verify).

### Seismic + volcanic

- **Lesser Antilles volcanic arc** — earthquake risk present; **Kick-'em-Jenny** (an active submarine volcano ~8 km north of Grenada) is monitored by the [UWI SRC](https://www.uwiseismic.com/). Factor seismic detailing into structural diligence for pre-1980 builds.

## Section: `--climate`

Universal logic per `shared/climate-projections.md`. Grenada-specific overrides:

- **Köppen-Geiger**: **Af / Am (tropical rainforest / monsoon)** in the interior highlands → **Aw (tropical savanna)** on the drier coast — wet season Jun–Nov, drier season Dec–May.
- **Topography**: volcanic + mountainous interior (highest point **Mount St. Catherine ~840 m AMSL**) — materially higher relief than coral-platform islands; coastal property concentrated on the south + west coasts.
- **Reference baseline**: regional Caribbean met series + IPCC AR6 + Caribbean Community Climate Change Centre (CCCCC).
- **Key projections** (IPCC AR6 + CCCCC, SSP2-4.5 / SSP5-8.5):
  - Annual mean temperature **+1.0 → +2.5°C** by 2100 (Caribbean SIDS regional band).
  - **Hurricane intensity**: Atlantic-basin Cat 4–5 share trending up — **Ivan 2004 + Beryl 2024 as calibration events** for a southern-Windwards island previously considered "south of the main track."
  - **Sea-level rise**: +0.20 → +0.85 m by 2100 SSP5-8.5 — exposure for low-lying south-coast parcels + St. George's lagoon frontages.
  - **Rainfall**: drier-season drier; wet-season more intense convective events — flash-flood + landslide risk on steep interior slopes.
  - **Coral bleaching**: increasing — affects beach + reef-tourism economics.
- **Acute-event flags**:
  - 🔴 Hurricane Cat 3–5 risk (Ivan 2004 = the calibration catastrophe; pre-2005 stock structurally vulnerable)
  - 🟠 Landslide / flash-flood risk on steep interior slopes in intense rainfall
  - 🟠 Insurance market cost — catastrophe premiums material + escalating
  - 🟡 Sea-level rise — 2050+ exposure for sub-5 m coastal parcels
  - 🟡 Heat stress increasing — cooling (and thus GRENLEC) cost escalation

### Climate verdict (typical south-coast residential, post-2005 OECS-code build)

## Section: `--currency`

- **Currency**: **Eastern Caribbean Dollar (EC$ / XCD)** — issued by the [ECCB](https://www.eccb-centralbank.org/), shared by the 8-member OECS/ECCU.
- **Peg**: **XCD 2.70 = USD 1.00 since 1976** — a hard regional peg, one of the long-standing fixed pegs (since 1976); unbroken through 2026.
- **USD circulates in parallel**: tourism + real-estate transactions are commonly **USD-denominated or USD-equivalent**; CBI-eligible villas are quoted in USD by design.
- **Practical effect for buyer**: **near-zero structural FX risk for USD-base buyers**; minimal risk for EUR/GBP/CAD buyers (USD-correlated). The 1976 peg has held through multiple external shocks; ECCB pooled reserves underpin credibility — but, like any peg, it is a **policy choice, not a structural certainty**.
- **No exchange-control gating** equivalent to Barbados' CBB registration is documented for Grenada property purchases — *but confirm any current capital-flow / repatriation rule with your attorney + bank before completion* (the gating step in Grenada is the **ALHL**, not FX registration).

## Section: `--visa` (CBI deep-dive)

Grenada's residency/citizenship hook is its **Citizenship-by-Investment (CBI)** programme — and uniquely among Caribbean CBI states, the **US E-2 Treaty-Investor pathway** (with the AMIGOS 3-year-residence caveat). See **Foreign buyer eligibility** above for the buyer matrix.

### CBI routes (VERIFIED — effective 1 July 2024)

| Route | Threshold | Hold / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Real-estate qualifying share** | **USD 270,000** + **USD 50,000 government fee** | **5-year hold**; up from USD 220,000 (1 Jul 2024) |
| **Real-estate full unit** | **USD 350,000** | **5-year hold**; unchanged in 2024 reform |
| **NTF donation** | **USD 235,000** (single applicant) | Family bands differ — *verify at IMA* |
| **Enterprise route** | USD 1.5M single / USD 350k per investor in ≥ USD 5M JV | Less common |

- **Administered by**: [Investment Migration Agency (IMA) Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/citizenship-by-investment/). Statutory basis: Citizenship by Investment Act 2013 (as amended) + Citizenship by Investment (Amendment) Regulations 2024.
- **2024 OECS price-floor harmonisation**: gives effect to the 2024 OECS CBI Memorandum of Agreement (Antigua & Barbuda, Dominica, Grenada, St. Kitts & Nevis, St. Lucia).
- **Anti-undercutting clause**: **discounting, rebates, and guaranteed buy-back arrangements are PROHIBITED** across all CBI routes (treat any "guaranteed return / guaranteed buy-back" CBI offer as a red flag).
- **5-year hold**: the approved real-estate investment may be resold after 5 years (potentially to the next CBI applicant — who typically must re-qualify).

(Source: [IMA Grenada](https://imagrenada.gd/citizenship-by-investment/) + Circular No. 3 of 2024 + UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor measure #5026 — VERIFIED, audit 2026-05-28. **Do NOT cite the old USD 220,000 figure.** *Verify exact NTF family-size bands at IMA before relying.*)

### US E-2 Treaty-Investor visa — the marquee differentiator + its caveat

- **Treaty in force since 3 March 1989** (US Dept of State E-2 treaty-country list — VERIFIED, audit 2026-05-28). Grenada is the **ONLY Caribbean CBI state with E-2 access** (none of St. Kitts, Dominica, Antigua & Barbuda, St. Lucia have it).
- **⚠️ AMIGOS Act (Dec 2022, FY23 NDAA, Pub. L. 117-263)**: CBI-naturalised citizens (Grenada + Turkey named) must have **resided in the treaty country ≥ 3 continuous years** before an E-2 application. **The "instant E-2" pitch is false.** (VERIFIED, audit 2026-05-28.)
- **Practical sequence**: CBI grant → genuine 3-year Grenada residence → E-2 application with an active, qualifying US business investment. The E-2 is a **nonimmigrant** visa — not a green card, not US citizenship.

(Source: [US Dept of State — E-2 treaty list](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/treaty.html) + [State Dept Grenada reciprocity](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country/Grenada.html) + AMIGOS Act / FY23 NDAA — VERIFIED.)

### Conveyancing + Land Registry (non-CBI flow)

- **Court / registry**: title + deeds recorded at the **Deeds and Land Registry of the Supreme Court of Grenada** (administered by the Supreme Court Registry; the superior court is the resident **High Court** division of the ECSC). Governing statute: **Deeds and Land Registry Act, Cap. 79** ([Laws of Grenada — Chapter 79 Deeds and Land Registry Act](https://laws.gov.gd/index.php/chapters/d-76-84a/133-chapter-79-deeds-and-land-registry-act)) + Conveyancing and Law of Property Act.
- **Online access**: **[eLandRegistry](https://elandregistry.gov.gd)** (Grenada Land Agency).
- **2019 reform**: good-title search period reduced **60 → 30 years**; original title deeds held in the Supreme Court Registry.
- **Mandatory attorney**: deeds of sale/transfer must be **prepared + executed by an attorney-at-law** (no DIY conveyancing).
- **Legal fees**: per the **Grenada Bar Association Scale of Fees** — conveyances start ~**2% of price** (sliding down as price rises; minimum ~XCD 1,000; some firms quote 1–3.5%). **Plus 15% VAT on the legal fee.**
- **Title-system caveat**: Grenada is a **deeds-registration (NOT Torrens)** jurisdiction; title reform is "long overdue," and statutory-declaration / adverse-possession ("squatter") + old undivided **"family land"** claims exist. **Insist on a full 30-year registry search by the attorney.**

**Foreign-buyer transaction flow (non-CBI):**
1. Agree price → sign sale agreement (deposit ~10%, held by attorney).
2. Attorney applies for the **Alien Landholding Licence** (~10% of value; ~3 months — police certificate, 2 character references, banker's reference).
3. On ALHL grant: pay **PTT 10% (non-citizen purchaser)** + legal fees + 15% VAT-on-fees.
4. Deed executed by attorney + recorded at the Deeds & Land Registry.

- **CBI route bypasses step 2 (ALHL waived)** but the property must be a government-approved CBI project.
