# Maldives 🇲🇻 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `mv`. Status: ✅ fully populated as of 2026-05-07.

> **🇲🇻 LEAD WARNING — read before any other section.** The Maldives is a **constitutional non-freehold jurisdiction for foreigners**. Article 251 of the 2008 Constitution **prohibits foreign ownership of any part of the territory**; foreign parties may only **lease** for business purposes. The 2015 amendment that briefly opened freehold for projects with ≥USD 1 billion investment + ≥70% reclaimed land was **REPEALED on 23 April 2019** by the 19th People's Majlis. Since then, the **only legal pathway for non-Maldivian property exposure is leasehold** — and by statute, leases cannot exceed **99 years**. Practical foreign-investor routes are: (1) **Resort-island Head Lease** under the Tourism Act (Law 2/99, as amended) — typically 50-year, USD-denominated, awarded by the Ministry of Tourism; up to 99-yr extension on payment of an extension fee (the **USD 5M** figure was the 16th-Amendment *discounted-window* rate, which LAPSED 5 Jun 2026; the post-window default reverts higher, ~USD 10M reference — verify per deal); (2) **Tourist guesthouse on inhabited islands** — foreign nationals capped at **49% shareholding** in a Maldivian-majority partnership (2019/2024 guesthouse-regulation reforms); (3) **Resort sub-lease / villa-buy programmes** structured as long-term leasehold rights inside a head-lease resort (legally distinct from freehold). **There is no residential-market route for foreign nationals to own a flat in Malé or Hulhumalé as freehold.** Six- to seven-figure decisions in the Maldives also demand a Maldives-bar attorney + Ministry of Tourism + Capital Market Development Authority (CMDA) due diligence — non-negotiable. Sources: Constitutional repeal — `https://constitutionnet.org/news/maldivian-parliament-repeals-2015-constitutional-amendment-authorized-foreign-ownership-land`; CTL Strategies practitioner alert — `https://www.ctlstrategies.com/articles/constitution-amended-prohibiting-ownership-of-land-by-foreigners/`; Maldives Independent — `https://maldivesindependent.com/politics/foreign-freeholds-amendment-repealed-from-constitution-144895`. Tourism Act consolidation — `https://www.tourism.gov.mv/dms/document/23d485632704b30cf178db46d41f0bff.pdf`.

## Country profile

- **Land area**: ~298 km² distributed across **~1,192 coral islands** organised into 26 natural atolls (administratively split into 20 atoll-level constituencies + the capital Malé). The land area is **<1%** of the territorial sea; the EEZ is ~916,000 km². Average **natural elevation is ~1.5 m above mean sea level** — among the lowest of any nation on earth (per UNDP + national communications under UNFCCC).
- **Population**: **515,132** (Census 2022, Maldives Bureau of Statistics — `https://census.gov.mv/2022/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Population_Census-2022_Report-Updated-130324.pdf`). World Bank 2024 estimate ~527,799 — `https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?locations=MV`. **Greater Malé** (Malé + Hulhumalé + Villingili + Hulhulé) holds **211,908 — about 41% of the resident population** (Census 2022). The remaining ~59% live across ~187 inhabited islands — more than 1,000 islands are uninhabited, of which **~170+ are operated as resort-only islands** ("one-island-one-resort" historic model; 2009 Decentralisation Act + later reforms allow guesthouses on inhabited islands).
- **Capital**: **Malé** — one of the most densely populated cities in the world (population density >65,000/km² on Malé's ~1.96 km² island core; per MBS).
- **Currency**: **Maldivian Rufiyaa (MVR / Rf.)** — managed peg to USD. Since **April 2011**, the rufiyaa has been **fixed within a ±20% band around USD 1 = MVR 12.85**, i.e., bounded between **MVR 10.28 and MVR 15.42** (Maldives Monetary Authority — MMA). As of late 2025 / early 2026, market trades close to the **upper band ~MVR 15.4 / USD 1** (per multiple secondary sources tracking MMA spot). The MMA publishes daily indicative rates at `https://database.mma.gov.mv/viya/series/4039`. **Resort transactions are USD-denominated by Tourism Act practice**; Greater Malé residential market is MVR-denominated but agency marketing routinely quotes both. Banks intermediate FX subject to MMA capital-flow guidance. Sovereign FX-reserves stress + IMF Article-IV consultations (2024-2026) keep the peg under regulatory watch — verify MMA monetary policy statements before relying on band stability for any long-tenor lease economic model.
- **Languages**: **Dhivehi** (national language, Indo-Aryan, written in Thaana script) + **English** (working language of government, courts, banking, tourism). Legal-grade documents, Tourism Act, MIRA tax legislation are bilingual; **agency listings + lease contracts in Greater Malé/resort sector typically English**.
- **Legal system**: **Mixed Sharia + common-law / civil-law influences**. The 2008 Constitution (`https://www.majlis.gov.mv/`) establishes Sunni Islam as state religion (Article 9(d)) and requires **Maldivian citizens to be Muslim**. **Non-Muslim foreign nationals cannot acquire Maldivian citizenship** — only naturalisation pathway is Muslim-faith adherence + 12-year residence (Citizenship Act). **Sharia overlay on inheritance, family, and personal-status law** (Maliki school dominant historically); commercial + property-leasehold + tax law largely codified-civil with English drafting heritage.
- **Cadastre / land registry — fragmented and largely paper-based**:
  - **Ministry of Infrastructure, Housing and Urban Development** — `https://infrastructure.gov.mv/en` — primary land-allocation authority on inhabited islands; oversees Greater Malé housing schemes (renamed from the Ministry of Housing, Land and Urban Development; land registration, building permits and the Hiyaa / Gedhoruveriya / Binveriya schemes all sit here) *(2026-09-18 verified — authority identity and URL only)*
  - **Housing Development Corporation (HDC)** — `https://www.hdc.com.mv/` — manages Hulhumalé reclamation, allocation, and registration; the dominant urban-development entity
  - **Ministry of Tourism** — `https://www.tourism.gov.mv/` — **the registry-of-record for resort-island Head Leases** + tourist establishment registration
  - **Local Councils** (under the Decentralisation Act 2010, as amended) — administer plot allocation on inhabited atoll islands
  - **There is no unified national digital cadastre** akin to a Western land registry; title-equivalent records sit with HDC (Hulhumalé), MoH (other Malé), Tourism Ministry (resorts), or Local Councils. **Independent title searches are practitioner-mediated** (Maldives-bar attorneys, often via the Capital Market Development Authority — CMDA — for corporate vehicles).
- **Memberships**: UN, Commonwealth (rejoined 1 February 2020 after 2016 withdrawal), SAARC, OIC, IORA, AOSIS (founding member; SIDS climate-vulnerability advocacy). NOT in OECD; signed FATCA Model 1 IGA (2016); committed to OECD Common Reporting Standard (CRS) — first exchanges 2018+.
- **Ownership types**:
  - **Freehold (Maldivian citizens only)**: traditional "rah" / family-allocated plots on inhabited islands; State land grants under MoH housing schemes; Hulhumalé apartment freehold under HDC unit-title regimes
  - **Leasehold — citizens + corporates (incl. foreign-shareholder companies)**: maximum 99 years per Article 251(b); typical durations **25 / 35 / 50 yrs (Tourism Act track) up to 99 yrs (Land Act track)**
  - **Resort Head Lease**: a Tourism Act leasehold awarded by Ministry of Tourism + Cabinet; the **operating instrument for ~170+ resort islands** in the Maldives
  - **Resort sub-lease / branded-residence**: long-term leasehold rights to a specific villa/unit inside a head-lease resort (e.g., Soneva Jani, Patina, Velaa branded-residence schemes); **NOT freehold** — buyer obtains a leasehold interest tied to the head-lease balance
  - **Hulhumalé apartment unit**: freehold/leasehold sale to **Maldivians only** (HDC schemes); secondary market is thin and citizenship-restricted

> ⚠️ **Single biggest deal-killer for foreign buyers**: **the Constitution prohibits foreign land ownership; the only legal exposure is leasehold.** "Buying a Maldives villa" in branded-residence marketing means **buying a long-term leasehold right tied to a head-lease**, NOT a freehold deed. The leasehold collapses if the underlying head lease collapses. See `--tax` and "Foreign-buyer rules" below for the operational map.

### Compute

1. **USD or MVR per m²** = price ÷ **internal floor area** (resort-villa marketing varies — some quote net internal, others quote total covered area incl. deck/plunge-pool deck; verify with developer)
2. **Trap — leasehold tail**: a USD 8M Soneva Jani villa with **22 years left on the underlying head lease** is a different asset than the same USD 8M villa with **80 years left**; the residual term is the dominant valuation driver beyond a 30-yr horizon. Always pull the **head lease remaining term** from Ministry of Tourism's lease registry before committing.
3. **Trap — head lease vs sub-lease**: the buyer of a "villa" is acquiring a **sub-lease right** subordinate to the developer's head lease. If the head lessee (resort operator) goes into receivership or fails to perform under the head lease, the sub-lease may be impaired even if the buyer's payment was on time.
4. **Trap — currency**: list prices in USD by branded-residence developers are typical, but if the **legal lease contract is denominated in MVR**, currency-band drift (peg upper band MVR 15.42) is the cap — the buyer's effective USD outlay can move within ±20% of contract date. Resort head leases are predominantly USD-denominated by Tourism Act practice.
5. **Trap — local-island guesthouse 49% cap**: a foreign investor entering a guesthouse partnership cannot exceed **49% economic + voting interest**; the Maldivian majority partner has structural control. Shareholders' agreement quality matters more than equity percentage.
6. **Trap — Maldivian-only Hulhumalé HDC allocation**: foreign nationals **cannot buy Hulhumalé HDC apartments** in any structure — neither freehold nor leasehold. Anyone marketing "buy a Hulhumalé apartment as a foreigner" is mis-selling — verify with HDC + Maldives-bar attorney.

## Section: `--traffic`

### Primary sources

- **Ministry of Transport and Civil Aviation** — `https://www.transport.gov.mv/` (verify URL — government site reorganisation occasional)
- **Maldives Transport Authority** — vehicle registration + road licensing (sub-agency of MoTCA — verify URL)
- **Ministry of Construction and Infrastructure** — bridges, road networks (Sinamalé Bridge / Greater Malé Connectivity Project)
- **OSM `highway` class** as universal fallback — Greater Malé well-mapped; outer-atoll inhabited islands sparser

> ⚠️ **No public AADT / DTV / TMJA database exists** for Maldives roads. Almost all atoll islands have **no roads exceeding ~5 km** (single ring road or village lane); private cars are rare outside Greater Malé. Treat any traffic figure as `est.` with LOW data confidence.

### Key context

- **Road network — minimal at national scale**: most inhabited islands have informal sand/concrete lanes, bicycle/motorcycle/electric-cart dominant
- **Greater Malé road network**:
  - **Malé island**: dense urban grid, **~50,000+ registered motorcycles** + cars on a 1.96 km² island core — chronic congestion + parking saturation
  - **Sinamalé Bridge (China-Maldives Friendship Bridge)** — opened 30 August 2018, links Malé ↔ Hulhulé ↔ Hulhumalé (~2.0 km, 4-lane); **the country's only major bridge**; carries ~30,000+ vehicle movements/day per published MoT data
  - **Greater Malé Connectivity Project (Thilamalé Bridge)** — under construction (Afcons / EXIM India financing, signed 2021); will link **Malé ↔ Villingili ↔ Gulhifalhu ↔ Thilafushi**; targeted completion mid-to-late 2020s — verify status with MoTCA
- **Hulhumalé**: planned-grid road network under HDC; less congested than Malé but rising vehicle density Phase 1 → Phase 2 development
- **Resort islands**: **walk + electric buggy + bicycle dominant**; no private cars; "boat is the road"
- **Inter-atoll**: domestic flights (**Maldivian, Manta Air, FlyMe**) + speedboat / dhoni ferries; **seaplane network** (Trans Maldivian Airways, Manta Air seaplane) is a defining feature for resort access
- **Vehicle density nationally**: dominated by Greater Malé concentration; outer atolls + resorts effectively car-free

### Verdict bands (Maldives-adapted)

- 🟢 **Resort islands + most inhabited atoll islands**: zero through to minimal motorised traffic; bicycle/buggy/walk
- 🟡 **Hulhumalé Phase 1–2 + secondary inhabited-island ring roads**: light to moderate; school-hour peaks
- 🟠 **Sinamalé Bridge + Hulhumalé–Malé corridor + Hulhulé airport access**: peak congestion 7–9 am, 4–6 pm; near-bridge residential carries audible-traffic flag
- 🔴 **Malé island core + Majeedhee Magu / Boduthakurufaanu Magu / Chandhanee Magu peaks**: extreme congestion + noise; probably the densest motorised environment in South Asia by per-km² measure; relevant only to those buying the rare Malé-island residential

> Anyone considering a Greater Malé apartment should weight **noise + air-quality + flooding** jointly — Malé island combines all three, and the Sinamalé Bridge approach amplifies the first two.

## Section: `--tax`

### Annual property tax — NONE for residential individuals

- **No annual property tax / IMU / council-tax equivalent** is levied on residential property in the Maldives at present (2026). The fiscal model relies on tourism-sector taxes (TGST + Green Tax + bednight + lease-rent) + GGST + Income Tax rather than on residential property recurring tax.
- **Land Rent (Kuli)**: leaseholders pay annual lease rent to Government per the Tourism Act (resort islands) or under separate Land Act / housing-scheme arrangements (residential allocations). **Resort minimum rent: USD 8 / m² / year** statutory floor (Tourism Act + amendments). Per-resort actual rent is set in the head-lease contract.
- **Property tax reform under discussion** since 2023 — IMF Article IV recommendations + domestic fiscal-consolidation pressure may introduce a recurring residential property tax in coming budget cycles. Verify Ministry of Finance + IMF Country Reports — `https://www.imf.org/en/Countries/MDV`.

### Transaction taxes — Stamp Duty + Registration

- **Stamp Duty Act (Law No. 84/76)**: applies fixed/ad-valorem stamp on certain instruments — **rates are nominal** by international comparison (often <1%); historically not the primary transaction-cost driver. **Verify current schedule** with MIRA — `https://www.mira.gov.mv/Pages/View/stampduty` (URL pattern — verify) — or Maldives-bar attorney. Sale-deed equivalent for citizen-to-citizen residential transfer carries modest registration fees at HDC (Hulhumalé) or Local Council.
- **Lease registration**: lease deeds (citizen-to-citizen residential, or company head-lease at Tourism Ministry) are registered at the relevant authority; registration fees are administrative-flat (not ad-valorem at >1%).
- **Resort Head Lease — premium + annual rent + extension fee**:
  - **Lease premium** (one-time at award): commercial-confidential; ranges USD 5M–USD 50M+ per Cabinet bid
  - **Annual lease rent**: minimum USD 8 / m² (Tourism Act floor); typical USD 8 / m² – USD 12 / m² for established resorts
  - **Lease extension fee** (initial 50-yr → 99-yr extension): the **USD 5M** figure was the *discounted* rate available under the 16th-Amendment window (LAPSED 5 Jun 2026, below); the **post-window default reverts higher — ~USD 10M reference for a 49-yr extension** — verify the binding fee against the Ministry of Tourism implementing regulation per deal
  - **16th Amendment to Tourism Act (December 2025)** discounted-extension window (6 December 2025 → 5 June 2026) has **LAPSED on its scheduled 5 June 2026 end date** (closure **confirmed** against the Ministry of Tourism's own amendment register, which lists the Tourism Act amendments through the 16th and no 17th as of 2026-08-06 — `https://www.tourism.gov.mv/en/legislation/tourism_act`; no Ministry post-deadline outcome statement located. NB this was the 3rd serial re-opening of the discount, so a future renewal is possible). Post-window the higher default extension fee applies — verify current status with Ministry of Tourism + CTL Strategies practitioner alert (`https://www.ctlstrategies.com/latest/tourism-act-16th-amendment/`)
- **Mortgage stamp / pledge registration**: nominal; banks (Bank of Maldives, MIB, BML) handle internally

### Tourism-sector tax stack (the dominant fiscal regime)

The Maldives raises the majority of public revenue from tourism-specific taxes — relevant for any buyer modelling **rental income from a resort sub-lease, branded residence, or tourist guesthouse**:

#### TGST — Tourism Goods and Services Tax

- **Rate**: **17% effective 1 July 2025** (raised from 16% by the **Seventh Amendment to the GST Act**)
- **Applies to**: all tourist establishments (resorts, hotels, tourist hotels, tourist guesthouses, tourist vessels, tourism-sector retail/services)
- **Source**: MIRA circular `https://www.mira.gov.mv/ContentItems/View/circular-issued-changes-to-tgst-rate` + Crowe Maldives `https://www.crowe.com/mv/insights/tgst-rate-to-increase-from-1-july-2025` + Travel Trade Maldives — verify current rate at `https://www.mira.gov.mv/Pages/View/gst`
- **Time-of-supply**: earlier of invoice issue OR payment received

#### Green Tax (per bednight)

- **Rates effective 1 January 2025** (doubled from prior schedule):
  - **USD 12 / day** for **resorts, integrated resorts, tourist hotels, hotels, tourist vessels** (any size)
  - **USD 6 / day** for **tourist guesthouses on inhabited islands with ≤50 registered rooms** (small-island reduced rate)
- **Children under 2** at check-in: exempt from 1 January 2025
- **Currency**: payable to MIRA in **USD**
- **Source**: MIRA — `https://www.mira.gov.mv/ContentItems/View/circular-re-changes-to-grt-rates-on-20250101-news` + `https://mira.gov.mv/Pages/View/FAQ_GreenTax`

#### Departure Tax / Airport Service Charge (passenger-borne, but built into operator pricing)

- Rates vary by class of travel (economy / business / first / private jet) — **USD 12 economy → USD 120 private jet** band per Civil Aviation regulations + Travel Trade Maldives reporting (verify current schedule with MIRA + Maldives Civil Aviation Authority)

#### General GST (non-tourism — domestic sector)

- **Rate**: **8%** standard rate (raised from 6% to 8% effective 1 January 2023; held at 8% as of 2026 — verify current rate at MIRA)

### Income Tax — individual + corporate

- **Income Tax Act (Law No. 25/2019)** — commenced **1 January 2020**, replacing the prior Business Profit Tax (BPT)
- **Personal Income Tax — progressive bracket** (annual remuneration income, MVR):
  - 0 – 720,000: **0%**
  - 720,001 – 1,200,000: **5.5%**
  - 1,200,001 – 1,800,000: **8%**
  - 1,800,001 – 2,400,000: **12%**
  - >2,400,000: **15%**
  - Source: MIRA — `https://www.mira.gov.mv/Pages/View/ictindividuals` (verify current bracket structure); Lloyds Bank Trade portal — `https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/maldives/taxes`
- **Corporate Income Tax** (incl. profits from leasing/letting):
  - 0 – MVR 500,000 profit: **0%**
  - >MVR 500,000: **15%**
  - **Banks**: **25%**
- **Non-resident** (incl. foreign-shareholder companies, foreign individual landlord receiving Maldives-source rent): **withholding tax 10%** on certain Maldives-source income flows (royalties, technical service fees, rent of immovable/movable property, interest, certain dividends — verify per category at MIRA)
- **Worldwide vs territorial**: residents taxed on worldwide income; non-residents/temporary residents taxed on Maldives-source income only — `https://www.mira.gov.mv/Pages/View/FAQ_IncomeTax`

### Capital Gains + Wealth Tax

- **Capital Gains Tax**: there is **no separate CGT**; gains on disposal of immovable property held by a business are caught under **Income Tax** (15% corporate / progressive personal). Non-resident sellers face withholding regime — verify per transaction.
- **Annual wealth tax**: NONE
- **Inheritance tax**: NONE in statute; **Sharia-based forced heirship** governs distribution of an estate (Maliki school) — succession outcomes substantively differ from civil-law / common-law jurisdictions; engage Sharia-knowledgeable counsel for succession planning involving Maldives assets

### Total transaction cost (foreigner-side, est. — branded-residence track)

| Component | Rate (est.) |
|---|---:|
| Sub-lease premium (one-time, to developer) | per developer schedule (commercial) |
| Stamp duty + registration | nominal (<1%) |
| Legal / attorney fees | ~1–2% of price (Maldives bar) |
| Bank transfer + escrow / FX | ~0.5–1% (depending on bank, channel) |
| **Foreign-buyer typical all-in transaction cost** | **~2–3% of price** (excl. dev-side premium) |
| **Resort Head Lease (corporate buyer, full-island)** | Cabinet bid + extension fee (USD 5M was the lapsed 16th-Amdt discounted rate; default reverts higher) + USD 8/m²/yr rent + Stamp Duty schedule |
| **Tourist guesthouse 49% foreign partnership** | Local Council allocation + Tourism Ministry registration + nominal SD + ~1–2% legal |

> The Maldives' **transaction-cost burden on the buyer side is materially LOWER than Seychelles' 18–19% standard track or Indonesia's 5–10%** — but **the absence of freehold for foreigners is the offsetting structural cost**. The economic exposure is concentrated in the **lease tail + tourism-tax stack on the income side** (17% TGST + USD 12 Green Tax + 15% corporate / WHT 10% on rent), not in the acquisition.

### Future risk

- **Property-tax introduction**: IMF Article IV consultations 2024-2026 have flagged residential property tax as a fiscal-consolidation lever; not yet legislated — verify Ministry of Finance budget books
- **TGST trajectory**: raised 16% → 17% effective 1 July 2025 (Seventh Amendment); further rises plausible given fiscal pressure
- **Green Tax trajectory**: doubled effective 1 January 2025 (USD 6 → USD 12 / USD 3 → USD 6 small); upward drift consistent with climate-finance + revenue strategy
- **Lease-extension window**: 16th Amendment discounted-extension window **6 Dec 2025 → 5 Jun 2026 has LAPSED** (closure confirmed against the Ministry's amendment register as of 2026-08-06 — no 17th Amendment; post-window the higher default extension fee applies — verify with the Ministry implementing regulation)
- **MVR peg pressure**: MMA peg upper band MVR 15.42 / USD has been binding since 2024; sovereign-FX-reserves stress + IMF consultations may drive a band recalibration during typical mortgage / lease horizons (10-30 yrs)
- **OECD Pillar Two minimum tax**: Maldives is a small CIT-15% jurisdiction with low-tax exposures via Special Economic Zones — Pillar Two top-up rules may evolve; verify with CMDA + MIRA

> ⚠️ Verification: request seller's last **MIRA tax-clearance certificate** (resort/guesthouse sellers) + **head-lease document + extension status** (resort sub-lease buyers) + Local Council allocation document (inhabited-island plot) + HDC unit-title document (Hulhumalé) before deposit.

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term residential

- **Greater Malé rental market**: thin supply of foreign-tradable units; most foreigners on Employment Approval / business-visitor visas rent serviced apartments via short-let-style operators or company housing
- **Hulhumalé**: rental market driven by **inward migration from outer atolls** seeking work in Malé/airport/Hulhumalé construction; Maldivian-tenant-dominated; "downward pressure on Greater Malé rents from Phase 2 housing supply" per Ministry of Finance + property.mv commentary
- **Tenant protection**: Civil Court resolves disputes; lease agreements are typically 1-yr with annual renewal; deposit 1–2 months
- **Long-let market for non-residents**: limited — most expatriate workers on short renewal cycles

### Short-let — TIGHTLY REGULATED

- **All tourist-accommodation operations require Ministry of Tourism registration** + Tourism Establishment Licence

### Specific risks

#### 🔴 Sea-level rise — EXISTENTIAL

- **Average natural elevation ~1.5 m above MSL**; the **highest natural point** in the entire country is ~5.1 m on Vilingili (Addu Atoll) — among the lowest of any nation
- **IPCC AR6 (2022)** sea-level rise central estimates relative to 1995-2014 baseline:
  - **2050**: SSP1-1.9 (very low) **+0.15–0.23 m**; SSP5-8.5 (very high) **+0.20–0.29 m**
  - **2100**: SSP1-1.9 **+0.28–0.55 m**; SSP5-8.5 **+0.63–1.01 m**
  - High-impact tail **up to ~2.0 m by 2100** under low-confidence scenarios with marine ice-cliff instability (NASA / IPCC AR6)
- **Practical implication**: a **2050 +0.25 m SLR + storm-surge stacking** materially threatens any coastal property < 2 m elevation — this includes **most non-Hulhumalé inhabited islands** + **most resort beach villas + overwater bungalows** + **the entire Malé island below the historic seawall crown**
- **Hulhumalé is reclaimed at higher elevation** (~2.0 m above MSL design, with seawall protection) — a deliberate climate-adaptation strategy under the Phase 1 (188 ha) + Phase 2 (244 ha) reclamation; Greater Malé Connectivity Project + Gulhifalhu reclamation extend the urban platform footprint
- **National adaptation policy**: Maldives a leading AOSIS / SIDS climate-vulnerable advocate; Voluntary National Determined Contribution + Strategic Action Plan emphasise **island consolidation** (population concentration on resilient islands like Hulhumalé) + **reclamation** + **seawall upgrades**

#### 🔴 Coral reef bleaching + reef ecosystem collapse

- **Reefs attenuate wave energy** — healthy fringing reefs reduce wave run-up by 70-90% (academic + UNESCO modelling); **reef collapse → direct shoreline erosion + storm-surge amplification**
- **Mass bleaching event chronology**:
  - **1998** (first global event): catastrophic Maldives bleaching; some atolls saw >90% live-coral mortality
  - **2016** (third global event): another major event; partial recovery between
  - **2023–2024** (fourth global event, NOAA confirmed 15 April 2024): Maldives DHW (Degree Heating Weeks) sustained >8 °C-weeks — the critical mortality threshold; 2024 outcomes documented in Springer Coral Reefs journal and Reefscapers field reports
- **NOAA Coral Reef Watch** real-time monitoring — `https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/product/vs/gauges/maldives.php`
- **Sources**: Springer 2025/26 paper — `https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00338-026-02850-x`; Save Maldives — `https://savemaldives.net/coral-bleaching-alert/`; NOAA — `https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-confirms-4th-global-coral-bleaching-event`

#### 🟠 Storm surge + extreme rainfall

- **Maldives sits south of the main Indian Ocean cyclone belt** — direct cyclone hits historically rare
- **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami (Sumatra-Andaman 9.1-9.3 Mw, 26 December 2004)**: tsunami waves **2-4 m** swept across most atolls; **~108 dead, ~12,000 displaced, ~USD 470M damage** (UNDP + government estimates); **20+ inhabited islands rendered temporarily uninhabitable**; entire Maldives seawall + warning system overhauled post-event
- **Tropical storms / depressions** (Indian Ocean Northeast monsoon, December–February): can deliver damaging swell + flooding even without direct cyclone hit; "swell waves" (Indian Ocean basin teleconnections) episodically inundate low-lying atolls
- **Future projection**: World Bank CCKP — increased extreme-rainfall variability + sea-state variability; cyclone-frequency change low-confidence in region

#### 🟡 Seismic — moderate baseline

- Maldives sits on the **stable Indian Plate** interior; local seismicity is low — no significant historical destructive intra-Maldives earthquake
- **Far-field tsunami risk** (Sumatra-Andaman, Makran, Carlsberg ridge sources) is real — 2004 demonstrated this

#### 🟡 Saltwater intrusion + freshwater scarcity

- Most inhabited islands rely on **rainwater harvesting + desalination** (RO plants) for potable water; **groundwater lenses are thin and vulnerable to saltwater intrusion**
- **2014 Malé water crisis** (MWSC desalination plant fire, 4-7 December 2014): Malé entirely without potable water for ~7 days; international airlift (India, China, US) supplied bottled water — illustrative of single-source desalination dependence
- Climate-change exacerbation: SLR + storm-surge contaminate freshwater lenses; investment in RO + storage redundancy is essential

#### 🟡 Fire — building density risk

- **Malé island** building density (>65,000/km²) creates a fire-spread risk in older mixed-construction zones; **fire-fighting capacity** historically constrained on outer islands
- **Resort fires** rare but high-consequence given remote-island response time

## Foreign-buyer rules — the deal-killer section

### Article 251 of the Constitution — the primary regulatory gate

- **Statute**: 2008 Constitution of the Maldives, Article 251(a): "No foreign party shall own any part of the territory of the Republic of Maldives." Article 251(b): foreign parties may not be granted leasehold exceeding 99 years.
- **2015 amendment (open freehold for ≥USD 1B project + ≥70% reclamation)**: enacted 22 July 2015 → **REPEALED 23 April 2019** by the 19th Majlis
- **Current regime (post-2019 repeal)**: only leasehold, max 99 years
- **Approving body**: Ministry of Tourism (resort head leases), Ministry of Housing (inhabited-island), Ministry of Economic Development (FIA for non-tourism)

### Routes available to foreign nationals

| Route | Mechanism | Tenor |
|---|---|---|
| **Resort island Head Lease** | Cabinet-approved lease via Ministry of Tourism; competitive bid + USD lease premium | 25 / 35 / 50 / up to 99 yrs |
| **Resort sub-lease / branded-residence** | Long-term leasehold inside developer's head lease | residual head-lease term |
| **Tourist guesthouse on inhabited island** | Maldivian-majority partnership (≤49% foreign) | per Local Council allocation |
| **Commercial / industrial lease** | Ministry of Economic Development + Local Council | up to 99 yrs |
| **Office / business premises lease** | for foreign-investor company FIA | up to 99 yrs |
| **Inheritance of leasehold** | possible — but no freehold transfer to foreigner | residual lease |

### What is NOT available to foreigners

- ❌ **Freehold of any kind** (constitutionally barred)
- ❌ **Hulhumalé / Malé / outer-island residential apartment freehold** (Maldivian-only)
- ❌ **State-allocated housing-scheme units** (Maldivian-only)
- ❌ **Agricultural / fishery plot ownership**

### Practical implication

For most international buyers, the actionable foreign-buyer routes are:

1. **Resort branded-residence sub-lease (Soneva, Patina, Velaa, Six Senses Laamu, Anantara, etc.)** — long-term leasehold, USD-denominated, marquee-brand operating standards, ~USD 4M–USD 20M+ entry; **the dominant retail-foreign-buyer route**
2. **Resort apartment / overwater suite leasehold pack** — smaller leasehold packages (~USD 1.5M–USD 5M) inside branded-residence schemes
3. **Tourist guesthouse on Maafushi / Thulusdhoo / Ukulhas / Dhigurah / Mathiveri / Fulidhoo / etc.** — Maldivian-majority partnership; ~USD 200k–USD 2M; lower-touch hospitality investment
4. **Full-island Head Lease** — institutional / family-office scale (USD 5M–USD 50M+ premium + USD 8/m² rent floor); requires Cabinet approval

> **Verification**: any serious enquiry should start with a **conditional opinion from a Maldives-bar attorney + tax advisor (e.g., S&A Lawyers, CTL Strategies, Crowe Maldives, Nasheed & Co)** — the regulatory environment evolves on Tourism Act amendments + Cabinet decisions, and post-2019 freehold-repeal precedent is now mature but enforcement specifics differ at island level.

### Visa pathways — GMI / Investor Visa (launched 1 July 2025)

- **Maldives Investor Visa (GMI)** — launched **1 July 2025** by Government of Maldives in partnership with Henley & Partners under President Muizzu's Vision 2040.
  - **Investment threshold**: **USD 250,000 minimum** into government-approved real-estate projects (luxury residential / resort developments). Qualifying investment is into a **leasehold interest** in approved developments — Article 251 leasehold-only regime preserved (NOT freehold).
  - **Visa term**: **5 years renewable**.
  - **Family inclusion**: spouse, children, parents, grandparents.
  - **Residency requirement**: NONE (no physical presence).

## Caveats unique to MV

- **Article 251 freehold ban** is constitutionally entrenched — the 2015 opening was reversed in 2019; expect stability in this regime barring extraordinary majority action; foreign investment must structure around leasehold permanently
- **Resort head lease residual term** is the dominant valuation driver beyond a 30-year horizon — buyers of branded-residence sub-leases must scrutinise the underlying head-lease balance + extension status
- **16th Tourism Act Amendment (December 2025)** discounted lease-extension window 6 Dec 2025 → 5 Jun 2026 has **LAPSED** on its scheduled end date (closure inferred as of 14 Jun 2026) — the optionality window for existing head-lessees has closed; post-window the higher default extension fee applies
- **No public sale-price API + thin transaction volume** — comp data is agency-anecdotal; commission a Ministry of Tourism / HDC / Local Council search per parcel
- **Greater Malé residential market is Maldivian-only** for ownership purposes — foreign exposure is leasehold-via-corporate or rent-only
- **MVR managed peg upper band MVR 15.42** — the contractual currency for any MVR-denominated residential lease creates ±20% FX exposure for non-MVR buyers; resort head leases USD-denominated typically
- **No CGT** as standalone tax; gains caught under Income Tax (15% corporate/personal top bracket)
- **Tourism-tax stack** (17% TGST + USD 12/6 Green Tax) is the dominant rental-income drag; budget gross-to-net materially
- **Climate exposure is existential** — average natural elevation ~1.5 m; any 21st-century horizon model must overlay IPCC AR6 SLR scenarios + storm-surge stacking + reef-bleaching teleconnection
- **Sharia-based forced heirship** governs estate distribution for assets in the Maldives — succession outcomes can differ materially from civil-law/common-law jurisdictions; engage Sharia-knowledgeable counsel
- **Citizenship is faith-conditional** — naturalisation requires Sunni Muslim adherence; not a route for non-Muslim foreign investors
- **Bank lending to non-residents**: limited; est. LTV ~40–60% for non-resident foreign buyers via Bank of Maldives + MIB + BML; est. - **No civil-law notary monopoly on conveyancing** — Maldives-bar attorneys handle conveyancing-equivalent work, but **public notaries are a distinct licensed category**: the **Bar Council of the Maldives** (statutory regulator under the **Legal Profession Act, Law No. 5/2019**, in force 27 June 2019) maintains a **Register of Public Notaries** alongside its Membership Roll, Register of Law Firms and Register of Legal Practices — `https://maldivesbarcouncil.org/` (2026-09-18 verified).
