# Cambodia 🇰🇭 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `kh`. Status: ✅ Fully populated (researched 2026-05).

> **🇰🇭 LEAD WARNING — read before any other section.** Cambodia is a **constitutionally land-restricted** market. Under **Article 44 of the 1993 Constitution**, only Khmer (Cambodian) natural and legal persons may hold ownership of LAND. Foreigners cannot hold freehold land in Cambodia under any structure. The two clean pathways are: (1) **Strata-titled co-owned units (the "strata law" — Sub-Decree No. 126 ANK.BK of 12 Aug 2009 + Law on Providing Foreigners with Ownership Rights in Private Units of Co-Owned Buildings (May 2010)**, "Foreign Ownership Law"), which lets foreigners own up to **70 % of any single co-owned building's private units** but **not the ground floor / 1st floor and below** (no land contact); and (2) **long-term lease** registered with the cadastre (typically marketed as 50-year leases renewable for another 50 years — only the registered term is enforceable as a real right). Other structures (Cambodian-spouse nominee, Cambodian-majority company, "trust" arrangements) carry varying enforceability risk and are NOT clean ownership. Cambodian title comes in three flavors — **Hard Title (national-registry), Soft Title (commune/district-only), and Strata Title (co-owned-unit)** — and many properties marketed to foreigners are on Soft Title which carries materially weaker provenance. Six- to seven-figure decisions in Cambodia demand a Cambodian-licensed lawyer who can run an MLMUPC cadastral search — non-negotiable. Skip the lawyer-fee line and you can lose the entire principal.

## Country profile

- **Population**: ~17.4M (2024 NIS estimate; 2019 General Population Census 15.55M, source NIS `https://www.nis.gov.kh/`). Population is younger than ASEAN average (median age ~27).
- **Postcode (postal code)**: 5-digit codes maintained by Cambodia Post (under the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications). Phnom Penh is `12xxx` (capital city, 14 districts (khans) including Daun Penh `12206`, Chamkar Mon `12300`, 7 Makara, BKK1, etc.). Sihanoukville (Preah Sihanouk province) is `18xxx`. Siem Reap is `17xxx`. Battambang `02xxx`. Source: Cambodia Post `https://cambodiapost.com.kh`.
- **Admin levels**: 25 provinces (ខេត្ត / khaet) + Phnom Penh capital (រាជធានី / reach thani) → districts (ស្រុក / srok) and city sections (ក្រុង / krong) and capital quarters (ខណ្ឌ / khan; Phnom Penh has 14) → communes (ឃុំ / khum) and sangkats (សង្កាត់ / urban quarters) → villages (ភូមិ / phum). Reference: Ministry of Interior `https://www.interior.gov.kh/`. The 2014 Sub-Decree on Decentralization re-classified some districts; counts shift periodically — verify against the official PSC (provincial-statistical-classification).
- **Currency** (dual system): KHR (Cambodian riel, ៛) AND USD circulate side-by-side. **USD dominates real-estate transactions, large-ticket commerce, hotel/restaurant pricing, and rental contracts (~80–90 % of urban transactions, per NBC monitoring)**. KHR is used for utilities, small purchases, taxes paid to the General Department of Taxation (which moved to riel-only assessment in many areas), and some payroll. **Reference rate** April 2026: 1 USD ≈ 4,050–4,150 KHR; the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC, Athnaki Cheati / ធនាគារជាតិ) defends a soft de facto peg via FX intervention. NBC daily rate: `https://www.nbc.gov.kh/english/economic_research/exchange_rate.php`. The de-dollarization policy is a long-term NBC objective (Project Bakong digital riel launched Oct 2020 to encourage KHR use); short-term, USD is unavoidable for real-estate.
- **Languages**: Khmer (ភាសាខ្មែរ, official, sole national language under Article 5 Constitution). Khmer-script title and notarial documents are standard; English is widely used in expat-targeted developer brochures, premium law firms, and condo project marketing — **but every cadastral document and notarial deed of registered title is in Khmer** and requires sworn translation for foreign buyers. French is residual among older administrators (legal vocabulary partly Civil-Law-French-derived).
- **Cadastre / land registry**: **Ministry of Land Management, Urban Planning and Construction (MLMUPC, ក្រសួងរៀបចំដែនដី នគរូបនីយកម្ម និងសំណង់)** — `https://www.mlmupc.gov.kh/`. Operates the **General Department of Cadastre and Geography (GDCG)** which runs the National Land Title Registration system. Provincial / municipal cadastral offices (សុរិយោដី / soriyodi) execute parcel transfers in person. There is **no nationwide free online parcel viewer** as of 2026-05; some commune-level data is online via the ODC (Open Development Cambodia, NGO-aggregated `https://opendevelopmentcambodia.net/`) but title verification must be done in person at the provincial cadastre.

- **Major reforms / regulatory landscape** (date-stamped — verify via `shared/regulatory-watch.md`):
  - **Land Law 2001 (Law on Land, RKM/0801/14, promulgated 30 Aug 2001)** — the foundational property statute. Source: Royal Government Council of Jurists / Cambodian Constitutional Council `https://www.ccc.gov.kh/`. Establishes the categories of land (state public, state private, private), the cadastral framework, and the foreign-ownership prohibition (Article 8: only natural and legal persons of Khmer nationality may own land).
  - **Sub-Decree No. 126 ANK.BK on Co-Ownership of Buildings (12 Aug 2009)** — created the legal concept of co-owned (strata) buildings and registered private units. Issued by the Royal Government.
  - **Law on Providing Foreigners with Ownership Rights in Private Units of Co-Owned Buildings (NS/RKM/0510/015, May 2010, "Foreign Ownership Law")** — opened strata-titled units in co-owned buildings to foreign ownership, capped at 70 % of total private-unit area per single building, excluding the ground floor / 1st floor and below. Source: Royal Gazette / National Assembly.
  - **Investment Law (LCI) 2021 (NS/RKM/1021/014, promulgated 15 Oct 2021)** — replaced the 1994 Law on Investment of the Kingdom of Cambodia; administered by the Council for Development of Cambodia (CDC). Real-estate development can qualify as a Qualified Investment Project (QIP) in some sectors but residential foreign ownership pathway is governed by Land Law / Foreign Ownership Law, not LCI.
  - **Law on Taxation 2023 (NS/RKM/0523/004, May 2023)** — consolidated and updated the prior 1997 Law on Taxation; affects Property Transfer Tax (Stamp Duty), Tax on Immovable Property (TOIP), Tax on Unused Land (TROD), rental income tax, and capital gains. Administered by the General Department of Taxation (GDT) `https://www.tax.gov.kh/`.
  - **Tax on Capital Gains** — **Prakas 346 MEF.PrK (1 Apr 2020) is ABROGATED on the GDT register**, replaced by **Prakas No. 1130 MEF.Prk.GDT dated 31 December 2025 (status: Valid)**. The regime is now FIXED, not pending: **Article 18(2) applies 20 % CGT to gains on the sale or transfer of IMMOVABLE PROPERTY arising from 1 January 2027 onward**, after repeated deferrals since 2020. **Article 18(1) is already live from 1 January 2026** for leases, investment assets, goodwill, IP and foreign currency. **Article 18(3) leaves INDIRECT capital transfers to separate regulation** — the status of that separate regulation is not established here; verify at GDT before structuring an indirect exit. Immovable-property gain is computed either by an **80 % standard deduction on proceeds** (⇒ 20 % × the remaining 20 % of proceeds = effective 4 % of proceeds) **OR** by actual costs. Law on Taxation 2023 (NS/RKM/0523/004) basis unchanged. (2025 Prakas; 2026-08-07 verified, source GDT `https://www.tax.gov.kh/` + `https://www.tax.gov.kh/u6rhf7ogbi6/gdtstream/db67bc15-3ee3-40ca-9ae4-4ca5caaca0d4`.)
  - **Tax on Immovable Property (TOIP)** — annual 0.1 % tax on immovable property valued > 100M KHR (~$25k); **does not apply to most agricultural land or to immovable property below the threshold**. Tax base is the official assessed value set by the Property Assessment Committee (committee is ad hoc; many properties have outdated assessments).
  - **Tax on Unused Land (TROD)** — annual 2 % on the market value of unused/undeveloped urban land — applies to vacant urban parcels above a size threshold. Re-energized 2018 to discourage land speculation, but enforcement is patchy.
  - **Proposed but NOT enacted as of 2026-05**: a comprehensive Real Estate Business Law (multiple drafts since 2018; a 2018 Sub-Decree on the Management of Real Estate Business and Pawnshop Business under MEF was a partial step). Verify cabinet status at Royal Government website.
  - **Sihanoukville (Preah Sihanouk Province) regulatory cycle**: **2019 online-gambling ban (sub-decree of 18 Aug 2019)** abruptly shut down ~$3.5B / yr Chinese-online-gambling sector that had driven Sihanoukville's 2017–2019 construction boom. Combined with COVID border closures (2020–2022), the city saw a wave of stranded / unfinished construction (estimated 300+ shell buildings as of 2022 according to municipal authorities and World Bank reporting). Partial recovery 2023–2025; foreign-buyer caution remains warranted on Sihanoukville off-plan transactions.

## 🇰🇭 LEAD SECTION: Foreign buyer eligibility

### Bottom line

Foreigners **cannot own land in Cambodia in their own name** under any direct structure. **Article 44 of the 1993 Constitution** reserves land ownership to "natural persons or legal entities of Khmer nationality." The 2001 Land Law (Article 8) restates this. The clean foreign-ownership paths are narrow:

1. **Strata-titled private unit in a co-owned building** under the May 2010 Foreign Ownership Law — capped at 70 % of total private-unit floor area per single building, **excluding ground floor / 1st floor and below**. THIS IS THE ONLY DIRECT FREEHOLD-EQUIVALENT FOR FOREIGNERS.
2. **Long-term lease registered with the MLMUPC cadastre** — typically marketed as 50-year + 50-year-renewal; only the registered term is enforceable as a real right (encumbrance). Renewal is contractual.
3. **Cambodian-spouse ownership** — the Cambodian spouse owns the land in their sole name; foreign spouse has no direct claim. Marital property law (Civil Code 2007) protects equitable interests but not against the constitutional prohibition.
4. **Cambodian-majority company structure (51 %+ Cambodian-held)** — a Cambodian limited liability company with majority Cambodian shareholders may hold land freehold. **Legal as a structure, but the foreign funder's side-protection is void by statute**: Land Law 2001 Art. 9 counts only the percentages stipulated in the articles of incorporation, and "Any private agreement signed by a shareholder that is contrary to this article is null and void" — so the side-agreement / share-pledge / power-of-attorney bundle offers no enforceable protection. No published MLMUPC / GDCG / GDT campaign against such companies was located (2026 data, source Land Law 2001 via asset.cambodia.gov.kh).
5. **"Trust" arrangements via the Trust Law 2019** — Cambodia's Trust Law (NS/RKM/0119/004, Jan 2019) created formal trusts under the Trust Regulator (under MEF). Some practitioners structure foreign-held property via a Cambodian trustee; legal enforceability for a foreigner-beneficiary is **untested at the constitutional level**, and the Trust Law does not override Article 44. Use with caution.

**Cannot**: own land freehold in own name, own a "house and lot" with the lot as freehold, own ground-floor (1st-floor) units in co-owned buildings, own undivided agricultural / rural land, own through purely fronted Cambodian-shareholder structures (anti-nominee risk).

### Path 1: Strata-titled unit (✅ recommended for most foreign buyers)

- **Statute**: **Law on Providing Foreigners with Ownership Rights in Private Units of Co-Owned Buildings (May 2010)**, building on Sub-Decree 126/2009 on Co-Ownership of Buildings.
- **70 % cap**: foreigners may collectively own up to **70 % of the total private-unit floor area of any single co-owned building**. The cap is per building, not per project (multi-building projects each have their own 70 % cap). Source: Article 4 Foreign Ownership Law 2010.
- **Ground-floor / first-floor exclusion**: foreigners may NOT own private units that are **on the ground floor or below the ground floor (basement)**, because these have direct land-contact and would imply land ownership. The 2nd floor and above is foreign-eligible (subject to the 70 % cap). Source: Article 4 Foreign Ownership Law 2010 + practitioner consensus per major Phnom Penh law firms (DFDL, Tilleke, BNG Legal — verify current published guides).
- **Border / boundary exclusion**: foreigners may NOT own units in a co-owned building **within 30 km of any land border** (with some exceptions for designated economic zones / SEZs / Special Provincial Areas). Source: Article 4 Foreign Ownership Law 2010 + implementing Prakas. This excludes most of the immediate Thai-border, Vietnam-border, and Lao-border regions for foreign-condo purchases.
- **Quota verification before deposit**: ask the developer (or the building's co-ownership association / បរិវេណរួម / "borivenh ruom") for a written **Foreign-Quota Confirmation** stating the current foreign-owned floor-area % and confirming the unit you intend to buy is within the 70 % cap. Without it, MLMUPC will not register the strata-title transfer to a foreign name.
- **MLMUPC strata title registration**: the developer must first register the building as a "co-owned building" with MLMUPC to obtain strata titles (unit-by-unit certificates). New developments frequently market and sell off-plan BEFORE strata titles are issued — meaning the buyer's interim instrument is a Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA) only. **Strata title issuance can take 12–36 months post-completion** and depends on the developer fully discharging mortgages on the parent land title and registering the master-strata plan. Off-plan strata-title buyers carry meaningful issuance risk.
- **No FX-form requirement**: unlike Thailand's FET form, Cambodia has **no specific cross-border-remittance form mandated for property transfer**. NBC Prakas on FX (Prakas B7-014-218, 2014) require banks to report inward / outward transfers above thresholds, but there is no buyer-side mandatory documentation tied to the property transfer. **Practical**: most foreign buyers wire USD into their personal Cambodian bank account (typically with ABA Bank, ACLEDA, Phillip Bank, Maybank Cambodia, or Canadia Bank) and make the purchase payment from there. Keep wire receipts for repatriation evidence.
- **Repatriation**: Cambodia maintains an **open capital account** under Article 5 of the Law on Foreign Exchange (1997). Funds from a property sale can in principle be wired out without exchange-control approval. This is one of the most permissive capital-account regimes in ASEAN. **Caveat**: bank-side AML / source-of-funds documentation has tightened since 2018; expect to provide the original property purchase contract, sale contract, and tax-receipt evidence.

### Path 2: Long-term registered lease

- **Statute**: Land Law 2001 Articles 244–248 (long-term lease over private and state-private land) + Civil Code 2007 (general lease provisions). A registered lease **≥ 15 years and ≤ 50 years** constitutes a real right (a "perpetual lease" / "long-term lease" / "leasing right" / សិទ្ធិជួលរយៈពេលវែង) opposable to third parties. **Transitional**: leases entered into **before 31 Dec 2011** (Civil Code implementation) could be up to **99 years** under transitional rules — material when buying a property encumbered by an older lease. (2026-05-27 verified, source BNG Legal + Cambodia Counsel.)
- **Maximum term**: **50 years**, with one optional renewal of up to **50 years** — total 100 years. Source: Article 247 Land Law 2001 (verify "1992 Land Law 70-year cap" claim with primary instrument — secondary sources also cite a 99-year historical cap). The 50-year cap applies to the registered term; the renewal must be re-registered at the cadastre — historically not automatic against successor landowners absent registered renewal-option language.
- **Registration**: leases > 15 years must be **registered with the provincial / municipal cadastre** to be enforceable as real rights. Registration fee ~4 % of the capitalized lease value (treated similarly to a transfer for tax-base purposes; verify current Prakas at GDT).
- **Practical use**: villas / land plots in Sihanoukville, Kampot, Kep, Siem Reap often marketed to foreigners on long-term lease structures (with the building separately owned, in some structures). Common arrangement: foreigner pays a lump-sum "lease premium" upfront equivalent to land market value + nominal annual rent (~$1/yr).
- **Renewal risk**: the second 50-year period requires fresh registration with the (then-current) landowner. If the original Cambodian lessor sells or dies, successor landowner is bound only by registered terms — verify the renewal-option language is registered, not just contractual.
- **Anti-nominee aspect**: a "100-year lease at $1/yr" effectively for $X-million premium can be re-characterized by the GDT as a sale (and the underlying structure as evasion of Article 44). Practitioners typically structure with realistic annual rent and a defined repurchase / pre-emption right.

### Path 3: Cambodian-spouse ownership

- Cambodian spouse owns land in their sole name. Foreign spouse signs an acknowledgement at the cadastre that funds belong to the Cambodian spouse's separate property and that the foreign spouse claims no constitutional ownership interest.
- **On divorce**: Civil Code 2007 governs marital-property division. Court-ordered equitable distribution is theoretically possible but cannot transfer LAND title to a foreigner (Article 44 Constitution); compensation in lieu may be ordered. Historical case law mixed; do not rely on equitable recovery.
- **On Cambodian spouse's death**: foreign surviving spouse can inherit the value but cannot directly hold the land — the land must be sold or transferred to a Cambodian heir within a reasonable period (case-law not fully harmonized; a Cambodian-citizen child can hold).
- This path has all the same risks as the Thai / Vietnamese / Filipino spouse paths — divorce risk, abuse risk, no enforceable equitable claim against Article 44.

### Path 4: Cambodian-majority company (⚠️ legal grey zone)

- **Mechanism**: a Cambodian limited liability company (under the **Law on Commercial Enterprises 2005**) is incorporated; ≥ 51 % of voting shares are held by Cambodian natural-or-legal persons; foreigner holds ≤ 49 % with control rights via preferential share class / shareholder agreement / director appointment.
- **Anti-nominee enforcement**: Constitution Art. 44 reserves land to Khmer natural / legal persons; Land Law 2001 **Art. 9** voids any private shareholder agreement contrary to the 51 % rule (only the articles-of-incorporation percentages count); **Art. 8** punishes a foreigner who falsifies national identity to own land "as determined under article 251" and seizes the property "as state property without compensation from the state". No published record of agency challenges to passive-shareholder structures was located — enforcement frequency: data not publicly available — verify at MLMUPC. Risks: deemed transfer, void title, capital gains tax assessment, criminal liability under the Law on Anti-Corruption (NS/RKM/0410/004, 2010).
- **Practical reality 2026**: the structure was widely used 2010–2018 in Phnom Penh / Sihanoukville landed-house markets; reputable Cambodian law firms (DFDL, Tilleke, Sciaroni & Associates, BNG Legal, HBS Law, R&T Sok & Heng) increasingly advise against thin-nominee structures and recommend strata-title or long-term lease pathways instead. Verify current firm guidance before relying on this path.
- **CDC Qualified Investment Project (QIP) angle**: under the LCI 2021, certain projects can obtain QIP status with corporate income tax holidays (3–9 years) and customs incentives. Real-estate development can qualify as a QIP in limited categories — but **QIP status does NOT exempt land-ownership prohibition** (foreigner-controlled QIP company still cannot hold land directly).

### Path 5: Trust Law structures

- **Statute**: Law on Trusts (NS/RKM/0119/004, Jan 2019), regulated by the Trust Regulator (under the Ministry of Economy and Finance, MEF, `https://www.mef.gov.kh/`).
- **Structure**: a Cambodian trustee holds the immovable property in trust for a beneficiary (which can be a foreigner under the Trust Law's permissive beneficiary definitions). The legal title sits with the trustee (a Cambodian natural / legal person, satisfying Article 44 in form).
- **Constitutional question**: whether a foreign-beneficiary trust over LAND survives an Article 44 challenge is **untested**. The Trust Law allows foreign beneficiaries; the Constitutional Council has not ruled on whether this creates a substantive foreign land-ownership in violation of Article 44. Use only with extensive legal advice and disclosure.
- Practical role 2026: limited; mostly used for movable assets, equity, and structured family-office holdings rather than as a property workaround.

### Anti-nominee enforcement summary

| Instrument | Enforcement intensity | Practitioner recommendation 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Strata title in foreign-eligible building | Clean | ✅ Recommended path |
| 50+50 long-term lease, registered | Clean for lease term | ✅ Acceptable for landed property |
| Cambodian spouse owns the land | Legal but vulnerable | ⚠️ Marital risk; not a planning path |
| 51 % Cambodian company (genuine partner) | Legal | ⚠️ Genuine partnership only |
| 51 % Cambodian company (nominee) | Side-agreements void by statute (Land Law Art. 9); prosecution statistics not published | ❌ Not recommended; foreign funder's protection unenforceable |
| Trust with foreign beneficiary over land | Untested constitutionally | ❌ Until tested, avoid for residential |

### Confidence

**HIGH** — Constitution Article 44, Land Law 2001 (NS/RKM/0801/14), Foreign Ownership Law 2010 (NS/RKM/0510/015), Sub-Decree 126/2009, Trust Law 2019 are all primary statutes published by the Royal Government / National Assembly. **MEDIUM** for parcel-level enforcement variation across provinces (Phnom Penh and Siem Reap have most-developed cadastral processes; outer provinces and Sihanoukville more variable). **MEDIUM** for anti-nominee enforcement intensity (no published prosecution statistics and no published enforcement campaign located; the statutory position — Land Law Art. 9 voidance of contrary shareholder agreements — is HIGH). **MEDIUM** for the 30-km border buffer (Article 4 Foreign Ownership Law 2010 mentions border-area restrictions; precise distance + SEZ exemptions vary by Prakas — verify current list at MLMUPC).

## Section: `--price`

### Primary sources

- **National Bank of Cambodia (NBC)** — quarterly Financial Stability Review and Real Estate Market Monitor (irregular). Portal: `https://www.nbc.gov.kh/`. NBC is the most authoritative source on aggregate real-estate credit and price-trend signals; granular district-level data limited.

### Compute

1. Listing price/m² = listing USD ÷ saleable area (the typical Cambodian-listing convention is internal floor area, similar to "carpet area"; verify which definition the developer uses — some include balcony at 50 % weighting).
2. Compare to Knight Frank / CBRE quarterly report for the same Phnom Penh district / Cambodian city.
3. Compare to nearest 3–5 same-building / same-street listings on Realestate.com.kh.
4. Pull the **MLMUPC official assessed value** (the "tax base" for Property Transfer Tax) — this is set by the Property Assessment Committee periodically and is typically **30–50 % of market value** in Phnom Penh prime; useful as a tax-base reference, not as a price benchmark. Verify at GDT or via tax counsel.
5. **Trap (off-plan strata-title risk)**: many Phnom Penh condos sell off-plan with 80–95 % paid before handover. Strata title issuance can lag completion by 12–36 months. Discount off-plan listings vs ready-Strata-title units accordingly (typically 10–20 %).
6. **Trap (Sihanoukville unfinished-project risk)**: as of 2026-05, multiple shell buildings from the 2017–2019 boom remain unfinished. Verify the project's current construction status, developer track record, and (if applicable) any new owners / restructuring before deposit.
7. **Trap (foreign-buyer premium)**: condo units actively marketed in English channels (Realestate.com.kh foreign-tab, IPS Cambodia) often list 5–10 % above Khmer-channel listings (Khmer24) for the same building.
8. **Trap (Soft Title villa)**: a "$X-per-m²" villa listing at a deceptively low number is sometimes Soft Title only — material risk premium / discount of 15–30 % vs Hard Title equivalent.

### Confidence

**MEDIUM** for Phnom Penh prime / mid-tier condo ranges (Knight Frank + CBRE + multiple listing platforms agree within ±15 %). **MEDIUM-LOW** for Sihanoukville (post-cycle data variance is high; many distressed units skew listing averages). **LOW** for tier-2/3 cities and rural land (effectively unreachable for foreign buyers anyway under Article 44, and listing data thin).

## Section: `--tax`

### Annual property tax — modest

> **Headline**: Cambodia has **two annual immovable property taxes**: TOIP (0.1 % on residential property value > 100M KHR) and TROD (2 % on unused urban land market value, applied to undeveloped urban parcels above a size threshold). Both are administered by GDT under the Law on Taxation 2023.

**Tax on Immovable Property (TOIP, ពន្ធលើអចលនទ្រព្យ)** — annual:

| Item | Rate / Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Tax rate** | **0.1 %** of taxable value |
| **Tax base** | Official assessed value set by the Property Assessment Committee (typically 30–50 % of market value in Phnom Penh prime; older / more conservative in outer provinces) |
| **Threshold** | Properties valued ≤ **100M KHR (~$25,000)** are exempt |
| **Exemptions** | Agricultural land, government property, religious / charitable property |
| **Filing** | Annual declaration at GDT; due by 30 September of each year |

- **Practical absolute amount**: a $300,000 Phnom Penh BKK1 condo with assessed value ~$120,000 would owe ~$120 / yr (= 0.1 % × $120k). A $1M condo would owe ~$400–$500 / yr.
- Source: Law on Taxation 2023 Articles on TOIP; original framework Law on Tax on Immovable Property (Sub-Decree introducing TOIP 2010). GDT `https://www.tax.gov.kh/`.

**Tax on Unused Land (TROD, ពន្ធលើដីដែលគ្មានអ្នកប្រើប្រាស់)** — annual:

| Item | Rate / Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Tax rate** | **2 %** of market value |
| **Tax base** | Market value as set by the Unused Land Appraisal Committee |
| **Coverage** | Vacant urban land (includes Phnom Penh, provincial capitals); minimum size threshold (verify current Prakas — historically ≥ 5 ha but reduced under 2018 reform) |
| **Exemptions** | Land with completed construction; ag land; land under 5 ha (check current threshold) |

- TROD's policy purpose is to discourage land speculation. **Enforcement is patchy**; many vacant parcels in Phnom Penh outer districts have not been assessed in years.
- Source: Law on Taxation 2023; original TROD framework Sub-Decree.

### Transaction taxes (one-time at purchase)

| Tax | Rate | Base | Who pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Property Transfer Tax / "Stamp Duty" (ពន្ធលើការផ្ទេរកម្មសិទ្ធិ)** | **4 %** of transfer value | Higher of declared sale price OR official assessed value (Property Assessment Committee) | Statutorily seller; **practically often shifted to buyer** in foreign-buyer purchases |
| **Registration fees / cadastre fees** | small flat fees, typically $50–$300 (USD-equivalent in KHR) | Per certificate | Buyer |
| **Notary / lawyer fees (foreign buyer)** | 1–2 % of price typical, or fixed $1,500–$5,000 | Negotiable | Buyer |
| **Brokerage fee** | 2–4 % (foreign-facing brokers may charge 3–5 %) | Sale price | Negotiable; commonly seller for resale |
| **VAT (new build)** | **10 %** | Sale price (commercial supply by registered developer) | Embedded in developer pricing — buyer ultimately bears |
| **Capital gains tax (sellers)** | **20 %** on gain (**Prakas No. 1130 MEF.Prk.GDT, 31 Dec 2025**, status Valid — supersedes the now-abrogated Prakas 346/2020); **immovable-property CGT applies to gains arising from 1 Jan 2027 onward** (Art. 18(2)); **Art. 18(1) categories live from 1 Jan 2026** (leases, investment assets, goodwill, IP, foreign currency); **indirect capital transfers left to separate regulation** (Art. 18(3) — status of that regulation not established; verify at GDT) | Gain computed either by **80 % standard deduction on proceeds** OR by actual costs | Seller |

### Worked example — foreign buyer of a $200,000 Phnom Penh BKK1 strata-titled condo (2nd floor +)

- Property Transfer Tax (4 %): $200,000 × 4 % = **$8,000** — often shifted to buyer
- Cadastre / strata-title transfer fee: ~$200 flat
- Notary / lawyer fee: $2,000–$4,000
- Brokerage (if buyer-facing): 2–3 %, often embedded in seller-side
- VAT (if new build): typically embedded in headline price; verify with developer
- TOIP first-year: $200,000 × ~40 % assessed × 0.1 % = ~$80 / yr
- **Total transaction cost (buyer all-in)**: typically ~5–7 % of headline price

### Rental income tax (for owners letting)

- **Tax on rental income (resident foreign owner of a Cambodian-situs property is treated as having Cambodian-source income)**:
  - **Resident** (foreign owner with > 182 days/yr in Cambodia OR principal place of business): progressive PIT (Tax on Salary or Tax on Income depending on classification); rental can also be filed as Tax on Rental Income at flat **10 %** of gross rent (post-2017 simplified).
  - **Non-resident** foreign owner: **withholding tax 14 %** on gross Cambodian-source rental income; tenant (or property manager) is the withholding agent. Source: Law on Taxation 2023; Prakas on Withholding Tax.
- VAT: rental of residential property by individuals is exempt; commercial / hotel-style rental can attract 10 % VAT if registered.

### Future risk

- **Capital gains tax (CGT) — regime now FIXED, not pending**: **Prakas No. 1130 MEF.Prk.GDT dated 31 December 2025 (status: Valid on the GDT register)** replaced the abrogated Prakas 346 MEF.PrK (2020); the Law on Taxation 2023 basis is unchanged. **Art. 18(2): 20 % CGT on gains from the sale or transfer of immovable property arising from 1 January 2027 onward** — after repeated deferrals since 2020 this is the operative date, not another rolling postponement. **Art. 18(1) is already live from 1 January 2026** (leases, investment assets, goodwill, IP, foreign currency). **Art. 18(3) leaves indirect capital transfers to separate regulation** — the status of that separate regulation is not established here, so treat company-structure / indirect exits as an open item and confirm the current position with GDT and tax counsel before committing. **Foreign sellers must price a 20 % exit tax into any disposal with gains arising from 1 Jan 2027**; gain is computed either by 80 % standard deduction on proceeds or by actual costs. (2026-08-07 verified, source GDT `https://www.tax.gov.kh/`.)
- **Real Estate Business Law**: drafts since 2018; if enacted, would create a regulator under MEF and impose developer-disclosure standards (escrow, completion guarantees). Not enacted as of 2026-05.
- **TOIP threshold and rate review** — periodic; rate has held at 0.1 % since 2011 introduction but threshold may move.

⚠️ Verification: request seller's last TOIP assessment letter from GDT; verify capital gains exposure with tax counsel; confirm VAT treatment with developer (new build) or with seller's tax counsel (resale).

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term rental (residential lease)

- Long-term residential leases in Phnom Penh / Sihanoukville / Siem Reap are typically **6–24 month USD-denominated**. Tenant deposit is 1–2 months; first-month rent paid up front.
- **Furnished vs unfurnished**: most foreign-targeted condo lets are furnished; unfurnished is more common for local Cambodian tenants. Furnished commands ~10–25 % premium.
- **Yields**: gross rental yields in Phnom Penh prime condos are typically **5–7 %** as of 2026 (Knight Frank Q4 2025; verify); **6–8 %** in mid-tier; **3–5 %** in luxury BKK1 due to high purchase prices. Sihanoukville yields are highly variable post-2019 (some buildings dark, others fully let).

### Short-term rental (Airbnb / vacation rental)

- Cambodia has **no specific national STR statute** as of 2026-05. STR operates in a regulatory grey zone, registered under hotel/guesthouse/serviced-apartment licensing where applicable.
- **Hotel licensing**: under the **Law on Tourism (NS/RKM/0609/006, June 2009)**, businesses providing accommodation services for compensation must obtain a licence from the **Ministry of Tourism** (`https://www.tourismcambodia.org/`) and the provincial Department of Tourism. STR units operating regularly without a licence are technically non-compliant; enforcement varies by province.
- **Building bylaws**: many co-owned strata buildings in Phnom Penh and Siem Reap have moved to forbid STR via the co-ownership association rules. Verify with the building's borivenh ruom (co-ownership association) BEFORE assuming Airbnb is permitted.
- **Sihanoukville STR**: was a major segment 2017–2019 but collapsed with the gambling ban + COVID. Partial recovery 2023–2025; foreign-buyer caution still warranted.
- **Tax treatment of STR**: same withholding (14 % non-resident) or 10 % resident-rental tax as long-term, plus 10 % VAT if hotel-licensed. Multi-unit professional operators register as enterprise.

### Existing competition

| Place | STR competition | Long-term rental yield band |
|---|---|---|
| Phnom Penh BKK1 | High Airbnb density (premium expat segment), but many buildings forbid STR | 3–5 % gross |
| Phnom Penh Daun Penh / Tonle Bassac | High STR; tourist + business-traveller mix | 4–6 % gross |
| Phnom Penh mid-tier | Moderate STR; long-let dominant | 6–8 % gross |
| Siem Reap (Old Market / Pub Street area) | High STR, tourist-driven, seasonal | 5–8 % gross |
| Sihanoukville | Variable — pre-2019 high; post-2019 partial recovery | 4–8 % gross (high variance) |
| Kampot, Kep, Battambang | Low STR; small markets | 4–6 % gross long-let |

### Sihanoukville cycle volatility (regional override)

- **2017–2019 peak** — Chinese-driven online-gambling sector + Belt-and-Road infrastructure investment drove prices and rents to multiples of 2015 levels. Some Sihanoukville condo offerings advertised 8–15 % "guaranteed yields" pre-2019; many failed.
- **2019 (18 Aug) online-gambling ban** — sub-decree banned online gambling activities; Chinese investors withdrew rapidly. Rents reportedly collapsed ~50–80 % in some buildings within months (magnitude anecdotal — verify).
- **2020–2022 COVID** — border closures + tourism collapse compounded the crisis. Multiple developers defaulted; estimated 300+ unfinished shell buildings remain (per municipal authorities and World Bank reporting 2022).
- **2023–2025 recovery** — partial; some ASEAN tourism + domestic investment; legitimate hotel and resort recovery; speculative-condo segment slow.
- **Foreign-buyer guidance**: Sihanoukville off-plan should be treated as **HIGH-RISK** unless the developer is well-established (e.g., long-track-record local groups), the project is at advanced construction (visible above-ground structure), and all approvals (construction permit + master strata-title plan filed at MLMUPC) are documented.

### Strategic notes

- **Phnom Penh BKK1 / Daun Penh prime condos** have stable yields; STR explicitly forbidden in many buildings — assume long-let unless verified.
- **Siem Reap STR** — dependent on Angkor tourism volume; new airport (16 Oct 2023) reshaped logistics — verify post-2023 STR data, not pre-2020.
- **Pool / view / WiFi** matter for STR pricing in tropical-tourist segments (Sihanoukville coast, Siem Reap, Kampot).
- **Property management**: foreign owner letting a Cambodian property typically uses a property manager (commonly 8–12 % of rent); verify the manager is acting as withholding agent for the 14 % non-resident WHT.

### Confidence

**MEDIUM** — long-let yields well-documented by Knight Frank / CBRE / Realestate.com.kh. **LOW-MEDIUM** for STR specifically due to regulatory grey zone and post-COVID + post-Sihanoukville-cycle data variance.

### Flood (ទឹកជំនន់)

- **Annual monsoon floods**: May–November (Southwest monsoon, peak July–October). The Mekong + Tonle Sap system **reverses flow** during peak monsoon — the Tonle Sap River reverses direction and the lake expands ~5x area, an unique seasonal flood-pulse system. This is normal; what is risky is **abnormally high years** (2000, 2011, 2013, 2020 — major flood seasons with property damage in Phnom Penh and provincial cities).
- **Phnom Penh flooding**: chronic during the rainy season; localized urban flooding from drainage capacity (legacy network from French colonial era + post-2000 expansion). Areas with chronic low-spot flooding include parts of Toul Kork, Sen Sok, and along the Tonle Sap riverbank during peak.
- **Sihanoukville flooding**: the 2017–2019 boom-era rapid construction outpaced drainage upgrades; flash-flooding episodes 2018–2020 highlighted under-built drainage. Coastal exposure is also storm-surge relevant.
- **Mekong / Tonle Sap rural areas**: extensive seasonal floodplain land. Land prices reflect flood exposure.
- **Verdict bands** (parcel-level requires provincial cadastre + MOWRAM flood-zone confirmation):
  - 🔴 Lower Mekong / Tonle Sap floodplain villages (Kandal, Prey Veng, Takeo low-lying); Phnom Penh riverside low spots (parts of Chroy Changvar peninsula, Tonle Bassac riverside)
  - 🟠 Phnom Penh inner low-elevation districts; Sihanoukville coastal-low; Siem Reap province north of NR6 in monsoon-flood-prone areas
  - 🟡 Phnom Penh BKK1 / BKK2 / Daun Penh higher ground; Siem Reap town higher ground
  - 🟢 Hilly upcountry (Mondulkiri, Ratanakiri, Kep highlands); Phnom Penh elevated districts

### Tropical cyclones / typhoons (ព្យុះត្រូពិច)

- Cambodia is **largely shielded** from direct typhoon hits by Vietnam's eastern coast. Most tropical-cyclone impact arrives as **weakened tropical depressions** (post-landfall in Vietnam / South China) bringing heavy rain rather than wind damage.
- **Historical**: rare direct impacts; 2009 Typhoon Ketsana (post-landfall) caused major flooding in northeast Cambodia.
- **Coastal Sihanoukville / Koh Kong / Kampot**: storm-surge risk from Gulf-of-Thailand systems is low-to-moderate; not a primary risk.

### Seismicity (រញ្ជួយដី)

- Cambodia is a **low-seismic country**. No major fault zones traverse the country at population centers. Phnom Penh, Sihanoukville, Siem Reap are all in low-seismic settings.
- Some risk from distant earthquakes (Myanmar 2025 M7.7 was felt very lightly in Phnom Penh; Sumatra-Andaman events generate small ground motion).
- **Not a primary risk class** for property; standard Cambodian construction does not include seismic detailing in most cases.

### Air quality (PM2.5, គុណភាពខ្យល់)

- **Phnom Penh dry-season (Dec–Apr)**: PM2.5 frequently 40–80 µg/m³ (WHO 24-hr guideline 15 µg/m³); wet season (May–Nov) cleaner. Sources: traffic + dust + agricultural burning + transboundary smoke from Thailand / Laos.
- **Sihanoukville, Siem Reap, coastal**: cleaner due to coastal ventilation / rural setting; typical PM2.5 20–40 µg/m³.
- **Northern provinces (Stung Treng, Ratanakiri, Mondulkiri)**: Feb–Apr crop-burning smog can be severe, but population exposure low.
- Source: limited public Cambodian Government dashboards; verify via IQAir / PurpleAir Phnom Penh stations + WHO Global Air Quality Database.

### Climate-change projections (CCCA + IPCC AR6 + MoE National Adaptation Plan)

- **Temperature**: +1.0–2.5 °C by 2050 vs 1986–2005 baseline (RCP4.5 → RCP8.5). Source: IPCC AR6 Atlas; MoE Cambodia National Adaptation Plan (NAP) 2021.
- **Sea-level rise**: +25–60 cm by 2100. Sihanoukville, Kep, Koh Kong coast exposed; coastal property below 5 m elevation faces compound risk by mid-century.
- **Extreme rainfall**: monsoon intensity expected to increase; flash-flooding risk rises in Phnom Penh and provincial capitals.
- **Drought**: dry-season drought severity increasing in north / northeast; affects rice and rubber agriculture but less directly residential.
- **Mekong flow disruption**: upstream Chinese + Lao + Thai dam construction is altering Mekong seasonality; combined with climate change, the Tonle Sap flood-pulse system is increasingly disturbed (multiple academic and MRC studies 2019–2024). Long-term implications for floodplain land value are negative.

### Build-era hazards

| Era | Hazards |
|---|---|
| **Pre-1979** (pre-Khmer Rouge / Khmer Rouge era) | Most pre-1975 urban housing damaged or unmaintained during 1975–1979; remaining French-colonial-era buildings have asbestos, lead paint, weak foundations, no seismic detailing |
| **1980s–1990s** (post-DK reconstruction) | Variable quality; informal contractors; asbestos still common; many buildings without proper construction permits |
| **2000s–2010** (early reform construction boom) | Variable quality; some reputable developers emerging; pre-2007 Civil Code era contracts, weaker buyer protection |
| **2010s–2018** (foreign-investor boom era) | Improving on premium; many mid-tier builds rushed for the boom; several Phnom Penh and Sihanoukville projects had structural deficiencies |
| **Post-2018 (Sihanoukville crash + Phnom Penh maturity)** | Surviving developers tend to higher quality; many Sihanoukville units stalled / unfinished; verify construction permit + as-built compliance |

> **The 2019–2022 Sihanoukville cycle** generated a wave of stalled / unfinished construction. As of 2022, municipal estimates referenced ~300+ shell buildings with paused or abandoned construction. Many remain unfinished as of 2025–2026. **Foreign buyers in Sihanoukville should physically inspect any project's construction status, demand current MLMUPC permits, and avoid off-plan unless from established developers with track records of delivered projects.**

## Section: `--mains`

### Water supply

- **Phnom Penh Water Supply Authority (PPWSA, ស្វយ័តទឹកស្អាតភ្នំពេញ)** — covers Phnom Penh capital + suburbs. `https://www.ppwsa.com.kh/`. PPWSA is one of Asia's most cited water-utility success stories (post-1993 turnaround) — Phnom Penh has reliable mains water in central and most outlying districts.
- **Provincial water utilities**: Sihanoukville Water Supply Authority, Siem Reap Water Supply Authority (SRWSA), and provincial-level utilities under Ministry of Industry, Science, Technology and Innovation (MISTI, formerly MIH). Coverage is much patchier than Phnom Penh.
- **Rural**: many villages on shallow well + storage tank; quality variable; arsenic contamination is a known issue in some Mekong-floodplain provinces (Kandal, Prey Veng).

### Sewer / wastewater

**Cambodia has very limited mains-sewer coverage compared to OECD baselines.**

- **Phnom Penh**: a centralized sewerage collection network exists in central districts (Daun Penh, Chamkar Mon, parts of Toul Kork) but coverage is partial. **Most strata-buildings rely on building-internal wastewater treatment plants (WWTP)** required by MLMUPC building approvals for buildings above a certain size. The Phnom Penh Wastewater Treatment Plant (Choeung Ek WWTP, completed in stages since 2018, JICA-supported) is expanding capacity. Source: Phnom Penh Capital Hall + JICA reporting.
- **Sihanoukville**: limited mains sewerage; most condos and villas on-site treatment + soakaway. The 2017–2019 boom outpaced sewerage expansion materially.
- **Siem Reap**: limited mains; on-site treatment is the norm. Hotel-grade buildings have building WWTPs.
- **Provincial cities + rural**: predominantly septic + soakaway; many older dwellings on simple cesspit (បំពង់ទឹកកខ្វក់).
- **Wastewater regulator**: Ministry of Environment + MLMUPC at municipal level.

### Verification path

1. **Phnom Penh**: call PPWSA hotline (023-724-046 main / verify current at PPWSA portal) for mains water; call Phnom Penh Capital Hall sanitation department for sewer specific to district.
2. **Provincial cities**: call provincial water authority + MISTI provincial office.
3. **Rural / villas**: assume septic; verify tank size, last desludge date, and soakaway condition with seller.
4. **Listing language scan**:
   - "Mains water / centralized water supply" / "ទឹកស្ងួត" = mains water
   - "Building WWTP / wastewater treatment plant" = on-site building treatment (typical condo / strata)
   - "Septic tank" / "ប្រព័ន្ធកំទេចទឹកកខ្វក់ផ្ទាល់ខ្លួន" = on-site septic + soakaway

## Active fiscal incentives (2026)

- **CDC Qualified Investment Project (QIP) status**: under LCI 2021, large-scale development projects (real-estate-development sector qualifying in some categories) can obtain corporate tax holidays (3–9 years), customs incentives, and import-duty exemptions. **Residential angle**: limited — incentives are corporate-developer-side, not for individual residential property buyers. Source: CDC `https://www.cdc.gov.kh/`.
- **Special Economic Zones (SEZ)**: ~50 SEZs across Cambodia (Sihanoukville SEZ, Phnom Penh SEZ, Manhattan SEZ, etc.) — corporate / industrial focus, limited direct residential property incentive.
- **Sihanoukville Economic Special Status**: post-2019, Cambodia announced various recovery initiatives and special incentives for Sihanoukville to attract new investment; verify current measures at MEF / Preah Sihanouk Provincial Hall.
- **Cambodia My Second Home (CM2H)**: **operational since July 2022** (Ministry of Interior + KHCA). 10-year renewable visa; USD 100k minimum real-estate investment in approved projects; processing 4–6 months; citizenship eligibility after 5 yrs residency. (2026-05-27 verified, source Harvey Law Group + cm2h.com + IMI Daily.)
- **Cambodia CBI** (NOT real-estate-based): discretionary CBI exists via Sub-Decree 24 ANK.BK (1996) — est. ~USD 245k donation OR est. ~USD 305k investment (practitioner-reported current thresholds, not fixed by the 1996 sub-decree itself — verify current amounts with the Ministry of Interior / a Cambodian immigration lawyer before relying on them); real-estate purchase is NOT a CBI route. CM2H is the property-linked residency programme.
- **2025 first-time-buyer transfer-tax exemption**: GDT extended a stamp-duty exemption for first-time home buyers of new residential property **≤ USD 210,000** from recognised developers — in effect through end of 2025; verify any 2026 extension at GDT. (2026-05-27 verified, source DFDL + IPS Cambodia.)

## Caveats unique to KH

- **Foreign land ownership prohibition is constitutional** — Article 44 of the 1993 Constitution; reinforced by Article 8 Land Law 2001. Foreigners cannot own land directly under any structure.
- **Strata title is the only clean foreign-ownership path** — under the May 2010 Foreign Ownership Law, capped at 70 % per single co-owned building, ground floor / 1st floor and below excluded.
- **Hard Title vs Soft Title vs Strata Title** — three distinct title forms with materially different provenance. Reject Soft Title for any foreigner-relevant transaction; insist on Hard Title or Strata Title.
- **30-km border buffer** — Article 4 Foreign Ownership Law 2010 excludes foreign-condo ownership in buildings within 30 km of any land border (with limited SEZ exceptions). Verify before deposit.
- **50 + 50 long-term lease** — registered lease is a real right; renewal must be re-registered to be enforceable against successor landowners. "100-year lease" marketing is technically inaccurate — only the registered term is enforceable.
- **Cambodian-majority company nominee structures** — legal as a structure, but any shareholder side-agreement contrary to the 51 % rule is void under Land Law 2001 Art. 9 — the foreign funder's protection is unenforceable.
- **Sihanoukville cycle volatility** — 2019 gambling ban + COVID created a wave of unfinished construction; foreign buyers should treat off-plan Sihanoukville with extreme caution.
- **USD-denominated transactions** — most property contracts and prices in USD; KHR used for taxes and small purchases. NBC's de-dollarization push (Bakong digital riel since Oct 2020) is long-term; near-term USD remains dominant.
- **Open capital account** — one of ASEAN's most permissive; original capital + property-sale proceeds can in principle be repatriated with bank documentation. Bank-side AML / KYC tightened since 2018.
- **No FX-form mandate at property transfer** — unlike Thailand FET; just retain wire receipts and source-of-funds documentation.
- **Capital gains tax 20 %** — **Prakas No. 1130 MEF.Prk.GDT (31 Dec 2025, status Valid)** replaced the abrogated Prakas 346/2020; Law on Taxation 2023 basis unchanged. **No longer open-ended: Art. 18(2) bites on immovable-property gains arising from 1 January 2027 onward**; Art. 18(1) categories (leases, investment assets, goodwill, IP, foreign currency) already live from 1 January 2026. Price the exit tax in. (2026-08-07 verified.)
- **No automatic property-linked residency visa** — CM2H discussed but not operational at scale; foreign owners typically maintain ER (retirement) or EB (business) extensions.
- **Off-plan strata-title issuance lag** — 12–36 months post-completion is common; verify developer's master-strata plan filed with MLMUPC and check construction-permit status before deposit.
- **All title documents in Khmer** — sworn translation + Cambodian-licensed lawyer mandatory for foreign buyers.
- **STR (Airbnb) regulatory grey zone** — many strata buildings forbid via co-ownership association rules; tax declaration required.
- **Trust Law 2019 over land — untested** — Trust structures with foreign beneficiaries holding land have not been tested at the constitutional level.
- **Mekong / Tonle Sap flood-pulse** — annual seasonal flooding is normal; abnormal years (2000, 2011, 2013, 2020) cause property damage. Verify parcel-level flood exposure.
- **Construction permit + occupancy compliance** — pre-2010 buildings may have informal permits; verify the building has a current construction permit + occupancy certificate.
- **Inheritance** — the Civil Code 2007 governs intestate succession; foreign heirs of a foreign-owned strata-titled unit must arrange transfer / sale within a reasonable period (no statutory 12-month deadline like Vietnam, but practically advisable to plan estate succession explicitly).

## Quirks to know

- **Hard Title is the only safe national-registry title for foreigner-relevant transactions** — Soft Title (commune ledger) carries materially weaker provenance and should be hard-rejected by foreign buyers (or by their local proxy in spouse / company structures). Many older / rural / non-LMAP-surveyed parcels are Soft Title; conversion ("hardening") to Hard Title via systematic registration is bureaucratic and slow.
- **Strata title issuance lag** — get a written timeline from the developer with penalty clauses; do not assume "imminent" issuance; many Phnom Penh and Sihanoukville projects have multi-year delays in master-strata-plan filings at MLMUPC.
- **Foreign-Quota Confirmation** for strata: get it in writing from the co-ownership association BEFORE deposit; without it, MLMUPC will not register a foreign transfer.
- **30-km border buffer** for foreign condo: Article 4 Foreign Ownership Law 2010; verify before evaluating any project in border provinces (Banteay Meanchey, Battambang west, Kampong Cham, Svay Rieng east, Koh Kong, Stung Treng, Ratanakiri, Mondulkiri).
- **Khmer-only legal documents** — all cadastral records and notarial deeds are in Khmer. Sworn translation + Cambodian-licensed lawyer mandatory for foreign buyers.
- **Notarization** — for transfers of titled land, the deed of transfer is signed before a public notary OR (for some parcel types) before the commune chief / village authority who certifies signatures; verify which is required for the specific parcel type.
- **USD vs KHR pricing** — most contracts USD; small-purchase + tax payments KHR. Round-trip currency risk is small (NBC defends a soft peg via FX intervention) but bank spread on conversion is meaningful (est. ~0.5–1.5 % each way — calibrate against current published USD–KHR rate cards from major Cambodian banks such as ABA / ACLEDA before budgeting).
- **Bakong digital riel** — NBC's blockchain-based payment network launched Oct 2020, gradually pushing KHR adoption for retail; long-term de-dollarization signal but does not affect property transactions in the near term.
- **Sihanoukville (Preah Sihanouk Province)** has a distinct regulatory + market microclimate post-2019 — apply Sihanoukville-specific due diligence; do not extrapolate Phnom Penh patterns.
- **MLMUPC digitization** — the General Department of Cadastre and Geography has been digitizing parcel records progressively; coverage uneven; physical office visits often still needed.
- **Capital gains tax activation date** — after **repeated deferrals since 2020**, **Prakas No. 1130 MEF.Prk.GDT (31 Dec 2025) fixes the date: 20 % on immovable-property gains arising from 1 January 2027 onward** (Art. 18(2)). **Plan exit liquidity assuming CGT applies from that date**, especially for high-gain Sihanoukville recovery trades. (2026-08-07 verified.)
- **Cambodia has no central published cadastre online viewer** — physical visits to provincial cadastre offices remain the verification standard.
- **Co-ownership association (borivenh ruom)** — the condo's management entity, established under Sub-Decree 126/2009. Manages common areas, fees, sinking fund, foreign-quota tracking. AGM participation rights for owners.
- **Phnom Penh khan (district) renaming / boundary changes** — periodic; some addresses reference legacy khans no longer current. Verify the current administrative boundary.
