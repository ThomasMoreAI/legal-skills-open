# Aruba 🇦🇼 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `aw`. Status: ✅ Fully populated (researched 2026-05).

> **Constitutional context**: Aruba is a **constituent country (*land*) within the Kingdom of the Netherlands** since 1986 (*Status Aparte* — separated from the former Netherlands Antilles by Koninkrijksbesluit 1 Jan 1986). It has its **own constitution (*Staatsregeling van Aruba*), own parliament (*Parlamento di Aruba* / *Staten van Aruba*), own legal system, own tax authority (DIMP — *Departamento di Impuesto*), own land registry (*Dienst Landmeetkunde en Vastgoedregistratie*, DLV), own currency (Aruban florin, AWG), and own courts of first instance**. Foreign affairs, defence, and Kingdom-level matters remain with The Hague. Aruba is **NOT part of the EU** (associated **OCT — Overseas Country and Territory** of the EU under Annex II TFEU) and **NOT in the Schengen Area**.

## Country profile

- **Capital**: Oranjestad (district *Oranjestad West* + *Oranjestad Oost*)
- **Admin levels**: republic-equivalent → **8 regions (*regio's* / *districten*)** for census/cadastral purposes — Noord/Tanki Leendert, Oranjestad West, Oranjestad Oost, Paradera, San Nicolas Noord, San Nicolas Zuid, Santa Cruz, Savaneta. Plus the resort strip **Palm Beach / Eagle Beach** in Noord and the older San Nicolas in the south
- **Population**: ~**108,000–110,000** (CBS Aruba — Central Bureau of Statistics — `https://cbs.aw/`, latest mid-decade estimates; 2020 census 108,166). Median age ~40; high foreign-born share (~30%+ — Venezuelan, Dutch, Colombian, US, Filipino expatriate workforce)
- **Surface**: ~**180 km²** (small flat ABC-island; highest point Jamanota 188 m)
- **Languages**: **Dutch** (sole language of legislation, court records, notarial deeds) + **Papiamento** (co-official, daily / parliamentary debate) + **English** + Spanish widely spoken. **All official property documents, cadastral records, and notarial deeds are in Dutch** — translate verbatim when citing numbers; commercial real-estate marketing is typically in English
- **Currency**: **Aruban florin (AWG / Afl.)** — pegged to **USD at AWG 1.79 = USD 1.00** since the florin's 1986 introduction at par with the former Netherlands Antillean guilder. Peg administered by **Centrale Bank van Aruba (CBA)** (`https://www.cbaruba.org/`). USD circulates freely in tourism / large-ticket transactions; real-estate listings often dual-quoted AWG / USD
- **Capital controls**: **None** material on AWG / USD movement for residents; CBA administers prudential reserve requirements; foreign-currency transactions above thresholds require commercial-bank documentation under AML rules (no cross-border movement restriction beyond standard AML reporting)
- **Cadastre / land registry**: **Dienst Landmeetkunde en Vastgoedregistratie (DLV)** — `https://www.gobierno.aw/en/dienst-landmeetkunde-en-vastgoedregistratie-dlv` — single integrated land-registry + mortgage-registry authority (it keeps both the cadastral and the *hypothecaire* books); modelled on Dutch *Kadaster* but a separate institution. Records: ownership (*eigendom*), long-lease (*erfpacht*), mortgages (*hypotheken*), easements (*erfdienstbaarheden*)
- **Identifier**: parcel reference (*kadastraal nummer*) — *afdeling / sectie / nummer* triplet (Dutch convention) per district
- **Key documents**: *akte van levering* (notarial deed of transfer), *eigendomsbewijs* (proof of title), *kadastraal uittreksel* (cadastral extract), *meetbrief* (parcel measurement certificate), *erfpachtakte* (long-lease deed where applicable), *splitsingsakte* (condominium / apartment-rights deed)
- **EU status**: **OCT (Overseas Country and Territory)** under EU Annex II TFEU + Council Decision 2013/755/EU — NOT EU member, NOT in customs union, NOT in Schengen. Aruban-passport holders are Dutch / EU nationals (Kingdom of the Netherlands citizenship); the **territory** sits outside EU customs + Schengen. Practical implication: EU consumers visiting Aruba pass passport control; goods cross-border like third-country trade
- **Foreign-buyer regime (CRITICAL)**: **Aruba imposes NO nationality restriction on real-estate ownership**. Non-residents — including non-Dutch foreigners — may acquire **eigendom (freehold)** and **erfpacht (long-lease)** on the same terms as residents. **No prior authorisation**, no quota, no nationality-based surcharge. Standard KYC / AML / source-of-funds review at notary + bank. See `--foreign-buyer` section below

## Section: `--foreign-buyer` (read first)

### Eligibility framework

| Buyer | Status |
|---|---|
| **Aruban / Dutch / Kingdom nationals** | Free to acquire — standard notarial process |
| **Non-Dutch foreign individuals (any nationality)** | **Eligible on identical terms** — no prior authorisation, no quota, no surcharge. Standard KYC / AML at notary + bank |
| **Foreign legal persons (companies / trusts)** | Eligible — UBO disclosure required under the Aruba UBO register, **mandatory from 1 January 2023**, kept by the **Kamer van Koophandel Aruba** (Chamber of Commerce) — `https://www.arubachamber.com/ubo-register`. The register is **not public**: full access is limited to the Central Bank of Aruba, FIU-Aruba, the Public Prosecution Service, the Tax Authorities, the Gaming Authority, the Aruba Police Corps and NCTVI; Lwtf-obliged service providers (banks, accountants, notaries) may buy limited extracts (2026 data, source Aruba Chamber of Commerce; 2026-09-18 verified) |

### Two land-tenure forms

1. **Eigendom (freehold)** — outright private ownership of land + building; the seller transfers full title via *akte van levering* registered at the DLV. No annual land-lease fee. Preferred form for resale / financing (per [Doing Business Dutch Caribbean, Aruba real-estate guide](https://www.doingbusinessdutchcaribbean.com/aruba/real-estate-construction-law/private-property-ownership-long-term-land-lease-short-term-land-lease/))
2. **Erfpacht (long-lease, *langdurige* / *kortdurende*)** — Government of Aruba (*Domein*) retains underlying ownership; titleholder has a 60-year long-lease term (renewable) with full rights of use / sale / mortgage. Annual *canon* (ground rent) payable to *Domein*. Common for Crown-land allocations; verify remaining lease term + canon escalator + renewal mechanism at Kadaster + Directie Infrastructuur en Planning (DIP)
3. **Huur van domeingrond (short-lease)** — short-term rental of state land, less common for residential property; transferability + buildability restrictions apply — verify on case-by-case basis

### Practical foreign-buyer process

- **Notarial deed in Dutch** is mandatory (Dutch civil-law system). Non-Dutch-speaking buyers should engage a sworn translator + bilingual notary or local *adviseur*
- **Source-of-funds documentation**: 12-24 months bank statements + employment / business proof; AML threshold AWG 25,000 (=~USD 14k) cash + cross-border transfers logged
- **Mortgage from Aruban bank**: typically requires **35–40% down payment** for non-residents (per *Doing Business Dutch Caribbean* + listing-agency guidance, secondary ⚠️); residents 20–30% standard
- **UBO disclosure** for corporate buyers via Aruba UBO register (in force 2021 — *Dienst Wetgeving en Juridische Zaken*)

⚠️ The "no restriction" headline is true at the LEGAL level. Practical access for non-resident foreign buyers is gated by **bank mortgage policy** (35–40% LTV cap, full income docs) + **notary AML review** (typically 4–8 weeks for non-resident first-time buyers) rather than by statutory prohibition.

## Section: `--tax`

### Annual property tax — Grondbelasting (Land Tax)

Aruba's recurring property tax is the **grondbelasting** (literally "land tax"), administered by **DIMP (Departamento di Impuesto — Tax Department)** — `https://www.impuesto.aw/` / `https://www.gobierno.aw/en/departamento-di-impuesto-dimp`.

**Bands and rates (per 2024 *Landsverordening grondbelasting* schedule, [Government of Aruba 2024 land-tax assessment notice](https://www.gobierno.aw/en/the-land-tax-2024-assessment-is-on-its-way) + cross-reference [Global Property Guide Aruba](https://globalpropertyguide.com/Caribbean/Aruba/Taxes-and-Costs); 2024 data, primary + ⚠️ secondary aggregator cross-check)**:

| Taxable value (AWG) | Rate |
|---|---:|
| Up to **120,000** (≈ USD 67k) | **0.00%** (exempt) |
| 120,000 – 250,000 | **0.20%** |
| 250,000 – 500,000 | **0.30%** |
| 500,000 – 750,000 | **0.40%** |
| Above **750,000** (≈ USD 419k) | **0.60%** |
| Non-resident-owned / legal-entity-owned | **0.60% flat** (above the AWG 120k threshold) |

- **Assessment cycle**: every **5 years** — *taxatie* (valuation) by DIMP determines the *belastbare waarde* (taxable value) which then applies for 5 years until reassessment
- **Payment**: 4 quarterly instalments annually
- **Primary-residence concession**: the AWG 120k floor + progressive low-rate structure means **a typical owner-occupied primary residence pays a modest annual grondbelasting** — verify per parcel via DIMP assessment notice (*aanslag grondbelasting*)
- **Verification path**: request the seller's most recent *aanslag grondbelasting* — single cleanest evidence of the parcel's current taxable value + annual bill
- **Phone**: DIMP land-tax helpdesk 522 7423 / 522 7424 ([gobierno.aw 2024 notice](https://www.gobierno.aw/en/the-land-tax-2024-assessment-is-on-its-way))

> ⚠️ The 5-year valuation cycle means **the *belastbare waarde* often lags market** — a recently-bought property may continue paying grondbelasting on a stale lower valuation until the next reassessment. Sellers + buyers should clarify the *aanslag-jaar* (assessment year) of the current valuation.

### Transaction tax — Overdrachtsbelasting (Transfer Tax)

Per **Landsverordening overdrachtsbelasting**, as amended by the **Landsverordening Belastingplan 2023** (AB 2022 no. 161, published in the *Afkondigingsblad van Aruba* of 21 December 2022), **in force from 1 January 2023**; levied by DIMP at notarial deed signing ([DIMP Belastingplan 2023](https://www.impuesto.aw/belastingplan_2023), primary source; 2026-09-18 verified — Belastingplan 2023 is still the most recent tax plan published on impuesto.aw):

| Property type | Up to AWG 250,000 | Above AWG 250,000 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **All real property (no main-residence distinction since 1 Jan 2023)** | **3%** | **6%** on the excess |

- **Tax base**: deed price (*koopsom*) OR *leggerwaarde* (DIMP-assessed value) — **whichever is higher**
- **Notary files + remits within 15 days of deed** under penalty (up to AWG 2,500 late filing / AWG 10,000 non-payment)
- **Scope widened on 1 Jan 2023** (Landsverordening Belastingplan 2023): transfer tax now also bites on acquisition of **economic ownership** and on **huurkoop**, and on **shares in a *vastgoedvennootschap*** — a company at least 30% of whose assets are Aruban real property where that property contributes at least 70% to acquisition / disposal / exploitation, with the share value floored at the *grondbelasting* value. Non-notarial transfers carry a **two-week reporting duty** (penalty up to AWG 100,000 or 10% of value) and a **12-month anti-cumulation credit** applies. ⚠️ A buyer structuring the purchase through a company therefore **no longer avoids overdrachtsbelasting** (2026-09-18 verified)
- **Key exemptions** (per DIMP Belastingplan 2023):
  - **Inheritance (*vererving*)** transfers — out of scope of transfer tax since 1 Jan 2023 (subject separately to inheritance tax); the primary says nothing about *schenking* (gift) — verify the gift limb with DIMP
  - **Acquisition by direct-line blood relative or surviving spouse of primary residence**
  - **Property transfer via marriage / divorce settlement** (exceptions for prenup-based)
  - **Inherited property where transfer tax was previously paid**
  - **Building on land you already own**

### Income tax — Personal (Loonbelasting + Inkomstenbelasting)

Per **Landsverordening inkomstenbelasting** as amended; **2025 reform effective 1 Jan 2025** ([Government of Aruba income-tax 2025 announcement](https://www.gobierno.aw/en/the-income-tax-and-payroll-tax-rate-will-change-from-january-1-2025); [Grant Thornton Aruba 2025 tax-savings note](https://www.grantthornton.aw/publications/aruba/tax-savings-ahead-2025/), primary + advisory):

- **Tax-free allowance**: **AWG 30,000** (≈ USD 16,760) unchanged 2024 → 2025
- **2025 reform (effective 1 Jan 2025)**: **first bracket reduced from 10% to 0%** — taxpayers with income up to **AWG 64,930** now pay **zero personal income tax**
- **Brackets above the new zero band**: progressive scale up to top marginal rate (~52% historic; verify currency at DIMP — `https://www.impuesto.aw/`). For 2024 the regime was 10% / 23% / 42% / 52% across four brackets above the AWG 30,000 allowance ([Orbitax 2024 brackets note](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Aruba-Maintains-Individual-Inc-54481), ⚠️ secondary)
- **Non-resident income tax on Aruba-source rental income**: levied on the Aruba-source rental net of allowable deductions; **no DTA with US**; Netherlands–Aruba Tax Arrangement (BRK/BRNC) coordinates Dutch-resident landlords

### Rental income — BBO / BAVP / BAZV

Aruba levies a **turnover-stage tax** on services + sales:

- **BBO (Belasting op bedrijfsomzet — turnover tax)** + **BAVP (Belasting Aanvullende Voorzieningen Politie)** + **BAZV (Belasting Algemene Ziektekostenverzekering — general health insurance levy)** combine into a typical ~**7%** combined rate on commercial-letting turnover (verify at DIMP — band tweaks year to year)
- **Short-term vacation rental** is subject to **BBO + tourism levies** (see Rental section)

### Capital gains

- Aruba **does not impose a stand-alone capital-gains tax** on real-estate disposals by individuals where the property is held privately as a personal asset; commercial / business-asset disposals are taxed under corporate / income-tax regimes
- Verify with notary + tax advisor at deal-stage — particularly for short-hold flips, foreign-corporate vehicles, and Kingdom-tax-residency edge cases

### Corporate / business tax

- **Winstbelasting (profit tax)** corporate rate: **25%** standard (2024–2025 schedule; verify at DIMP)
- **Imputation Payment Company (IPC)** + **Free Zone** + **San Nicolas Special Tax Status** regimes provide targeted reductions — primarily relevant for entity-vehicle property holdings (verify with HBN Law & Tax / Grant Thornton Aruba)

### Future risk

- **2025 income-tax reform** is fresh — full-year implementation feedback + 2026 budget tweaks possible
- **Vacation rental policy** under final-phase legislative draft (2024 announcement; verify status at Government of Aruba — `https://www.gobierno.aw/`) — likely new annual permit fee + AQHA (Aruba Quality & Hospitality Authority) registration
- **OECD BEPS Pillar Two** — small jurisdiction with limited large-MNC exposure but indirect alignment pressures

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term residential

- Civil-law rental framework derived from **Burgerlijk Wetboek van Aruba (BWA)** Book 7 — tenancy provisions modelled on Dutch *huurrecht* with Aruba-specific carve-outs
- **No statutory rent-cap** equivalent to Dutch *puntenstelsel*; market-set rents prevail
- **Standard lease**: 1 year + 1-year automatic renewal common; longer fixed terms negotiable
- **Deposit (*waarborgsom*)**: typically 1–2 months
- **Tenant protections**: notice periods + termination grounds governed by BWA; less protective than Dutch mainland *huurrecht*

### Short-term / vacation rental (Airbnb / vacation villa)

- **Tourist accommodation regulation**: vacation rentals are subject to **BBO 7%** (turnover tax) + **12.5% tourism levy** + **USD 3 per occupied room-night special levy** ([Trippz Aruba tourist-tax guide, 2025](https://trippz.com/tourist-tax/aruba); [BDO Aruba tourist & environmental levy](https://www.bdoaruba.com/en-gb/tax-system-aruba/tax-system-aruba/tourist-environmental-levy), advisory + primary cross-check)
- **Registration with DIMP** required ([Aruba tax-office unregistered-vacation-rental enforcement note, 2024-25](https://arubanews.ca/aruba-rental-property-taxation/); ⚠️ secondary news — enforcement against unregistered Airbnb hosts active 2024-2025 via specialised software collaboration with ATA)
- **Pending vacation-rental policy** (Government of Aruba 2024 announcement — "final phase") — introduces formal permit + annual fee + AQHA (Aruba Quality & Hospitality Authority) registration ([Government of Aruba vacation-rental-policy notice, 2024](https://www.gobierno.aw/en/vacation-rental-policy-is-in-its-final-phase); verify current legislative status)
- **Environmental levy**: USD 20 environmental sustainability fee per visitor (DIMP collected at arrival 2023 onwards; supports infrastructure)

### Yield benchmarks (est.)

- **Long-term gross yield** mid-tier apt 2025 ≈ **5–8%** (higher absolute than European microstates; reflects USD-pegged tourism economy + lower entry prices vs Caribbean luxury islands like Cayman / BVI)
- **Short-let gross yield** Palm Beach / Eagle Beach condo with professional management: **8–12%** range claimed in agency marketing ⚠️ (verify with actual occupancy + ADR + cost stack; agency-published yields are pre-cost / pre-tax)

⚠️ Yield headlines from agency / aggregator marketing tend to be **gross, pre-management-fee, pre-tax**. Realistic NET after 7% BBO + 12.5% tourist levy + USD 3/night + AHATA / management fees (25–35%) + maintenance + insurance + grondbelasting is materially lower — typically **3–5% net** for well-managed Palm Beach condos.

## Section: `--risks`

### Hurricane / tropical storm

- **Aruba sits at ~12.5° N**, **OUTSIDE the Caribbean hurricane belt** (canonical belt 10–25° N concentrated north of 14°). The island is technically inside the belt latitude but the **southern Caribbean / Venezuelan coast track climatology** keeps Aruba in a low-strike-frequency corridor
- **Last direct/near hurricane strike**: **Hurricane Felix (2007)** — passed close but caused minor damage. Prior: tropical storms occasionally graze (Ivan 2004, Tomas 2010) but historic full-hurricane direct hits are **rare (<1 per decade)**
- Per **NOAA / NHC historical hurricane track database** (`https://coast.noaa.gov/hurricanes/`) Aruba/Curaçao/Bonaire (ABC islands) carry one of the **lowest direct hurricane-strike rates in the Caribbean** (qualitative — verify via NOAA HURDAT historical track query for parcel-specific risk)
- **Meteo / hazard monitoring**: **Meteorological Department Aruba** — `https://www.meteo.aw/` — issues local weather + tropical-cyclone advisories; coordinates with NOAA NHC

### Seismic

- Per **PARATUS project (Dutch Caribbean seismic-hazard assessment, 2025)** ([paratus-project.eu seismic-hazard Dutch Caribbean note, 2025](https://www.paratus-project.eu/2025/02/11/seismic-hazard-for-the-dutch-caribbean/), academic): **seismic hazard for Aruba is LOWER** than for Sint Maarten / Saba / Sint Eustatius (northern Dutch Caribbean) due to **Aruba's distance from the active Caribbean–South-American plate-boundary subduction zone** to the south
- Aruba sits on the stable **Aruba block** of the South Caribbean Deformed Belt; felt seismicity is **low-moderate**, dominantly distant events from the southern subduction zone or Venezuelan onshore faulting
- **No equivalent of Italian INGV / Japanese JMA seismic-zonation maps for ABC islands**; building code references Dutch NEN standards adapted for the territory but seismic-detailing intensity is materially below STX / SXM levels

### Coastal erosion / sea level

- **NIA / Directie Infrastructuur en Planning (DIP)** monitors coastal stability — `https://www.dip.aw/` (verify URL liveness)
- Aruba's **leeward (west) coast** (Palm Beach, Eagle Beach, Druif) is the protected resort coast — calmer surf, but beach-sand redistribution + dune-loss observable post-storm + with sea-level rise (~3–4 mm/yr regional). Windward (east) coast (Andicuri, Boca Prins) is rocky / cliff-dominated, naturally erosion-resistant
- **Climate trajectory**: sea-level rise + intensifying tropical-storm rainfall (per IPCC AR6 small-island assessment) — flood / storm-surge insurance trajectory upward for low-lying leeward beach-strip parcels

### Flood

- **Aruba is semi-arid** (annual rainfall ~440 mm) — sustained pluvial flood is **rare** relative to wetter Caribbean islands. Flash-flood signal exists for **rainfall-saturated wadi / cunucu channels** during occasional tropical-system passes
- Storm-surge risk concentrated on west coast resort strip (Palm Beach / Eagle Beach) during rare hurricane near-misses

### Other natural hazards

- **Wildfire**: low — semi-arid, sparse fuel load
- **Volcanic**: NO active volcano on Aruba (unlike Saba / Statia / Mt. Pelée Martinique to the north)
- **Tsunami**: very low — distance from subduction zones; no historic tsunami inundation recorded
- **Drought / water scarcity**: meaningful background hazard — Aruba relies on **desalination** (WEB Aruba seawater RO) for nearly 100% of potable water; drought + supply-chain disruption risk is non-zero (mitigated by WEB capacity + storage reserves)

### Build-era hazards

| Era | Hazards |
|---|---|
| **Pre-1950 (old San Nicolas / Oranjestad core)** | Heritage stock; shallow footings; lead-paint residual; asbestos-cement in 1940s–60s additions; minimal hurricane-rated detailing |
| **1950–1980 refinery boom** | Refinery-worker housing (San Nicolas); concrete-block + galvanised-roof; older stock without modern hurricane straps; legacy asbestos roofing |
| **1980–2010 tourism boom** | Concrete-frame condos (Palm Beach / Eagle Beach high-rises); generally hurricane-engineered (post-Hugo 1989 + Felix 2007 lessons); some EIFS-clad early stock now ageing |
| **2010+** | Modern hurricane-rated concrete-frame; improved energy codes (Aruba moving toward solar-PV integration); cyclone-rated impact-glazing common in premium tier |

### Climate change projections

- Tropical-storm intensification (per IPCC AR6 Caribbean small-island chapter) — **Aruba's strike frequency remains low but per-event severity rises**
- Sea-level rise: ~3–4 mm/yr regional; +0.3–0.5 m by 2050 plausible (SSP2-4.5 to SSP5-8.5 range; IPCC AR6 Caribbean) — material for leeward beach-strip resorts
- Temperature: +1.0–2.5 °C by 2050 (consistent with tropical Atlantic projections)
- Drought variability: ENSO-modulated rainfall variability — dry-year stress on water-supply infrastructure

## Section: `--currency`

### Currency status

- **Aruban florin (AWG / Afl.)** — pegged to **USD at AWG 1.79 = USD 1.00** since 1986 ([Centrale Bank van Aruba — A brief history](https://www.cbaruba.org/cba/getPage.do?page=ABOUT_US_HISTORY): "pegged to the U.S. dollar at a rate of Afl. 1.79 = US$ 1.00. - USD circulates freely alongside AWG in retail + tourism; large-ticket real-estate transactions often dual-quoted / executed in USD

### Capital controls

- **None material** on AWG / USD residents' movement
- AML thresholds aligned with EU-AMLD norms via CBA implementation
- Source-of-funds documentation required for cross-border transfers above AWG 25,000 / USD 14,000 thresholds

## Section: `--visa`

Universal logic per `shared/visa-programs.md`. Aruba-specific:

### Investor Permit (Vergunning tot Tijdelijk Verblijf — Investeerdersregeling)

- **Real-estate investment threshold**: **`est.` USD 200,000** in real property (house, condominium, commercial property or land) — **this figure rests only on commercial residency-marketing sites (tier-4) and is NOT confirmed at a primary source; the statutory minimum is not publicly available at a live primary URL — verify with the *Departamento di Integracion, Maneho y Admision di Stranhero (DIMAS)* — `https://www.gobierno.aw/en/department-for-the-integration-management-and-admission-of-foreign-nationals-dimas` (contact card only) before relying on it**

- **Physical-presence requirement**: light — typically annual renewal visit required; full-time residence NOT required to maintain status (verify at DIMAS)

### Other residence pathways

- **Employed / self-employed permit** — standard employer-sponsored route
- **Family reunification** — spouse / dependent of Aruban / Dutch national

- ⚠️ **Aruba does NOT operate a CBI / golden-passport scheme**. Naturalisation is via the **Kingdom-of-the-Netherlands Rijkswet op het Nederlanderschap** — 5 years lawful continuous residence + Dutch-language proficiency + integration test
- Successful naturalisation grants **Kingdom of the Netherlands citizenship** (Dutch passport — EU national)
- **Dual nationality** is permitted only in specific cases under Dutch nationality law (limited carve-outs; consult immigration lawyer)

## Section: `--notary`

### Process

- **Civil-law notary (*notaris*)** mandatory for all real-estate transfers — Dutch civil-law tradition; profession governed by **Landsverordening op het notarisambt (Aruba notary statute)**
- *Notaris* role: drafts the *akte van levering* (deed of transfer) in **Dutch**, verifies identity (apostilled passport for non-residents), confirms title via *kadastraal uittreksel*, withholds + remits **overdrachtsbelasting (transfer tax)** + Kadaster filing fees, files the deed at the DLV

### Standard timeline (typical residential resale)

| Step | Day | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. *Koopovereenkomst* (preliminary contract) | D0 | Buyer pays **deposit 10%** (held by notaris in escrow); 3-day Dutch-law cooling-off applies for residential consumer buyers |
| 2. AML / source-of-funds review | D7–D30 | Notaris + bank collect documents |
| 3. Mortgage approval (if applicable) | D14–D45 | Bank valuation + approval letter |
| 4. Kadaster *uittreksel* + *meetbrief* | D30–D50 | Notaris orders pre-deed |
| 5. *Levering* (deed signing) at notaris | D45–D90 | Buyer pays balance; notaris withholds **overdrachtsbelasting** |
| 6. Kadaster registration (*inschrijving*) | D90–D100 | Notaris files; title perfected |

⚠️ **Non-resident foreign buyers**: timeline extends to **60–120 days** typical due to (a) **bank AML / source-of-funds review** for non-resident first-time, (b) apostille / legalisation of foreign documents, (c) bank account opening if not pre-arranged.

### Closing-cost stack (buyer side, est. 2026)

| Item | Typical |
|---|---|
| **Overdrachtsbelasting** (transfer tax) | **3% up to AWG 250k + 6% on excess** |
| **Notaris fee** (regulated *kostentarief*) | typically **1.0–1.5%** of price |
| **Kadaster registration + meetbrief** | **AWG 500–2,000** (~USD 280–1,120) flat-ish |
| **Estate agent commission** (where engaged) | typically **3% buyer + 3% seller** (sometimes 2–3% buyer only — confirm in *opdracht*); ARAB MLS schedule reference |
| **Bank mortgage closing fees** | **0.5–1.5%** if financed |
| **Total transaction cost (buyer side, cash, resident)** | **~5–7%** of price (excl. agent if seller-paid) |
| **Total transaction cost (buyer side, financed, foreign)** | **~6–9%** of price |

### Foreign buyer practical

- **Power of attorney** acceptable: notaris accepts apostilled PoA for absent buyers
- **Source-of-funds**: notaris + bank both apply AML / CRS reviews
- **UBO disclosure**: corporate buyers register UBO with Aruba UBO register (in force 2021)
- **Dutch-language closing documents**: *akte van levering* drafted in Dutch; non-Dutch-speaking buyers should engage a sworn translator (*beëdigd vertaler*) for verbatim review

- **Energy transition pressure**: high — current ~81% fossil-fuel electricity is both an emissions issue and a cost issue (USD 0.205/kWh tariff); PV retrofit increasingly economic for residential
- **Water**: 100% desalination — energy-intensive but well-managed via WEB Aruba

### Property-level ESG

- **No mandatory energy-performance certificate** equivalent to EU EPBD at sale (Aruba is OCT, not EU)
- Premium properties increasingly market PV + battery + impact-glass + drought-resilient landscaping as ESG differentiators
- **Retrofit window**: pre-2010 concrete-block + galvanised-roof stock often AC-leaky; PV + insulation + impact-glass retrofit ~USD 150–400/m² for typical Noord villa
- **Hurricane resilience** (impact-glass + concrete + tied roof) increasingly priced into mid-tier as climate insurance trajectory bites

### Verdict (ESG lens for typical mid-altitude Aruba condo)

🟡 **Watch** — energy-intensive AC + desal economy; high electricity tariff drives PV + envelope economics. Climate-resilience premium emerging for impact-glass + tied-roof + PV-equipped stock.

## Caveats unique to Aruba

- **No nationality-based foreign-buyer restriction** — Aruba is among the most open Caribbean regimes for residential acquisition
- **Two land-tenure forms: eigendom (freehold) + erfpacht (long-lease, 60-year)** — verify per parcel at Kadaster
- **Dutch civil-law notarial system** — *akte van levering* in Dutch; foreign-language buyers need sworn translator
- **AWG pegged to USD at 1.79 since 1986** — 40-year peg track record; near-zero FX risk for USD buyers
- **NOT EU member** (OCT under Annex II TFEU); **NOT in Schengen**; Aruban-passport holders are Dutch nationals (Kingdom citizenship)
- **No CBI / golden-passport**, **5-year naturalisation** under Kingdom Rijkswet op het Nederlanderschap
- **Hurricane belt margin position** — lower historic strike rate than Eastern Caribbean but rising climate-trajectory underwriting pressure
- **Electricity cost USD 0.205/kWh** — top-quartile Caribbean; AC + desal economy drives high utility load
- **5-year grondbelasting taxable-value cycle** — *belastbare waarde* often lags market; verify *aanslag-jaar*
- **Single integrated state utility (Utilities Aruba N.V.)** — Elmar + WEB + Setar all state-owned (sole providers in core services)
- **Vacation-rental regulatory tightening** in 2024-2026 legislative pipeline — AQHA registration + permit fee forthcoming
- **No published HPI / Kadaster price index** — cross-reference ARAB MLS + RE/MAX + Coldwell Banker active-listing inventory + Kadaster individual-deal extracts
- **No DTA with US**; **BRK (Belastingregeling voor het Koninkrijk)** coordinates Kingdom-tax residency between AW / CW / SX / NL
- **Bank LTV: ~60–65% for non-resident foreign buyers** — 35–40% down payment is the practical equity bar
- **US Customs Preclearance at AUA** — uncommon Caribbean advantage; simplifies US-bound travel
