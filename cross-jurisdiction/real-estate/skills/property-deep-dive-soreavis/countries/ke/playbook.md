# Kenya 🇰🇪 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `ke`. Status: ✅ Fully populated (researched 2026-05).

## Country profile

- **Postcode**: 5 digits (e.g., `00100` Nairobi GPO, `00200` Nairobi City Square, `00603` Nairobi Lavington, `80100` Mombasa GPO, `40100` Kisumu, `20100` Nakuru, `30100` Eldoret) — first two digits cluster regionally
- **Admin levels**: 47 counties (since 2010 Constitution devolution) → 290 sub-counties → wards. National capital Nairobi = own county. Source: `https://www.cog.go.ke/` (Council of Governors)
- **Population**: ~55M (KNBS 2024 mid-year est.; 2019 Census recorded 47.6M) — verify at `https://www.knbs.or.ke/`
  - Nairobi Metro: ~5M; Mombasa County: ~1.4M; Nakuru: ~600k; Kisumu: ~600k; Eldoret (Uasin Gishu): ~500k
- **Currency**: KES (Kenyan shilling) — managed-float; CBK active intervention; informal USD pricing common in prime Nairobi suburbs (Karen, Lavington, Westlands, Kileleshwa, Runda, Muthaiga, Kitisuru) and coastal premium (Diani, Watamu, Lamu)
- **Languages**: English + Kiswahili (both official, 2010 Constitution Art. 7); major regional: Kikuyu, Luo, Luhya, Kamba, Kalenjin; Swahili dominant on the coast; **English is the language of contracts, courts, and conveyancing**
- **Cadastre — DUAL / TRANSITIONING SYSTEM**:
  - **Ministry of Lands and Physical Planning** at Ardhi House, Nairobi + 47 County Land Registries: `https://www.lands.go.ke/`
  - Four legacy regimes being unified under Land Registration Act 2012: **RTA (Registration of Titles Act)**, **RLA (Registration of Land Act)**, **GLA (Government Lands Act)**, **LTA (Land Titles Act)** — most current titles still cite a legacy register
  - **LIMS (Land Information Management System)** — digitization since 2019; migration to **Ardhisasa** platform (`https://ardhisasa.lands.go.ke/`) ongoing 2024–2026 (Nairobi pilot earliest, county rollout phased — verify status at Ardhisasa for parcel)
  - **Directorate of Surveys — Survey of Kenya** (State Department for Lands; HQ Ruaraka, off Thika Road; no standalone website since `survey.go.ke` was withdrawn): cadastral surveys; the Registry Index Map (RIM) for a parcel is obtained from the Directorate or the County Land Registry where the land is registered — `https://lands.go.ke/` (2026-09-18 verified; the RIM product itself is not described on the ministry site — request it at the Directorate)
- **Identifiers**:
  - **LR No.** (Land Reference Number) — legacy under RTA / GLA (e.g., `LR No. 209/14021`)
  - **Block / Parcel Number** under new RLA / Land Registration Act 2012 (e.g., `Karen/Block 1/123`)
  - **Title Deed (Free Title)** for freehold; **Certificate of Lease** for leasehold (the dominant document for non-citizen holdings)
- **Ownership types**:
  - **Freehold** — outright (`Title Deed`); **citizens only** since 2010 Constitution Art. 65
  - **Leasehold** — max 99 years renewable (`Certificate of Lease`); the **only legal interest a non-citizen can hold** in land
  - **Customary / Community land** — ~13% of land per National Land Commission; held by communities under Community Land Act 2016
  - **Public land** — held by national or county governments per National Land Commission

## 🇰🇪 Foreign buyer eligibility (READ FIRST — deal-killer zone)

### Constitutional regime: 2010 Constitution Art. 65 (FOUNDATIONAL)

The **2010 Constitution of Kenya, promulgated 27 August 2010**, fundamentally restructured land tenure for non-citizens. Source: `https://www.parliament.go.ke/sites/default/files/2017-05/The_Constitution_of_Kenya_2010.pdf`

| Buyer type | Freehold | Leasehold | Agricultural land |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kenyan citizen (incl. naturalised)** | ✅ Allowed | ✅ Allowed | ✅ Allowed |
| **Non-citizen individual** | ❌ **Prohibited** | ✅ **Max 99 yrs** | ❌ Generally prohibited (see Land Control Act) |
| **Company majority Kenyan-owned** | ✅ Treated as citizen | ✅ Allowed | ✅ Allowed |
| **Company majority foreign-owned** | ❌ Treated as non-citizen | ✅ Max 99 yrs | ❌ Restricted |

- **Automatic conversion (Art. 65(2))**: Any freehold held by a non-citizen on **27 August 2010 was automatically converted to a 99-year leasehold** running from that date. Pre-2010 freehold deeds held by foreigners are now leasehold by operation of law — verify at Lands registry before any resale.
- **Agricultural land (Land Control Act Cap 302)**: A non-citizen cannot acquire agricultural land except via a Kenyan-controlled company; **Land Control Board (LCB) consent** is required for any agricultural-land transaction (sale, lease, charge, sub-division). LCB consent fee: KES 1,000 per transaction (verify current fee at Lands portal).
- **Implementing legislation**: Land Act 2012, Land Registration Act 2012, National Land Commission Act 2012, Community Land Act 2016. Source: Kenya Law Reports `http://kenyalaw.org/`

### Practical foreign-buyer path

1. Identify whether the parcel is freehold or leasehold (check Title Deed / Certificate of Lease at Lands).
2. If freehold and buyer is non-citizen → title will be **converted on transfer to a 99-year leasehold from the Government of Kenya** (or sometimes a fresh lease from a Kenyan-citizen seller, structured as a 99-year sublease — verify mechanism with conveyancer).
3. If agricultural → require LCB consent (months-long process; consent denial is real risk in counties with active LCBs).
4. **Sectional Properties Act 2020** allows foreigners to hold long leases (e.g., 99 yrs) in apartment blocks (sectional title) — the workhorse vehicle for non-citizen apartment ownership in Nairobi/Mombasa.

⚠️ **Title fraud + double allocation is a material, frequent risk** in Kenya — see `--risks` and `--notary` sections. Even with a clean Certificate of Lease, a parallel allocation may exist on the older RTA register. Always commission an **official Lands search** (KES 500 per parcel via Ardhisasa or in-person at the County Land Registry) and a **physical site inspection by a licensed surveyor**.

### Compute

1. KES/m² = listing price / advertised m² — clarify floor area vs plot ("plinth area" common in older listings; sectional title quotes interior area)
2. Cross-check HassConsult quarterly mix-adjusted index for the suburb (Nairobi-only; coast not covered by HassConsult)
3. ### Coarse fallback (OSM `highway`)

- `motorway` = Thika Superhighway (A2), Nairobi Expressway (toll), Nairobi Southern Bypass — 50,000–150,000 v/d typical
- `trunk` = A-class (A1, A2, A104, A109 Mombasa Rd) — 20,000–80,000 v/d
- `primary` = B-class + arterials (Ngong Rd, Waiyaki Way, Limuru Rd) — 10,000–40,000 v/d
- `secondary` = suburban arterials (James Gichuru Rd, Argwings Kodhek Rd) — 3,000–15,000 v/d
- `tertiary` = local connector — 500–3,000 v/d
- `residential` = quiet street — < 500 v/d

### Verdict bands

- 🟢 < 1,000 v/d (suburban residential — typical Karen, Runda interiors)
- 🟡 1,000–5,000 v/d (urban side street, Kileleshwa interior)
- 🟠 5,000–20,000 v/d (busy urban arterial — Ngong Rd, Argwings Kodhek)
- 🔴 > 20,000 v/d (A-class trunk, Mombasa Rd, Thika Superhighway, Waiyaki Way)

**Caveat**: KeNHA does NOT publish parcel-level AADT in a single open dataset. For decision-grade traffic counts, request the Project Information Memorandum for the relevant highway from KeNHA. OSM class is reliable for tier; absolute volumes are estimates.

⚠️ **Nairobi Expressway (toll)** opened 2022 (operated by Moja Expressway under PPP with KeNHA): runs Mlolongo–JKIA–Westlands; reduces travel time but adds a toll-cost factor for properties along the Mombasa Rd / Uhuru Highway corridor. Base rates are set by gazette — **Kenya Gazette Notice No. 17419, Public Roads Toll Act (Cap. 407), Vol. CXXV—No. 266, 19 Dec 2023**: Class 3 (saloon) KSh 170–500 per trip excluding VAT depending on entry/exit pair, Class 4 ×1.5, Class 5 ×4, Class 6 ×5, motorcycles and three-wheelers prohibited (2023 gazette; 2026-09-18 verified). ⚠️ The schedule carries an indexation clause — "the Base Toll Rates may be adjusted as per the CPI and Exchange Rate" against a USD:KSh reference of 1:143.7588 at 15 Aug 2023 — so the amount charged today may differ; verify the current schedule at [`https://nairobiexpressway.ke/frequently-asked-questions`](https://nairobiexpressway.ke/frequently-asked-questions).

## Section: `--tax`

### Stamp Duty (one-time, buyer pays) — Stamp Duty Act Cap 480

**Statute**: Stamp Duty Act Cap 480 (KRA advisory only since 2024). **Payment platform (since 2024)**: Ministry of Lands & Physical Planning **Ardhisasa** — `https://ardhisasa.lands.go.ke/`. Buyers must register on Ardhisasa to assess + e-pay stamp duty before LSK can register the transfer. **The legacy KRA URL `https://www.kra.go.ke/` returns HTTP 404 as of 2026-05 — payment workflow migrated to Lands** (2026-05-27 verified, source: shared/regulatory-watch.md KE Ardhisasa migration entry). Statute hosted at `http://kenyalaw.org/`.

| Property type / location | Stamp Duty rate |
|---|---|
| Urban land + buildings (gazetted municipalities) | **4 %** of declared value |
| Agricultural / rural land | **2 %** of declared value |
| Mortgage / charge (additional) | 0.1 % of secured amount |

(Source: Stamp Duty Act Cap 480 — Kenya Law Reports `http://kenyalaw.org/`; payment via Ardhisasa `https://ardhisasa.lands.go.ke/`)

- **Valuation basis**: KRA Valuation Department / Government Valuer assesses; where declared value < market value, KRA applies its own valuation. Under-declaration is endemic and subject to reassessment per KRA enforcement notices.
- **Stamp Duty exemption — first home (proposed Finance Bill 2023, partially enacted)**: certain first-time-buyer reliefs under the Affordable Housing Programme — verify current scheme conditions at the State Department for Housing.

### Capital Gains Tax (CGT) — seller, on disposal

- **Rate**: **15 %** of net gain (raised from 5 % by Finance Act 2022, effective 1 January 2023). Source: KRA CGT: `https://www.kra.go.ke/individual/filing-paying/types-of-taxes/capital-gains-tax`
- Net gain = transfer value − (acquisition cost + capital improvements + incidental costs)
- Primary-residence relief: case-by-case treatment (no broad PPR exemption equivalent to UK). Inheritance + spouse-transfer + group-restructuring exemptions narrowly defined per Income Tax Act Eighth Schedule.
- Filed on iTax via the Capital Gains Tax form within 30 days of transfer.

### Land Rates (annual, owner pays) — county-level under Rates Act Cap 267 + Valuation for Rating Act Cap 266

Annual property tax is **set by each county government** based on the **valuation roll** (typically reviewed every 10 years; many rolls are decades old in practice — Nairobi's 1980 roll was extended for years before the 2019 revaluation).

| County | Rate (residential typical) | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| **Nairobi City County** | ~0.115 % p.a. of unimproved site value (USV) per Valuation Roll | Nairobi Finance Act + `https://nairobi.go.ke/` |
| **Mombasa County** | similar tier on USV per County Finance Act | `https://mombasa.go.ke/` |
| **Kiambu County** (suburban Nairobi) | per County Finance Act | `https://www.kiambu.go.ke/` |
| **Kisumu County** | per County Finance Act | `https://www.kisumu.go.ke/` |
| **Nakuru County** | per County Finance Act | `https://nakuru.go.ke/` |
| **Kwale County** (Diani) | per County Finance Act | `https://kwalecountygov.com/` |
| **Kilifi County** (Malindi/Watamu) | per County Finance Act | `https://kilifi.go.ke/` |

(Verify current rates per county Finance Act each year — county tariff cycles vary.)

⚠️ Land Rates are billed annually; arrears attract penalties + interest; **a Rates Clearance Certificate (Land Rates + Land Rent for leasehold) is required before transfer can register at Lands**. Endemic arrears (especially on inherited / family-held properties) are a frequent transfer-blocker — surface-test before offer.

### Land Rent (annual, leasehold only) — Land Act 2012 + Lands Rent Department

- Annual ground rent paid by leaseholder to the Government of Kenya for parcels on Government leases (most non-citizen-held parcels)
- Typical: KES 200–KES 2,000+ per acre per year for older Government leases (varies by parcel and lease terms — check the lease document)
- Arrears clearance certificate also required before transfer
- Source: Lands portal `https://www.lands.go.ke/`

### VAT — VAT Act 2013

- **Standard rate 16 %** (VAT Act 2013). Temporarily reduced to 14 % from 1 Apr 2020 to 31 Dec 2020 as a COVID stimulus measure; **restored to 16 % from 1 Jan 2021** per Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2020 / Finance Act 2020 (2026-05-27 verified, source: KRA VAT).
- **First sale of a residential dwelling by a developer**: 0 % (zero-rated) under VAT Act
- **Commercial real estate first sale + lease**: 16 %
- **Rental of residential premises**: exempt from VAT (separate Rental Income Tax — see `--rental`)
- Source: KRA VAT `https://www.kra.go.ke/individual/filing-paying/types-of-taxes/value-added-tax`

### Personal Income Tax (rental implications) — Income Tax Act Cap 470

- Graduated PIT after Finance Act 2024 reforms: **10 % / 25 % / 30 % / 32.5 % / 35 %** brackets — top 35 % rate added by Finance Act 2023 (effective 1 July 2023; further refined Finance Act 2024)
- Source: KRA PIT `https://www.kra.go.ke/individual`
- Affordable Housing Levy: **1.5 % of gross salary employer + 1.5 % employee** (Finance Act 2023; **under judicial review 2024–2026** — Court of Appeal upheld revised levy structure but constitutional challenges ongoing; verify current status at `http://kenyalaw.org/`)

### Total transaction cost (buyer side, urban)

For a KES 30,000,000 cash purchase, Nairobi (Karen leasehold residential):
- Stamp Duty (4 %): KES 1,200,000
- Lands registration: KES 5,000
- Lands search + due diligence: ~KES 5,000
- Conveyancer (LSK scale): ~KES 250,000–KES 400,000 (incl. VAT)
- Survey beacon verification: ~KES 50,000
- **Total: ~KES 1,510,000–KES 1,660,000 (~5–5.5 % of price)**

For the same purchase with mortgage (70 % LTV): add mortgage stamp duty 0.1 % of secured amount + bank legal fees ~KES 100,000–KES 200,000 → **total ~6–7 %**.

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term residential rentals

**Governing law**:
- **Rent Restriction Act Cap 296** (caps for rents below KES 2,500/month — narrow scope; effectively legacy)
- **Landlord and Tenant (Shops, Hotels and Catering Establishments) Act Cap 301** (commercial premises)
- **Common law + contract** for most modern residential leases (above the Cap 296 threshold, which captures virtually all market rentals)
- **Sectional Properties Act 2020** (sectional units)

**Key obligations**:
- Written lease standard for any meaningful tenancy
- **Deposit**: typically 1–2 months' rent + 1 month advance (3 months total at move-in is common Nairobi); no statutory escrow scheme like UK TDS — held by landlord
- Notice: typically 1–3 calendar months per lease
- Eviction: Magistrate's Court process; in practice 2–6 months; private "auctioneer" service common but legally fraught — court order mandated for lawful eviction

### Tax treatment — Residential Rental Income Tax (Monthly Rental Income — MRI)

- **Final-tax simplified regime**: **7.5 % of gross rent** for residential rental income between **KES 288,000/yr and KES 15,000,000/yr** (raised threshold via Finance Act 2023 — originally 10 % then reduced to 7.5 %; verify current at KRA)
- Below KES 288,000/yr: exempt
- Above KES 15,000,000/yr: standard PIT at marginal rates with full deductions
- Filed monthly via iTax MRI form by 20th of following month
- Source: KRA Residential Rental Income Tax: `https://www.kra.go.ke/individual/filing-paying/types-of-taxes/residential-rental-income`

### Short-term rentals (STR / Airbnb)

**Status**: regulated but **enforcement uneven**.

- **Tourism Regulatory Authority (TRA)** under Tourism Act 2011 + Tourism Regulations 2014: STR providers technically require a TRA classification + license as "vacation rental" — `https://www.tourismauthority.go.ke/` — verify current registration thresholds
- **County-level tourism levies**: many counties (Nairobi, Kwale, Kilifi) impose tourism / accommodation levies on short-term lets — verify per County Finance Act
- **Body Corporate / sectional rules**: many Nairobi sectional schemes (Westlands, Kilimani) ban STR via by-laws — verify before purchasing for STR purpose
- **Catastrophe Insurance Levy 0.05 %** on STR turnover (insurance-related — verify current under Insurance Act)
- **Practical reality**: informal Airbnb market is large in Karen, Westlands, Kilimani, Diani, Watamu, Lamu — formal TRA registration is the minority; KRA pursues income-tax compliance via platform data-sharing under Finance Act 2023 digital-marketplace withholding (verify current implementation)

**Tax treatment for STR**:
- If casual: standard rental tax (MRI 7.5 % up to KES 15M turnover)
- If operated as trade: standard PIT/CIT with full deductions
- VAT 16 % registration mandatory if turnover > **KES 5,000,000/12 months** (current threshold per KRA — verify; threshold history: KES 5M since 2018)

**Strategic notes**:
- Coastal seasonality strong: Dec–Mar peak (European winter), Jul–Aug shoulder (Northern Hemisphere summer); long rains Apr–May are low season
- Diani + Watamu yields among strongest on East African coast; Lamu archipelago premium-niche but security advisories persist (Northern coast — see `--risks`)
- Karen + Westlands + Lavington dominate Nairobi STR; weekly business-traveller demand (UN, NGOs, multinationals) supports midweek occupancy

  - **Carjacking** historic + ongoing in Nairobi; gated communities + electric perimeter fence + 24-hr armed guard standard for premium estates
  - **Petty theft / muggings** in CBD + downmarket Eastlands at night
  - **Al-Shabaab cross-border attacks** in Northern Kenya (Garissa, Mandera, Wajir, parts of Lamu County) — UK FCDO + US State Department maintain "do not travel" / "reconsider travel" advisories for these zones; **affects coastal Lamu archipelago specifically** (mainland Lamu Island generally safe; outer islands + Boni Forest hinterland advisory zones)
  - **Protests + civil unrest**: 2023 + 2024 (June 2024 anti-Finance-Bill protests) — periodic; transit + business disruption; rare property impact in residential areas
  - Source: National Police Service `https://www.nationalpolice.go.ke/`; SAPS-style suburb-level data not published — use neighbourhood inquiry + private security firm coverage (G4S, KK Security/GardaWorld, Securex) as practical proxy
- **Floods**:
  - **Long rains Mar–May + short rains Oct–Dec**; Nairobi urban flooding is annually recurrent in low-lying neighbourhoods (Mathare, parts of Eastlands, Roysambu, riparian Nairobi River corridor)
  - **East Africa Floods 2024 (Mar–May 2024)** — catastrophic; KMD declared one of the wettest seasons on record; landslides + dam-burst events in Mai Mahiu (Old Kijabe Dam, ~50 fatalities)
  - **Lake Victoria + Lake Naivasha rising**: 2020–2021 lake-level surges submerged shoreline properties in Naivasha, Bogoria, Baringo, parts of Kisumu — verify shoreline elevation
  - **Tana River Basin** (coastal hinterland) seasonal flooding
- **Drought**: 2020–2023 worst drought in 4 decades (NDMA); 23 ASAL counties drought-shocked; affects rural / agricultural property economics
- **Tectonic / seismic**: Rift Valley moderate seismic hazard; **Kenya straddles the East African Rift** — moderate quakes M4–M5 occasional in Nakuru, Eldoret, Naivasha corridor; major M6+ events historically rare but possible. Source: Geological Survey of Kenya
- **Landslide**: rainy-season landslides in Murang'a, Nyeri, Meru, Bungoma, Elgeyo-Marakwet — escarpment counties with steep slopes + heavy rains
- **Forest fires**: less material than ZA; periodic in Mt Kenya forest, Aberdares, Mau Forest dry-season
- **Mau Forest deforestation + Lake Naivasha lowering** (long-term water-stress driver) — affects rural / horticultural property
- **Coastal erosion + storm surge**: Mombasa, Kilifi, Lamu shorelines progressively impacted; SLR projections 30–80 cm by 2100 (IPCC AR6 + KMD)

### Key context

Kenya is a **moderate-to-high crime environment by global standards** — UNODC 2018 estimates put intentional homicide at **~5.0 per 100,000** (above the global median but well below Latin American + Southern African peers — UNODC 2018, >12 months old; rates/figures may have changed since). Property crime + carjacking is the dominant concern; **homicides are concentrated in informal urban settlements + arid northern counties** (banditry + cattle-rustling). **Premium gated suburbs** (Karen, Runda, Muthaiga, Kitisuru, Lavington) — protected by 24-hr armed guard + electric perimeter fence + alarm + armed-response — operate at far lower risk than the average national rate.

### Specific risk vectors

- **Carjacking**: Nairobi urban arterials at night; Mombasa Rd corridor; periodic clusters in Eastlands + along Thika Road
- **Petty theft + mugging**: CBD nightlife zones; Eastlands; bus terminals (River Road, Tea Room)
- **Burglary**: lower in gated estates; higher in single-family stand-alone outside electric perimeter
- **Cybercrime + digital fraud**: M-Pesa SIM-swap fraud, "wash-wash" 419-style scams (Ministry of ICT + Communications Authority of Kenya have legislated under Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act 2018)
- **Land fraud / title fraud**: see `--notary` — endemic and material; criminal penalties under Penal Code + Land Registration Act 2012 sec 80
- **Terrorism (al-Shabaab)**: targeted historical attacks (Westgate 2013, Garissa University 2015, DusitD2 2019); current threat level **MODERATE** with active counter-terrorism by KDF + NPS + NIS; specific advisories for **Lamu County, Garissa, Mandera, Wajir, Tana River, Kilifi north of Malindi**
- **Civil unrest**: 2023 (anti-tax) + Jun 2024 (anti-Finance-Bill, "Gen Z protests") — episodic; rare property impact

### Verdict bands (KE-specific — vs national average)

- 🟢 < 50% national rate (premium gated estates with armed response: Karen, Runda, Muthaiga, Kitisuru interiors)
- 🟡 50–110% national rate (typical mid-tier residential — Lang'ata, South B/C, Kileleshwa)
- 🟠 110–200% one+ category (urban arterials at night; Eastlands typical)
- 🔴 > 200% national in multiple categories (informal settlements + restive northern counties + UK FCDO red-zone areas)

### Confidence

MEDIUM. Kenya does NOT publish per-station or per-suburb open crime data. Aggregate trends, FCDO advisories, neighbourhood inquiry, and private security-firm coverage maps are the practical reference. **Walk the area at night with a local fixer + verify private security response coverage before any premium-suburb purchase**.

## Section: `--currency`

### Kenyan Shilling (KES) — managed-float framework

- **Regime**: managed float; CBK actively intervenes via FX reserves + open-market operations
- **Currency code**: KES; symbol KSh
- **Q1 2026 reference**: 1 USD ≈ KES 128–135 range (verify daily — significant 2022–2024 volatility; KES depreciated from ~115 to peak ~165 in early 2024 then partially recovered to ~130 range mid-2024 onwards); 1 EUR ≈ KES 140–150
- **Inflation Q1 2026**: ~6–8 % CPI (KNBS) — verify current at `https://www.knbs.or.ke/`
- **Reserves**: CBK maintains ~4–5 months of import cover (verify current at CBK statistical bulletin)
- Source: CBK rates `https://www.centralbank.go.ke/rates/forex-exchange-rates/`

### What this means for buyers

- **USD-denominated buyers**: meaningful FX risk on purchase + ongoing remittance; informal premium-Nairobi pricing already in USD partially hedges this
- **EUR / GBP buyers**: full FX exposure to KES; consider USD-leg hedge
- **Practical hedging**: forward contracts via major banks (Stanchart, Absa, Stanbic, NCBA) for USD/KES legs > USD 100k; typical 6–12-month forward bid-ask 50–150 bps
- **Informal USD pricing in prime Nairobi** (Karen, Lavington, Westlands premium, Runda, Muthaiga, Kitisuru) and coast premium (Diani, Watamu, Lamu): purchase commonly contracted in USD with KES equivalence at signing date — protects seller from KES depreciation

### Capital controls

- **Outbound FX**: liberalised; no residency or amount cap for repatriation of sale proceeds, BUT KRA tax clearance + CBK reporting required for transfers > USD 10,000 equivalent
- **Source-of-funds**: required for inbound transfers > USD 10,000 under Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act (POCAMLA) 2009 + amendments
- **Foreign Exchange Act Cap 528** (1967) provides framework; CBK guidelines liberal in practice
- **Sale proceeds repatriation for non-resident sellers**: tax-clearance from KRA + CBK reporting; typically achievable but document early

⚠️ **KES tail risk**: 2023–2024 sharp depreciation cycle (115 → 165 → 130) is a recent reminder of single-direction-pressure events on the KES. Foreign buyers should consider FX-hedge or USD-pricing structure for transactions above USD 250k.

## Section: `--notary`

### Conveyancing process — common-law solicitor system

Kenya inherits the **English common-law solicitor / advocate system** (vs civil-law notary). Property transfers are handled by **Advocates of the High Court of Kenya** (qualified to practise; member of the Law Society of Kenya). The Law Society of Kenya regulates: `https://www.lsk.or.ke/`

**Key roles**:
- **Vendor's advocate**: prepares Sale Agreement, Transfer instrument, vendor's title-search confirmation
- **Purchaser's advocate**: conducts due diligence, official Lands search, registers transfer at Lands
- **Lands Registrar** (per County): registers the transfer + issues new Title Deed / Certificate of Lease
- **Government Valuer (KRA)**: assesses Stamp Duty value
- **Land Control Board (where applicable — agricultural)**: consents to transfer
- **County Government (Rates, Land Rent)**: issues clearance

### Standard timeline (typical residential urban transfer)

| Step | Day | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Letter of Offer / Reservation | D0 | Indicative deposit (typically 5–10 %) — non-binding pending due diligence |
| 2. Official Lands search | D0–D7 | KES 500/parcel via Ardhisasa or in-person — confirms registered owner, encumbrances, charges |
| 3. Sale Agreement | D7–D21 | Drawn by vendor's advocate; deposit (typically 10 %) into vendor advocate's client account |

| 5. LCB consent (if agricultural) | D30–D90+ | Land Control Board sitting — **major delay risk** |
| 6. Stamp Duty payment | D45–D75 | **Ardhisasa stamp-duty assessment + 4 % / 2 % e-paid via Ardhisasa** (statute Stamp Duty Act Cap 480; KRA advisory only since 2024 migration — 2026-05-27 verified) |
| 7. Transfer execution + lodgement | D60–D90 | Transfer instrument signed at Lands; new Title Deed / Certificate of Lease issued |
| 8. KRA + tax clearance | D75–D120 | Capital Gains Tax filing by seller; transfer-of-name to county for rates |

### Title fraud + double allocation — material risk

⚠️ **Title fraud (forged title deeds, parallel registration on legacy registers, fake conveyances) is a real, frequent, and material risk in Kenya**. Documented in Lands ministry audits + Court of Appeal rulings:

- **Double allocation**: same parcel registered to two different parties on different legacy registers (RTA vs RLA vs GLA) — surface only at conveyancing or after years
- **Forged title deeds**: counterfeit Title Deeds presented to bona fide purchasers; sometimes with insider Lands collusion (multiple cases prosecuted under Penal Code + Land Registration Act 2012 sec 80)
- **"Sand-witched" parcels**: encroachment on adjoining parcels via incorrect cadastral demarcation
- **National Land Commission revocations**: NLC has revoked titles previously irregularly issued (esp. former public land + community land); **revocation risk persists for titles tracing to public-land allocations 1990s–2000s**

Mitigants:
1. Official Lands search at the County Land Registry **and** Ardhisasa where digitised — verify both
2. Beacon verification by **registered land surveyor** (Survey of Kenya register) + on-site walk
3. Title chain trace via Lands archive — **verify back to the original Government allocation** if possible
4. Engage two independent advocates (vendor + purchaser, never shared) — LSK rules require separation
5. Title insurance is **available but limited** — Kenya has small title-insurance market via Britam, ICEA LION (case-by-case underwriting); not a substitute for due diligence
6. Beneficial-ownership disclosure for any company-vehicle vendor (BO Regulations 2020)

⚠️ The Lands ministry has launched **Ardhisasa** (`https://ardhisasa.lands.go.ke/`) since 2021–2024 to digitise titles + cadastre; rollout is phased (Nairobi + Murang'a piloted earliest); **digital title is the gold standard going forward**, but legacy paper titles still dominate outside Nairobi/Murang'a — verify both digital + paper records.

### Retirement to Kenya scenario

Kenya is an **established retirement destination** for European + American retirees, particularly on the coast (Diani, Watamu, Malindi) and in Nairobi premium suburbs (Karen, Runda). - **No specific retirement-named visa** (unlike Portugal D7, Spain Non-Lucrative)

- 3-bed detached Karen / Runda rental: KES 250,000–KES 600,000/month (~USD 1,900–4,600)
- 3-bed Diani Beach rental: KES 100,000–KES 300,000/month (~USD 770–2,300)
- 2-bed Lavington apartment rental: KES 120,000–KES 250,000/month (~USD 920–1,920)

- Eating out: mid-range KES 1,500–3,000/meal; high-end KES 5,000+
- Utilities: KES 12,000–KES 35,000/month (KPLC + water + internet + gas)

### Tax position for retirees

- **No tax on foreign pension income** received in Kenya (per Income Tax Act + various DTAAs — verify per home-country DTAA)
- **PIT applies to Kenyan-source income** at graduated rates (top 35 % under Finance Act 2023)
- **CGT 15 %** on Kenyan property disposal
- **Stamp Duty 4 %/2 %** on property purchase
- **VAT 16 %** standard
- **Affordable Housing Levy 1.5 % + 1.5 %** on Kenyan-employment salary (under judicial review) — not applicable to foreign passive income
- **No wealth tax, no general inheritance tax** (Estate Duty Act repealed 1982); BUT **CGT 15 %** applies on transfer-on-death where deemed disposal applies — verify with advocate
- DTAAs with UK, Germany, Canada, India, France, several others — verify per pension origin

- **Public**: Kenyatta National Hospital (Nairobi referral), Coast General Hospital (Mombasa), county hospitals — variable quality
- **SHIF (Social Health Insurance Fund)** since 1 Oct 2024 (replaced NHIF) — for residents incl. permanent residents; verify retiree eligibility at `https://www.sha.go.ke/`
- **Private**: world-class for tropical medicine + general — Aga Khan University Hospital, Nairobi Hospital, MP Shah, Karen Hospital, Mater Misericordiae; coastal: Aga Khan Mombasa
- **International medical evacuation insurance** (AAR, Jubilee, AMREF Flying Doctors, MEDPAC) common for expats — annual ~KES 50,000–KES 200,000

### Verdict for retirement-first buyers

🟢 **Go** for established expat-community zones (Diani, Watamu, Malindi coast; Karen, Runda, Lavington Nairobi) — mature ecosystem, English-language legal + medical, USD-pricing partial hedge, attractive lifestyle, strong NGO/UN community in Nairobi. **🟡 Watch** for: title fraud + double allocation risk (mandates careful conveyancing); KES depreciation tail; security perimeter (premium gated essential in Nairobi); coastal Lamu County security advisories.

## Section: `--digital-nomad`

### Visa pathway

Kenya has **NO formal "digital nomad visa"** (a notable absence vs Mauritius PA + Cape Verde + Seychelles in regional emerging-market peers).

Workable pathways for remote-working visitors:

1. **eVisa (Tourist)** — 90 days/visit, multi-entry — work technically prohibited, short remote-stay tolerated; eVisa system since 2024 via `https://etakenya.go.ke/`
2. **Class K (Independent Means)** — KES 200,000+/month verifiable foreign passive income — most legitimate path; 5-yr renewable
3. **Class A1 (Investor)** — USD 100,000+ in Kenyan business — for those running a business AND working remote
4. **Class D (Employer-sponsored)** — for those with Kenyan employer

⚠️ Tourism is the de facto channel for short-stay remote workers; for stays > 90 days or ongoing, Class K is the recommended legal path.

### Connectivity

- **Internet**: fibre FTTH widespread in Nairobi premium suburbs (Karen, Lavington, Westlands, Kileleshwa, Runda partial) + Mombasa coast premium areas; Safaricom Home Fibre + Zuku Fibre + Faiba (Jamii Telecom) dominant; **gigabit available** Karen / Westlands; coastal Diani decent fibre + Safaricom 5G
- **Mobile**: Safaricom (~70 % market share), Airtel, Telkom; 5G live in Nairobi + Mombasa + Kisumu since 2022 (Safaricom)
- **Cost**: 100 Mbps home fibre KES 5,000–KES 8,000/month; mobile bundles 50 GB ~KES 2,500–KES 5,000/month
- **Co-working**: Nairobi Garage (multi-site), Workstyle Africa, Senteu Plaza, iHub (CBD), The Mint Hub, regus-Spaces — KES 25,000–KES 60,000/month hot desk
- **Time zone**: UTC+3 (East Africa Time) — favours EU + Asia overlap; 7-hour gap to US East Coast

### Cost-of-living for remote worker (Q1 2026)

- Studio Westlands / Kilimani rental: KES 50,000–KES 90,000/month (~USD 385–690)
- 1-bed Diani Beach rental: KES 60,000–KES 150,000/month
- Mid-range eating-out: KES 1,500–3,000/meal
- Single-meal local: KES 300–800 (nyama choma + ugali + sukuma)
- Monthly transport: Bolt / Uber dominant in Nairobi/Mombasa; KES 15,000–KES 40,000/month typical
- Co-living: emerging — Talanta House, Kilimani Hub — KES 80,000–180,000/month

### Tax position

- **PIT applies only to Kenya-source income** for non-residents (residence threshold: 183+ days/yr OR principal home in Kenya)
- **Tourist visit (90 days)** → no PIT exposure if no Kenya-source income
- **183-day residency** triggers Kenyan tax residence on worldwide income (subject to DTAA reliefs); Class K holders typically structure to remain non-resident for tax where DTAA permits
- **VAT 16 %** on services + consumption
- **Catastrophe Insurance Levy + Affordable Housing Levy 1.5 %**: salary-based, not applicable to non-Kenyan salaries

### Verdict for digital-nomad-first buyers

🟡 **Watch / Adapt** — Kenya is favorable for established remote workers (strong fibre + coworking + tropical lifestyle + cost-effective premium suburbs), but **no DNV** + **183-day tax-residence trap** + **title fraud risk + KES volatility** mean medium friction. **Diani Beach + Watamu** are excellent coastal bases; **Karen + Westlands** for Nairobi-base. Consider Mauritius PA visa for clean DNV-equivalent + safer property regime if Kenya-based work is not specifically required.

- **Affordable Housing Levy 1.5 % + 1.5 %** — under judicial review 2024–2026; modified Finance Act 2024 + Court of Appeal rulings — final disposition pending; verify at `http://kenyalaw.org/`
- **CGT 15 %** on disposal (raised from 5 % by Finance Act 2022) — seller pays
- **Rental Income Tax (MRI) 7.5 %** on residential gross rent KES 288k–15M
- **VAT 16 %** on commercial; first-sale residential by developer 0 %; rental of residential exempt
- **Water rationing in Nairobi** — Ndakaini reservoir + Athi water source variable; almost all premium Nairobi homes have rooftop tanks + boreholes regardless of NCWSC connection
- **KPLC outages + load-management** — backup generators + solar+inverter standard for premium homes (KES 400k–3M)
- **Premium Nairobi USD-pricing convention** in Karen / Lavington / Westlands / Runda / Muthaiga / Kitisuru — partial FX hedge

- **No property-linked golden visa** — Kenya has no RBI/CBI via real estate; Class K (KES 200k+/month foreign income) is the practical retirement path
- **Coastal Lamu County security advisory** — UK FCDO + US State Department maintain heightened advisories for parts of northern coast; affects insurance + rental yield

- **Konza Technopolis** — anchor smart-city south of Nairobi; greenfield; long-term execution risk
- **Building-collapse risk** — informal-sector mid-rise; verify NCA contractor + structural engineer's stamp before off-plan / new-build commitment
