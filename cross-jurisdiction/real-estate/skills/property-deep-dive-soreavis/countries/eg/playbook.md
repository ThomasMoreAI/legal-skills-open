# Egypt 🇪🇬 — Property Due-Diligence Playbook

ISO2: `eg`. Status: PARTIALLY RESTRICTED for foreign buyers + currency-volatile (EGP managed-float post-IMF EFF Mar 2024). Researched 2026-04 / 2026-05.

## Country profile

- **Population**: ~110 million (2024 CAPMAS estimate, source `https://www.capmas.gov.eg/`); largest Arab-world country by population, 95%+ concentrated along Nile Valley + Delta + coastal strips
- **Currency**: EGP (Egyptian Pound) — **managed float** since 6 March 2024 (IMF EFF approval USD 8 bn). Trajectory: EGP ~15.7/USD (2021) → EGP ~30/USD (early Mar 2024) → EGP ~50/USD (post-float Mar 2024) → EGP ~50–51/USD (2026-05-01) → all-time low ~54.9/USD (Mar 2026, Trading Economics intraday) → EGP ~49/USD (2026-07-02, CBE official 49.08 buy / 49.18 sell) → EGP ~51.4/USD (2026-09-23, CBE average market rate 51.3749 buy / 51.4749 sell) (CBE rates, source `https://www.cbe.org.eg/en/economic-research/statistics/exchange-rates`)
- **Languages**: Arabic (official, MSA + Egyptian colloquial); English widely used in business, tourism, and gated-community sales offices; French in older elite circles
- **Legal system**: Hybrid — civil law (Napoleonic-influenced via 1949 Civil Code) + Sharia for personal status (family, inheritance). Property/contract law is civil-code; **inheritance for Muslims is mandatory Sharia shares** (foreigners may opt for home-country law via prior declaration but Sharia applies by default to any Egyptian-titled real estate of a Muslim decedent)
- **Cadastre / registry**: **Real Estate Publicity Authority (REPA — مصلحة الشهر العقاري والتوثيق)** under Ministry of Justice — `https://sherpa.gov.eg/` ❌ DEPRECATED — link dead (verified 2026-06-13); verify with moj.gov.eg (REPA under the Ministry of Justice — co-cited) and `https://moj.gov.eg/`. Two parallel registration systems still in transition:
  - **Sijil al-shakhsi** (personal/deed-recording, السجل الشخصي) — older, name-based, ~95%+ of Egyptian properties historically
  - **Sijil al-‘ayni** (real/property-based, السجل العيني, "Torrens-like" parcel-based) — modernization since 1964, very low coverage (~5–10% nationally; mostly New Cairo, NAC, North Coast new developments)
  - **Practical implication**: most older properties have only a notarized contract chain (`عقد ابتدائي + عقد نهائي`) NOT a state-guaranteed title. Title insurance and chain-of-deed verification are critical
- **Major reforms 2023–2024**:
  - **Citizenship by Investment — Law 140/2019** (number per GAFI's citizenship application form; some secondary sources cite 190/2019) (foundational CBI statute, GAFI-administered) + **Prime Ministerial Decree 876/2023 (2 March 2023)** implementing thresholds + **Law 26/1975 Articles 6+11 amendments (April 2023)** (nationality-law constitutional basis); **September 2023 Cabinet reversal restored the real-estate threshold to USD 500,000** (from the USD 300k March 2023 cut) (2026-05-27 verified, source [Library of Congress 2023-05-07](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2023-05-07/egypt-house-of-representatives-approves-new-amendments-to-citizenship-law/) + SIS.gov.eg + africanews.com). Note **Law 160/2023** is a SEPARATE statute amending Investment Law 72/2017 — governs investment incentives (Golden License, tax refunds, free zones), NOT CBI thresholds.
  - **EGP free-float March 2024** + IMF Extended Fund Facility (USD 8 bn) — collapsed parallel-market premium overnight
  - **Real Estate Transaction Tax (RETT) — Law 30/2023** (15 June 2023, amends Income Tax Law 91/2005) — 2.5% on official tax-authority-assessed value OR actual sale price (whichever **higher**); raised first-home exemption EGP 2M → EGP 3M for properties under 200 m² (2026-05-27 verified, source PwC Egypt Law 30/2023 PDF + Wafeq + Lexology). NB: **Law 175/2023** is the personal-income-tax amendment (raised PIT exemption to EGP 45,000/year, cancelled 2.5% PIT bracket); NOT a property-CGT statute.
  - **Real Estate Tax (Law 196/2008) — 2024–2026 amendments** raised the **primary-residence (owner-occupied) exemption to EGP 100,000/year of net annual rental value OR EGP 8M property value** (from the EGP 24,000/year general floor). Per Daily News Egypt 2026-02-15: only ~2M of 55M Egyptian properties now liable (2026-05-27 verified, source Wafeq + Daily News Egypt + Nawy blog).
- **Foreign-buyer regime**: PARTIALLY RESTRICTED — see `## Foreign buyer eligibility` below

## Foreign buyer eligibility

**The single most consequential section of this playbook for non-Egyptian buyers.** Egypt is partially restricted under **Law 230/1996** (تنظيم تملك غير المصريين للعقارات المبنية والأراضي الفضاء) and several overlapping security/zone decrees. Misreading these limits voids title.

### Law 230/1996 — core limits

(Primary source: Ministry of Justice consolidated text `https://moj.gov.eg/`; secondary commentary GAFI `https://www.gafi.gov.eg/english/StartaBusiness/Laws-and-Regulations/Pages/default.aspx`)

| Limit | Threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Max properties per foreigner** | **2** | Counted across all of Egypt (one or both may be jointly with spouse) |
| **Max area per property** | **4,000 m²** | Combined plot + building footprint |
| **Holding period before resale** | **5 years** minimum | Waivable only by **Prime Ministerial Decree** under Law 230/1996 (per the special-resolution mechanism — opaque + slow in practice; 2026-05-27 verified, source Al Tamimi & Co + Youssry Saleh) |
| **Vacant residential land — construction obligation** | Foreigner must **start construction within 5 years from REPA registration** (Law 230/1996) | Failure to start construction triggers further restrictions |
| **Use restriction** | Residence by buyer / family | NOT pure speculation; commercial use needs separate licensing |
| **Forbidden zones** | See list below | Title void if violated |

### Forbidden / restricted zones for foreign ownership

- **Sinai Peninsula (entire)** — North Sinai + South Sinai governorates (incl. Sharm El Sheikh, Dahab, Nuweiba, Taba). Special security regime under **Sinai Decree 2018** (Presidential Decree 444/2018 designating border/strategic areas). Foreigners can hold **usufruct (حق انتفاع) up to 50 years renewable** in designated tourist zones (e.g., parts of Sharm El Sheikh) but NOT freehold. (Source: Presidential Decree 444/2018 + amendments)
- **Border governorates without security clearance** — Matrouh (parts), Red Sea (parts), New Valley, Aswan border belts: case-by-case Ministry of Defense + Interior security review
- **Within 5 km of Suez Canal** — restricted under canal-zone strategic regulations
- **Designated military / strategic zones** — case-by-case; gazetted by Presidential Decree (recent expansions 2017–2023 for "national projects" lands)
- **Agricultural land (الأراضي الزراعية)** — **Egyptian nationals only** under Law 15/1963 (still in force despite reform proposals). Foreigners cannot own farmland in any quantity, irrespective of Law 230/1996 quotas
- **Antiquities buffer zones** — UNESCO/SCA-designated heritage perimeters (Giza Plateau, Luxor West Bank, etc.) have separate restrictions

### Law 140/2019 (CBI foundational; GAFI form — some secondary sources cite 190/2019) — Residency / Citizenship by Investment (RBI / CBI)

Foundational statute: **Law No. 140 of 2019** regulating the process of applying for Egyptian citizenship, per GAFI's own citizenship application form (primary); some secondary guides cite it as Law No. 190 of 2019 — verify the number in the Official Gazette. Implementing thresholds: **Prime Ministerial Decree 876/2023 (2 Mar 2023)** initially cut the real-estate threshold from USD 500k → USD 300k; **Cabinet decision 14 Sept 2023 restored it to USD 500,000**. Constitutional basis: **April 2023 House of Representatives amendments to Articles 6 + 11 of Law 26/1975 (Nationality Law)** (2026-05-27 verified, source Library of Congress + SIS.gov.eg + africanews.com + BPROL).

| Path | Threshold | Outcome | Holding | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-refundable government donation** | USD 250,000 | **Citizenship** per GAFI form (US State Dept 2025 ICS: permanent residency — unreconciled) | n/a | Forfeited to state |
| **Real estate** | **USD 500,000** in real estate owned by the State or public legal persons per GAFI form (Sept 2023 restoration per LoC/SIS; US State Dept 2025 ICS still gives USD 300,000 — unreconciled) | **Citizenship** per GAFI form (US State Dept 2025 ICS: permanent residency — unreconciled) | Hold property ≥3 yrs typical | Must be FROM the foreigner's USD inflow via Form 4 (see Currency section) (2026-05-27 verified) |
| **Business investment** | USD 350,000 **+** USD 100,000 per US State Dept 2025 ICS (Decree 876/2023) / GAFI form: USD 400,000 at min 40% of project capital — unreconciled | **Citizenship** per GAFI form (US State Dept 2025 ICS: permanent residency — unreconciled) | Project execution | GAFI-approved sectors; total ~USD 450k |
| **Bank deposit (CBI variant)** | USD 500,000 3-yr per US State Dept 2025 ICS (Decree 876/2023) / GAFI form: **USD 1,000,000 refundable after 3 yrs** or **USD 750,000 refundable after 5 yrs** (in EGP, no interest) — unreconciled | **Citizenship** per GAFI form (US State Dept 2025 ICS: permanent residency — unreconciled) | 3-year lock (ICS 500k / GAFI 1M) or 5-year lock (GAFI 750k); refunded in EGP without interest per GAFI form | NOT non-refundable / forfeited (the USD 250k donation tier is the non-refundable option) (2026-05-27 verified) |
| **State fee** | **USD 10,000** on top of all routes | — | — | Application/processing fee |

NB: **Law 160/2023 is a separate statute** (amends Investment Law 72/2017) governing investment incentives (Golden License, tax refunds, free zones, Special Incentives extended to Oct 2026) — it does NOT govern CBI thresholds.

**Verification path before relying on any of these tiers**:
1. GAFI English investor portal `https://www.gafi.gov.eg/english/Pages/default.aspx`
2. The thresholds **changed substantially in 2023–2024** — confirm current USD figures AND the immigration outcome in writing with the Citizenship Applications Assessment Unit before transferring funds — GAFI's citizenship application form frames every Decree-3099/2019 route as citizenship, while the US State Dept 2025 Investment Climate Statement frames the Decree-876/2023 routes as permanent residency (unreconciled)
3. Use a **GAFI-licensed agent** (not a developer's in-house lawyer) for the citizenship file

**est. fees on top of investment threshold**: ~USD 10,000–25,000 in legal + government fees, varies by path (verify with GAFI; published fee schedules are not always current online)

### Sinai usufruct workaround (when freehold is forbidden)

In Sinai tourist zones (e.g., parts of Sharm El Sheikh, Dahab, El Gouna-style Sinai-side projects), foreigners commonly hold:
- **حق انتفاع (usufruct)** — up to 50 years, renewable
- Title remains with the Egyptian state / Tourism Development Authority (TDA)
- Bank financing extremely limited; resale market thinner than freehold

Buyers of "Sinai apartments" should verify: is this freehold (likely VOID for foreigner) or usufruct (legal but not equivalent to ownership)? Many sales offices conflate the two.

### Critical caveat — title diligence even when eligible

Even when a foreign buyer is fully within Law 230/1996 quotas + outside forbidden zones, the **sijil al-shakhsi vs sijil al-‘ayni** problem (above) means many "title deeds" presented at signing are actually a chain of notarized sale contracts (`عقد ابتدائي`) NOT a state-guaranteed Torrens-style title. **Demand a REPA real-property registration (`شهر بالسجل العيني`) where available**, especially in NAC, New Cairo, North Coast new developments where it now is.

### Compute

1. Listing price/m² = listing EGP / built-up m². **Clarify "built-up" (مسطح) vs "net usable" (صافي)** — Egyptian listings often advertise built-up incl. shared walls + lobby allocation, sometimes ~15–25% larger than net usable
2. Convert to USD at **CBE official rate** (`https://www.cbe.org.eg/en/economic-research/statistics/exchange-rates`) — not at parallel/black-market rate (parallel market collapsed post-Mar 2024 float, but premium can re-emerge during EGP stress)
3. Compare against the asking-side band table above for the relevant market
4. **Trap — dual pricing**: sellers in foreign-buyer markets (North Coast, El Gouna, Sharm) often quote in **USD or EUR with EGP listed only for show**. The USD price is what they actually expect. Confirm currency of payment IN WRITING before signing
5. **Trap — off-plan installment price ≠ cash price**: a "USD 100k" off-plan unit on a 7-year installment is often available at ~USD 70–80k cash. Always ask for the cash equivalent
6. **Trap — declared vs actual price**: REPA-declared price (for stamp duty + CGT) is often 30–50% below actual cash price in EGP-domestic resale; for foreign USD-cash buyers via Form 4, declaration is closer to actual

### Confidence

MEDIUM — asking-price listings are abundant, but **transaction-verified price data is not publicly available in Egypt**. REPA records exist but are not a queryable public DB. Foreign buyers should commission a local broker for 3–5 sold-comparable estimates.

## Section: `--traffic`

### Primary sources

- **General Authority for Roads, Bridges and Land Transport (GARBLT)** — `https://garblt.gov.eg/` ❌ DEPRECATED — dead on 2026-09-18; verify with the authority named in this row — national highway network operator; publishes annual traffic flow summaries for tolled highways (Cairo–Alexandria Desert Road, Cairo–Suez, Cairo–Sokhna, regional ring roads)
- **Ministry of Transport** — `https://mot.gov.eg/` — sectoral statistics
- **CAPMAS Transport & Communications Yearbook** — `https://www.capmas.gov.eg/` — annual: vehicle registrations by governorate, road km by class, accident statistics
- **Cairo Traffic Control Center / Cairo Governorate** — operational, NOT public-data

### Key term

**No standardized AADT-equivalent published per-segment** comparable to FR TMJA, DE DTV, UK AADT. Egyptian traffic data is mostly published as **annual aggregate flows on tolled corridors**, plus **vehicle-registration density per governorate** (a poor proxy).

### Coarse fallback

OSM `highway` class (Egypt OSM coverage is good in Cairo, Alexandria, Red Sea coast; thinner in Upper Egypt + Western Desert):
- `motorway` = highway (طريق سريع) — Cairo Ring Road, Cairo–Alexandria Desert Road, Cairo–Sokhna
- `trunk` = main inter-city (طريق رئيسي)
- `primary` = governorate main road
- `secondary` = governorate secondary
- `tertiary` = local arterial
- `residential` = neighborhood

### Verdict bands (estimated, calibrated to qualitative noise impact)

- 🟢 < 1,000 v/d (rural Western Desert / Upper Egypt secondary)
- 🟡 1,000–10,000 v/d (governorate secondary, urban side street)
- 🟠 10,000–50,000 v/d (urban arterial, busy primary)
- 🔴 > 50,000 v/d (Cairo Ring Road segments, major Cairo arterials, Alexandria Corniche)

### Cairo-specific notes

- Cairo Ring Road (الطريق الدائري) — among the world's most heavily trafficked urban ring roads; segments est. 100,000+ v/d
- Mehwar / Autostrade / Salah Salem / Corniche El Nil — major arterials; consistently noisy
- **Air pollution** is often the bigger issue than noise — Cairo PM2.5 frequently exceeds WHO guidelines by 5–10× (source: WHO Global Air Quality Database)

### Confidence

LOW — per-segment traffic counts comparable to EU/UK datasets are **not publicly available** for Egypt. Use OSM road class + governorate vehicle density as coarse proxy; for high-stakes purchases, a site visit during peak hours (Cairo: Sun–Thu 7–10am, 4–7pm) is the only reliable signal.

## Section: `--tax`

### Annual property tax — Real Estate Tax (الضريبة العقارية)

**Law 196/2008** as amended (most recently 2020) — administered by the **Real Estate Tax Authority** under Ministry of Finance + Egyptian Tax Authority (`https://eta.gov.eg/`).

**Formula (residential)**:
```
Annual tax = 10% × (annual rental value − maintenance allowance − exemption threshold)
```

| Item | Value 2025 |
|---|---|
| **Rate** | **10%** of annual rental value (after deductions) |
| **Maintenance allowance (residential)** | **30%** of annual rental value |
| **Maintenance allowance (commercial)** | **32%** of annual rental value |
| **Annual exemption threshold (general / non-primary)** | **EGP 24,000/year** of rental value (raised from EGP 6,000 in earlier reform; verify current threshold with ETA) |
| **Primary-residence exemption (2024–2026 amendments)** | **EGP 100,000/year of net annual rental value** OR **EGP 8,000,000 property value** — narrows tax base; only ~2M of 55M Egyptian properties liable post-amendment (2026-05-27 verified, source Daily News Egypt 2026-02-15 + Wafeq + Nawy blog) |
| **Re-valuation cycle** | Every **5 years** by joint committee of MoF + Ministry of Housing |

**Rental value** = the committee's assessed annual rentable value, NOT actual rent received. Assessment incl. location, finish, age, services. Heavily under-assessed historically; some 2024 update efforts.

### Example calculation (residential, primary, NAC apartment)

A finished 150 m² apartment in NAC, committee-assessed rental value ~EGP 60,000/year:
- Less 30% maintenance allowance: 60,000 − 18,000 = 42,000
- Less EGP 24,000 exemption: 42,000 − 24,000 = 18,000
- × 10% = **EGP 1,800/year (~USD 36/year @ EGP 50/USD)**

Even for a high-end Zamalek apartment with assessed annual rental EGP 200,000:
- Less 30%: 140,000
- Less 24,000: 116,000
- × 10% = **EGP 11,600/year (~USD 232/year)**

**→ Annual real estate tax in Egypt is very low for residential**, comparable in absolute USD to Italian IMU on a small holiday flat. The bigger fiscal drag is at transaction (below).

### Transaction taxes (one-time at purchase)

| Item | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stamp duty / registration (rasm tasgil)** | **2.5% of declared price** typical residential; **3% in some categories incl. some foreigner-buyer cases** | Verify current rate with REPA + ETA per transaction; rates have been adjusted in recent years |
| **Notary fees** | Included in registration; nominal | Notarization at REPA office |
| **Lawyer fees** | **~1–3%** of price typical (negotiable, often 1.5%) | Foreign buyer should always retain independent counsel — NOT the developer's lawyer |
| **REPA registration filing** | nominal flat fee | Per-document |
| **Real estate brokerage** | 2.5–5% typical | Often paid by both sides; negotiable |

**Total foreigner-buyer transaction cost**: est. **5–10% of price** depending on whether one or both sides commission independently + lawyer rate.

### Real Estate Transaction Tax (RETT — ضريبة التصرفات العقارية) on sale — colloquially "CGT" but technically a transaction tax on gross value

Statute: **Law 30/2023** (15 June 2023, amends Income Tax Law 91/2005, real-estate disposal provisions) — NOT Law 175/2023 (which amends personal income tax brackets) (2026-05-27 verified, source PwC Egypt Law 30/2023 PDF + Wafeq).

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| **RETT on sale of built real estate (residential)** | **2.5%** of **official tax-authority-assessed value OR actual sale price (whichever HIGHER)** — NOT net gain |
| **Withholding** | At REPA registration of resale |
| **First-home exemption** | EGP **3,000,000** property value for properties under **200 m²** (raised from EGP 2M by Law 30/2023) |

**This is unusual internationally** — taxing gross sale value (not net gain) means even a loss-making sale is taxed. A USD 200k sale = USD 5k RETT regardless of original purchase price. Plan for this in any IRR model.

### Future risk

- Real estate tax revaluation cycle continues; assessed rental values being raised in some governorates 2024–2026
- Fiscal pressure post-IMF EFF → broader real-estate tax base expansion likely
- Stamp duty rate adjustments have happened multiple times — verify at transaction

### Confidence

MEDIUM-HIGH for the legal framework (Law 196/2008 + Law 30/2023 are public, ETA portal documents rates). MEDIUM for exact threshold values — Egyptian tax thresholds have been adjusted multiple times since 2020 and the most current figure should always be reconfirmed with ETA at transaction time.

## Section: `--rental`

### Long-term residential

Egypt's residential rental market is dual-tier:

| Tier | Regulation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Old Rent Law (الإيجار القديم) — pre-1996 contracts** | Frozen rents under Law 49/1977 + Law 136/1981 | Notoriously low (some EGP 50–500/month for Cairo apartments). **Constitutional Court ruled key provisions unconstitutional in 2024**; reform legislation in pipeline. **Avoid buying any property with old-rent tenants in place** — ejection is legally + politically fraught |
| **New Rent Law (الإيجار الجديد) — post-1996 contracts** | Free-market under Law 4/1996 | Standard fixed-term lease; most foreign-buyer relevant tier |

### Income tax on rental (foreigner / non-resident)

- **Non-resident landlords** taxed under Egyptian Income Tax Law 91/2005 + amendments
- Rental income from Egyptian real estate is **Egypt-sourced** → taxable in Egypt regardless of landlord residency
- Rate: **graduated PIT brackets** (currently 0–27.5% across bands) OR **special property income regime** at flat 10–15% in some configurations — verify with ETA + a local tax advisor
- Maintenance/expense allowance: 50% of gross rental typically deductible
- Reportable annually; foreign-resident filer often through tax representative

### Short-term rentals (Airbnb / Booking)

**Status**: legal but undergoverned. No national STR registration regime equivalent to FR meublé classé / IT CIR / EU Reg 2024/1028 (which doesn't apply to Egypt).

- **Hotel licensing** required if operated as professional accommodation (Tourism Police + Ministry of Tourism)
- Most informal Airbnb in Cairo / North Coast / Red Sea operates **without** tourism license — regulatory grey zone
- **Compound HOA rules** in NAC, El Gouna, North Coast gated communities frequently **ban** short-let entirely or limit to ≥30-day rentals — **read the HOA bylaws BEFORE buying for STR**
- **Hurghada + El Gouna + North Coast**: high seasonality — peak Jun–Sep + Christmas/NY; very weak Oct–May
- **Sharm El Sheikh + Hurghada**: large Russian + Eastern European + GCC tourist base; STR demand robust but pricing competitive vs hotels

### Strategic notes

- **HOA / compound bylaws** are the binding constraint, not national law. NAC + Mountain View + Sodic + Mountain View Hyde Park type compounds often forbid short-let
- **Hurghada older buildings (non-compound)**: easier STR
- **El Gouna (Orascom)**: own STR licensing scheme via Orascom Tourism — operator-managed
- **Currency**: STR receipts often in USD/EUR (foreign tourists) — must convert via CBE-authorized bank to be repatriable; cash USD outside the banking system is **not Form 4-traceable** and creates exit-friction later

### Confidence

MEDIUM — Egyptian STR landscape is fragmented + undergoverned at national level; HOA bylaws + governorate tourism authorities are the binding rules. Verify at parcel level before underwriting any rental yield.

- **Work permit (تصريح عمل)** — required for any foreigner to be employed by an Egyptian entity. Issued by **Ministry of Manpower** (`https://www.manpower.gov.eg/`); typically tied to specific employer + job title + 1-year renewable
- **Residency tied to work permit** — separate residency permit from Ministry of Interior (passport stamp); usually 1 year
- **Investor / property residency** — the PM Decree 3099/2019 investor tiers (above; citizenship vs permanent-residency outcome unreconciled between GAFI and the US State Dept 2025 ICS) provide an alternative path NOT tied to employment

- **Remote work for foreign employer / digital nomad** — no specific Egyptian visa, but tourist visa stays + extensions are widely used in practice (legal grey zone for income — technically Egypt-sourced if performed on Egyptian soil)

### Flood risk

- **Nile Valley + Delta**: Aswan High Dam (1970) eliminated annual Nile flooding. Modern flood risk = flash floods on wadis + urban drainage failure
- **Cairo flash floods**: rare but devastating when they occur (1994, 2014, 2020); urban drainage systems undersized; depressed neighborhoods (e.g., parts of Maadi, eastern Cairo) most affected
- **Red Sea coast (Hurghada, El Gouna, Marsa Alam)**: wadi flash floods from Eastern Desert during rare heavy rain events (Oct–Mar); developments in wadi outlets are exposed
- **North Coast (Sahel)**: winter storms + occasional flooding of low-lying compounds
- **Alexandria + Delta**: **sea-level rise + subsidence** is the major long-horizon risk — Alexandria is among the world's most SLR-vulnerable major cities (IPCC AR6); 30–50 cm SLR by 2050 + Delta subsidence ~4 mm/yr (varies) → meaningful exposure for coastal Alexandria + Rosetta + Damietta corridor

### Seismicity

- **Moderate overall** — Egypt sits on the African Plate, with active zones along:
  - **Gulf of Suez + northern Red Sea rifting** — moderate (M 5–6 events historic; 1969 Shedwan M 6.6)
  - **Aqaba Gulf** — Sinai eastern edge (1995 Nuweiba M 7.2)
  - **Cairo–Suez region** — 1992 Cairo earthquake M 5.8, ~545 deaths from building collapse (poor construction quality, NOT high seismicity per se)
  - **Mediterranean offshore (Hellenic Arc)** — distal source for tsunami risk on Alexandria coast (1303 historical event)
- **Building quality matters more than seismic intensity** — informal/illegal construction (esp. Cairo informal districts) is extremely vulnerable
- For New Cairo + NAC + planned developments built post-2010 to modern Egyptian code → 🟢 acceptable
- For older Cairo informal areas → 🔴 not safe; avoid

### Climate risks (rising)

- **Heat extremes**: Cairo summer days >40°C are increasing; >45°C events possible. Urban heat island effect strong
- **Drought + water stress**: Egypt is **already extremely water-stressed** (~600 m³/capita/year, well below 1,000 m³ scarcity threshold); GERD (Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam) upstream tensions; long-term irrigation pressure on Delta
- **Sandstorms (khamaseen)**: Mar–May; visibility + air quality + agricultural impact
- **Air pollution**: Cairo PM2.5 chronically high (WHO data: annual mean often 5–10× WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³); cardiovascular + respiratory health implications

### Build-era hazards

| Era | Hazards |
|---|---|
| **Pre-1952 (royal era)** | Beautiful but often unmaintained; wood-frame elements; lead pipes; weak foundations |
| **1952–1973 (Nasser/early Sadat)** | Often well-built reinforced concrete; aging utilities |
| **1973–2000 (Open Door + boom)** | Highly variable — mix of luxury (Maadi, Zamalek extensions) and informal/illegal construction (vast Cairo expansion) |
| **2000–2010** | Compound boom (New Cairo, 6th October); generally better-engineered; some off-plan delivery failures |
| **2010–2020** | Modern compound era; mostly to current code |

**Informal/illegal construction (المباني المخالفة)**: a vast share of Greater Cairo's building stock is informal (no permits, structural risk). Government **legalization (تقنين)** programs since 2019 (Law 17/2019 + Law 1/2020) — buyers should **insist on legalization certificate** for any older urban property

### Asbestos + lead

- Asbestos in pre-1990 cement panels + insulation likely; removal ad-hoc, no specific national disposal regime comparable to EU
- Lead pipes in pre-1980 Cairo + Alexandria buildings common; replace at renovation

## Caveats unique to EG (HEAVY — read all)

- **EGP devaluation risk on USD-cash foreign buyer**: EGP depreciated ~70%+ against USD between 2021 and 2024. Buying with USD and selling in EGP exposes the seller to potentially severe FX loss. Holding period + Form 4 paper trail are essential for USD repatriation
- **Form 4 (نموذج 4) — capital flow tracking**: USD inflows for property purchase MUST be transferred via a CBE-authorized bank, with the bank issuing a **Form 4** documenting the inflow. Without Form 4, **future USD repatriation of sale proceeds is structurally blocked** (or severely restricted). This is the #1 procedural risk for foreign buyers — get the Form 4 at purchase or accept that exit liquidity may be EGP-only
- **Off-plan completion delay risk**: Egyptian off-plan delivery is **routinely 1–4 years late**, sometimes more. Major developers (Mountain View, Sodic, Palm Hills, Talaat Mostafa) are more reliable; smaller developers have failed. Use developer-financed installment plans cautiously
- **Dual pricing (USD vs EGP)**: foreign-buyer markets quote in USD/EUR with EGP for show. Confirm contract currency in writing
- **Sinai-zone deed issues**: foreigners cannot freehold in Sinai (incl. Sharm El Sheikh, Dahab). What looks like "freehold" is usually 50-year usufruct from TDA. Many sales offices misrepresent this — verify with REPA + TDA
- **Sijil al-shakhsi vs sijil al-‘ayni**: most older properties have only a notarized contract chain, NOT a state-guaranteed real-property title. Demand REPA registration in sijil al-‘ayni where available
- **Old rent law (إيجار قديم)**: do NOT buy property with pre-1996 tenants in place. Constitutional Court 2024 ruling means reform is coming but ejection remains legally + politically fraught. Verify tenant status pre-1996 vs post-1996
- **Informal/illegal construction (مباني مخالفة)**: large share of Cairo stock is unpermitted. Insist on legalization certificate (تقنين) under Law 17/2019 / Law 1/2020 for any older urban property
- **Real estate tax revaluation cycle**: assessed rental values being raised in some governorates 2024–2026 — annual bills may rise
- **CGT on GROSS sale price (2.5%) — not net gain**: even loss-making sales are taxed; model accordingly
- **HOA bylaws are the binding STR rule**, not national law. Many compounds ban Airbnb
- **GERD (Ethiopian dam) + water stress** is a long-horizon macro risk for Delta agricultural value + cooling water; less direct for urban residential
- **Inheritance — Sharia mandatory shares for Muslims** on Egyptian-titled real estate; non-Muslim foreign buyers can typically opt for home-country law via prior declaration but should consult an Egyptian estates lawyer at purchase
- **Anti-money-laundering**: source-of-funds documentation required for transactions >EGP 500k typical; foreign buyers should pre-prepare bank statements + source-of-wealth letter
- **Sandstorm + air quality**: not a deal-breaker but a livability factor — Cairo PM2.5 chronically high

## Quirks to know

- **Form 4 paper trail**: the single most important procedural quirk for foreign buyers — it gates future USD exit. Ask the bank for the Form 4 the moment USD lands in the Egyptian account. Keep originals
- **Off-plan installment culture**: Egyptian developers commonly sell on 7–10 year zero-interest (or low-interest) installment plans, with delivery 3–5 years out. Headline price ≠ cash equivalent (often 30–50% lower for cash). Always ask for cash-equivalent price
- **NAC speculative dynamics**: New Administrative Capital is a government-led mega-project with massive supply pipeline; pricing is speculative + dispersed; resale liquidity unclear pre-occupancy maturity (~2027–2030 forecast); compare carefully to mature New Cairo / Sheikh Zayed alternatives
- **Sijil al-shakhsi vs sijil al-‘ayni transition**: Egypt has been "transitioning to Torrens-style" since 1964 with very slow implementation; most older properties are still personal-deed-chain. New developments (NAC, North Coast new compounds) do offer real-property registration — strongly preferred
- **Compound vs non-compound**: in foreign-buyer practice, gated compounds (Mountain View, Sodic, Palm Hills, Mountain View Hyde Park, ORA, Marassi, Hacienda, El Gouna, etc.) offer dramatically simpler title + utilities + security than non-compound stock. The premium reflects this
- **Dual currency contracts**: a contract may say "USD 200,000 or EGP equivalent at time of signing" — clarify which currency is binding for installment payments + EGP devaluation interim
- **Furniture transfer**: in resale, "with furniture (مفروشة)" can be a sleight to inflate apparent value; insist on itemized inventory
- **5-year holding rule waiver**: the Cabinet-of-Ministers approval to sell within 5 years exists in theory but is opaque + slow in practice. Plan to hold 5 years minimum
- **Visa / residency renewal annual nuisance**: even with property residency, Egyptian residency permits typically require annual renewal at Mogamma / Abbassia immigration — bureaucratic, time-consuming
- **Two parallel exchange rates historically**: until Mar 2024 there was a parallel/black market premium of 30–60% over CBE rate; post-float these have largely converged but stress events can re-create a gap. Always transact at CBE rate for documentation purposes
