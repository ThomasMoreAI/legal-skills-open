# Universal `--insurance` Section

Per-country property insurance landscape across all 87 supported countries (Tier-1: AE, CL, CO, DO, JP, TH, TR, US, UY, ZA + Tier-2: EG, GE, ID, IL, MA, MY, PH, VN added April 2026 + Tier-3: SG, HK, KR, TW, LI, MO, AD, MC, MD added May 2026 + Tier-4: QA, SA, PE, EC, PY, AM, AZ, TN added May 2026 + **Tier-5: IN, NG, KE, JO, OM, BH, KW, LB added May 2026**): catastrophic-risk reinsurance schemes, flood/earthquake availability, ballpark premium %, mortgage-mandatory rules, 2025-26 climate-risk reforms.

**Snapshot**: April–May 2026 (Tier-5 additions).

**Anti-hallucination**: every premium percentage is a broker-rule-of-thumb range; should not be quoted as authoritative for a specific transaction. `[verify]` flags figures that could not be confirmed against a primary source.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:
1. **Catastrophic-risk reinsurance scheme** if any
2. **Flood-policy availability**: universal? optional? denied in zones
3. **Earthquake-policy availability**: who must / who optional / who denied
4. **Average homeowner premium** as % of insured value (rebuild cost basis)
5. **Mortgage-mandatory or owner-optional**
6. **2025-26 climate-risk reforms**
7. **Confidence**: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW

## Output template

```markdown
## Insurance Profile

**Country**: <ISO2>
**Catastrophic scheme**: <name + mandatory surcharge if any>
**Flood**: <universal | optional | denied in zones>
**Earthquake**: <universal | optional | denied>
**Premium % insured value**: <range>
**Mortgage-mandatory**: <yes | no>
**2025–26 reforms**: <list>
**Confidence**: <HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW>
```

**Standing caveats**:
- "Mortgage-mandatory" universally means lenders require fire/building cover at minimum; contents and liability are owner-optional
- "Insured value" = reconstruction cost basis in most civil-law jurisdictions; "sum insured" in common-law
- Climate reforms move quickly — 2026 figures are as of April 2026 and several are pending implementing decrees

## Insurability cliff (flood / peril) — mortgage & resale knock-on (cross-link: --climate)

> **This is NOT a new flag.** It is a bounded cross-link carve-out shared by the existing
> [`--insurance`](insurance.md) and
> [`--climate`](climate-projections.md) sections.
> Invoke it from either: `--climate` surfaces the hazard zone; `--insurance` answers
> "will *any* insurer write this address, at what loading, and does that block the loan/resale?"
> Scope is the **binary insurability question + the national backstop schemes that decide it** —
> deliberately *not* a 121-country sweep.

**Snapshot**: May 2026. Every scheme below is date-stamped to its enabling instrument; figures >12 months old may have moved — re-verify at the cited authority.

The **insurability cliff** is the point at which no insurer will write peril (flood / storm / quake)
cover on a specific address — or will write it only at a near-full-dwelling-limit loading or with a
flood sub-limit so low it is cosmetic. At that point the property is *effectively uninsurable*.
Because virtually every mortgage lender requires buildings insurance as a loan condition,
**uninsurable → unmortgageable → the buyer pool collapses to cash → resale value impaired**.
A national backstop scheme is what usually *prevents* the cliff (it forces availability and/or
caps price); markets with no backstop are where the cliff bites hardest.

### National peril/flood backstop schemes

- **UK — Flood Re** (operational since April 2016; flood reinsurance scheme, scheduled to **end 2039** then transition to risk-reflective pricing). Lets home insurers cede the flood element of high-risk household policies into a pooled, levy-funded reinsurer, so cover stays *available and affordable*. **Excludes homes built/completed on or after 1 January 2009** (deliberate, to deter building on flood-risk land) — post-2009 new-builds in flood zones are the UK's primary cliff exposure. Companion **Build Back Better** scheme (Flood Re, from 2022): up to £10,000 of resilient-reinstatement work funded on top of a flood claim. Authority: [Flood Re](https://www.floodre.co.uk/) · [House of Commons Library CBP-8751](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-8751/) · [ABI Flood Re explained](https://www.abi.org.uk/products-and-issues/topics-and-issues/flood-re/flood-re-explained/). Cliff verdict: **largely prevented for pre-2009 dwellings; live for post-2009 new-builds and most commercial/leasehold-block risks (Flood Re is household-only)**.
- **France — régime de catastrophes naturelles ("Cat Nat")** (loi n° 82-600 du 13 juillet 1982). Cat-Nat cover (flood, mudflow, drought-subsidence, etc.) is **automatically bundled** into every multirisque-habitation / building policy via a state-set surcharge; CCR provides state-backed reinsurance. The surcharge rose **from 12% to 20% of the property premium on 1 January 2025** (first revaluation in ~25 years; scheme in deficit since 2016). Crucially, the **Bureau Central de Tarification (BCT)** can *compel* an insurer to issue a policy when an owner has been refused — the legal backstop against an outright cliff. Authority: [CCR — régime Cat Nat](https://www.ccr.fr/) · [Légifrance loi 82-600](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/) · BCT (bureaucentraldetarification.fr) · surcharge change reported [Sovos](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/vat/france-substantial-catnat-premium-rate-increase-as-of-january-2025/). Cliff verdict: **structurally prevented** — cover is mandatory-bundled and the BCT forces issuance; risk is price (rising excess / surcharge), not availability.
- **Spain — Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros (CCS)**. Extraordinary-risks cover (flood, storm, earthquake, tsunami, volcanic) is **compulsorily bundled** into property/life damage policies via a CCS surcharge; CCS (public insurer) pays the extraordinary-peril element on a flat, mutualised, non-risk-rated tariff. Flood ≈ 70% of CCS payouts (1987–2022, €10.6bn). Because the surcharge attaches to *any* qualifying private policy regardless of address risk, there is **no address-level flood-availability cliff** once a base policy exists. Authority: [Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros](https://www.consorseguros.es/en/) · [CCS extraordinary-risk FAQ](https://www.consorseguros.es/en/preguntas-frecuentes/seguros-de-riesgos-extraodinarios) · [Lloyd's — Spain Consorcio charges](https://www.lloyds.com/market-resources/tax-information/taxation-news/spain-consorcio-charges). Cliff verdict: **prevented for extraordinary perils while a base policy is in force**.
- **United States — NFIP (FEMA) + Risk Rating 2.0 + private flood**. The NFIP is the federal flood backstop; flood is *not* in standard homeowners' cover. **Risk Rating 2.0** (full roll-in October 2021) prices NFIP policies on property-specific risk rather than flood-zone band — premiums in high-risk locations now climb toward true cost (multi-year glide path, statutory ~18%/yr cap on most increases), which is the US affordability cliff even though *availability* is maintained. **Mandatory-purchase rule**: a property in a mapped **Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA)** with a federally backed / federally regulated / GSE-purchased mortgage **must** carry flood insurance — so in an SFHA, uninsurable literally blocks the loan. Private flood is the alternative if NFIP is unavailable/insufficient. Authority: [FEMA NFIP](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) · [Risk Rating 2.0 (FEMA)](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance/risk-rating) · [CRS R45999](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R45999) · [FEMA mandatory-purchase evaluation](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/fema_nfip_eval_mandatory_purchase_requirement.pdf). Cliff verdict: **availability backstopped (NFIP), but price cliff under RR2.0 + a hard SFHA loan-condition gate**.
- **Australia — NO general national flood scheme + ARPC cyclone reinsurance pool** (Treasury Laws Amendment (Cyclone and Flood Damage Reinsurance Pool) Act 2022; ARPC pool live **1 July 2022**, ~$10bn annually reinstated government guarantee). The pool reinsures **cyclone and cyclone-*related* flood damage** (targeted at Northern Australia), lowering reinsurer cost to improve affordability there — but **riverine/flash flooding outside a cyclone is NOT backstopped**. Post-2022-floods Australia has a recognised flood-cover affordability *and* availability crisis: insurers materially load or decline high flood-risk addresses with no compelled-issuance mechanism. Authority: [ARPC — Cyclone Pool](https://arpc.gov.au/reinsurance-pools/cyclone/) · [APRA — cyclone & related flood pool](https://www.apra.gov.au/cyclone-and-cyclone-related-flood-damage) · [Treasury fact sheet](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-12/c2021-226351-factsheet.pdf). Cliff verdict: **highest-exposure developed market for non-cyclone flood** — no compelled cover, real declines/full-loading; cyclone-zone risk partially mitigated by ARPC.
- **New Zealand — Natural Hazards Commission Toka Tū Ake (NHC) + Natural Hazards Insurance Act 2023** (in force **1 July 2024**; replaced the Earthquake Commission Act 1993, EQC → NHC). Statutory first-layer cover for natural-hazard damage (earthquake, landslip, volcanic, tsunami, geothermal — and storm/flood for *land* in defined cases) is **automatically attached to any residential property that holds private fire insurance**, capped per dwelling, with private insurers covering above the cap. The "must hold fire insurance to get the NHC layer" linkage means the cliff appears only if the *base* private policy itself becomes unobtainable. Authority: [NHC Toka Tū Ake — Natural Hazards Insurance Act](https://www.naturalhazards.govt.nz/about-nhc/how-we-work/natural-hazards-insurance-act-2023/) · [NHC](https://www.naturalhazards.govt.nz/). Cliff verdict: **statutory layer prevents the quake/landslip cliff while base fire cover exists; pure-flood (building) availability still depends on the private market.**
- **No public backstop — fully market-priced, cliff risk highest**. Most of the remaining ~95 supported countries have **no compelled-issuance and no national peril pool** for flood — cover is wholly underwriter-discretion. data not publicly available as a single registry — verify per country at the national insurance regulator / supervisor (e.g., the country's central bank or insurance authority) via [`--insurance`](insurance.md). In this bucket the cliff is real and unmitigated: an underwriter can simply decline, or quote a flood sub-limit/excess that is economically a refusal. Treat *absence of a named backstop above* as elevated cliff risk and require a real address-level quote pre-offer.

### The cliff → mortgage/resale knock-on

1. **Lender condition.** Mortgage offers near-universally require buildings/fire insurance in force at completion and maintained for the loan term. No acceptable buildings cover → **no draw-down / breach of mortgage conditions**.
2. **Peril carve-out is enough to fail it.** A policy that *exists* but excludes flood (or sets a flood sub-limit far below rebuild cost, or an excess at near-dwelling-limit) can still fail the lender's "adequate buildings cover" test for a flood-exposed address.
3. **Buyer pool collapses to cash.** Uninsurable/unmortgageable removes every mortgage-dependent buyer. Remaining demand is cash-only at a discount that prices in self-insurance of the peril.
4. **Resale + valuation impairment.** Surveyors/valuers down-value or qualify the valuation ("insurance availability to be confirmed"); the impairment compounds each transaction until mitigation or a backstop changes the risk.
5. **Backstop = the circuit-breaker.** A scheme that *compels issuance* (FR BCT), *mandatorily bundles* the peril (FR Cat Nat, ES Consorcio, NZ NHC), or *pools/reinsures it affordably* (UK Flood Re, US NFIP, AU ARPC for cyclone) is precisely what keeps an address on the mortgageable side of the cliff.

### Pre-offer cross-check

1. **Pull the hazard zone from `--climate`.** Get the address-level flood/peril classification (national flood map, SFHA, Flood Re-eligibility band, AU flood layer, etc.) — coordinates from preflight.
2. **Request a REAL insurer quote at the exact address pre-offer.** Not a regional rule-of-thumb — a bindable quote (or written decline) for *this* address. A decline or a flood sub-limit far below rebuild cost = cliff hit.
3. **Check the national backstop actually applies to this property.** UK: built before 1 Jan 2009 and household (Flood Re)? FR: Cat-Nat bundled + BCT route if refused? ES: base policy in force (Consorcio attaches)? US: in an SFHA, and is NFIP/private available? AU: cyclone-related (ARPC) vs ordinary riverine flood (no backstop)? NZ: base fire policy obtainable (NHC layer attaches)?
4. **Confirm the lender will accept the cover.** Send the actual quote/scheme structure to the prospective lender (or mortgage broker) and get written confirmation it satisfies the buildings-insurance condition — including any flood sub-limit/excess.
5. **Price the loading and excess into the offer.** Annual loaded premium + the flood/peril excess + any resilient-reinstatement cost is a recurring cost and a resale drag — bake it into the bid, not a post-offer surprise. If steps 2–4 cannot be cleared, treat the address as a 🔴 cliff and walk or re-price to cash terms.
