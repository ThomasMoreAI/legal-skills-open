## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **DN-visa status does NOT equal public-system access in most jurisdictions.** The DN visa is a residency permit, not a healthcare entitlement. Public-system enrollment is triggered by formal tax-residency + work-status registration, not by holding a DN visa. HR DN Permit, CY DN Visa, EE Digital Nomad Visa, MT Nomad Residence Permit, JP DN Visa, KR Workation Visa, TH LTR, TH DTV, MU Premium Visa, BB Welcome Stamp all explicitly require private insurance throughout. Only when a DN-visa holder converts to a long-term residence permit + registers for tax does public-system enrollment typically open.
2. **Public-system enrollment timeline varies from day-1 to 12 months.** DE Krankenversicherung mandatory day-1 (€1,000+ backdated penalty for failure); CH KVG mandatory within 3 months (50% surcharge for late enrollment); FR PUMA after 3-mo continuous residence; NL zorgverzekering compulsory + 4-mo grace with backdating; JP National Health Insurance within 14 days of move; KR National Health Insurance within 6 months auto-enrolled; TW NHI after 6-month residency; IL Bituach Leumi 6-month residency rule; ES Convenio Especial after 1-year residence (€60-160/mo by age band); CA 3-month provincial Medicare wait. The day-1 vs 12-month difference is critical for foreign DNs' insurance gap planning.
3. **Tax-residency triggers carry healthcare consequences.** Becoming tax-resident in CH/DE/AT/NL triggers mandatory public-system enrollment with backdated premiums for the unenrolled period. Remaining tax-non-resident (e.g., on a tourist visa or short-DN-visa) typically excludes public-system access. The interaction is asymmetric: DN visa unlocks long stays without unlocking healthcare; tax-residency unlocks healthcare with full tax obligations.
4. **EHIC + GHIC + Reciprocal Health Care Agreements work for tourists, not for DNs.** EHIC covers EU/EEA/CH residents TEMPORARILY abroad in EU/EEA — not DN visa holders STAYING in the destination. UK GHIC post-Brexit replaces EHIC for UK with similar tourist-only coverage. AU Reciprocal Health Care Agreement covers UK/IT/SE/NL/NO/FI/IE/MT/BE residents temporarily in AU — not DN-visa-holding remote workers. DNs cannot rely on home-country reciprocal coverage; private insurance is the bridge.
5. **Private insurance accepted for DN visas varies by jurisdiction.** EU DN visas typically accept any EU-licensed insurer + Schengen-compatible private (Cigna Global, Allianz Care, BUPA Global, etc.). Some jurisdictions (HR DN, CY DN) require local-issued or local-registered insurance. AE/SA/QA mandate locally-licensed insurers (DAMAN, Bupa Arabia, Tawuniya). US is unique: no national system, all insurance is private (HMO/PPO/Marketplace), DNs must source via Aetna International / Cigna Global / GeoBlue or US-based plans with international travel coverage.
6. **Post-disaster + crisis disruptions reshape healthcare access overnight.** IL post-7-Oct-2023 + Northern conflict disrupted Galilee + Negev healthcare; Olim/new-immigrant supplementary insurance critical pre-Bituach Leumi enrollment. LB ongoing crisis (post-2019 collapse + 7-Oct-2024 conflict) damaged sector; American University Hospital + Hôtel-Dieu de France require fresh-USD upfront. UA post-Feb-2022 invasion = humanitarian access only. TR post-6-Feb-2023 earthquake = regional healthcare overload. MA post-8-Sep-2023 Al Haouz earthquake = reconstruction zone healthcare gaps.
7. **Tax regimes that attract DNs (PT NHR2.0/IFICI, ES Beckham, IT 24-bis, GR Non-Dom) often DO NOT include healthcare benefits.** PT NHR2.0/IFICI (Lei 82/2023, in force 1 Jan 2024) restricts to specific scientific/innovation roles + does NOT auto-enroll in SNS. ES Beckham (RD 1008/2023, extended to teleworkers Jan 2023) creates 24% flat-tax window but Convenio Especial healthcare separate. IT 24-bis flat-tax (€200k from 10 Aug 2024) does not include SSN auto-enrollment. GR Non-Dom (Article 5A, 7-year flat tax €100k) requires separate ESY enrollment. NL 30%-ruling (reduced to 27% from 1 Jan 2027) is tax-only — zorgverzekering still mandatory. The tax-attractive jurisdictions require DN to navigate healthcare separately.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

Post-COVID Europe has produced two parallel paths to working-age healthcare access for digital nomads, remote workers, and self-employed expats: (1) **dedicated DN-visa pathways** (PT D8, ES DNV, IT Lavoro Autonomo Nomadi Digitali, GR DN Visa, MT Nomad Residence Permit, IS Long-term Remote Worker, NO Self-Employed) — all of which **require comprehensive private health insurance for the visa application itself** (typically €30,000+ EU-wide cover, no copay, no waiting period); and (2) **standard residency pathways** (DE Anmeldung-triggered Krankenversicherung, CH KVG within 3 months, NL zorgverzekering within 4 months, FR PUMa after 3 months) which trigger **mandatory enrollment in the public system as a function of residency status, not visa type**.

The structural rule across the region: **public-system enrollment is residency-triggered, not visa-triggered**. A DN visa unlocks legal stay; it does not unlock the public-system. Year-1 expats almost universally rely on private insurance. Public-system enrollment is then gated on (a) tax-residency registration (codice fiscale + AIRE-equivalent in IT, NIF + AIMA residence card in PT, padrón + autónomo in ES), (b) elapsed residency (1-year minimum for Convenio Especial in ES; 6 months for IS Sjúkratryggingar; 3 months for FR PUMa), or (c) employer/self-employment social-security registration (DE GKV, AT SVS, BE INAMI, LU CCSS).

EU/EEA/CH citizens carry **EHIC reciprocity** for temporary cover when residence is elsewhere; UK nationals post-Brexit use **GHIC** (replaced EHIC) for the same purpose, but GHIC does NOT cover residents — only travellers. Long-term UK residents in the EU need either S1 (UK pensioners), local enrollment, or private cover.

Three structural traps recur: **(a) day-1 mandatory regimes** in DE (Krankenversicherung is a precondition for Anmeldung, missing premiums are backdated), CH (KVG within 3 months or insurer assigns + 50% surcharge available to cantons), NL (4-month grace then automatic CAK enrollment + €528 fines); **(b) 12-month exclusion windows** in ES (Convenio Especial only after 1 year), IE (HSE "ordinarily resident" = 1-year intent), AD (passive residents are explicitly excluded from CASS); **(c) microstate dependence on private insurance** (AD passive, MC non-employed, MT NRP holders, LI residents — all require private cover sized to the public-system equivalence test).

The 2024-2026 reform wave reshaped tax-residency incentives that DN-visa holders weigh against healthcare access: PT NHR ended Dec 2023 and was replaced by **IFICI (Lei 82/2023)** restricted to scientific/innovation roles; ES extended the **Beckham regime to teleworkers under RD 1008/2023** (Jan 2023); IT raised the **24-bis flat-tax to €200,000 from 10 Aug 2024** with a €300,000 increase legislated for new entrants 2026; NL announced reduction of the **30%-ruling to 27% effective 1 Jan 2027**; CH KVG premiums rose **+4.4% on average for 2026** (BAG/FOPH 26 Sep 2025); DE thresholds for 2026 are **JAEG €77,400 / BBG €69,750** (Bundesgesundheitsministerium).

### Country one-liners

**AD (Andorra)** — No DN visa; closest is *passive residency* (residència passiva) requiring ~€40,000/yr income (≈300% Andorran SMI, [2026 reform](https://www.assap.ad/en/assegurances-andorra/blog/residencia-passiva-a-andorra-requisits-actualitzats-amb-la-normativa-2026)). Passive residents are **explicitly excluded from CASS** (the public health fund) and must hold qualifying private health insurance covering the full family unit; only Category D (active) residents contribute to CASS. **Trap**: the 2026 stricter regulation tightened income + investment minimums; private cover must demonstrate equivalence to CASS, not just travel-grade. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**AT (Austria)** — No dedicated DN visa; closest pathway is the *Red-White-Red Card for Self-Employed Persons* (economic-impact test). Self-employed residents are mandatorily enrolled in **SVS** (Sozialversicherungsanstalt der Selbständigen) once income exceeds the marginal-earnings threshold; e-card issued within 14 days of registration. **Trap**: the visa application requires Schengen-grade private cover (≥€30,000) regardless of subsequent SVS enrollment. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (DN-pathway is workaround, not bespoke).

**BE (Belgium)** — No DN visa; expats access via Single Permit / professional card. Health-insurance enrollment with a *mutualité / ziekenfonds* is mandatory **within 90 days** of arrival; INAMI/RIZIV oversight, identical reimbursement rates across funds; non-registration fines up to €2,500. National-register number is the gating prerequisite. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**CH (Switzerland)** — No DN visa; remote workers use B-permit (employed) or self-employment residency. **KVG (LAMal) basic insurance is mandatory within 3 months of arrival**, counted from arrival date not permit issuance ([BAG/FOPH](https://www.bag.admin.ch/en/health-insurance-requirement-to-obtain-insurance-for-persons-resident-in-switzerland)). 2026 average adult premium **CHF 393.30/mo** (+4.4% YoY, BAG 26 Sep 2025); cantonal spread ~CHF 245/mo. **Trap**: missed deadline = canton assigns insurer + premiums backdated to arrival; standard deductible CHF 300, max CHF 2,500. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**DE (Germany)** — No DN visa; freelance via *Freiberufler/Selbständige* residence permit (§21 AufenthG). **Krankenversicherung is mandatory from day 1** and is a precondition for Anmeldung. 2026 **Versicherungspflichtgrenze (JAEG) €77,400/yr**; **Beitragsbemessungsgrenze (BBG) €69,750/yr** ([Feather](https://feather-insurance.com/blog/private-health-insurance-threshold-yearly-changes)); above JAEG the PKV switch becomes available. GKV ~14.6% + Zusatzbeitrag (~1.7% avg 2026) of capped income — working-age cost €700-900/mo at BBG ceiling. **Trap**: backdated premiums + fines if Anmeldung occurs without proof of cover. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**DK (Denmark)** — No DN visa; remote workers via Pay Limit Scheme or self-employment route. CPR registration triggers public-healthcare entitlement and yellow *sundhedskort* issuance (~2-3 weeks). **6-week emergency-only gap** post-arrival under the Sundhedsloven — only acute/chronic incidents covered until card arrives. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**ES (Spain)** — Dedicated **DN Visa** under Ley 28/2022 (Startup Law); income threshold ~200% SMI ≈ €2,850/mo as of 2026. Visa requires private health insurance from a Spain-authorised insurer, *sin copagos sin carencias*, equivalent to public-system cover. **Convenio Especial** (voluntary public buy-in) **only available after 12 months legal residency**: ~€60/mo under 65, ~€157/mo over 65 ([Ministerio de Sanidad](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/en/servCiudadanos/internacional/convenioEspecial.htm)). **Trap**: Beckham-Law extension to teleworkers under **RD 1008/2023** (24% flat tax on Spanish-source income up to €600k, 6 years) — a tax win, but Convenio Especial healthcare access is a separate clock. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**FI (Finland)** — No DN visa; long-term stay via residence permit. **Kotikunta** (municipality-of-residence) status gates KELA enrollment — non-EU citizens staying >1 year apply via Migri/DVV after residence permit + HETU issuance. Working >4 months auto-triggers public health-insurance registration. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**FR (France)** — No DN visa; closest is *visa long séjour profession libérale* / *passeport-talent*. **PUMa eligibility after 3 months continuous, regular residence** ([service-public.gouv.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F34308)); CSM (cotisation subsidiaire maladie) at 6.5% on capital income above 50% PASS (€24,030 in 2026) when professional income <20% PASS, capped at 8 PASS (€384,480). **Trap**: non-EU expats posted by foreign employer may be excluded from PUMa for first 5 years (S1/equivalent regime); private cover required during that window. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**GR (Greece)** — Dedicated **Digital Nomad Visa** launched Sep 2021 under Law 4825/2021; income threshold €3,500/mo net (+20% spouse, +15% per child); 50% income-tax exemption for up to 7 years available to those becoming Greek tax-resident under Article 5C, Law 4172/2013. Visa requires comprehensive private cover (≥€30,000) for full duration. **ESY/EOPYY** access activates only on tax-residency + social-insurance contributions. **Trap**: ESY weak primary-care coverage prompts most DN-holders to retain private cover (Hellenic Healthcare Group, MITERA) even after enrollment. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**IE (Ireland)** — No DN visa; remote workers via Stamp 1G/Critical Skills/spouse routes. HSE access requires **"ordinarily resident"** status — living or intending to live in Ireland for **at least 1 year**; proof = property/rental + funds transfer + visa. Year-1 expats lean on **VHI / Laya / Irish Life** private cover. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**IS (Iceland)** — Dedicated **Long-term Remote Work Visa** introduced Oct 2020; 180-day max, non-renewable, income threshold **ISK 1,000,000/mo** (~€6,900) individual / ISK 1,300,000 with dependents. Health insurance proof of **ISK 2,000,000 minimum coverage** required, valid Iceland + Schengen, full duration. SI (Sjúkratryggingar Íslands) public access requires **6 months of legal residency** — DN visa holders therefore stay on private cover throughout. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**IT (Italy)** — Dedicated **Visto per Lavoratori Autonomi Nomadi Digitali / Lavoratori da Remoto** in force April 2024 (DL 4/2022 + DM 29 Feb 2024); income threshold ~**€28,000/yr (3× minimum exemption)**, raised to ~€32,400 by some consulates. Comprehensive private cover required for visa. **SSN access** unlocks via tax-residency registration (codice fiscale + iscrizione anagrafica). Freelancers under Lavoro Autonomo can enroll in SSN; remote employees of foreign employer typically remain on private cover. Family member SSN access via €2,000 voluntary contribution. **Trap**: Article **24-bis flat-tax raised to €200,000** for new tax-residents from 10 Aug 2024 (was €100,000), with €300,000 announced for new entrants from 2026. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**LI (Liechtenstein)** — No DN visa; residency tightly controlled (lottery + economic interest). **OKP (obligatorische Krankenpflegeversicherung) mandatory for every resident**; private insurers compete under regulated framework. Cross-border workers Liechtenstein→CH are NOT subject to compulsory CH insurance (different from FR/DE/AT/IT cross-border rules). **Confidence: MEDIUM** (small jurisdiction; English-language documentation thin).

**LU (Luxembourg)** — No DN visa; remote work via salaried-worker / independent / *Carte Bleue Européenne*. **CNS enrollment is mandatory** for any stay >90 days. Voluntary affiliation costs **€151.41/mo** (2026, CCSS-set); **3-month delay before coverage starts** under voluntary route — fees still owed during gap. Cross-border workers (~230,000, STATEC end-2024) are CNS-enrolled. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**MC (Monaco)** — No DN visa; *carte de séjour* requires substantial financial proof + housing + clean record. CCSS (Caisse de Compensation des Services Sociaux) covers employed/self-employed residents — employee 8.95%, employer 27.13% of salary. **Non-employed residents must hold comprehensive private insurance valid in Monaco** as a residency-application precondition. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**MT (Malta)** — Dedicated **Nomad Residence Permit** (Jun 2021); minimum gross income raised to **€42,000/yr from 1 Apr 2024** (was €32,400) ([Residency Malta](https://nomad.residencymalta.gov.mt/nomad-eligibility/)). NRP holders are **explicitly NOT entitled to free public healthcare**; must show ≥**€30,000 private cover** valid for the full 1-year permit period, renewable. **Trap**: state-sector access excluded for entire NRP duration regardless of tax-residency triggers. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**NL (Netherlands)** — No DN visa; remote workers via DAFT (US treaty) / highly-skilled-migrant / self-employed routes. **Zorgverzekering compulsory within 4 months** of municipal registration ([CZ](https://www.cz.nl/en/zorgverzekering/zorgpremie)); 2026 average basic premium **~€159/mo** (range €142-185), mandatory deductible **€385/yr**. Penalties: 3 successive €528 fines then automatic CAK enrollment at higher premium. **Trap**: 30%-ruling reducing to **27% from 1 Jan 2027**. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**NO (Norway)** — Dedicated [self-employment residence permit](https://www.udi.no/) (no formal "DN visa" branding). National Insurance (folketrygd) membership **compulsory for residency >12 months**; HELFO administers; fødselsnummer issued post-permit triggers enrollment. Patient contribution capped **NOK 2,040/yr (2026)**. 8% gross-income contribution from working expats. Pre-registration window requires private travel/health cover. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**PT (Portugal)** — Dedicated **D8 Digital Nomad Visa** (Oct 2022, Lei 23/2007 art. 61º-B); income threshold ~**€3,680/mo (4× minimum wage €920 in 2026)**, savings ≥€11,040. Visa requires private health insurance ≥**€30,000 EU-wide** including evacuation. **SNS access** via residence card + NIF for those becoming Portuguese resident. **Trap 1**: post-2023 SEF dissolution → AIMA backlog delays residence-card issuance, blocking timely SNS registration. **Trap 2**: NHR ended end-2023; replaced by **IFICI (NHR 2.0) under Lei 82/2023** restricted to scientific/innovation/qualified roles — fewer DN-holders qualify than under old NHR. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**SE (Sweden)** — No DN visa; remote workers via work permit / self-employed visa. **Personnummer via Skatteverket** = automatic healthcare-system enrollment for stays >1 year; separate Försäkringskassan registration confirms social-insurance status. Primary-care visit fees SEK 100-460 capped SEK 1,450/yr. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**SM (San Marino)** — No DN visa; residency via *permesso di soggiorno* with financial-means + housing test. ISS (Istituto per la Sicurezza Sociale) administers healthcare/pensions/social services for all legal residents; tessera sanitaria issued post-residency registration. Insurance during application = private. Working residents contribute via payroll. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (small jurisdiction; documentation primarily in Italian).

**UK (United Kingdom)** — No DN visa; remote workers via Skilled Worker / Global Talent / Self-Sponsorship / Innovator Founder routes. NHS access for residents tied to settled/pre-settled status or visa with **Immigration Health Surcharge** (£1,035/yr 2024+ adult). **GHIC** (post-Brexit replacement for EHIC) covers UK residents for *temporary* state-care needs in EU/EEA + CH (NOT a residence-cover instrument) — UK long-term residents in the EU need S1 (pensioners), local enrollment, or private cover. **Confidence: HIGH**.

## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

The CEE-EU bloc led the EU in formalising digital-nomad pathways post-COVID: **Estonia first** (1 Aug 2020, PPA), **Croatia second** (1 Jan 2021, MUP — first formal EU DN permit by category), then **Romania** (Law 22/2022, Jan 2022), **Hungary White Card** (2022), **Latvia** (29 Jun 2022), **Czech Republic** (Gov. Resolution 475/2023, effective 1 Jul 2023), and the late arrivals **Slovenia** (21 Nov 2025) and **Bulgaria** (applications opened 20 Dec 2025). Cyprus runs a quota'd "Visitor – Digital Nomad" category since 3 Mar 2022 (raised 500 → 1,000 in Mar 2025; further reopened 30 Oct 2025). Lithuania, Poland, Slovakia have **no dedicated DN visa** — remote workers route through Type-D national-visa, freelance/trade-license, or self-employment residence permits.

The Western Balkans are heterogeneous: **Albania** (Unique Permit under Law 79/2021, operational 2022), **Montenegro** (Programme adopted Dec 2021, operational 2022, extended to end-2026) and **Moldova** (launched 20 Sep 2025) have formal DN tracks; **Serbia, Bosnia & Herzegovina, North Macedonia** have none — applicants use self-employment residence permits or business-registration routes.

The **Caucasus is a parallel haven**: Armenia offers 180-day visa-free entry to most OECD nationalities (then "private entrepreneur" registration for residency), Azerbaijan has no DN-specific track (eVisa + standard residence), and Georgia's **Remotely from Georgia (Aug 2020) was a COVID-era online application portal**, not a separate visa — Georgia's underlying 1-year visa-free regime for ~95 nationalities is what most "DN" stays still rely on; the bespoke portal has effectively been deprecated.

**Healthcare pattern is consistent**: in nearly every country listed, the DN visa requires **private health insurance** (typically €30k–€60k coverage, Schengen-valid where applicable) for the application AND the entire stay. Public-system enrolment (NFZ-PL, VZP-CZ, NEAK-HU, CNAS-RO, HZZO-HR, ZZZS-SI, GeSY-CY, Haigekassa-EE, NHIF-BG) is contingent on **tax residency + contribution**, not on the DN visa itself. The **trade-license/self-employment route** (CZ Živno, PL JDG, SK živnosť) is the main alternative that *does* enrol the holder into the public system via mandatory contributions — at the cost of full social-security exposure. Holders of the HR DN permit are explicitly tax-exempt and exempt from mandatory HZZO enrolment (private insurance throughout); HU White Card holders explicitly cannot access NEAK; CY DN holders cannot use GeSY as proof for the application but can register after residency is granted.

**2024–2026 reform tempo is high**: SI launched Nov 2025, BG opened Dec 2025, MD opened Sep 2025; HU raised income threshold from €2,000 to €3,000 (2024); CY doubled its quota from 500 to 1,000 (Mar 2025) and reopened applications 30 Oct 2025; SK *tightened* its trade-license/business-residence route to a 700/yr global quota effective 1 Jul 2025. The macro implication: DN-permit-holder healthcare is a private-insurance market across the entire region.

## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

Healthcare for working-age digital nomads across this 13-country region is dominated by **employer-sponsored mandatory private insurance** rather than NHS-style universal public coverage. The GCC bloc (AE, SA, QA, BH, KW, OM) has converged on a model where private health insurance is a precondition for residency — the employer purchases it for sponsored workers, and self-purchasers (DN, Premium Residency, Golden Visa, freelance permit) must show valid IPMI or local-licensed cover at visa application and renewal. Outside the GCC, MENA states (EG, MA, TN, JO, LB) operate hybrid systems where a national social-insurance fund (CNAM/AMO/JHIC/NHIS) covers nationals and salaried locals, but expats remain in the private channel by default.

The 2023-2025 reform wave has been substantial: **UAE Federal Decree-Law 48/2023** (insurance regulation) plus the **MOHRE Basic Health Insurance package effective 1 Jan 2025** extended mandatory cover to the five Northern Emirates that previously had no scheme (Sharjah, Ajman, UAQ, RAK, Fujairah), bringing AED 320-700 (USD 87-190) basic-tier products to market. **Bahrain SEHATI** soft-launched Q3 2023 for citizens with expat IPMI roll-in across 2024. **Oman Dhamani** Phase 1 (large employers) began rollout in 2023 under CMA supervision, with phased extension to SMEs and visitors. **Qatar Law 22/2021** (mandatory health insurance for expats and visitors) came into partial force May 2022. **Kuwait** raised the standard expat residency-linked health-assurance fee to KD 100 (USD 326) effective 23 Dec 2025 (was KD 50; earlier Dhaman product priced at KD 130).

**Israel** is the regional outlier with a Beveridge-style 4-Kupot-Holim system (Clalit/Maccabi/Meuhedet/Leumit) funded by Bituach Leumi (NII) contributions, but new arrivals face a **6-month residency waiting period** before NHI eligibility unless they are Olim under the Law of Return (immediate via Teudat Zakaut) or pay a NIS 16,860 buy-in (as of 1 Jan 2026). Pre-eligibility, supplementary private insurance is essential. The post-7-October-2023 war and the 2024 Northern Front escalation degraded healthcare access in border-adjacent areas (Western Galilee, Upper Galilee, Negev).

**Türkiye** uses SGK (Sosyal Güvenlik Kurumu) with the GSS (Genel Sağlık Sigortası) general health insurance branch open to foreign residents — but the premium is calculated as a percentage of the gross minimum wage rather than the holder's own income, and that rate **doubled from 3% to 6%** of the minimum wage from 1 Dec 2025, sharply repricing GSS for opt-in foreigners. **Lebanon** is in ongoing crisis: 2019 financial collapse, 2020 Beirut port blast, 2024 Israel-Hezbollah war damage to ≥23 health facilities since 17 Sep 2024, and a "fresh USD" cash-only payment culture at flagship hospitals (American University of Beirut Medical Center, Hôtel-Dieu de France) where the Lebanese pound is no longer accepted for major procedures.

Across the region, **international IPMI products** (Cigna Global, Allianz Care, BUPA Global, GeoBlue, Aetna International, IMG Global Select) are the standard fallback for DN visa applications and Premium Residency holders. Working-age (30-50) annual premiums typically fall in the USD 2,000-8,000 band depending on deductible, area-of-cover (excluding US is 30-40% cheaper) and inpatient/outpatient mix. Local-licensed products (Bupa Arabia / Tawuniya / MedGulf in SA, Daman / Oman Insurance in AE, GIG Gulf / Solidarity in BH, MSH / AXA in QA, CNIA Saada / Wafa in MA) are usually mandatory for visa-class minimum-tier compliance.

Hidden trap: phased-rollout schedule has slipped multiple times. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**QA (Qatar)** — No formal DN visa; mandatory private health insurance for all expats, residents, and visitors under **Law 22/2021** (regulating health services), published 4 Nov 2021, with the expat-and-visitor provisions in force from May 2022; employers must purchase MOPH-approved cover for staff and eligible dependants; visitors need Standard Mandatory Health Insurance (SMHIP) at visa-application stage. Hidden trap: French-speaking medical-tourism corridor is well-developed in Tunis/Sousse but quality varies sharply outside the capital and coastal cities. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**TR (Türkiye)** — No formal DN visa (uses Short-Term Residence Permit / Kısa Dönem İkamet İzni); SGK (Sosyal Güvenlik Kurumu) auto-enrols salaried employees with employer co-contribution; foreign residents not in employment can opt into GSS (Genel Sağlık Sigortası) by application after one year of residence, with premium calculated as a percentage of the **minimum wage** (not the holder's actual income) — rate was 3% of minimum wage through 2024-2025 and **doubled to 6% effective 1 Dec 2025** per SGK announcement. ## APAC + Central Asia

### Regional pattern

APAC is the world's most active digital-nomad-visa launch zone of the 2022-2025 cycle. Five new schemes went live: **Malaysia DE Rantau Nomad Pass** (Oct 2022), **Thailand LTR Visa** (Sep 2022, refined 2024), **Korea Workation Visa F-1-D** (Jan 2024 pilot), **Japan Digital Nomad — Designated Activities Notice 53** (1 Apr 2024), and **Thailand Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)** (15 Jul 2024). **Indonesia E33G Remote Worker KITAS** (Apr 2024) and the **Philippines Digital Nomad Visa** under EO 86 (launched Jun 2025) round out the cohort. **Vietnam Talent Visa** (Decree 221/2025/NĐ-CP, in force 15 Aug 2025) is the only new long-stay route in VN — there is still no dedicated DN visa. Sri Lanka's DN Visa launched Feb 2026 (verify against immigration.gov.lk).

The dominant healthcare pattern is **mandatory private insurance for the DN visa itself**, with public-system access decoupled from visa status. JP Notice 53 explicitly excludes holders from National Health Insurance (Kokumin Kenko Hoken) because residency registration (jūsho) requires a stay >3 months *and* a visa category that supports it — DN holders satisfy neither cleanly, and the 6-month no-extension hard cap forecloses long-tenure NHI enrolment. KR's NHIS is the opposite trap: long-stay foreigners are auto-enrolled at the 6-month mark whether they want it or not (mandatory since 2019; dependent rules tightened 3 Apr 2024). TW NHI similarly requires a 6-month continuous-residence wait for ARC holders without an employer (Gold Card holders qualify).

Employer-driven public health is the second cluster: **SG, MY, HK, CN**. SG's MediShield Life is for citizens/PRs only — EP/Tech.Pass/ONE Pass holders rely on employer-sponsored private cover, and the SGD 5,600 / 6,200 EP threshold raise effective 1 Jan 2025 (renewals 1 Jan 2026) shifts the marginal applicant. CN's UEBMI is mandatory for formally employed foreigners on a Z visa; bilateral social-security treaties do not exempt medical-insurance contributions. HK's Hospital Authority subsidises ~97% for HKID holders (TTPS/employment-visa holders qualify after issuance).

The "deposit-or-insurance" structure recurs in **TH LTR** (USD 50k cover OR USD 100k bank balance OR Thai SSO benefits) and shows up implicitly in **TH DTV** (THB 500k deposit; some embassies ask for USD 50k cover at their discretion — not a universal MFA requirement). **ID Second Home Visa** binds on the IDR 2bn (~USD 130k) deposit, not on health proof; private cover is practically necessary because BPJS Kesehatan is restricted to KITAS holders working for Indonesian-registered companies (foreign work is not eligible).

Central Asia is structurally different. **KZ OSMS** (Compulsory Social Health Insurance) covers permanent-residence-permit holders on equal footing with citizens (Law on CSHI, 2015); temporary residents face restrictions. **UZ** runs a 30-day visa-free regime for ~90 nationalities (per gov.uz) — the "60-day" framing in the prompt is not the universal default; the 60-day allowance applies bilaterally (e.g., KG, MD), and US citizens were added at 30 days from 1 Jan 2026 by November 2025 decree.

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

LATAM + Caribbean was the **first region in the world to operationalize a digital-nomad visa**: Barbados launched the Welcome Stamp on 30 Jun 2020 (Cabinet has renewed the programme through 31 Dec 2026), pre-dating Estonia, Croatia, and Portugal. The region then spread through Bahamas BEATS (Oct 2020), Panama Decree 198 (7 May 2021), Brazil's VITEM XIV under CNIg Normative Resolution 45/2021 (effective 24 Jan 2022), Argentina's Disposición DNM 758/2022 (11 May 2022), Belize "Work Where You Vacation" (Apr 2022), Ecuador's Visa de Residencia Temporal Rentista para Trabajo Remoto (Jun 2022), Costa Rica Ley 10.008 (Aug 2022 — Estancia Rentista para Trabajadores Remotos), Colombia V-DNV (Oct 2022), and Uruguay's Hoja de Identidad Provisoria Nómada Digital (May 2023). Note: per `liveinuruguay.uy`, the Uruguay launch was May 2023 — the Aug 2023 date in the original brief is incorrect. The Dominican Republic does NOT have a formal DN visa — that attribution conflates DR with **Dominica (DM)** which runs the "Work In Nature" programme.

**Healthcare access splits the region into three buckets**. (1) Visa requires private insurance only — no public-system entry: BB Welcome Stamp, BS BEATS, BZ WWYV, PA Decree 198, BR VITEM XIV (USD 30k minimum coverage per Citizen Remote summary of consular checklists), CO V-DNV (Cancillería requires private health valid in Colombia), EC Rentista DN (policy must explicitly cover Ecuador), UY Permit (proof of coverage for stay duration), AR DNM 758/2022 (private health expected). (2) Visa requires private insurance AND opens a path to public-system enrollment after residency: MX Temporary Resident → voluntary IMSS opt-in once `tarjeta de residente` issued; PE Rentista → voluntary EsSalud after carné; CL Temporary Residency → ISAPRE/FONASA via RUT once enrolled. (3) Hybrid mandatory: **Costa Rica is the regional outlier** — the original CCSS-mandatory rule for residency categories (Rentista/Pensionado) is **explicitly waived** under Ley 10.008 for the DN sub-category (per Lexology / Consortium Legal coverage of the implementing reglamento) — DN holders must carry private medical insurance throughout but are NOT required to enrol in CCSS. Wider residency tracks (Pensionado, Rentista, Inversionista) still require CCSS contribution calculated as a percentage of declared income.

**Tax-residency interaction is the under-researched trap**. Most LATAM DN visas explicitly preserve non-resident tax status (BB Welcome Stamp is the cleanest — `investbarbados.org` confirms holders are non-resident for income tax; PA, CO, BR similar). But the line is `183-day physical presence` plus, in some jurisdictions, "centre of vital interests" — UY, MX, and CR all use this combined test, and a renewed visa often pushes holders past it. Two reforms reshape this layer in 2025-2026: **Argentina lifted the cepo cambiario on 14 Apr 2025** (per MercoPress, US State Dept Investment Climate 2025, OECD), removing the USD 200/month bank-transfer cap and enabling direct USD repatriation by individuals — materially simplifying USD-denominated insurance premium payment for AR-resident DNs. **Mexican consulates rebased the Temporary Resident economic-solvency criteria to UMA in Jul 2025** (per `mexperience.com`), with the 2026 UMA at MXN 117.31 — pushing the income threshold to roughly USD 2,800-4,200/month depending on consulate and FX, an effective tightening from the 2024 figure cited in some guides.

**Caribbean carries hurricane-disruption risk** that mainland LATAM does not. **Hurricane Beryl (Jul 2024)** caused USD 9.05 bn aggregate damage including USD 995 m in Jamaica, USD 96.5 m in Barbados (per Wikipedia compiling Moody's/insurance-journal estimates); CCRIF parametric paid Jamaica USD 26.6 m. **Hurricane Melissa (Oct 2025)** triggered a record USD 70.8 m CCRIF payout to Jamaica plus a USD 150 m World Bank cat bond (per Reinsurance News, CNBC). Insurance-market hardening followed both events.

## Africa + Anglo-non-EU

### Regional pattern

Across the 12-country set, two regimes co-exist with almost no overlap. **Anglo-non-EU (AU/CA/NZ/US) has no formal digital-nomad visa** — instead a working-age remote worker enters either as a tourist/visitor (Canada explicitly green-lit this on 31 Jan 2024 via IRCC's "Temporary residents: Digital nomads" guideline; US allows up to 90 days under VWP/ESTA or B-1; AU/NZ tolerate visitor-status remote work but route serious stays through employer-sponsored visas). For longer stays the path is sponsorship: AU **Skills in Demand (SID) subclass 482** (replaced TSS on 7 Dec 2024), CA Express Entry / Tech Talent Strategy work permits (Jun 2023), NZ **AEWV** (rolling reforms through 2025), US H-1B / O-1.

**Africa is the digital-nomad-visa first-mover bloc.** Mauritius launched the **Premium Visa on 23 Oct 2020** — the first formal DN-style visa on the continent — followed by Cabo Verde (Dec 2020), Seychelles **Workcation Retreat Program (Apr 2021)**, and Kenya **Class N Permit (gazetted 1 Oct 2024, applications open Apr-May 2025)**. South Africa published its **Remote Work Visa via the Third Amendment to the Immigration Regulations on 8 Oct 2024** (gazette effective 9 Oct 2024); applications opened in early 2025. Nigeria, Ghana, and Rwanda do **not** offer a formal DN visa.

**Healthcare landscape splits sharply.** Anglo-non-EU runs a hybrid: AU Medicare (residency-gated, plus 11 reciprocal-agreement countries — UK, IT, NL, NZ, IE, FI, NO, SE, BE, MT, SI, per Services Australia), CA provincial Medicare (BC MSP has a ~3-month wait for new residents; ON OHIP removed its waiting period post-COVID), NZ public + ACC (with the critical caveat that **temporary visa holders are not entitled to publicly-funded health care** — they get only ACC's accident-only coverage, no illness coverage), and the US which has **no national system** for working-age non-citizens. DN/work-visa holders in all four are pushed onto private insurance.

**Africa runs a public-plus-mandatory-private model with one major reform in flight.** Kenya **replaced NHIF with the Social Health Authority (SHA) and SHIF on 1 Oct 2024** (Social Health Insurance Act 2023; salaried contribution 2.75% of gross). Nigeria **enacted the NHIA Act on 19 May 2022**, replacing the NHIS with the National Health Insurance Authority and making coverage mandatory for all residents. Rwanda's **Mutuelle de Santé / RSSB CBHI** covers ~91% of the population (one of Africa's highest); contribution rates were updated 24 Feb 2026. Ghana's NHIS continues with G-DRG case-based payments and a 2024 push to expand capitation to 22% of services. South Africa's public hospitals are heavily-loaded post-COVID; medical aid (Discovery, Bonitas, Momentum, Medihelp) is mandatory for the Remote Work Visa.

**The hidden traps cluster around three patterns**: (1) **age- or wait-period gates** — AU's Lifetime Health Cover loading (2% per year over age 30, max 70%, applies if you don't take private hospital cover by 1 July after your 31st birthday), CA's BC MSP 3-month wait; (2) **scope gaps** — NZ's ACC covers only accidents not illness, US lack of any national working-age system; (3) **island-evacuation costs** — SC and CV serious cases routinely require Reunion / Mauritius / Lisbon evacuation.

### Country one-liners

**AU (Australia)** — No formal DN visa; Working Holiday (subclass 417/462) for under-30s/35s and **Skills in Demand (SID) subclass 482** (replaced TSS on 7 Dec 2024) for sponsored skilled work; SID requires OVHC under Visa Condition 8501 unless the citizen's country has a Reciprocal Health Care Agreement. Medicare access is via permanent residency or one of 11 RHCA countries (UK, IT, NL, NZ, IE, FI, NO, SE, BE, MT, SI per Services Australia); private insurers Bupa / Medibank / nib / HCF dominate. **Trap**: **Lifetime Health Cover loading** — 2% per year past age 30 (maximum 70%) applies if private hospital cover not taken by 1 July following 31st birthday; new migrants get a one-year grace from date of first Medicare registration. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**CA (Canada)** — No standalone DN visa; **Tech Talent Strategy** announced 27 Jun 2023 explicitly markets Canada as a DN destination using the existing 6-month visitor track (clarified 31 Jan 2024 IRCC guideline); longer stays via Express Entry, Global Talent Stream, or open work permit. Healthcare is provincial — **BC MSP imposes a wait of the arrival month plus 2 calendar months** (~3 months); **Ontario removed the OHIP wait post-COVID**; Alberta and Quebec also impose waits. **Trap**: bridging private insurance (BLOC-Canada, Manulife, Sun Life) is mandatory during the wait — uninsured care is at full hospital rates (CAD 3-4k/day inpatient at large urban hospitals). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**NZ (New Zealand)** — No formal DN visa; **AEWV (Accredited Employer Work Visa)** is the main sponsored route, reformed through 2025 (work-experience requirement reduced from 3 to 2 years on 10 Mar 2025; wage thresholds removed Feb-Mar 2025; ANZSCO L4-5 visas extended to 3 years on 10 Mar 2025; AEWV thresholds re-pegged to NZD 33.56/hr median wage on 18 Aug 2025 per INZ news centre). Healthcare access: temporary visa holders generally **cannot use publicly-funded health care** (Immigration NZ explicit) — only ACC covers them, and **only for accidents, not illness** (acc.co.nz "what we cover"). **Trap**: DNs misread "ACC covers everyone" as universal cover; cancer / cardiac / chronic-illness costs are out-of-pocket without private insurance (Southern Cross, NIB, AIA). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**US (United States)** — No DN visa. Short-stay remote work tolerated under Visa Waiver Program / ESTA (90 days) or B-1 visitor; longer stays require H-1B (lottery), O-1, L-1, or E-2/E-3. **No national health system for working-age non-Medicare population**: Medicare is 65+ / disability-qualified, Medicaid is income-tested (and tightening — **OBBBA signed 4 Jul 2025** imposes 80-hr/month work requirements on Medicaid expansion enrolees ages 19-64 by 1 Jan 2027 plus 6-month redeterminations). Private cover for working-age DNs runs USD 300-1,500/mo via employer plans, ACA marketplace, or expat carriers (Cigna Global, GeoBlue, IMG). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**ZA (South Africa)** — **Remote Work Visa** gazetted by DHA on 8 Oct 2024 (3rd Amendment to Immigration Regulations of 2014; Schreiber announcement); minimum gross income **ZAR 650,976/year** (per DHA notice), applicants must work for foreign-domiciled employer. Critical Skills List last gazetted 3 Oct 2023 with 18 Oct 2024 points-based system overhaul (minimum 100 points). Healthcare: state hospitals are over-burdened, so DNs / professionals route to medical aid (Discovery, Bonitas, Momentum, Medihelp) at typical working-age rates of ~ZAR 2,500-7,000/mo (≈ USD 130-380/mo, est.). **Trap**: NHI Act signed 15 May 2024 sits unimplemented and politically contested. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**MU (Mauritius)** — **Premium Visa** launched 23 Oct 2020 (first DN-style visa in Africa) per EDB Mauritius; renewable, valid 1 year, requires monthly income of **USD 1,500 individual + USD 500/dependent** (per residency.mu and EDB FAQ Dec 2023), proof of foreign-source income, and travel + health insurance for the full stay. Public healthcare is free at point of use for residents; private (Apollo Bramwell, Fortis Clinique Darné, Wellkin) common for expats. Tax interaction: 183-day rule triggers Mauritian tax residency. **Trap**: insurance must cover the full visa duration up-front — short-renewal policies are rejected at extension. **Confidence: HIGH**.

**SC (Seychelles)** — **Workcation Retreat Program** launched April 2021 (seychelles.com / Tourism Department); valid 1 month to 1 year; one-time fee USD 46 / EUR 45 (covers Visitor Workcation Permit + Health Travel Authorization). Requires international health insurance with **emergency / hospital cover** explicitly including COVID-19 and accommodation booking. Public healthcare on Mahé / Praslin is free for citizens; small private sector (Health Care Seychelles). **Trap**: serious cases routinely require **medical evacuation to Reunion / Mauritius / South Africa** — insurance must include emergency repatriation. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**CV (Cabo Verde)** — **Remote Working Program** launched Dec 2020 (visit-caboverde.com — official tourism portal); 6 months renewable for another 6, EUR 20 visa fee + EUR 34 airport fee. Income test: **average bank balance ≥ EUR 1,500/month for individuals (EUR 2,700 for families) over the prior 6 months** (per visit-caboverde.com). Travel + health insurance covering medical evacuation is mandatory. Public healthcare is limited; serious cases routinely evacuated to **Lisbon** (Portugal). **Trap**: evacuation costs to Lisbon can reach EUR 30-60k for ICU air-ambulance — insurance schedule must show this limit explicitly. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**KE (Kenya)** — **Class N (Digital Nomad) Permit** established by the Kenya Citizenship and Immigration (Amendment) Regulations gazetted 1 Oct 2024; applications opened on the Foreign Nationals Portal Apr-May 2025. Income requirement: **assured USD 55,000/year from non-Kenyan sources**; processing fee USD 200 + annual issuance USD 1,000; valid 1-2 years renewable. Public health insurance: **NHIF was replaced by the Social Health Authority (SHA) and SHIF on 1 Oct 2024** (Social Health Insurance Act 2023; salaried contributors pay 2.75% of gross, capped under tariff rules); private insurance (AAR, Jubilee, Madison, CIC) standard for expats. **Trap**: SHIF is in active rollout — provider network and benefit caps are still settling, and Class N permit rules don't explicitly require enrolment, so private cover is the safe default. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**NA (Namibia)** — **Digital Nomad Visa exists** (run jointly by NIPDB + Ministry of Home Affairs) — but **all terms are advisory/aggregator-tier, NOT anchored to a gov.na/NIPDB primary page** (per the playbook): ~USD 2,000/mo income main applicant (~USD 1,000 spouse, ~USD 500/child), NAD 3,300 (~USD 180) fee, **6 months + one 6-month extension** (no PR/citizenship path); income from sources outside Namibia. **No health-insurance requirement is stated in the surfaced (aggregator) terms — do not assume one; verify at [NIPDB](https://www.nipdb.com/) / [gov.na](https://www.gov.na/).** No public health-insurance scheme is documented as foreigner-accessible here; expats use private hospitals (Lady Pohamba, Windhoek; coastal private at Swakopmund/Walvis Bay) alongside public Windhoek Central / Katutura State. **Trap**: outside Windhoek + the coastal corridor distances are large — serious-case medical access often means travel to Windhoek, so **emergency-evacuation cover is the practical default**; NAD is pegged 1:1 to ZAR (Common Monetary Area), simplifying premium FX. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (DN-visa existence noted, but income/fee/duration are aggregator-tier — verify at NIPDB before relying).

**BW (Botswana)** — **No formal DN visa** (none confirmed at a primary source per the playbook); no Welcome-Stamp- or GCCP-style remote-worker route — a remote worker routes through the standard residence/work-permit framework (gov.bw), with no income-threshold remote-work visa. No DN-visa healthcare requirement exists because there is no DN visa; no foreigner-accessible public health-insurance scheme is documented here. Expats use private hospitals (Bokamoso, Sidilega — Gaborone) alongside public Princess Marina (Gaborone) / Nyangabgwe (Francistown). **Trap**: specialist + complex cases are **frequently referred to South Africa (Johannesburg/Pretoria)** — private cover with cross-border + evacuation logistics is the practical default for any remote parcel. **Confidence: HIGH** on the no-DN-visa finding, MEDIUM on healthcare-access specifics (referral pattern documented, foreigner-private pricing not sourced).

**NG (Nigeria)** — **No formal DN visa**. Remote workers use Tourist Visa (90 days), STR (Subject-to-Regularization) for employment, Temporary Work Permit (R11, 6 months), or — for long stays — CERPAC (Combined Expatriate Residence Permit and Aliens Card, USD 2,000/year). **NHIA Act signed 19 May 2022** by President Buhari replaced the NHIS, making health insurance **mandatory for all residents**, and shifting fund management from HMOs to State Health Insurance Schemes. Practical reality: expats use private (Avon HMO, Hygeia, AXA Mansard, Leadway). **Trap**: NHIA enrolment is technically mandatory but enforcement against expats is patchy; budget for private cover regardless. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**GH (Ghana)** — **No formal DN visa**. Remote workers use tourist visa (60 days) or apply for work + residence permit through Ghana Immigration Service. **NHIS** (National Health Insurance Authority) covers citizens and is open to legal residents; payment uses G-DRG case-based reimbursement plus capitation, with 2024 reforms expanding capitation to 22% of services. Private supplements (Glico Healthcare, Vanguard, Acacia) common for expats. **Trap**: NHIS provider network excludes most premium private hospitals — for serious care, private cover is necessary. **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**RW (Rwanda)** — **No formal DN visa**, but Rwanda offers visa-on-arrival for most nationalities (30 days) and the East African Tourist Visa (90 days, multi-entry, RW+KE+UG). Longer stays via work permit or investor route. Healthcare: **Mutuelle de Santé / CBHI** under RSSB covers ~91% of the population; rates updated 24 Feb 2026 with annual contributions of RWF 3,000-7,000 (~USD 2.70-6.30) for self-paying tiers. Private (Radiant, Britam, MUA, Eden Care) for expat-grade care. **Trap**: foreigners typically can enrol in CBHI as legal residents but most expats use private — King Faisal Hospital (Kigali) and Rwanda Military Hospital are the main referral options; complex cases route to Nairobi or Johannesburg. **Confidence: LOW** (limited primary-source documentation on foreigner-specific CBHI enrolment terms — verify at rssb.rw/index.php?id=17).

## 7 universal mitigations

1. **Buy private health insurance BEFORE the DN visa application — and verify the policy is accepted by the consulate.** Most DN visa applications require evidence of private health coverage at submission stage. Cigna Global / Allianz Care / BUPA Global / GeoBlue / IMG Global Select / Aetna International are the global standards; some jurisdictions (HR, CY, MT, AE) require locally-licensed or locally-registered policies. Working-age (25-50) cost: €70-300/mo EU; USD 100-500/mo APAC; USD 300-1,500/mo US; USD 50-150/mo Caucasus + SE Asia.
2. **Time the DN-to-residence conversion against tax-residency thresholds.** Most jurisdictions count tax-residency at 183 days/year. DN visa holders staying <183 days remain tax-non-resident (no income tax exposure, no public-system mandatory enrollment) but ALSO no public-system access. Crossing 183 days triggers public-system enrollment in CH/DE/AT/NL/IT/FR/ES — verify the exact tax-residency rule + public-system trigger date BEFORE crossing the threshold.
3. **For DE/CH/NL — enroll in mandatory public health insurance day-1 of registration to avoid backdated premiums.** DE Krankenversicherung day-1 mandatory (€1,000+ backdated penalty); CH KVG within 3 months (50% surcharge for late enrollment); NL zorgverzekering 4-mo grace with backdating. Use Commerzbank/Deutsche Bank international desk pre-arrival in DE; engage cantonal Krankenkasse before B-permit issuance in CH; enroll with NL Zorgverzekering within first 4 months.
4. **For tax-attractive regimes (PT NHR2.0/IFICI, ES Beckham, IT 24-bis, GR Non-Dom) — verify healthcare access is SEPARATE from tax election.** PT NHR2.0 / ES Beckham / IT 24-bis / GR Non-Dom all create attractive tax windows but do NOT bundle public healthcare; DN must enroll in SNS / Convenio Especial / SSN / ESY separately via residency + tax-registration. Budget for both tax-election fees + healthcare contribution.
5. **For US-based DNs (or returning US persons) — purchase Marketplace HDHP + HSA OR international PPO BEFORE leaving the US.** US has no national health system; DNs reliant on (1) employer-sponsored ACA-Marketplace coverage if formally employed, (2) self-purchased Marketplace HDHP + HSA, (3) international PPO (Aetna International, Cigna Global Healthcare, GeoBlue Xplorer, IMG Global Patriot), (4) IBNR coverage if working remotely from non-US country. Lifetime coverage continuity is the binding constraint for chronic conditions.
6. **For Caribbean + small-island DN destinations — verify EMERGENCY MEDICAL EVACUATION coverage in private insurance.** BB / BS / BZ / JM / MV / SC / CV all face hurricane disruption + small-island healthcare evacuation costs. Private insurance must include emergency repatriation (typically USD 250k+ coverage). Lloyd's syndicates + medical-evacuation specialists (International SOS, Global Rescue) are the practical filter.
7. **Re-verify regulator URLs + DN visa terms + healthcare-enrollment thresholds within 30 days of decision-day.** This document's `Last verified: 2026-05-10`; DN visa programmes + healthcare reforms have a half-life of 12-18 months. Most volatile categories: PT NHR2.0/IFICI Lei 82/2023 implementation; ES Beckham extension RD 1008/2023; IT 24-bis raised to €200k 10 Aug 2024 (PLF 2026 may reduce); JP Digital Nomad Visa launched 31 Mar 2024 (terms still settling); KR Workation Visa Jan 2024; TH DTV Jul 2024; SI DN Visa Nov 2025 (newly launched); CR Estancia Rentista Aug 2022; CO V-DNV Oct 2022; BR DN Visa RNM 16/2022; PT post-2023 SEF dissolution + AIMA delays; CH revised KVG premium increases 2024-2026; AE Federal Decree-Law 13/2023 mandatory health insurance Jan 2025; ZA Remote Worker Visa announced 2024 (implementation 2025); KE Class N DN Visa launched 2024; US OBBBA Jul 2025 Medicaid eligibility tightening. Decision-makers should pull current regulator notice + visa scheme rulebook + most recent healthcare-enrollment threshold before relying on any specific number here.
