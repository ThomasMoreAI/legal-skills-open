# Universal `--surveyor` Section — Pre-Offer Building Inspection · Inspector Credentialing · Buyer's Duty-to-Inspect

Foreign-buyer **surveyor / building-inspection** layer: how a buyer obtains an independent structural condition assessment *before* committing, who is credentialed to produce it, what it costs, what it covers, and — critically — where the law puts the inspection burden on the buyer. Covers the regulated-cert framework per country (RICS Home Survey Levels, ASHI/InterNACHI, AS 4349.1, diagnostiqueur + COFRAC, geometra perizia, NBR 16747, 既存住宅状況調査技術者), cost bands, deliverable format, heritage-specific surveyor accreditation, and the duty-to-inspect / caveat-emptor posture. Distinct from `--notary` (legal completion, not technical inspection) and `--type=heritage` (listing constraints, which mention but do not source a national surveyor rolodex). This section answers: *can I get a credible condition survey here before I'm bound, who from, and what happens legally if I skip it?*

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *pre-offer technical-inspection* layer — the professional body/qualification a foreign buyer hires for a condition survey, the cost band, the report's standard scope, the heritage-accredited variant, and the jurisdiction's duty-to-inspect / hidden-defect posture. It does **not** restate the conveyancing/notary completion process (`--notary`) or heritage-listing constraints (`--type=heritage`). A foreign-buyer typically needs three pieces of intel before the offer: (1) who is credentialed to survey here and how to verify the credential, (2) what the survey costs and covers, (3) what the law does if a latent defect emerges after completion — i.e. is the survey the buyer's only protection.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Regulated-cert framework** — the professional body / qualification a buyer hires for a condition survey, named with its statute (e.g. UK RICS Home Survey Level 1/2/3 + RPSA/SAVA; FR expert bâtiment — note the mandatory DDT pack is health/safety/energy disclosure, NOT a structural survey; IT geometra/architetto perizia tecnica; ES arquitecto técnico + ITE; DE Bausachverständiger; US ASHI/InterNACHI; AU AS 4349.1; JP 既存住宅状況調査技術者; BR ABNT NBR 16747:2020). State explicitly where inspectors are **unregulated** or licensing is **sub-national**.
2. **Cost band** — typical fee for a standard house condition survey, local currency + EUR/USD, date-stamped, source-tier tagged (market-norm ⚠️ / primary). Where no public band exists, `data not publicly available — verify by direct quote`.
3. **Deliverable format + standard scope** — what the report covers (foundation / roof / damp / structure / services / energy / asbestos-radon-pest as applicable to the jurisdiction).
4. **Heritage-specific surveyor** — the conservation-accredited variant (RICS Conservation-Accredited; FR architecte du patrimoine; IT geometra/architetto-restauratore; ES arquitecto técnico de rehabilitación) where it exists.
5. **Buyer's duty-to-inspect / caveat-emptor posture** — does the law put the inspection burden on the buyer (SE Jordabalken 4:19 undersökningsplikt; NO undersøkelsesplikt; DE/AT/CH caveat emptor unless proven concealment; NZ strict caveat emptor), or is there an implied condition warranty / statutory hidden-defect remedy (and its prescription clock)?
6. **Named-firm / register pointer** — only where surfaced from a citable source, tagged by tier; never an endorsement, always "verify against the public register".
7. **Confidence**: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **A mandatory "diagnostic / disclosure pack" is NOT a structural condition survey.** France's DDT (DPE / CREP lead / amiante / termites / gaz / électricité / ERP / Carrez), Spain's certificado energético + ITE, Italy's APE, Portugal's certificado — these are health, safety and energy *disclosures* required for sale. None assesses structural condition. Assuming the legally required pack tells you whether the building is sound is the single most common — and most expensive — foreign-buyer error in civil-law Europe, where no RICS-equivalent voluntary structural survey is built into the process.
2. **Several jurisdictions put the inspection burden legally on the BUYER — skipping the survey forfeits the remedy.** Sweden's *undersökningsplikt* (Jordabalken 1970:994, 4 kap. 19 §) — hiring a besiktningsman does not even discharge it; Norway's *undersøkelsesplikt*; Germany/Austria/Switzerland caveat emptor defeated only by *arglistig* / proven deliberate concealment (DE § 444 BGB); New Zealand strict caveat emptor. In these markets the survey is not optional diligence — it *is* the buyer's legal protection.
3. **"Licensed inspector" is not a given — regulation is often absent or sub-national.** New Zealand inspectors are unregulated and NZS 4306:2005 is voluntary, not law. US licensing is state-by-state (~15 states + DC do not license, including CA/CO/GA/PA). Canada licenses only in BC and Alberta — Ontario's Home Inspection Act 2017 passed but was **never proclaimed**. Australia requires a licence only in Queensland (QBCC). Verify the individual's specific credential against the named register; do not assume a national standard exists.
4. **A seller-disclosure form does not replace a survey or defeat an "as-is / voetstoots" clause.** South Africa's Property Practitioners Act 2019 mandatory disclosure form (in force 1 Feb 2022) does **not** abolish the *voetstoots* clause — the buyer must still prove the seller's deliberate concealment of a known defect. US state seller-disclosure + "as-is" sales are analogous. The disclosure form is a complement to, not a substitute for, an independent survey.
5. **Mandatory developer/construction-side inspection is NOT a buyer's pre-offer condition survey.** Türkiye conflates three distinct things: *yapı denetim* (Law 4708 — supervises the contractor's build for the developer), the SPK-licensed *ekspertiz* (a mortgage *valuation*), and an optional private buyer structural inspection. Only the third protects the buyer. Equivalent confusion exists wherever statutory build-supervision is mistaken for a buyer survey.
6. **Where no survey culture exists, the fallback is a defect/warranty statute — and its clock is short.** Civil-law hidden-defect actions (*vicios ocultos* / *vizi occulti* / *Gewährleistung*) and statutory builder-warranty/decennial regimes (AE 10-yr structural + ≥1-yr non-structural per Dubai Law 6/2019; JP 品確法 10-yr; SG 12-month DLP + 15-yr structural; MY 24-month DLP; BR CC vícios redibitórios) are *post-sale litigation*, not pre-purchase certainty, and each carries a short prescription period. Knowing the clock is part of the diligence.
7. **A clean survey does not cure title/ownership risk.** Where the structure cannot be fully foreigner-owned (TH 49% condo / no land, ID nominee voidness, PH 60/40, CN land-use-right, GCC designated zones), the survey assures construction quality only. Route title viability to `--notary` / `compare`; a sound building on an unsecurable title is still a loss.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

This region splits into three survey-culture families. (1) **Common-law "buyer beware, survey is your only protection"** (UK, IE) — *caveat emptor* is the default, no statutory seller condition disclosure, and a voluntary RICS/SCSI survey is the buyer's primary risk tool with well-defined professional product tiers (RICS Home Survey Level 1/2/3). (2) **Civil-law "regulated diagnostic package, NO voluntary structural-survey product"** (FR, BE, PT, ES partially, IT) — a *statutory diagnostic dossier* (FR DDT, PT certificado energético, ES ITE for old buildings) is mandatory and seller-paid, but it is a health/safety/energy disclosure, NOT a structural condition survey; a voluntary structural inspection exists only as an unregulated/loosely-regulated *expert bâtiment / perizia / informe* market with no RICS-equivalent consumer-tiered product (the most common foreign-buyer misconception in FR/IT/ES). (3) **Nordic + DE "buyer's statutory undersökningsplikt / undersøkelsesplikt / caveat-emptor-unless-arglistig"** (SE, NO, FI, DK, DE) — law shifts the inspection burden onto the buyer, and skipping a survey can forfeit the right to claim *visible* defects entirely; NO/DK/SE have a regulated seller-side condition-report regime (tilstandsrapport / besiktning) that doubles as the buyer's protection, and missing it is materially adverse.

### Country one-liners

- **UK United Kingdom** — Framework: **RICS Home Survey** Level 1 / Level 2 / Level 3 (RICS, primary professional body; old names = Condition Report / HomeBuyer Report / Building Survey respectively — confirmed [RICS consumer guide](https://www.rics.org/consumer-guides/house-surveys-uk-the-costs-types-and-benefits-of-an-rics-home-survey)); parallel **RPSA / Sava Home Condition Survey** (Residential Property Surveyors Association + Sava/BRE accreditation scheme — [rpsa.org.uk](https://www.rpsa.org.uk/), [sava.co.uk](https://sava.co.uk/the-sava-hub/hub-article/rpsa/)). Cost: Level 1 ~£300-900; Level 2 ~£400-1,000; Level 3 ~£700-1,500+ (2025-26, ⚠️ aggregator/market-norm cross-check across [hoa.org.uk](https://hoa.org.uk/advice/guides-for-homeowners/i-am-buying/how-much-does-a-house-survey-cost/), [surveymatch.co.uk](https://surveymatch.co.uk/rics-survey-cost/); RICS itself states only "a few hundred pounds" to ">£1000" — no fixed tariff). Report: condition-rating system **1 (no repair) / 2 (defects, not urgent) / 3 (urgent / serious)** per element — roof, damp, timber, structure, services, with Level 3 adding repair methodology + structural commentary. Heritage: **RICS Conservation-Accredited** surveyor (RICS register). Buyer-duty: **caveat emptor** — no statutory seller condition disclosure (only TA6 property-information form, seller-completed, not a survey); the survey *is* the buyer's protection. Firms: e.surv (Level 2 product page, ⚠️ company self-published). **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **FR France** — Framework: TWO distinct things. (a) **Mandatory, seller-paid statutory package** = the **Dossier de Diagnostic Technique (DDT)** performed by a **diagnostiqueur immobilier certifié** (certification mandatory; certifying bodies COFRAC-accredited per [Arrêté du 1 juillet 2024 JORFTEXT000049890008](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000049890008)). DDT contents (confirmed [service-public.gouv.fr F10798](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F10798)): DPE; CREP/plomb (pre-1949 builds); état d'amiante (permit pre-1 July 1997); termites (designated zones); état gaz (>15 yr install); état électricité (>15 yr install); **état des risques (ERP, ex-ESRIS — natural/mining/seismic/radon)**; assainissement non collectif; bruit aérien; mesurage Loi Carrez (copro); CIL/carnet d'information du logement (post-2023). (b) **Voluntary structural inspection** = an **"expert bâtiment" / architecte / ingénieur** producing an *expertise* or *pré-état des lieux* — an *unregulated title*, no consumer-tiered product. **France has NO statutory or RICS-equivalent voluntary structural condition survey** — the DDT is a health/safety/energy disclosure, NOT a structural survey ([service-public.gouv.fr F10798](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F10798) explicitly: no mandatory structural/condition survey exists in FR law); RICS does operate in FR (since 1933) but for *valuation* (Red Book), not a home-buyer survey product. Cost: DDT pack ~€250-600 for a house (2025, ⚠️ aggregator [mcdiagnostics.fr](https://mcdiagnostics.fr/vente-location/tarif-diagnostic-immobilier/)) ❌ DEPRECATED — dead on 2026-09-18; verify with the authority named in this row; voluntary expert bâtiment ~€400-1,000+ visit + report (2025, ⚠️ market-norm, [drklein-equivalent FR firm pages]). Heritage: **architecte du patrimoine** (Ordre des architectes register; obligatory adviser within sites patrimoniaux remarquables / abords de monuments). Buyer-duty: no statutory duty-to-inspect, but *vices cachés* claim (Code civil art. 1641) is weak if the seller inserted a *clause d'exclusion de garantie des vices cachés* (valid for non-professional sellers) — so a voluntary expert visit is materially advisable. Firms: none surfaced from a citable register — do not invent. **Confidence**: HIGH (DDT + COFRAC sourced to gov; voluntary-market cost MEDIUM).
- **IT Italy** — Framework: no statutory pre-purchase structural survey; the buyer commissions a **perizia tecnica** from a **geometra** (Collegio dei Geometri register), **ingegnere** (Ordine degli Ingegneri) or **architetto** (Ordine degli Architetti) — title regulated, the *perizia* product is not consumer-tiered. Three legal grades: *perizia semplice* (no legal value), *perizia asseverata* (technician attests, criminal liability for falsity), *perizia giurata* (sworn before a court clerk/giudice di pace — strongest). The dominant Italian pre-purchase need is **conformità urbanistica + catastale** (cross-check of as-built vs cadastral planimetry / building permits) — a documentary-legal check folded into the perizia, distinct from a structural condition survey. Mandatory at sale: **APE (Attestato di Prestazione Energetica)** by a certified technician (energy only, not structural). Cost: perizia ~€100-300 typical, up to ~€1,000 for large/complex (2025, ⚠️ aggregator [businessonline.it](https://www.businessonline.it/articoli/quanto-costa-una-perizia-tecnica-di-un-geometra-giurata-e-non-sul-valore-di-una-casa-prezzi-medi.html)); + marca da bollo €16/4 pages if giurata. Report: structure, roof, damp, plumbing, electrical, chimneys + catastale/urbanistica conformity. Heritage: **geometra/architetto restauratore**; properties under *vincolo* (Codice dei beni culturali D.Lgs. 42/2004) require Soprintendenza-qualified professional. Buyer-duty: *vizi occulti* (Codice civile art. 1490) claim within 1 yr / 8-day notice — short windows make a perizia advisable. ⚠️ Note: the abruzzoruralproperty/aggregator advice that rural renovation property should "only be bought with a written geometra stima" is a market-norm 🟡, not a statute. Firms: none from a citable register. **Confidence**: HIGH (regulated bodies + APE gov-mandated; cost MEDIUM aggregator).
- **ES Spain** — Framework: TWO things. (a) **Mandatory for older buildings**: the **ITE (Inspección Técnica de Edificios)** / in some CCAA the **IEE (Informe de Evaluación de Edificios)** — obligatory generally **before the building reaches 45-50 years' age** (varies by Comunidad Autónoma / municipality; e.g. Cataluña Llei 18/2007), performed by an **arquitecto técnico / aparejador** (Consejo General de la Arquitectura Técnica — CGATE — colegiado), arquitecto, or ingeniero de la edificación. ⚠️ Critical foreign-buyer point: where the building is ITE-obligated, the **seller must hand the buyer a copy of the ITE + certificado de aptitud at transfer** ([unir.net summary of obligation](https://www.unir.net/revista/ingenieria/que-es-inspeccion-tecnica-de-edificios/) ⚠️ aggregator; underlying = Ley 18/2007 / Ley de Vivienda + autonomic ITE decrees). (b) Mandatory at sale regardless: **certificado de eficiencia energética**. (c) Voluntary pre-purchase building survey = an **arquitecto técnico** *informe técnico / nota de estado* — title regulated, product not consumer-tiered. Cost: ITE ~€200-600 depending on CA + building size (2025, ⚠️ aggregator [certicalia.com](https://www.certicalia.com/inspeccion-tecnica-de-edificios)); voluntary informe similar order. Report (ITE): structure, façade, roof/cubierta, common installations, accessibility, energy (IEE variant). Heritage: **arquitecto técnico de rehabilitación**; BIC (Bien de Interés Cultural) properties need a heritage-qualified arquitecto. Buyer-duty: *vicios ocultos* (Código Civil art. 1484) 6-month action window — short; ITE is a partial safety net for shared elements only, not the private dwelling interior. Firms: none from citable register. **Confidence**: HIGH for ITE obligation existence; MEDIUM for the per-CA age threshold (varies — must verify at the specific Comunidad Autónoma decree).
- **PT Portugal** — Framework: no statutory pre-purchase structural survey. Mandatory at sale: **Certificado Energético** by a **Perito Qualificado** — an arquitecto (Ordem dos Arquitectos) or engenheiro (Ordem dos Engenheiros) with ≥5 yrs experience + ADENE exam pass; legal basis **Decreto-Lei n.º 101-D/2020** (alt. DL 102/2021) ([oern.pt](https://www.sce.pt/)). Voluntary structural inspection = **engenheiro civil** (Ordem dos Engenheiros) or **arquitecto** (Ordem dos Arquitectos) producing a *vistoria técnica / relatório de inspeção* — title regulated via the Ordens, product not consumer-tiered. Cost: certificado energético ~€28 (T0-T1) to ~€65 (≥T6) for housing (2025, [peritosqualificados.pt](https://www.peritosqualificados.pt/pt/certificado-energetico/) — company page but matches ADENE tariff bands ⚠️); voluntary vistoria ~€200-600+ market-norm 🟡 (no citable tariff). Report: structure, roof/cobertura, damp/humidade, infiltrations, services. Heritage: arquitecto with IGESPAR/património experience for *imóveis classificados*. Buyer-duty: *defeitos / vícios* (Código Civil art. 905, 913) — 1-yr window for immóveis; voluntary vistoria advisable, no statutory duty. Firms: none from citable register. **Confidence**: HIGH (cert. energético + Ordens gov-sourced); voluntary cost LOW (market-norm only).
- **SE Sweden** — Framework: **besiktningsman** (no state licence; market bodies SBR Byggingenjörerna / Swedish standard *Sveriges Byggingenjörers Riksförbund*) performs an *överlåtelsebesiktning* (transfer inspection). **The material trap**: under **Jordabalken (1970:994) 4 kap. 19 §** the buyer carries a statutory **undersökningsplikt** (duty to investigate): defects that *should have been discoverable on a careful inspection* **cannot be claimed against the seller afterwards**. Per Högsta domstolen doctrine, **hiring a besiktningsman does NOT discharge the duty** — if the inspector misses a discoverable defect, the *buyer* (not seller) bears it; and a noted remark *expands* the duty (warning-signal effect). Source: [Jordabalken Riksdagen 4 kap. 19 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/jordabalk-1970994_sfs-1970-994/), [FMI consumer page](https://fmi.se/vad-galler-vid-formedling/marknadsforing-och-visning/koparens-undersokningsplikt/). Cost: besiktning ~SEK 5,000-15,000 (~€440-1,300) typical house (2025, ⚠️ market-norm aggregator). Report: visible structure, roof, damp, foundation, attic/crawl-space; *not* hidden/concealed elements. Heritage: byggnadsantikvarie for *byggnadsminne*. **Confidence**: HIGH (statute + FMI gov-adjacent sourced; cost MEDIUM).
- **NO Norway** — Framework: regulated **tilstandsrapport** to standard **NS 3600** (latest NS 3600:2025, prior NS 3600:2018) by a certified *bygningssakkyndig* (members typically via Norsk takst / takstmann). The change effective **1 January 2022** is the *forskrift til avhendingslova* (**FOR-2021-06-08-1850**), made under the amended **avhendingslova (LOV-1992-07-03-93)** — the act itself was amended by **LOV-2019-06-07-20** (it is NOT a new act dated 2022). Effect: for consumer sales, general **"solgt som den er" / "as-is" reservations have NO legal effect (avhendingslova § 3-9)**, and a compliant NS 3600 tilstandsrapport (§ 3-10 + forskrift) shifts protection — documented defects in the report **cannot be claimed by the buyer**. Buyer's **undersøkelsesplikt** (duty to inspect, § 3-10): defects the buyer should have discovered pre-contract / failed to inspect when asked cannot be claimed *unless seller acted grossly negligently / dishonestly*. Source: [Avhendingslova Lovdata LOV-1992-07-03-93](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1992-07-03-93), [standard.no NS 3600](https://standard.no/fagomrader/teknisk-tilstandsanalyse-av-bolig---ns-3600/teknisk-tilstandsanalyse-ved-omsetning-av-bolig--ns-3600/). Cost: tilstandsrapport ~NOK 8,000-20,000 (~€680-1,700) (2025, ⚠️ market-norm aggregator). Report: NS 3600 building-element walkthrough graded **TG0-TG3** (TG = tilstandsgrad). Heritage: antikvar for *fredet* building (kulturminneloven). **Confidence**: HIGH (Lovdata + Standard Norge primary; cost MEDIUM).
- **FI Finland** — Framework: **asuntokaupan kuntotarkastus** to performance instruction **KH 90-00394**, by an inspector holding the **AKK (Asuntokaupan Kuntotarkastaja) pätevyys** granted by **FISE** (requires building-foreman/engineer/technician degree + ≥5 yrs experience + exam) ([suomenkuntotarkastus.fi](https://www.suomenkuntotarkastus.fi/asuntokaupan-kuntotarkastus-kh-90-00394/) ⚠️ company; KH 90-00394 itself is the citable instruction, [isokyro.fi PDF copy](https://fise.fi/en/)). Buyer-duty trap: the kuntotarkastus report supports the seller's *selonantovelvollisuus* (duty to inform) and reciprocally the buyer's **selonottovelvollisuus** (duty to find out) — buyer who skips/ignores a noted issue weakens any later *virhe* claim under Maakaari (540/1995). Cost: ~€400-800 incl. one structural opening; small outbuilding ~€250 (2025, ⚠️ company-page market-norm). Report: KH 90-00394 scope — structure, roof, damp, ventilation, with one *rakenneavaus* (structural opening). Heritage: restaurointi-qualified architect for *suojeltu* building. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (KH 90-00394 + FISE pätevyys real; cost from company pages only — LOW for cost).
- **DK Denmark** — Framework: **tilstandsrapport** under the **huseftersynsordningen**, written by a *beskikket bygningssagkyndig* (state-authorised, Sikkerhedsstyrelsen-approved form) — legal basis the consumer-protection law on real-property transfer + Bekendtgørelse om huseftersynsordningen ([retsinformation.dk BEK 2015/60](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2015/60/dan/pdf), [boligejer.dk](https://boligejer.dk/tilstandsrapport)). Buyer-duty trap: the tilstandsrapport (+ elinstallationsrapport + ejerskifteforsikring offer) is **not strictly lovpligtig**, BUT without it the seller retains the full **10-year liability for hidden defects** — i.e. the scheme is the mechanism by which the seller *escapes* liability, so a buyer facing a sale *without* a tilstandsrapport should treat it as a red flag (seller has not bought out of the 10-yr regime). Cost: ~DKK 4,000-10,000 (~€535-1,340) (2025, ⚠️ aggregator [boligsiden.dk](https://www.boligsiden.dk/guides/boligkoeb/hvad-er-en-tilstandsrapport)). Report: visible damage, signs of damage, missing elements, obvious building-reg breaches (Sikkerhedsstyrelsen schema). Heritage: bygningssagkyndig with *fredede bygninger* experience (Slots- og Kulturstyrelsen). **Confidence**: HIGH (Retsinformation + boligejer.dk gov-sourced; cost MEDIUM).
- **DE Germany** — Framework: **Bausachverständiger / Baugutachter** — title NOT statutorily protected; trust signals = *öffentlich bestellt und vereidigt* (IHK Sachverständigenverzeichnis) or DEKRA/TÜV/DIN-18205-aligned certification ([drklein.de](https://www.drklein.de/bausachverstaendiger.html) ⚠️ broker). NO statutory pre-purchase survey; NO seller condition disclosure. **The material trap**: German residential resale runs on **caveat emptor reinforced by a near-universal *Gewährleistungsausschluss* ("gekauft wie gesehen")** in the notarial deed — valid between private parties, it **bars the buyer's defect claims** *unless the seller fraudulently concealed (arglistig verschwiegen) a defect* — and the buyer must *prove* the seller's *Arglist*, which is hard (§ 444 BGB; the "wie gesehen" clause does not protect against arglistig concealment, [aurentum-immobilien.de](https://www.aurentum-immobilien.de/post/maengelhaftung) ⚠️ broker-summary of § 444 BGB). Practical effect: a voluntary Bausachverständiger inspection *before* signing is the buyer's only real protection — there is no Nordic-style mandatory report and no UK-style product tiers. Cost: inspection + verbal advice ~€400-600; written beweissicherndes Gutachten ~€1,200-1,500 (2025, ⚠️ broker [drklein.de](https://www.drklein.de/gutachter-hauskauf.html)). Report: structure, damp, roof, services, modernisation-cost estimate. Heritage: Sachverständiger für Denkmalschutz / restaurator (Landesdenkmalamt). Firms: DEKRA / TÜV (cert bodies, not survey firms — do not cite as buyer firms). **Confidence**: HIGH for the caveat-emptor/Arglist trap (§ 444 BGB); cost MEDIUM (broker pages).
- **AT Austria** — Framework: **Bausachverständiger** — title not uniformly protected; *allgemein beeidete und gerichtlich zertifizierte Sachverständige* (court-certified register) are the trust tier. No statutory pre-purchase survey. Buyer-duty: *Gewährleistung* under ABGB §§ 922-933 — 3-yr warranty for immovables, but a *Gewährleistungsverzicht* / "wie besichtigt" clause is common (limited by ABGB § 928 — patent/known defects not warranted; and not enforceable for *arglistig* concealment). A voluntary Bausachverständiger Begehung before notarisation is the practical protection (parallels DE). Cost: hourly ~€100-200; full written Gutachten ~€1,000-2,000; inspection+defect-doc ~€1,050 at ~€150/h (2025, ⚠️ aggregator [infina.at](https://www.infina.at/ratgeber/bausachverstaendiger-in-oesterreich/), [planradar.com](https://www.planradar.com/at/kosten-baugutachter/)). Report: structure, damp, roof, services, modernisation cost; ⚠️ AT-specific add-on — *Konsensprüfung* (as-built vs Baubewilligung) increasingly advised, missing/defective Baubewilligung is a flagged AT buyer risk. ## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

Across this entire region there is **no UK/RICS-style voluntary "buyer's homebuyer survey" culture**. The norm at purchase is: (a) **no independent inspection at all** (most common, especially apartments), or (b) a **court-expert / certified-engineer technical expertise** (posudek / ekspertiza / expertiză / технически паспорт / tehniskā apsekošana) — a quasi-forensic document designed for litigation, lender, or permit purposes, *retro-fitted* by a foreign buyer to a pre-offer condition check. A foreign buyer must commission this privately; it is not a standard conveyancing step and most local sellers/agents will not expect it. The regulated cert that exists is the **chartered/authorised structural engineer** (per-country chamber) and, separately, the **court-appointed expert** (Ministry-of-Justice registry) — these are engineering/litigation credentials, NOT consumer-protection inspectors. Pan-regional seismic note: RO (Vrancea), AL, ME, BA, MK, parts of HR/SI/BG, and GE/AM/AZ are moderate-to-high seismic; a structural/seismic expertiză (esp. for pre-1990 reinforced-concrete panel blocks and unreinforced masonry) is the single highest-value private commission in this region — far more so than a generic "condition survey".

## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

No country in this region has a statutory buyer's pre-offer condition survey. The recurring trap is conflating the **developer/construction-side supervision regime** (TR yapı denetim under Law 4708, AE consultant supervision) or the **mandatory SPK valuation/appraisal** (TR eksper raporu) with an independent buyer condition survey — they are distinct, and only a separately-hired private civil engineer's structural inspection is the buyer's actual condition tool. The load-bearing legal fact across the region is the **statutory decennial defect-liability regime** (~10 years for structural collapse/defects), which is the legal recourse that makes a buyer's survey actionable, not the survey itself — with two important exceptions: TR fixes building structural-defect limitation at **5 years** (extended to 20 where the seller is at gross fault) and BH at **5 years, not 10** (the GCC outlier). AE and IL have mature buyer-commissioned snagging/bedek-bait markets; LB is an active-crisis environment where the building-safety oversight regime has collapsed.

### Country one-liners

- **AE United Arab Emirates** — Framework: No statutory buyer's survey. A **buyer-commissioned snagging / building inspection** is a large, mature foreign-buyer business (esp. Dubai off-plan handover). Inspection consultancies are licensed by the relevant emirate's Department of Economy/Municipality; engineers signing structural assessments are expected to be registered with the **Society of Engineers — UAE**; many firms additionally hold RERA/DLD alignment and private InterNACHI® accreditation (private cert, not a state licence — 🟡 firm marketing). ## APAC + Central Asia + South Asia

### Regional pattern

APAC is bifurcated: NE-Asia (JP/KR/TW/HK/MO/SG) has formalised inspector qualifications and statutory defect/warranty regimes, while SE-Asia + South/Central Asia largely have **no buyer-survey statute** — and in TH/ID/PH/VN the inspected structure is often *also* not fully foreigner-ownable, so survey value is mostly construction-quality assurance, not title. None of the 20 has a mandatory *buyer* pre-purchase building-survey law; the closest buyer-protective statutes are defect/warranty regimes (JP 品確法 10 yr / 重要事項説明; SG 12-mo DLP + 15-yr structural; MY 24-mo DLP; VN 60-mo apartment warranty; IN RERA 5-yr; KR 하자담보) — these are the load-bearing facts, not a survey mandate (PK has NO RERA-equivalent; VU's protection is hazard-driven structural review, not a statute). A recurring trap is presenting maintenance/age-driven statutory inspection regimes (HK MBIS, SG PSI) or developer-handover acceptance as buyer transaction tools; they are not.

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

A voluntary buyer-commissioned structural survey is NOT a normalised step in any LATAM market. The dominant transactional protection across civil-law LATAM (MX/BR/AR/CR/PA/CL/CO/UY/PE/EC/PY) is the **civil-code hidden-defects warranty** (*vicios ocultos* / *vícios redibitórios* / *vicios redhibitorios*) — a *post-sale litigation remedy*, not a pre-purchase inspection. Brazil is the exception with the strongest formal building-inspection regime (ABNT NBR 16747:2020). The Caribbean common-law states (BS/JM/BB/BZ) are the inverse: RICS-style chartered-surveyor pre-purchase reports exist there because of UK legal heritage and expat/mortgage-lender demand, and under common-law *caveat emptor* the independent survey is the *primary* protection (not a fallback). A foreign buyer who wants pre-offer condition certainty must *proactively* commission and pay for it almost everywhere in this region — it will not be offered by default.

## Africa + Anglo non-EU

### Regional pattern

This region splits between Anglo non-EU markets with formalised (but sub-national or voluntary) home-inspection structures and African markets where the relevant credential is the statutory engineering regulator, not a buyer-inspector profession. US/CA/AU home-inspector licensing is **sub-national** (only 1–2 exemplars named per the contract, not over-generalised); NZ inspectors are **unregulated/unlicensed**; ZA has **no statutory inspector licence** and the decisive lever is the PPA 2019 mandatory disclosure form vs the voetstoots clause. Across the African markets (NG, KE, EG, GH, RW, MA, TN, MU, CV, SC, NA, BW) there is no buyer-inspector profession — condition assessment routes through a statutory engineering body (COREN, EBK, Engineers Syndicate, GhIE, SLA) or a civil-law professional order, with pre-purchase residential practice largely uncodified and pricing not publicly available (withheld with `verify via quote`, not estimated). The two **Southern-African Roman-Dutch common-law** additions (NA, BW) sit closest to ZA — *voetstoots*-style latent-defect allocation and an independent building inspector/engineer as the route — but neither surfaced a named statutory inspector regulator or public pricing, so both are flagged honestly.

## 7 universal mitigations

Apply across all jurisdictions, strongest in duty-to-inspect and unregulated-inspector markets:

1. **Commission an independent pre-offer condition survey** from a credentialed professional, and **verify the credential against the named public register** — not the agent's or seller's recommendation.
2. **Make the offer conditional on the survey** (inspection contingency / subject-to-survey clause) so an adverse finding is an exit, not a sunk cost.
3. **In civil-law Europe, hire an *expert bâtiment* / geometra / arquitecto técnico separately from the mandatory diagnostic pack** — the legally required disclosures do not assess structure.
4. **In duty-to-inspect / caveat-emptor jurisdictions (SE, NO, DE, AT, CH, NZ), treat the survey as the legal remedy, not optional** — skipping it forfeits the claim.
5. **For listed / pre-1920 stock, instruct the heritage-accredited variant** (RICS Conservation-Accredited / architecte du patrimoine / geometra-restauratore) — a general inspector will under-scope conservation risk and cost.
6. **Budget the cost band as a fixed line, not a discretionary one** — it is the cheapest insurance against the largest hidden cost; cross-ref `--tco`.
7. **Convert survey findings into a priced renovation scope** (`--renovation`) before finalising the offer price — a defect list without a cost attached does not protect the budget.
