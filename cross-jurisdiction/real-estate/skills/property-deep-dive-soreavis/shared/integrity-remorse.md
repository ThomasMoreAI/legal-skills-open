# Universal `--integrity` Section — Foreign-Buyer Buyer's-Remorse Pattern Extension

A pre-offer surfacing of the documented 6-12-month post-completion regret triggers a foreign buyer of the property's country systematically under-prices. Loaded when `--integrity` is invoked, alongside the base data-honesty checks.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: the base `--integrity` layer audits the *honesty of the brief's own data* (no fabricated figures, source-tier discipline, address-vs-commune trap). This doc adds an orthogonal **forward-looking** layer: the recurring drivers of foreign-buyer regret in this specific country, surfaced *before* the offer as a checklist — so a buyer prices the heating bill / strata levy / catastral revaluation / service-charge volatility *now*, not at month 9. It is a **pattern alert, not a verdict**: forum clusters are demand evidence, not statistics; a remorse percentage is stated only with a named survey/regulator source or not at all.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Top 1-2 documented 6-12-month foreign-buyer remorse triggers** for this country
2. **Basis** — a property ombudsman / consumer-protection authority / statistics office / regulator (named + year + URL), or `forum-cluster only (anecdotal — r/X)` if that is all that exists
3. **Pre-offer check** — the specific question the buyer should put *before* offering, that surfaces the trigger
4. **No fabricated incidence** — "X% regret Y" appears only with a named survey/regulator source+year; otherwise the driver is described qualitatively with the authority to verify at
5. **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract

## Verdict bands

| Band | Trigger | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Remorse drivers are knowable and fully priceable pre-offer from primary sources | "Run the pre-offer checks; if priced, the regret risk is controllable." |
| 🟡 | Strong recurring driver documented by a consumer body / ombudsman; commonly under-priced by foreign buyers | "Explicitly model this cost line before offering; it is the usual regret source here." |
| 🟠 | Multiple compounding drivers and/or the principal driver is structural (heating regime, catastral revaluation, service-charge volatility) and routinely discovered only post-completion | "High under-pricing risk; require written figures before offer, not estimates." |
| 🔴 | The dominant driver is a structural ownership/legal feature foreign buyers systematically misunderstand (leasehold/tenure, nominee, restricted-zone) | "Treat as a comprehension gap, not a cost line — confirm the buyer understands the structure before proceeding." |

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **Forum signal is demand evidence, not a statistic.** A loud r/HousingUK damp-regret cluster proves buyers *report* the pain; it does not license "X% of buyers regret it". Cite the cluster as anecdotal colour and anchor the verdict on a consumer body / ombudsman where one exists.
2. **The regret is usually a knowable cost line discovered late, not a hidden defect.** Spanish IBI/valor-catastral revaluation, French DPE-F/G heating cost, Italian IMU + utility-setup lag, Dubai post-handover service-charge volatility are all *priceable before offer* — the failure is timing, not concealment. The mitigation is a pre-offer question, not an inspection.
3. **Foreign buyers under-price the recurring more than the upfront.** Transaction taxes are visible at purchase; the regret cluster is dominated by *annual* costs (community/strata fees, property tax reassessment, heating, management) that don't appear on the listing.
4. **Catastral / rateable-value revaluation often triggers on transfer.** In several jurisdictions the taxable base resets to the sale price at purchase, so the seller's historical tax bill systematically understates the buyer's. Always ask for the *post-sale* projected figure, never the seller's current one.
5. **"Cheap to buy" and "cheap to hold" are different questions.** Rural-isolation heating, septic/SPANC, single-glazing retrofit, and listed-building maintenance turn a low purchase price into a high holding cost — the single most common cross-country regret theme.
6. **Strata/HOA special assessments are the Anglo-world analogue.** AU special-levy / defect-levy, US HOA special assessment, CA reserve-fund shortfall are the structural-fund version of the EU service-charge surprise — same regret, different label; always pull the fund balance + levy history pre-offer.
7. **Tenure misunderstanding is a comprehension gap, not a cost.** Leasehold ground rent/enfranchisement, foreign-quota condo, nominee structures, restricted-zone trusts generate the deepest regret because the buyer misunderstood *what they bought*, not what it costs — screen for understanding, not just price.

## Universal mitigations

Applicable across all 126 countries:

1. **Convert each documented trigger into a written pre-offer question** to the seller/agent/管理 body and require a figure, not a reassurance.
2. **Demand the post-sale projected property-tax / catastral figure**, never the seller's historical one.
3. **Pull the community/strata/HOA fund balance + 3-5-year special-assessment history** before offering (the recurring-cost regret is here).
4. **Model the annual holding cost (tax + fees + heating + management) as a line item next to the purchase price** — the regret cluster lives in the annual column.
5. **Confirm tenure comprehension explicitly** (freehold vs leasehold vs foreign-quota vs nominee vs restricted-zone trust) before anything else where the country's dominant driver is structural.
6. **Treat a forum-only driver as a prompt to verify at the named authority**, not as a quantified risk.
7. **Re-price, don't rationalise** — if a pre-offer check surfaces a material recurring cost, reflect it in the offer rather than treating it as acceptable noise.

## Regional patterns

### Western Europe + EFTA + micro-states + UK (R1)

- **ad** Andorra — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: discovering the property purchase does NOT itself confer residency (active/passive residency is a separate, capital- or presence-tested track) so a "moved to Andorra" plan stalls post-completion; high notary + registration + community-fee load on small market with thin resale liquidity — basis: forum-cluster only (anecdotal — r/Andorra), verify residency rules at Govern d'Andorra immigration (govern.ad). Pre-offer check: "Show me in writing exactly which residency permit this purchase qualifies me for, and the separate net-worth/deposit/physical-presence conditions I must independently satisfy."

- **at** Austria — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: post-completion discovery that a non-EU (or even non-resident EU) buyer needed Grundverkehr (land-transfer commission) approval the contract did not secure, voiding/freezing the purchase; recreational/Zweitwohnsitz (second-home) zoning bans that block the intended holiday-home use in Tyrol/Salzburg/Vorarlberg — basis: Austrian provincial Grundverkehrsbehörde rules + BMF (bmf.gv.at); cross-check with provincial Grundverkehrsgesetz. Pre-offer check: "Is this parcel in a Grundverkehr-restricted or Zweitwohnsitz-banned zone, and is my specific nationality/use cleared in writing before I sign?"

- **be** Belgium — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: registration duty (registratierechten/droits d'enregistrement, region-dependent, historically up to 12.5% in Wallonia/Brussels) far exceeding what the headline price suggested; mandatory post-purchase repair shock where the pre-2024 EPC/PEB rating signals heavy renovation obligation under regional energy-renovation mandates — basis: Belgian FPS Finance (financien.belgium.be) + regional energy agencies (Fluvius/Bruxelles Environnement); EPC obligation may have changed. Pre-offer check: "What is the total registration duty for my region and buyer profile, and what energy-renovation obligation does this EPC/PEB rating legally trigger and by when?"

- **ch** Switzerland — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: Lex Koller blocking or restricting the non-resident foreign purchase (cantonal quotas, holiday-home only, resale lock-in) discovered too late; recurring imputed rental value (Eigenmietwert) income tax + high transaction/notary costs + cantonal wealth tax on the property — basis: forum-cluster (anecdotal — r/Switzerland) corroborated by Swiss federal Lex Koller (fedlex.admin.ch) + ESTV/FTA on Eigenmietwert; Eigenmietwert reform under federal vote — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "Does Lex Koller permit my nationality/residency to buy THIS unit for THIS use, and what is my annual Eigenmietwert + cantonal wealth-tax cost on it?"

- **cy** Cyprus — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: title-deed transfer NOT delivered for years because the developer's underlying land mortgage stays attached to the paid-for unit; failing to deposit the sale contract at the Land Registry within the statutory window, leaving the buyer exposed to the developer's creditors — basis: Cyprus Department of Lands and Surveys (dls.moi.gov.cy) + secondary legal commentary cross-checked; trapped-buyer relief legislation evolving — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "Is there a developer mortgage on this land, will I receive a clean separate title at completion, and exactly when must my contract be deposited at the Land Registry?"

- **de** Germany — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: post-completion transaction-cost shock (Grunderwerbsteuer 3.5–6.5% by Land + notary + agent commission, total often 9–12%); for apartments, inheriting the Wohnungseigentümergemeinschaft (WEG) reserve shortfall and approved Sonderumlage special assessments not disclosed pre-sale — basis: German Land tax authorities + Notarkammer; WEG reserve disclosure governed by WEG-Gesetz. Pre-offer check: "Show me the WEG protocols, reserve-fund balance and any approved/pending Sonderumlage for the last 3 years, and my all-in Grunderwerbsteuer for this Land."

- **dk** Denmark — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: discovering the non-EU/non-resident buyer needed Justitsministeriet (Ministry of Justice) permission that the deal lacked, and that no prior 5-year residence generally bars holiday-home purchase; andelsbolig (cooperative) buyers learning they bought a usage right, not real property, with cooperative debt and price-cap rules — basis: Danish Ministry of Justice (justitsministeriet.dk) + secondary; cooperative valuation rules evolving. Pre-offer check: "Does my residency status legally permit this purchase without Ministry of Justice consent, and is this an ejerbolig (real ownership) or andelsbolig (cooperative usage right with shared debt)?"

- **es** Spain — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: post-completion IBI / valor catastral revaluation pushing annual tax well above the figure quoted; plusvalía municipal and non-resident imputed-income tax (IRNR) surprises the seller's notary never warned of (notary is not the buyer's adviser) — basis: secondary (PTI/spaineasy) cross-checked against Spanish municipal IBI framework + AEAT non-resident guidance; valor catastral revisions municipality-specific — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "What is the current IBI, is a valor catastral revision (revisión catastral) scheduled for this municipality, and what are my annual IRNR and one-off plusvalía obligations?"

- **fi** Finland — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: buying a housing-company share (asunto-osakeyhtiö) and only later discovering the company's loan share (yhtiölaina/taloyhtiölaina) and looming pipe-renovation (putkiremontti) special charge that can rival the purchase price; non-EU buyers missing the Defence Ministry acquisition-permit requirement — basis: Finnish Ministry of Defence permit rules + secondary on housing-company law (Asunto-osakeyhtiölaki); putkiremontti cost driver well-documented in Finnish housing practice. Pre-offer check: "What is the unit's share of the housing company's debt, and is a putkiremontti or façade/roof renovation decided or pending in the next 5 years?"

- **fr** France — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: rural isolation + winter heating-bill shock on a DPE-F/G "passoire thermique" with looming legal letting/sale renovation obligations; post-purchase taxe foncière (and second-home taxe d'habitation surcharge in tension zones) plus copropriété charges/fonds-travaux not modelled — basis: forum-cluster (anecdotal — r/vosfinances, r/france) corroborated by ADEME/service-public.fr on DPE obligations + DGFiP on taxe foncière; passoire-thermique letting bans phasing in — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "What is the DPE letter, which letting/sale renovation obligation does it trigger and by when, and what are the annual taxe foncière + copropriété charges + fonds-travaux on this lot?"

- **gr** Greece — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: post-completion discovery of unauthorised/semi-outdoor (ημιυπαίθριοι) building irregularities requiring costly legalisation that block resale/financing; annual ENFIA and the bureaucratic AFM/utility-transfer lag delaying liveability for months — basis: Greek AADE on ENFIA (aade.gr) + secondary on arbitrary-construction legalisation (N.4495/4178); legalisation regime amended periodically — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "Does the engineer's certificate (βεβαίωση μηχανικού) confirm zero unauthorised constructions, and what is the actual ENFIA for this property?"

- **ie** Ireland — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: assuming the purchase confers a right to live in Ireland — ownership and immigration permission are entirely separate, leaving non-EEA buyers with a home they may occupy only ~90 days at a time; underestimating Local Property Tax revaluation + management-company service charges on apartments/managed estates — basis: secondary (Investropa/Revenue.ie) cross-checked against Irish Revenue LPT + INIS immigration rules; LPT valuation bands revised periodically — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "Does owning this give me ANY right to reside, or is my stay capped by visa rules, and what is the LPT band plus annual management-company charge?"

- **is** Iceland — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: not registering the deed (þinglýsing) promptly, leaving ownership unenforceable against an undisclosed mortgage or a simultaneous double-sale; ISK currency volatility wiping out value for a buyer earning in another currency — basis: secondary (Knight Frank / jarniascyril) cross-checked against Iceland's registration framework + Central Bank of Iceland (sedlabanki.is) on ISK. Pre-offer check: "Will the deed be registered (þinglýst) immediately at completion with a clean title certificate, and have I stress-tested an adverse ISK move against my home currency?"

- **it** Italy — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: bureaucracy + utility-setup lag making the home unliveable for months, and the €1-house / rural-renovation contractor cost-overrun far exceeding budget; post-purchase IMU (second home) + TARI + condominio arrears inherited from the prior owner — basis: forum-cluster (anecdotal — r/ItalyExpat, r/italy) corroborated by Agenzia delle Entrate on IMU + secondary; municipal IMU rates vary — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "Is there outstanding IMU/TARI/condominio debt attached to this property, and have I obtained an independent renovation quote (computo metrico) — not the seller's estimate?"

- **li** Liechtenstein — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: discovering acquisition is effectively closed to non-residents without a residency permit and an actual-personal-use requirement, so an investment thesis collapses post-offer; extremely thin resale market and high transaction cost — basis: data not publicly available — verify at Liechtenstein Grundverkehr / Amt für Justiz (llv.li). Pre-offer check: "As a non-resident of my nationality, am I legally permitted to acquire and hold this for my intended use, in writing, before any deposit?"

- **lu** Luxembourg — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: transaction-cost and notary-fee shock plus among Europe's highest entry prices leaving little resale headroom; for apartments, copropriété reserve/charges and the annual property tax base (impôt foncier) reform raising future costs — basis: data not publicly available — verify at Administration des contributions directes / Administration du cadastre (impotsdirects.public.lu); property-tax reform pending — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "What are the full notary + registration costs, the copropriété charges and reserve, and how will the planned impôt foncier reform change my annual bill?"

- **mc** Monaco — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: agency commission + notary costs (often ~the highest absolute in Europe) plus syndic charges and mandatory works on aged towers eroding return; the purchase NOT conferring residency, which is a separate Carte de Séjour process with bank-deposit and accommodation proof — basis: data not publicly available — verify at Monaco Direction de la Sûreté Publique (residency) + Direction de l'Habitat (gouv.mc). Pre-offer check: "Confirm total acquisition costs and annual syndic + planned major works, and that this purchase alone does NOT grant residency."

- **mt** Malta — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: needing an AIP (Acquisition of Immovable Property) permit outside Special Designated Areas and a minimum-value/one-property restriction blocking the intended use or letting; perpetual/temporary ground-rent (ċens) and redemption costs the foreign buyer did not price — basis: data not publicly available — verify at Malta Capital Transfer Duty / Notarial Council + Lands Authority (cfr.gov.mt). Pre-offer check: "Is this inside a Special Designated Area or do I need an AIP permit with restrictions, and is the title freehold or subject to ground-rent (ċens) I must redeem?"

- **nl** Netherlands — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: overbidding far above asking in a tight market then post-completion VvE (Vereniging van Eigenaren) discovering an under-funded reserve / approved special levy and erfpacht (ground-lease, esp. Pre-offer check: "What is the VvE reserve and any approved levy (MJOP), and if erfpacht — what is the canon, when does it reset, and is it bought off?"

- **no** Norway — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: treating a borettslag (cooperative) flat like a condo and missing the fellesgjeld (shared debt) that can add hundreds of thousands of NOK to true cost, plus felleskostnader rising with rates; document/registration fee (dokumentavgift 2.5%) on freehold not budgeted — basis: secondary (Investropa) cross-checked against Norwegian Kartverket (registration) + cooperative-law framework. Pre-offer check: "Is this a selveier (freehold) or borettslag, and if borettslag what is the fellesgjeld per unit and the monthly felleskostnader trend?"

- **pt** Portugal — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: contractor cost-overrun on renovation + AIMA (ex-SEF) and utility-connection backlog delaying liveability/residency for many months; post-purchase IMI revaluation, AIMI (wealth surcharge on higher values) and condomínio arrears not disclosed — basis: forum-cluster (anecdotal — r/PortugalExpats) corroborated by Portuguese AT on IMI/AIMI (portaldasfinancas.gov.pt); Golden Visa property route ended — may have changed. Pre-offer check: "What is the current IMI / VPT and any AIMI exposure, is there condomínio debt, and have I a written independent renovation quote and realistic AIMA/utility timeline?"

- **se** Sweden — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: treating a bostadsrätt like a condo and not analysing the BRF cooperative's debt, deferred maintenance and planned avgift increases — fees can jump sharply; stamp duty (lagfart 1.5%) + mortgage-deed fee (2%) underestimated — basis: secondary (Investropa/jarniascyril) cross-checked against Swedish housing-cooperative law + Lantmäteriet on lagfart. Pre-offer check: "Show me the BRF's annual report — debt per sqm, maintenance plan, and decided/planned avgift increases — plus my lagfart and pantbrev costs."

- **sm** San Marino — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: foreign acquisition heavily restricted/conditioned (residency-linked, limited stock) so the purchase thesis collapses post-offer; very thin resale market with Italy-dependent practicalities — basis: data not publicly available — verify at Repubblica di San Marino Ufficio del Registro / Segreteria Affari Interni (gov.sm). Pre-offer check: "Am I, as a foreign non-resident, legally permitted to acquire and resell this property — confirmed in writing by a San Marino notary before any deposit?"

- **uk** United Kingdom — Top documented 6–12mo foreign-buyer remorse triggers: post-purchase damp/condensation + structural repair shock on older housing stock the survey under-scoped or was skipped on; leasehold service-charge / ground-rent escalation and short-lease extension cost, plus SDLT non-resident + additional-property surcharges — basis: forum-cluster (anecdotal — r/HousingUK) corroborated by The Property Ombudsman (tpos.co.uk) + HomeOwners Alliance survey on housing regret + HMRC SDLT; leasehold reform in progress — may have changed. ## Why lifestyle moves END — the structural drivers (not a statistic)

The patterns above are 6–12-month *cost* regret. This block extends the contract one notch — to the multi-year question of why a foreign-property move *ends* — but as a **mechanism list, never a rate**. The standing refusal first:

⚠️ **No authoritative cross-country dataset measures how many foreign buyers leave within N years.** Destination registers do not record exits by motive (OECD 2024 return-migration work; IOM Migration Data Portal documents the gap). The viral "X% leave [country] in 24 months" figures (e.g. "73% leave Spain") trace to a single non-peer-reviewed sample and **must not be cited as a statistic**. What *can* be stated are the documented **structural** drivers that end moves — each a pre-offer question, not a probability.

| Structural exit driver | The mechanism (not a rate) | Pre-offer self-check |
|---|---|---|
| **Residency doesn't renew** | the purchase did not confer residency, or the visa/permit renewal failed at year 2/5 (golden-visa rule changes, day-count shortfalls, income-floor drift) | "Is my right to stay renewable **independent** of this property — and what breaks the renewal?" → `--visa` + `--cross-border=timing` |
| **Healthcare / care need with age** | the dominant retirement-return driver in the migration literature (qualitative, IRM review) — public-system access, distance to specialist care, a care need the destination can't meet | "Have I priced healthcare **as I age in place**, not just today's premium?" → `--digital-nomad` healthcare carve-out + `--retirement` |
| **Exit tax + FX / recapture shock** | selling trips a CGT-exclusion-clock miss, a currency-conversion gain (§988-type), depreciation recapture, or an exit-tax event not modelled at purchase | "What does it cost to **unwind** this — CGT clock, FX, recapture — if I leave in 5 years?" → `shared/journey-sellrent.md` + `--exit` |
| **Thin-resale liquidity lock-in** | a rural / single-buyer-pool / restricted-title asset that can't be sold on the mover's timeline, forcing a fire-sale or a forced stay | "If I had to sell in 6 months, **who buys it** — at what discount?" → `--price` comparables + `--exit` |

**Honest tail**: community fit, loneliness and "is this the right place" are **visit-and-trial-rental** questions, not dataset questions — self-selected expat surveys (InterNations-style) are anecdotal and must not drive a verdict (the sourceable policy-level signal is the integration-environment data under `--demographics`). This block adds zero new fabrication surface: it converts an un-measurable retention rate into four sourced, mechanism-level pre-offer questions.

## Corrections folded (research-surfaced)

- **Turkey citizenship-by-property threshold**: widely repeated figures of USD 250,000 are stale — the minimum was raised to **USD 400,000** in June 2022 (verify current figure at tkgm.gov.tr / Presidency of Strategy and Budget, as it may have changed again). Listings still marketed at the old threshold are a documented remorse driver (buyer fails to qualify).
- **Portugal Golden Visa via residential property**: all real-estate routes (residential and commercial) were **removed** (Mais Habitação, Lei 56/2023, in force 7 Oct 2023). Briefs/agents still pitching "buy a flat in Lisbon/Porto for the Golden Visa" are selling an ended programme — a primary post-offer regret trigger. Verify current routes at AIMA.
- **Cyprus / Bulgaria / Greece "8–10% extra costs"** figure is a secondary-source rule of thumb, NOT a regulator statistic — it is used here only directionally; actual transfer fees/taxes must be computed from the named primary registry/tax authority per transaction.
- **"60%+ of new builds" / "30–40% of properties" Cyprus encumbrance figures** come from a single secondary aggregator (Investropa) and are NOT corroborated by the Cyprus Department of Lands and Surveys; treated here as qualitative ("title-deed delay and developer-mortgage attachment are common"), NOT as a statistic.
- **Switzerland Eigenmietwert (imputed rental value)**: a federal reform to abolish it has been progressing through Parliament/popular vote — do not state it as permanently fixed; verify current status at ESTV/FTA before relying on the annual-cost claim.
- **Saudi Arabia foreign ownership**: pre-2026 blanket statements that "foreigners cannot own residential property in Saudi Arabia" are now stale — the new law is effective January 2026 but is zone-restricted with implementing regulations still maturing; neither the old blanket-ban nor a "fully open market" framing is accurate.
- **Hong Kong stamp duty (widely-cited stale claim)**: pre-2024 guides still quote a 15% Buyer's Stamp Duty + 15% New Residential Stamp Duty + Special Stamp Duty on non-permanent-resident / foreign buyers. The Stamp Duty (Amendment) Ordinance 2024 (effective 28 Feb 2024) cut BSD/SSD/NRSD to 0% — foreign buyers now pay only Scale-2 ad valorem duty (info.gov.hk, 2024). Treat any source claiming "30% stamp duty for foreigners in HK" as superseded.
- **New Zealand "you can buy if you get a visa" (widely-cited stale/over-optimistic claim)**: the 2018 Overseas Investment Amendment foreign-buyer ban still applies to almost all non-residents. The Dec 2025 amendment (Royal Assent 19 Dec 2025) is a NARROW carve-out — only Active Investor Plus / Investor visa holders, only homes valued over NZ$5m, from early 2026. Generic "foreigners can now buy NZ property" framing is wrong.
- **Mexico fideicomiso "you own it outright" (widely-cited soft claim)**: in the restricted zone (50 km coast / 100 km border) a foreigner does NOT hold direct title — the bank trust (fideicomiso) holds legal title with the buyer as beneficiary, carries a recurring US$500–800/yr fee (2025, Brevitas/MexLaw) and must be renewed/transferred. Marketing that calls it "the same as freehold" understates the annual cost and the trust-renewal mechanics.
- **Dominican Republic vs Dominica conflation (recurring error)**: the Dominican Republic (DO) has NO citizenship-by-investment or digital-nomad visa program; those belong to the Commonwealth of Dominica (DM), which is out of scope. Do not attribute Dominica's CBI/DN schemes to DO property buyers.
- **Thailand "leasehold renews automatically for 90 years" (widely-cited stale claim)**: the Supreme Court ruling of 18 Mar 2025 confirmed only the first 30-year lease term is statutorily guaranteed; renewal options for years 31–90 are contractual and not enforceable against a non-cooperating/changed landowner (fosrlaw.com analysis, 2025). The common "3×30 = secure 90 years" sales line is superseded.
- **Mauritius "foreigners can buy any property over USD 375k" (widely-cited stale claim)**: since the 2025–26 budget, non-residents may buy ONLY within approved schemes (PDS/IRS/RES/Smart City/G+2); open-market purchase outside schemes is no longer permitted, and the Finance Act 2025 rise of non-citizen duty/transfer tax to 10% from 1 Jul 2026 was repealed by Finance Act 2026 (gazetted 13 Aug 2026). Pre-2025 "buy any apartment over USD 375k" guidance is superseded.
