# Universal `--schools` Section — Schools / Education Access

Foreign-buyer **schools / education-access** layer for working-age families with school-age dependents. Covers international schools (IB / British / American / French Lycée AEFE / German Schule networks + Nord Anglia / Cognita / GEMS / ISP / Globeducate / Inspired / QSI / UWC), local public school landscape (language of instruction + tuition + foreigner-enrollment + assessments), local private schools (religious vs secular, bilingual, cost bands), residency priority / catchment-area rules (FR carte scolaire / UK Local Authority catchment / JP school district gakku / CN xuequ / NL postcode-loting / DE Schulbezirk / US public school district by property tax), and the 2024-2026 reform calendar that re-shapes school access. Distinct from `--retirement` (retirement-age), `--digital-nomad` (working-age healthcare), `--demographics` (population / aging / family-friendly metrics — separate quantitative layer).

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *education-access* layer — what international + local school options exist for the property's location, what fee bands + waitlist horizons apply, what residency-priority rules gate enrollment, what specific high-quality schools serve the major city, and what 2024-2026 reforms shape the landscape. The *demographics* layer (population, aging, family-friendly metrics) lives in `shared/demographics.md`. A foreign-buyer family typically needs three pieces of intel: (1) which international school options are geographically near the property, (2) what fees + waitlist + residency-priority rules apply, (3) what local public option exists as a fallback or complement.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **International school landscape** in capital/major city — name 2-5 top international schools (with network: Nord Anglia / Cognita / GEMS / ISP / Globeducate / Inspired / QSI / UWC; or independent); curriculum (IB / British IGCSE+A-Level / American Diploma + AP / French Lycée AEFE / German Schule / Bilingual / Cambridge); annual fee band in local currency + USD/EUR equivalent; KHDA/ADEK rating where applicable.
2. **Annual fee bands** (local currency + USD/EUR equivalent) — Day-school IB Diploma year. Working-age family budget. Examples: SG SAS USD 26-43k; HK ESF USD 17-26k vs HKIS USD 26-45k; JP ASIJ USD 17-23k; SG/HK/Tokyo top tier; AE Dubai GEMS USD 16-30k; CH Ecolint CHF 30-45k; UK ISL GBP 25-40k; NYC Trinity USD 50-65k (highest globally); IN Mumbai/Delhi/Bangalore USD 9-18k (Asia tier-1 lowest); BR São Paulo Graded USD 17-25k; MX City ASF USD 14-22k; CV/SC USD 3-15k (Africa lowest tier); BB Lyford Cay IS USD 25-40k (Caribbean highest, gated community).
3. **Waitlist horizon** for top-tier — months / years. Year-of-entry constraints (Year 6 / Year 9 critical). Examples: SG/HK 12-36 months; AU Sydney private 5-10 years (waitlist from birth); US NYC Trinity 2-3 years; CH Geneva Ecolint 18-24 months; FR Paris Lycée International Saint-Germain 5+ years; NL Amsterdam capacity crisis 2+ years; PT Lisbon CAIS 12-18 months; ZA Johannesburg St John's 3-7 years.
4. **Local public school landscape** — language of instruction + tuition (typically free for residents) + foreigner-enrollment process + quality variance. Examples: FR Carte scolaire (catchment); DE Schulpflicht with track-decision at age 10 (Gymnasium / Realschule / Hauptschule); UK Local Authority catchment + selective grammar; NL postcode-loting; CH cantonal Stundenplan; ES bilingual variance (Madrid + Cataluña + Castilla-La Mancha); IT liceo tracks at age 14; PT agrupamentos catchment-based; JP gakku district + jusho; KR jeonse + work-permit + Korean-language; CN hukou-style residence + property ownership for prestigious xuequ school district; HK ESF + Direct Subsidy Scheme; SG Primary One Registration + parents-alumni priority + sibling priority; MY SJK(C) Chinese-medium / SJK(T) Tamil-medium options; IN Hindi/English/regional-medium + state variance under NEP 2020; ZA former Model C catchment; KE/NG/GH state schools by district + variable quality.
5. **Catchment area / residency priority rules** — does property address gate school access? Critical for property-value premium. Examples: **CN xuequ school district** in Beijing/Shanghai/Shenzhen drives 30-300% property-price premium for Haidian/Xicheng/Pudong-Chuansha catchment; **JP gakku** by jusho; **UK Local Authority catchment** with "school district premium" effect; **AU NSW state school zones + Selective High Schools** (Sydney 19 selective by exam); **US public school district** correlated with property tax (Westchester/Marin/Palo Alto/Fairfield County premium 30-100%); **NZ Auckland Grammar zone** Epsom + Mt Eden premium; **FR carte scolaire** gates collège; **NL postcode-loting** for oversubscribed Amsterdam.
6. **2024-2026 reforms** — see Reform calendar below.
7. **Enrollment sequence** (relocating family) — public-system intake window + the **mid-year-entry** route (a right under compulsory-education law, not the annual cycle), **foreign-credential placement** (by age ±1–2 yrs + assessment almost everywhere; no school-grade equivalence body except PT's *equivalência*), and the **newcomer host-language entitlement** — named + statutory in continental EU (FR UPE2A / DE Willkommensklasse / NL ISK / ES LOE Art. 79 / PT PLNM) vs school-led + discretionary in UK / IE / AU. See § Enrollment sequence for relocating families.
8. **Confidence**: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **UK 1 Jan 2025 VAT shock applies to UK + ripples globally.** Labour government's Finance Act 2024 imposed **20% VAT on private school fees from 1 Jan 2025**, plus removed business-rates relief from same date. Effective 15-20% fee increase for ~600,000 UK private school pupils + ripple effect on global British school networks (some IB+British schools abroad have raised fees citing UK-parity). Foreign-buyer families with UK private school plans face material budget shift.
2. **Catchment-area + property-value premium is the single largest hidden cost.** Beijing/Shanghai xuequ school district can add 30-300% to property price; US Westchester/Marin/Palo Alto/Fairfield 30-100%; NZ Auckland Grammar zone (Epsom + Mt Eden) 20-40%; UK London catchment-area premium for Kingston upon Thames / Chiswick 15-30%; FR Paris carte scolaire — wrong arrondissement = no popular collège access. Foreign-buyer families MUST verify school-district / catchment status BEFORE the offer.
3. **Waitlist horizons range from weeks to a decade.** AU Sydney top private (SCEGGS Darlinghurst, Cranbrook) waitlist from birth for popular years (5-10 years). ZA Johannesburg St John's College legacy + sibling priority dominant. SG SAS / UWC / Tanglin 12-24 months; HK ESF/HKIS 12-36 months; CH Geneva Ecolint 18-24 months; FR Paris Lycée International Saint-Germain 5+ years for some sections; NL Amsterdam international school capacity crisis since 2023. Smaller markets (DK / SE / NO / FI / BB / JM) typically 3-12 months. Foreign-buyer families should apply 12-24 months pre-arrival for popular destinations.
4. **Public-system access varies — language of instruction is the binding constraint for foreigner kids.** EU public schools are typically free for residents incl. foreigners, but instruction-language matters: ES Madrid bilingual programmes vs Cataluña Catalan-immersion; CH Geneva French-medium vs Zürich German-medium; BE Flemish vs French Communities; NL Dutch-medium with extra-language classes for non-Dutch kids. Asia public-system access is often residency-coupled: JP/KR/CN/TW require local jusho/jeonse/hukou + tax-residency. US public-system free with property-tax-paid residency. Africa: KE/NG/GH free state schools but quality variance + English/Swahili/Yoruba/Hausa/Igbo medium.
5. **Top-tier networks dominate but are priced premium.** Nord Anglia (75+ schools globally) / Cognita (95+ schools) / GEMS (60+ schools, AE-dominant) / Inspired Education (110+ schools) / Globeducate (60+ schools) / ISP (85+ schools) / Dulwich College International (10 schools) / Harrow International (multiple) / King's College Madrid + Latam — all premium pricing. Independent flagship schools (UWC, Atlantic College, École nouvelle de la Suisse romande, Le Rosey, Aiglon) have boarding tradition + waitlist. QSI franchise dominates emerging markets (Tashkent, Yerevan, Tbilisi, Sarajevo, Phnom Penh, Tirana) with USD 12-22k bands. Foreign-buyer families need to triangulate network vs independent vs QSI based on city size.
6. **Curriculum choice (IB vs British vs American vs Bilingual) shapes university pathway.** IB Diploma (45 max points) accepted globally — international university entry; British IGCSE + A-Level for UK Russell Group + AU/NZ entry; American AP + SAT for US Ivy League + state university; French Bac for French universities + Grandes Écoles. Foreign-buyer families with multi-country mobility (e.g., DN-visa families) should choose IB for portability. Single-destination families (US-bound, UK-bound) may benefit from country-specific curriculum.
7. **Post-disaster + crisis disruptions reshape school access overnight.** IL post-7-Oct-2023 + Galilee/Negev school evacuations + supplementary OECD/UN funding; LB ongoing crisis — IC + ACS Beirut maintained operations but enrollment dropped 30-50%, parents pay in fresh USD; UA post-Feb-2022 invasion = humanitarian access only; TR post-6-Feb-2023 earthquake = regional school overload + reconstruction; CN post-2024 expat repatriation eased waitlists; BNO HK emigration aftermath similar; NG Lagos AISL Victoria Island/Ikoyi tied to oil-expat demand. Foreign-buyer families should monitor stability of destination + verify school continuity before signing offer.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

Across this 23-country bloc, foreign-buyer families face a sharp three-way split: (a) **state public school is free** to legal residents in every country, but instruction language gates access — French in FR, German in DE/AT/parts of CH, Dutch in NL, the regional language in CH cantons (DE/FR/IT/RM) and Spanish CCAA (Catalan, Basque, Galician, Valenciano); (b) a **state-funded "international section" track** exists in only a few markets — FR (sections internationales: AEFE network + the public Lycée International St-Germain-en-Laye), DE (JFK Schule Berlin, free German public school with US+DE bilingual track), LU/BE (the European Schools, free for Cat I = EU institution staff, fee-paying for Cat III = third-party families), and the Dutch DIPS hybrid (publicly subsidised, ~EUR 5,000-8,500/yr parental contribution, eligibility = 2 of past 4 years abroad); and (c) **fully private international schools** (IB, British IGCSE+A-Level, American AP, French Bac, German Reifeprüfung, plus bilingual hybrids) at EUR 12,000-55,000/yr.

The headline 2024-2026 reform shock is the **UK 1 January 2025 introduction of 20% VAT on private school fees** (Finance Act 2025, sections 47-49, effective for any term starting on/after 1 Jan 2025; anti-forestalling captures pre-payments back to 29 July 2024). This applies to **all fee-paying schools serving UK compulsory-school-age pupils up to 19**, including international schools operating in the UK (ISL, ACS Cobham/Hillingdon/Egham, Tasis England). HMG forecast 37,000 pupils leaving private sector and £1.51bn revenue in 2025/26. ISL London publicly states "All fees and costs will be liable for 20% VAT from January 2025."

The **post-2023 international-school capacity squeeze** affects Amsterdam, Lisbon, and Madrid specifically. ISA Amsterdam Year-1/Year-7/Year-12 popular grades require applications between Oct and Feb of the prior year. Lisbon's CAISL and St Julian's report waitlists at popular grade entries after the 2022-2024 NHR-driven family influx. Geneva's Ecolint advises applying 12-18 months before expected arrival for La Grande Boissière + Collège du Léman.

**Catchment-area / residency-priority** rules silently gate state-school access in three large jurisdictions: FR carte scolaire (proof-of-domicile required for collège enrollment in your sector), UK Local Authority distance-based Year-7 allocation (and the underlying "school district premium" capitalised into house prices, especially around Buckinghamshire/Kent grammar-school zones), DE Schulbezirk for Grundschule, and NL postcode-based loting (lottery) for oversubscribed Amsterdam/Utrecht primary schools. CH adds a cantonal layer: Gymnasium acceptance and curriculum gate at age 12-15 by canton.

Outside FR/DE/UK/CH/NL/BE, the structural fact is **a single dominant IB or British school per capital** (Vienna VIS, Copenhagen CIS, Stockholm SIS, Oslo OIS, Helsinki ISH, Reykjavík ISI, Valletta Verdala, Monaco ISM, Andorra BCA), so capacity is genuinely narrow even where no formal "crisis" is declared. Microstates with no domestic international school — SM (San Marino) — require commute to nearby Italian schools or boarding abroad.

## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

The 21-country band splits cleanly into three fee tiers and two language-policy regimes. **Tier-1 capitals** (Warsaw, Prague, Budapest, Bucharest, Sofia, Zagreb, Tallinn, Riga, Vilnius, Belgrade) host either an "American/Anglo-American" anchor school or a Nord Anglia / IB World School in the EUR 17-32k/yr band; **Tier-2 capitals** (Ljubljana, Bratislava, Limassol, Nicosia) cluster at EUR 8-22k with a mature Greek-Cypriot private market in CY pulling fees down; **Tier-3 capitals** (Tirana, Yerevan, Sarajevo, Chișinău, Podgorica, Skopje) are dominated by the **QSI franchise** using a single curriculum template and US State Department fact-sheet pricing transparency. Baku is the regional outlier: **TISA Baku** at USD 26,600/yr tuition + USD 6,000 first-year development fee reflects oil-money premium pricing.

The **Baltic Russian-language transition** is the dominant 2024-2025 reform story in EE/LV. Latvia **completed the transition to Latvian-only instruction on 1 September 2025** (grades 3, 6, 9 — final phase) after the Constitutional Court upheld the 2022 law in two July 2024 judgments. Estonia **started in 2024 with kindergarten + grades 1+4**, scheduled to complete by 2029-30 with EUR 41M allocated in the 2023 budget and EUR ~300M projected over four years. Lithuania has not run a parallel transition — Russian-medium schools are a much smaller share — but did launch new digital Matura certificates and split mathematics into general/extended exam tracks from 2024-25.

The **post-2022 Ukrainian refugee influx** sustained capacity pressure in PL/RO. Romania reports **97.44% of Ukrainian minors under temporary protection (48,767 children) enrolled in Romanian schools by mid-2024**, with Emergency Ordinance 69 allowing 9th-grade enrollment to exceed maximum places. Poland accommodates the largest Ukrainian school-age population in the EU; Reform26 ("Compass of Tomorrow") phases in from 2025-26 through 2031-32, with new core curriculum hitting preschool + Y1 + Y4 from 1 September 2026.

**EU mobility floor** — the **Erasmus+ 2021-2027 envelope is EUR 26+ billion** with EUR 4.6bn for 2024 alone — gives EU-citizen children of digital-nomad families free access to PL/CZ/HU/RO/HR/BG/EE/LV/LT/SI/SK/CY public schools regardless of buyer-parent residency status. The 17 EU + 4 non-EU countries in this band cluster around 8-9-year primary cycles followed by 3-4 year general secondary (gimnazija/gimnazjum/gimnazija/gymnasium pathway) feeding the matura/maturita/bacalaureat exam.

**QSI franchise structural caveat**: 8 of the 21 capitals run QSI as the dominant or sole credible international option. QSI publishes via US State Department fact sheets that historically lag by 1-2 academic years; **2025-26 QSI fees in Tirana / Yerevan / Sarajevo / Chișinău / Podgorica / Skopje are not publicly published** as of May 2026 — the K-12 baseline of USD 17,000-26,000 + USD 1,600-8,000 capital fund is from 2023-24 fact sheets and should be treated as *est. for 2025-26* until each campus releases its 2025-26 packet. AISB Budapest, AAS Sofia, AISZ Zagreb, ISL Riga, TISA Baku, and AISB Bucharest publish fees directly.

## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

The 13-country MENA / GCC / Israel / Türkiye foreign-buyer schools landscape splits cleanly along **four regulatory regimes**: (1) **GCC inspectorate-driven private market** (UAE/SA/QA/KW/BH/OM) where Anglophone-curriculum schools dominate expat education, and inspection ratings (KHDA Dubai, ADEK Abu Dhabi via Irtiqa'a, SPEA Sharjah via Itqan, MOEHE Qatar, MOE Saudi) are publicly published on a 6-band scale (Outstanding → Very Weak) and materially affect property values within school catchments; (2) **Levant fragmented confessional / crisis-disrupted** (LB, JO, IL) where Lebanon's 18-confession schooling, Jordan's Tawjihi reform and Israel's post-7-Oct-2023 northern/southern displacement create unique foreign-family selection problems; (3) **Maghreb French-bilingual + AEFE Lycée network** (MA, TN) where Lycée Français schools (subsidised by AEFE Paris) coexist with American/British alternatives at fees roughly half GCC levels; (4) **Türkiye / Egypt mixed-language, currency-volatile** where TRY (Türkiye) and EGP (post-3rd-flotation, 6 March 2024, ~50 EGP/USD) volatility makes fee-band comparisons across years unreliable.

## APAC + Central Asia

### Regional pattern

APAC hosts the world's densest concentration of international schools, anchored by three Tier-1 hubs (SG, HK, Tokyo) each with 40-50+ schools and a long tail across emerging-market capitals. Foreign-buyer families lean heavily on international schools because local-language barriers are higher than in EU markets — Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, and Khmer are all hard pivots for school-age children, so the "send to local public school" fallback that works in Lisbon or Berlin rarely works in Asia. Premium fees scale roughly as USD 9-18k (IN/UZ/KH/LK/MO) → USD 22-35k (TH/VN/MY/PH/KZ) → USD 35-58k (CN-tier1 / HK / SG / KR / JP-elite).

China is the structural outlier: the *xuequ* (学区, school district) regime ties high-status state primary admission to *hukou* registration plus property ownership in a designated catchment, which has historically created 5-19% premiums in Shanghai and ~11% (top-59 schools) in Beijing per peer-reviewed evidence — premiums that the 2021 teacher-rotation pilot has begun to compress (Shanghai dual-school-district premiums down ~3.5% post-rotation per Sci-Direct study). The 2021 "double reduction" (双减/*shuangjian*) ban on for-profit K-9 academic tutoring remains in force and reshaped the entire shadow-education economy; MOE released draft national tutoring regulations Feb 2024.

Hong Kong's post-2020 BNO emigration wave (~150k departures 2021-2024) eased ESF and premium-school waitlists at the same time as ESF pushed through a 4.8% fee hike for 2025/26 (Y10 jumped HKD 152,200 → 181,100). Note ESF's quota rule: at least 70% of seats must go to "non-local" passport holders (HKSAR + BNO are *excluded* from "non-local"), so BNO families compete in the smaller local pool.

Japan's MEXT free-high-school reform takes the income cap off public *and* private subsidies from April 2026 (~JPY 457,000/student), broadly matching average private fees but not even denting the JPY 3.0-3.5M international-school tier. The *gakku* (residence-based public school assignment) remains the operative constraint for any family using state schools. Korea's 2022-2024 *jeonse* fraud crisis (~38,500 recognized victims, foreign tenants explicitly excluded from public-housing relief) is a school-district-adjacent risk: foreign families using *jeonse* to qualify for a school catchment can lose deposit AND placement.

Emerging markets — HCMC, Hanoi, Phnom Penh, Tashkent, Almaty — saw rapid international-school capacity additions 2020-2025 (Vinschool ADB USD 150M facility expansion 2025; BST Tashkent new campus opens Aug 2026), pushing waitlists down to 3-12 months in most. India sits in its own band: NEP 2020's 5+3+3+4 structure is rolling out state-by-state (Maharashtra and Karnataka leading 2025), and elite international fees (DAIS Mumbai, AES Delhi) remain the cheapest tier-1 expat option in Asia at INR 8-16 lakh / USD 9-18k.

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

**Curriculum split.** Continental Latin America runs a clean two-tier system: state schools deliver national curricula in Spanish (Portuguese in BR, Spanish + Guarani in PY) free of charge to residents — including foreigners on Temporary/Permanent Resident permits — while a parallel private market of US-, British-, French-, German-, and IB-curriculum schools serves the diplomatic / corporate-expat / wealthy-local segment. The American Schools network (US State Department Office of Overseas Schools, OOS) anchors the top tier in nearly every capital with published, USD-denominated fact sheets — the most reliable cross-country dataset available. IB DP penetration is highest in CL (Nido), CO (CNG), MX (ASF, Greengates), CR (CDS), BB (Codrington), BS (Lyford Cay).

**Caribbean variant.** JM, BS, BB, BZ run English-medium state schools and converge on the **Caribbean Examinations Council (CXC) CSEC + CAPE** secondary credential — interoperable with UK/US university admissions. Top international schools (Lyford Cay BS, Codrington BB, AISK JM) all run the IB DP route in parallel, with steep USD-denominated fees and tighter waitlists driven by smaller absolute pools.

**Property-school clustering.** Top international schools cluster geographically and the property-price premium in those districts is real and quantifiable: Polanco/Lomas/Santa Fe (MX City), Jardim America/Morumbi (SP), Recoleta/Belgrano (BA), Las Condes/Vitacura (Santiago), Chapinero/Usaquén (Bogotá), Miraflores/San Isidro (Lima), Carrasco (Montevideo), Costa del Este/Punta Pacifica (Panama City), Escazú/Curridabat (San José), Piantini/Naco (Santo Domingo), Cumbayá/Tumbaco (Quito valley). Foreign buyers planning K-12 schooling should treat school-district choice as a *prerequisite* to neighbourhood selection, not the reverse — top-tier waitlists in CL/PE/BR run 12-24 months and dictate move-in date.

**Reform turbulence 2024-2026.** Five jurisdictions are mid-reform: AR (Milei "Libertad Educativa" + 40% real budget cut 2023→2024), BR (Lei 14.945/2024 partial-repeal of 2017 Novo Ensino Médio), CL (SAE budget-suspension fight Nov 2024), CO (Ley Estatutaria 2024 making education a fundamental right), PA (Rediseño Curricular 2025-2029 first new framework since 1946). Reform churn typically affects state schools first; private/international schools shielded for 12-24 months but eventually align on accreditation and exit credentials.

**Fee bands USD 8-40k.** Bottom: Belize (~USD 8-15k), PY ASA, UY British Schools (USD 12-18k). Middle: most LATAM capitals (USD 14-22k). Top: Caribbean — Lyford Cay BS (~USD 25-35k for upper grades), CR Country Day (USD 14.5-20k base + capital fees), CL Nido (USD ~21k base + 350 UF / ~USD 14.7k incorporation). Capital-donation / one-time entrance fees of USD 6k-16k per student are the hidden line-item — they are universally amortised by long-term expat families but punishing on 1-3-year postings.

## Africa + Anglo-non-EU

### Regional pattern

The Anglo-non-EU bloc (AU/CA/NZ/US) hosts the world's largest, deepest private + boarding-school markets outside the UK, with annual day-school fees clustering USD 25,000-70,000 for Tier-1 institutions and boarding pushing USD 65,000-90,000 — top-band Phillips Exeter at USD 69,537 boarding for 2024-25 and NYC's Trinity at USD 69,000 day for 2025-26 sit at the global ceiling. The defining structural fact for foreign buyers is that **public-school catchment maps directly onto property values**: Auckland's Epsom Girls' Grammar in-zone homes carry an 89.7% premium (NZD 2.45M vs NZD 1.29M out-of-zone); US districts like Palo Alto Unified spend USD 20,500+/student funded primarily by local property taxes.

The **UK 20% VAT on private-school fees took effect 1 January 2025** under sections 47-49 of the Finance Act 2025 (Labour government), with anti-forestalling provisions backdated to 29 July 2024. Government estimated 37,000 pupils (~6%) would exit the private sector — secondary effect: increased pressure on top international schools in AU/NZ/CA/AE/HK that recruit displaced UK families, and on Crown Dependencies (out of scope here). Global British school networks (Brookhouse, Lincoln Community, British International School Lagos) are not subject to UK VAT but face UK-led parent recalculation of relative value.

The **US K-12 layer is uniquely fragmented by state/district**, with private schools at ~10% market share. The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed 4 July 2025**, raised 529-plan K-12 withdrawal limits from USD 10,000 to USD 20,000/yr effective 1 January 2026 and expanded qualified expenses to include tutoring, books, therapy — directly material for foreign-buyer families using 529s. Pell Grant restrictions effective 1 July 2026 affect higher-ed not K-12.

**Africa splits 3 ways**: ZA has a deep private + boarding tradition (St John's R220,828/yr day 2025; Bishops R97,200-128,767/yr day); MU/SC/CV/RW have very limited international-school capacity, often forcing families to send children to PT/FR/MU; KE/NG/GH cluster international schools in capitals at oil-/expat-premium (AISL Lagos charges USD 17,638-32,165/yr — Africa's highest). The **South African BELA Act (Act 32 of 2024)** commenced 24 December 2024 and shifts admission/language-policy authority from School Governing Bodies to the provincial Head of Department — first regulations gazetted June 2025. Load-shedding suspended 1 Apr 2024-27 Feb 2025 (325 days), with brief return Stage 3 on 7-10 Mar 2025 — verify school-day backup capacity at visit. Fee bands span USD ~3,000 (Cabo Verde state-adjacent) to USD ~80,000 (US top boarding).

## Enrollment sequence for relocating families (public-system intake)

Distinct from the international-school waitlists above: this is the *public-system* path a relocating family with a school-age child actually walks. Two facts hold across the corpus. (1) **Mid-year admission is a right, not a favour** — compulsory-education law obliges the authority to place a resident child at any point in the year; you do NOT wait for the September/January cycle. (2) **The variable that bites is newcomer-language support** — every continental EU system here has a *named, statutory* host-language programme, while the Anglo systems (UK / IE / AU) treat it as school-led and discretionary. Grade placement is **by age** almost everywhere (±1–2 years after an assessment), NOT by foreign-credential equivalence — there is no school-grade equivalence body in most of the corpus (ENIC-NARIC / Nuffic / IDW cover higher-ed + labour-market recognition only; **PT** is the exception, with a conversion-table *equivalência* regime).

⚠️ **Trap**: families over-assume a guaranteed immersion class. The *right to a school place + an assessment* is statutory; a dedicated newcomer-language class is **space-limited** even where it is named (FR UPE2A "si l'établissement en dispose"; IE / UK / AU sanction it provisionally subject to numbers). Verify the specific school's provision before the offer — not the national policy — and budget private tutoring as a bridge.

**🟢 Statutory newcomer-language framework (named entitlement):**

- **FR (France)** — compulsory **3–16**; enrol via the *mairie* (école) / DSDEN-CASNAV (collège+); **mid-year = a right** of admission "sans délai"; placement **by age ±1–2 yrs** after a CASNAV assessment (no school-grade equivalence). Support: **UPE2A** — the assessment is statutory, the dedicated class space-limited; rural fallback = ordinary class + FLS from all teachers ([circ. n°2012-141](https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/imported_files/documents/BO_MEN_11-10-12_228832.pdf)).
- **DE (Germany)** — compulsory **~6–15/16** (full-time, varies by Land) → part-time *Berufsschulpflicht* to ~18; enrol at the catchment *Grundschule* via the *Schulamt*; **mid-year = yes** (Schulpflicht attaches on taking up residence); placement age/assessment-based. Support: **Willkommens- / Vorbereitungsklasse** (named per Land — Berlin *Willkommensklasse*, BW *VKL*), a statutory intensive-German prep class ([Berlin SchulG §42](https://www.schulgesetz-berlin.de/berlin/schulgesetz/teil-iv-schulpflicht/sect-42-beginn-und-dauer-der-allgemeinen-schulpflicht.php)).
- **ES (Spain)** — compulsory **6–16** (LOE Art. 4); admission run by the *Comunidad Autónoma* (devolved); **mid-year = "fuera de plazo"** routed through the area *Comisión / Servicio de Escolarización*, not the school; placement by age + initial assessment (LOE Art. 78). Support: **LOE Art. 79** statutory duty → regional programmes (Madrid *aulas de enlace*, Cataluña *aules d'acollida*) ([BOE — LOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-7899)).
- **PT (Portugal)** — compulsory **6–18**; enrol via the *Portal das Matrículas* (~Apr–Jul); **mid-year = yes** (*matrícula fora de prazo*, in person at the *área-de-residência* agrupamento); **prior schooling converted** via the *equivalência de habilitações estrangeiras* regime (DL 227/2005 — the corpus's main non-age-based placement). Support: **PLNM** (*Português Língua Não Materna*) — statutory entitlement, A1–B1 levels ([DGEstE](https://www.dgeste.mec.pt/sinf/faqsmatriculas/)).
- **NL (Netherlands)** — compulsory **5–16** (*leerplicht*); apply **directly to the school** (decentralised, no council portal); **mid-year = yes** ("het hele jaar aanmelden" when relocating); no school-level credential body (Nuffic / IDW = higher-ed / labour only). Support: **ISK** (*internationale schakelklas*, secondary) / *nieuwkomersschool* (primary) — "drempelloos" NT2 immersion ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/basisonderwijs/onderwijs-voor-nieuwkomers)).

**🟡 Discretionary / space-limited support (school-led, no guaranteed national programme):**

- **IE (Ireland)** — compulsory **6–16** (or 3 yrs post-primary); apply **per-school** under each *admissions policy* (Education (Admission to Schools) Act 2018); **mid-year subject to space**; placement by age + principal discretion. **EAL** support is sanctioned *provisionally*, subject to confirmation of eligible enrolments each September — not a guaranteed entitlement ([gov.ie — EAL](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/guidelines-for-applying-for-eal-supports-for-criteria-a-new-entrants-for-the-202627-school-year/)).
- **UK (United Kingdom)** — compulsory **5–16** (England); normal round via the Local Authority (secondary 31 Oct / primary 15 Jan), but relocating families use **in-year admissions** (School Admissions Code 2021); placement **by date of birth**, no national equivalence. **EAL** is school-led + discretionary — **no ring-fenced national newcomer grant** (no England UPE2A / Willkommensklasse equivalent) ([School Admissions Code 2021](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/60ebfeb08fa8f50c76838685/School_admissions_code_2021.pdf)).
- **AU (Australia)** — compulsory **~6–17** (state-set); catchment-**guaranteed local place**, year-round enrolment (no annual lottery); placement by age, principal-determined. **EAL/D** support is eligibility-gated — e.g. NSW *New Arrivals Program* / Intensive English Centres — and some temporary-visa children pay an **international-student fee** (e.g. NSW Temporary Residents Program) ([NSW DoE — PD-2002-0006-01](https://education.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/pd-2002-0006-01)).

**Raising kids in a non-native language — the decision note.** The younger the child, the lower the language risk: under ~8, immersion in the host-language public system is usually the strongest long-run outcome (free, full fluency within 1–2 years, a local peer network). The risk rises at the **secondary cliff** — a 14–16-year-old dropped into a statutory-exam track (DE *Abitur*, FR *brevet* / *bac*, NL exam streams, ES ESO) in a language they do not yet hold can lose a critical exam cycle; for that cohort an international / IB school or the home-curriculum route is often worth the fee specifically to protect the university pathway (see mitigation 4 below + `--visa` § Reality-check for the parallel "the cheap option carries hidden costs" pattern). Either way, the entitlement and the actual class are different things — verify the named provision at the specific school.

**Confidence**: HIGH for the 8 documented systems (each fact primary-sourced to the education ministry / statute / Local Authority code + date-stamped 2026; built grounded-research → independent audit, which fixed the FR circular NOR reference, the ES LOE article granularity, and confirmed the statutory-vs-discretionary split). MEDIUM on federal/devolved internal variation (DE 16 Länder, ES 17 CCAA, AU 8 states/territories each differ in detail — verify the specific Land / CCAA / state). **Last verified**: 2026-06-14.

## 7 universal mitigations

1. **Apply to international schools 12-24 months pre-arrival for popular destinations.** SG SAS / UWC / Tanglin / HK ESF/HKIS/Harrow / AU Sydney top private (SCEGGS Darlinghurst, Cranbrook) / NZ Auckland Kings / US NYC Trinity / CH Geneva Ecolint / FR Paris Lycée International Saint-Germain / NL Amsterdam ISA all have 12-month-plus waitlists. AU Sydney top private waitlist from birth for popular Year 7 entry. Late applications accept rejection — apply early or accept 2nd-tier.
2. **Verify catchment-area / school-district BEFORE the offer for state schools.** CN Beijing Haidian / Shanghai Pudong-Chuansha xuequ district adds 30-300% property premium; US Westchester / Marin / Palo Alto 30-100%; UK Kingston upon Thames / Chiswick / Sevenoaks 15-30%; NZ Auckland Grammar zone Epsom + Mt Eden 20-40%. Confirm exact catchment boundary with the relevant LA / EDB / district + verify the property address is in-zone for the target school.
3. **Budget for UK 1 Jan 2025 VAT shock if British private school is the destination.** 20% VAT plus business-rates relief withdrawal = effective 15-20% fee increase. Foreign-buyer families with UK private school plans for 2025+ entry should add 15-20% to budget. Some schools have absorbed partial cost; others passed through fully.
4. **Choose IB Diploma for foreign-buyer family mobility, country-curriculum for single-destination.** IB DP (45 max points) accepted globally for university entry; British IGCSE + A-Level for UK + AU/NZ; American AP + SAT for US; French Bac + Grandes Écoles for France. DN-visa + multi-country families benefit from IB; single-destination families may save fees with country-specific.
5. **For Asia: verify residency-coupled school access before relocation.** JP gakku district by jusho — must have residence registered; KR jeonse + work-permit + Korean-language proficiency; CN hukou + property ownership for xuequ; HK ESF priority for English-speakers + sibling/legacy. Asian public-system access is more residency-restrictive than EU.
6. **For Africa: clustering matters more than single school.** Nairobi ISK + Brookhouse + Hillcrest Karen / Westlands cluster; Lagos AISL + BIS Victoria Island / Ikoyi; Johannesburg St John's + Roedean + St Stithians clustered; Cape Town Bishops + SACS + Herzlia. Foreign-buyer families should buy property within 30-min commute of their school cluster (security premium in NG/KE/ZA also factor).
7. **Re-verify school list + fees + waitlist + reform-calendar within 30 days of decision-day.** This document's `Last verified: 2026-05-10`; school admission cycles + reforms move on annual cadence. Most volatile categories: UK 1 Jan 2025 VAT enforcement settling; AE KHDA/ADEK annual ratings (Outstanding/Very Good/Good/Acceptable/Weak); CH Geneva Ecolint waitlist updates; Asia BNO HK emigration aftermath; SG PSLE reform + DSA expansion; AU AUSTRAC Tranche 2 1 Jul 2026; CN xuequ rules adjustments. Decision-makers should pull current school's published fee schedule + admission calendar + most recent inspection rating before relying on any specific number here.
