# Universal `--notary` Section — Deed-Anatomy Sub-extension

Clause-level forensic checklist for the **sale-deed text itself** — what to confirm is *present* in the draft deed before signing. Loaded when `--notary` is invoked (deed-anatomy sub-extension), alongside the transaction logic in `shared/notary-process.md` and the succession layer in `shared/notary-forced-heirship.md`.

**Snapshot**: June 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *deed-CONTENT* layer — is there a citable prescribed transfer form or mandatory-content statute for the property's country, and which clauses/panels can a buyer check against it. It is distinct from three neighbours: `--notary` / `notary-process.md` (the *process* — notary-required, days offer→deed, closing-cost %); `--language` (which deed *version* legally controls + sworn translation); `--remote` (the *execution form* — POA, RON, eIDAS, §925-BGB simultaneous presence). This is deed CONTENT only — what the deed must say, not how it is signed, in what language, or by which process.

## ⚠️ THE BOUNDARY RULE (read first — anti-advice guardrail)

This section is **decision-support, NOT legal advice.** Every item below is an **existence / contents check** — *"confirm clause X is present"* / *"confirm it says Y"* / *"cross-check value Y against source Z"* — and **NEVER** a corrective recommendation (*"this clause is wrong, change it to Z"* / *"you should hold as tenants in common"* / *"negotiate the deposit to X%"*). A clause-level "is clause X present and does it protect you" checklist is defensible decision-support; a "this clause is wrong, here is the value to insert" recommendation crosses into legal advice and is **out of scope**. The per-clause *correct value* is transaction-specific and is the job of the buyer's **independent counsel** — this section always routes that decision ("which tenure, which deposit %, whether to accept this servitude") to counsel and never resolves it. Slot-filling an invented "standard deed" for the ~100 jurisdictions with no citable form is the single biggest authoring hazard — and is forbidden.

Three universal moves precede every per-country check:

1. **Demand the draft deed in writing, a meaningful number of days before signing** — the *borrador* (ES/LATAM), *projet d'acte* (FR/francophone), *Entwurf* (DE/AT/CH), *bozza/rogito* draft (IT), or draft TR1 (UK) — so you and your adviser have time to read it, never a same-day signing.
2. **Read the CONTROLLING local-language version, not a courtesy translation** — in almost every civil-law jurisdiction the local-language deed is the legally binding text and the English translation has no legal force (see `--language`).
3. **Independent counsel — your own lawyer/notary, NOT the seller's, the developer's, or the agent's** — confirms each clause below is present and reads as you expect. The seller's/developer's notary acts for the transaction's validity, not for your interests.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Is there a citable prescribed deed form / mandatory-content statute?** — a named anchor (e.g. UK TR1, ES Ley Hipotecaria art. 9, AT §433 ABGB, BC Form A) OR the honest *"no public standard form — the deed body is notary-drafted to local practice."*
2. **The buyer-actionable clause/panel checklist** — which of the universal red-flag items below can be checked against that anchor's named content.
3. **Source tier** — PRIMARY-OFFICIAL (statute/prescribed government form) · SECONDARY (text mirror, cross-check the primary) · NONE-CONFIRMED (no public form — structural contrast + verification path).
4. **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract.

## The UNIVERSAL buyer-side red-flag checklist

These are **existence / value-match checks** the buyer (with counsel) applies to the draft deed + binding contract in any country. Where a strong anchor exists, the named panel is given; where the item is contract-practice rather than statute, that is flagged. **None of these tell you the "correct" value — only whether the clause is present and protects you.**

1. **Declared price vs real price.** An under-declared price (off-the-books cash top-up) is tax fraud the buyer co-signs and bears — it shrinks your future capital-gains cost base, exposes you to back-tax + penalties on reassessment, and can void title-insurance/mortgage cover. **Check**: the consideration in the deed matches what you wired/escrowed *and* the signed preliminary contract; on UK TR1 this is **Panel 8** (words *and* figures — cross-check against the bank transfer record). If they differ, raise with independent counsel before signing. *(TR1 Panel 8 — PRIMARY; tax-consequence framing is universal contract-practice.)*
2. **Un-discharged mortgage / charge — discharge clause.** If the seller's charge is not discharged at/before completion, the lender's security can survive and attach to you. **Check**: the deed (or the completion mechanism) states all charges are released on/before completion and part of the price redeems them. Cross-reference a **fresh** register extract pulled days before signing (UK Official Copy / ES nota simple / FR état hypothécaire / DE Grundbuchauszug Abt. III). *(Register-extract freshness overlaps `--title-monitoring` + the finance/redemption mechanism.)*
3. **Undisclosed servitudes / easements / rights-of-way / covenants.** A burden runs with the land and binds you though you never agreed — it can block extensions or kill a development plan. **Check**: the deed discloses (or references the register entry for) every easement/servitude/right-of-way/covenant burdening or benefiting the property, and the list matches the register extract + cadastral plan. **Absence of mention in the deed does NOT mean absence of the burden — read the register itself** (ES LH art. 9 requires the inscription to record the nature/extent/conditions of rights). *(ES LH art. 9 — PRIMARY.)*
4. **Condition-precedent presence** (condition suspensive / subject-to-financing / subject-to-survey). A condition precedent lets you walk away without losing your deposit if finance falls through, a survey reveals a defect, or searches come back bad. **Check**: the binding preliminary contract (FR *compromis* + *conditions suspensives*; common-law "subject to" clauses) expressly contains (a) subject to finance by a stated date, (b) subject to a satisfactory survey, (c) subject to clear searches/title — each with its deadline + consequence if unmet. **The single most protective clause-existence check for a foreign buyer. Do NOT rely on it being implied.** *(Contract-practice — NONE-confirmed as a universal statute.)*
5. **Deposit amount + forfeiture symmetry.** A fair contract is symmetric: if the seller pulls out you should get the deposit back (often plus a like amount). **Check**: the contract states the amount/percentage, **who holds it (escrow/notary/agent client-account — NOT the seller directly)**, the forfeiture triggers, *and* what happens if the seller defaults. In ES the arras election matters: *arras penitenciales* (Código Civil art. 1454) let either side withdraw (buyer loses deposit, seller repays double); *arras confirmatorias* do not — confirm WHICH is named. The check is that symmetry exists + the holder is independent. *(ES CC art. 1454 — [BOE art. 1454](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763#art1454), PRIMARY; deposit-symmetry generally is contract-practice.)*
6. **Completion date + penalty / default clause.** Without a firm date + a stated consequence, a seller can stall while you carry bridging costs. **Check**: the contract states (a) the completion date (or its fixing mechanism) and (b) the default/penalty clause (penalty interest, notice-to-complete, right to rescind) — and it is reciprocal. On UK TR1, **Panel 3** carries the form-printed instruction *"Remember to date this deed with the day of completion, but not before it has been signed and witnessed"* (TR1 form text — the gov.uk guidance prose phrases it as "do not do this until after the transfer has been executed") — the deed is dated at completion; the late-completion remedy lives in the contract, so confirm the contract carries it. *(TR1 Panel 3, form-printed note — PRIMARY; penalty mechanism is contract-practice.)*
7. **Cost allocation — who pays notary / transfer tax / registration / agent fee.** Completion costs can add 7–15% on top of the price; ambiguity can land you with charges you assumed the seller bore. **Check**: the contract names + assigns each cost line — transfer tax/stamp duty, notary fee, registry fee, agent commission, any seller CGT withholding (e.g. ES 3% retención on non-resident sellers). Confirm no clause silently shifts the seller's tax or the agent's commission onto you. The check is that each cost is named + assigned — not whether the split is "fair." *(Contract-practice — NONE-confirmed as a universal statute; the local notary-fee tariff is separate.)*
8. **Tenure / co-ownership election** (joint tenants vs tenants in common / declaration of trust). How two+ buyers hold the property decides what happens on death and divorce — the wrong election can disinherit a child from a prior marriage or hand a co-buyer's share to the survivor against intentions. **Check**: the election is actually made and matches intention. On UK TR1 this is **Panel 10** with three boxes — *"as joint tenants"* / *"as tenants in common in equal shares"* / *"on trust:"* (free-text for unequal shares); **the registrar enters a Form A restriction UNLESS the joint-tenants box is X'd**, so confirm the box reflects your choice and that a separate declaration of trust exists for unequal shares. Civil-law analogues (FR indivision vs SCI; community-vs-separation marital regime) carry the same election. Confirm it is present — **not which one is "right" for you (counsel's call).** *(TR1 Panel 10 — PRIMARY, the strongest content-level anchor for this check; overlaps `notary-forced-heirship`.)*
9. **Vacant-possession warranty.** Without it you can complete and find tenants, a holdover seller, squatters, or a relative in situ — removal can take months to years. **Check**: the contract states the property is sold with **vacant possession on completion** (or, if sold tenanted, that this is expressly stated + the lease attached/disclosed + any protected-tenancy status). Flag absence to counsel. *(Contract warranty — NONE-confirmed as a statute; overlaps `--squatter` + tenancy-eviction timelines.)*
10. **Area / boundary cross-reference to cadastre / registered plan.** The deed's area + boundaries must match the cadastral/registered plan, or you may pay for land you don't get or buy a boundary dispute. **Check**: the deed gives surface + boundaries (*linderos*) AND cross-references the cadastral reference / title plan, and the figures match the extract you pulled. ES LH art. 9(a) mandates the inscription contain the description + *referencia catastral* + Catastro graphic-coordination status, so for ES you can check the deed against that statutory list. UK Panel 1 (Title number) + Panel 2 (Property) serve the same cross-reference. Flag any mismatch to counsel + a surveyor. *(ES LH art. 9(a) — PRIMARY.)*
11. **Seller identity + capacity + marital-regime / co-owner consent.** If the named seller is not the true registered owner, lacks capacity (e.g. an unverified POA), or is married under a community regime where the spouse must consent, the sale can be void. **Check**: the deed names the seller(s) exactly as the registered proprietor(s) on the current extract (UK TR1 **Panel 4** Transferor — full names; companies' registered/overseas-entity number per the Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022); any attorney references a valid current POA; and where the seller is married under a community regime OR the property is co-owned, **all required consents/co-owner signatures are present**. Cross-check transferor name vs register vs ID. *(TR1 Panel 4 — PRIMARY; marital-regime consent is civil-law contract-practice.)*

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **Existence-check, never advice.** The hard line: confirm clause X is present and reads as expected; *never* tell the buyer the "correct" deposit %, tenure election, or whether to accept a servitude. That decision is the buyer's independent counsel's, always — a 126-jurisdiction clause checklist invites slot-filling invented "correct" templates; do not cross the line.
2. **Form-of-execution ≠ deed-content.** Witnessing, eIDAS, RON, §925-BGB simultaneous presence belong in `--remote`. Deed *language* / controlling version belongs in `--language`. This section is deed CONTENT/panels only — reference the neighbours, do not duplicate.
3. **Registration-extract content ≠ deed body.** IT art. 2659 governs the *nota di trascrizione* (the registration extract) and art. 2657 the *form-of-title* — neither is a clause-list for the *rogito* body, which is notary-drafted. Likewise AT §433 is the *registration-deed* content. Do not imply these are "standard deed-content forms" for the substantive contract.
4. **Registration-constitutive vs opposability systems differ — don't template-paste.** In AT/DE/CH/NL/Torrens (AU/NZ/CA-BC) registration (*Einverleibung / Eintragung / inschrijving*) is what transfers title — ownership passes only on registration. In FR/IT/BE registration is mainly for opposability to third parties. The "ownership passes on signing vs on registration" point is jurisdiction-specific.
5. **DE Grundschuld is non-accessory.** Unlike a Hypothek, a "paid-off" Grundschuld does not auto-clear — it survives in Abt. III until formally discharged and can still secure live debt. Demand the *Löschungsbewilligung*/lender payoff; a stale-looking Grundschuld is a buyer red flag.
6. **DE Beurkundung ≠ Beglaubigung.** Only full notarial *Beurkundung* (§311b) satisfies the form requirement and forces the *entire* agreed content into the deed; mere *Beglaubigung* (signature certification) does not. A side agreement kept out of the notarised deed is void for form.
7. **A prescribed form is the floor, not the ceiling.** TR1's fixed Panel-7 wording ("do not add to or amend") and BC Form A's "no consideration → not registrable" are statutory minimums; the protective contract terms (conditions precedent, deposit symmetry, completion penalty, cost allocation) live in the *preliminary/binding contract*, not the registry transfer form. Read both.

## HONEST TAIL — the ~100+ jurisdictions with no public standard deed-content form

For most of LATAM escritura practice, MENA Arabic-language deeds, post-Soviet civil codes, and much of Africa and Asia, the deed's content is drafted by the local notary / avvocato / lawyer to local practice and **there is no public standard-form template to check against clause-by-clause.** BE (Belgium) sits here too: a transfer is an *acte authentique* drawn before a notaire and registered at the Bureau Sécurité juridique, but the load-bearing notarial-form + transcription statutes were not primary-verified this run — for further Southern-European/Mediterranean jurisdictions (GR, MT-civil, CY-civil) likewise no primary deed-content statute was verified within the call cap.

The honest output for these is a **structural contrast + verification path**, never an invented clause list:

- **Demand the draft deed** in writing a meaningful number of days before signing (borrador / projet d'acte / Entwurf / bozza) — read it with your adviser, not on signing day.
- **Read the controlling local-language version**, not a courtesy translation (`--language`).
- **Have independent counsel** confirm each of the eleven universal clauses above is present and reads as you expect — your own lawyer/notary, not the seller's or developer's.
- Then: **`data not publicly available — verify the specific deed-content requirements with the local notary / land registry`** (the país's Registro de la Propiedad, Land Registry, Grundbuchamt, conservatória, or notarial chamber; for BE: notaris.be / fisconetplus.be).

**Do NOT treat the absence of a citable form as licence to assume a clause exists.** Its absence from this checklist's anchored set means you must confirm it on the actual draft with counsel — and any per-country "standard deed template" an integration agent might add for the tail is a fabrication and a breach of the boundary rule.
