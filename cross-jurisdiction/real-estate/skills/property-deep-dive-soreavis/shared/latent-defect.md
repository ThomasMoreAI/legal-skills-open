# Universal `--latent-defect` Section — Post-Completion Hidden-Defect Recourse · Burden of Proof · Hidden Encumbrances

Foreign-buyer **latent-defect** layer: once the deed is signed and a hidden physical defect or a hidden title encumbrance surfaces, what is the recourse window, who bears the burden of proof, what remedy exists, and how realistic is suing the seller from abroad. Covers the statutory hidden-defect regime and its civil-code basis, the recourse/prescription clock, the burden-of-proof and visibility-at-viewing test, the rescission-vs-price-reduction remedy, the hidden-title-encumbrance flavour (usufruct / *enfiteusi* / undisclosed servitude / life interest / *terreno de marinha*), the cost and duration of litigation from abroad, and the caveat-emptor-vs-implied-warranty default. Distinct from `--surveyor` (pre-offer physical inspection — this is post-completion legal recourse), `--notary` (conveyance mechanics — though it documents where the notary's defect/encumbrance duty stops), `--scams` (a defect is not fraud), and `--inherited-noncompliance` (regularise-or-demolish, which runs with the property — this section is recourse AGAINST the seller). It is the natural post-completion bookend to `--surveyor`.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *post-completion recourse-against-seller* layer — the statutory defect-warranty period, burden of proof, rescission rights, the cost and duration of suing the seller, and the hidden-title-encumbrance flavour. It does **not** restate the pre-offer survey (`--surveyor`) or the regularise-or-demolish regime for inherited illegality (`--inherited-noncompliance`). A foreign buyer typically needs three pieces of intel before the offer: (1) if a hidden defect emerges after completion, how long is the window and who must prove what, (2) is the warranty routinely contracted away here, (3) what hidden title encumbrances (usufruct, *enfiteusi*, servitude, leasehold) could surface only at or after the deed.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Statutory hidden-defect regime + civil-code article + term** (FR vices cachés CC art. 1641-1649 · IT vizi occulti CC art. 1490 + 1495 · ES saneamiento por vicios ocultos CC art. 1484-1490 · PT defeitos CC · DE Sachmängelhaftung § 434/§ 437 BGB · CZ § 2129 OZ · PL rękojmia KC art. 556/568 · JP 契約不適合責任 CC art. 562-566 · UK/IE caveat emptor + Misrepresentation Act 1967 + TA6).
2. **Recourse window / prescription clock** — and whether it runs from discovery or from delivery (the buyer-hostile case).
3. **Burden of proof + visibility-at-viewing test** — must the buyer prove the defect was hidden, pre-existing, and not visible at viewing.
4. **Remedy** — rescission (*actio redhibitoria*) vs price reduction (*quanti minoris* / estimatoria) vs damages.
5. **Hidden-title-encumbrance flavour** — usufruct, IT *enfiteusi*/uso/abitazione, undisclosed servitude/easement, life interest, FR *viager*, BR *terreno de marinha* + *laudêmio*, IL Israel Land Authority leasehold; surfacing at/after the deed; the notary's title-check duty boundary.
6. **Cost + duration of suing the seller from abroad** — the practical litigation reality.
7. **Caveat-emptor vs implied-warranty default + seller-disclosure interaction** + **Confidence** HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **The hidden-defect warranty is routinely contracted away in private resale.** *Clause de non-garantie* (FR) / *Gewährleistungsausschluss* (DE/AT) / *vista e piaciuto* (IT) / *ouderdomsclausule* (NL) / "sold as-is" / *voetstoots* (ZA) — valid in most jurisdictions for a non-professional seller and defeated only by proven seller bad faith. This is the single biggest foreign-buyer blind spot: the statutory protection a buyer assumes they have is usually waived in the contract they sign.
2. **The recourse clock is short and often runs from DELIVERY, not discovery.** ES 6-month *caducidad* from delivery (art. 1490); IT 1-year from *consegna* + an 8-day *denuncia* on discovery (art. 1495); PH 6 months from delivery; RS 6-month default. Months, not years — and a clock that started before the buyer even noticed.
3. **The burden of proof is buyer-hostile.** The buyer must typically prove the defect was hidden, pre-existing at sale, and not reasonably visible at viewing (explicit in PT) — and common-law caveat-emptor jurisdictions (UK, IE, SG, HK) give even less, with recourse only via misrepresentation, fraud, or an express warranty.
4. **Hidden TITLE encumbrances are the dominant flavour and surface only at/after the deed.** Usufruct, IT *enfiteusi*, undisclosed servitude/easement, life interest, FR *viager*, BR *terreno de marinha* + *laudêmio* (the União-owned 33 m coastal strip with a per-transfer levy), IL Israel Land Authority leasehold (~93% of land). A physical-condition survey will not find these — the notary's title check has a boundary, and these live just past it.
5. **This is recourse AGAINST the seller — know which regime you are actually in.** It is distinct from `--inherited-noncompliance` (regularise-or-demolish, runs with the property) and from `--scams` (a defect is not fraud). Choosing the wrong remedy track wastes the short clock.
6. **The remedy may be price-reduction only, not rescission — and suing from abroad routinely costs more than the defect.** Many regimes default to *quanti minoris* (a discount) rather than unwinding the sale; cross-border litigation against a seller, with translation, local counsel and enforcement, frequently exceeds the value of the defect itself.
7. **The GCC short *uyub* seller-warranty (~1 year) is NOT the 10-year decennial — different obligors.** The resale buyer's recourse against *their seller* is the short *uyub* clock; the 10-year structural *décennale* is owed by the original contractor/engineer, not the seller. Conflating them overstates buyer recourse. The one reliable region-wide lever: an "as-is" exclusion is universally void against the seller's *deliberate concealment*.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

The single biggest foreign-buyer blind spot is not the existence of a hidden-defect regime — it is that the regime is routinely contracted away in private resales (FR clause de non-garantie · DE Gewährleistungsausschluss · IT vista e piaciuto · ES "cuerpo cierto/estado en que se encuentra" · CH/AT/LI Gewährleistungsausschluss · NL ouderdomsclausule · BE/LU clause de non-garantie), defeated only by seller bad faith — a far weaker post-completion position than the headline civil-code "guarantee" implies. The common-law/caveat-emptor jurisdictions (UK, IE, CY) give even less: protection is only as good as the questions asked on the disclosure form (TA6 / requisitions) and the buyer's own commissioned survey. The Nordic group (SE 10-yr / NO 5-yr post-2022 / FI 5-yr / DK insurance-substitution / IS disclosure-driven) is comparatively buyer-protective on the long-stop but offsets it with a strong investigation duty and a short "reasonable time" reklamation trigger. Article numbers are sourced to the cited code/authority; where an article could not be verified to a primary source it is marked `verify in <code>`.

### Country one-liners

- **FR France** — garantie des vices cachés, Code civil art. 1641–1649 (défauts de la chose vendue sub-section per Légifrance LEGISCTA000006165624); defect must be hidden, serious, pre-existing; action within **2 ans from discovery** (art. 1648 al.1; Cass. 2026 confirmed it applies to rédhibitoire/estimatoire/indemnitaire alike) with outer **5 ans from the sale** long-stop under general prescription art. 2224; burden on buyer (hidden + anterior + grave); remedy art. 1644 (rédhibitoire = rescission/full price back OR estimatoire/quanti minoris = keep + partial refund) + damages art. 1645 if seller knew; hidden encumbrances — servitudes, usufruit, droit d'usage et d'habitation, viager, droit de préemption surface at/after the acte authentique (notaire runs title chain + état hypothécaire but non-registered servitude / oral bail rural / droit d'usage can escape); DDT diagnostics are disclosure not warranty and a clean DDT does not bar a claim; in private non-pro resales the **clause de non-garantie des vices cachés is standard and valid (art. 1643), defeating the warranty entirely unless seller bad faith**; Tribunal judiciaire, expertise judiciaire (~€1,500–4,000 expert + lawyer), 18–36 months. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **IT Italy** — garanzia per i vizi della cosa venduta, Codice civile art. 1490 (+ mancanza di qualità art. 1497, aliud pro alio); buyer forfeits unless the defect is **denounced to the seller within 8 days of discovery** ("scoperta" = full objective knowledge of nature/cause/gravità, not first symptom) AND the action is **time-barred 1 year from delivery (consegna), NOT from the rogito/deed** (art. 1495); 8-day denuncia not required if seller acknowledged/concealed (art. 1495 al.2); burden on buyer (vizio + hidden + pre-existed delivery); remedy art. 1492 (azione redibitoria = rescission OR azione estimatoria/quanti minoris = price reduction, election irrevocable once judicial demand filed) + damages art. 1494 unless seller proves no fault; hidden servitù, usufrutto, uso/abitazione, enfiteusi, diritto di prelazione (agrario / storico-artistica) — notaio does a 20-year visure but non-transcribed servitude / uso civico can slip; clause "vista e piaciuta" ubiquitous in resale preliminari, limits apparent-defect claims only (not true hidden vizi or seller bad faith); Tribunale, 2–4 yrs first instance, CTU mandatory. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **DE Germany** — Sachmängelhaftung, BGB § 434 (Sachmangel) → remedies § 437 (Nacherfüllung, Rücktritt, Minderung, Schadensersatz); for a Bauwerk/Grundstück the window is **5 years from Übergabe** (§ 438); if seller arglistig verschwiegen (fraudulently concealed) § 438 III diverts to regelmäßige Verjährung (3 yrs from end-of-year of knowledge, max 10 yrs, never shorter than the 5-yr building period); burden on buyer (Sachmangel; for fraud-extension also seller knowledge + concealment); remedy primarily Rücktritt or Minderung + Schadensersatz where seller at fault / gave a Beschaffenheitsgarantie; Rechtsmangel (undisclosed Grunddienstbarkeit, Nießbrauch, Wohnungsrecht, Reallast, Vorkaufsrecht) checked by the Notar via the constitutive reliable Grundbuch — residual risk is non-registrable public-law Baulasten (in the municipal Baulastenverzeichnis, NOT the Grundbuch) and old Altlasten; virtually every private resale carries a full **Gewährleistungsausschluss ("gekauft wie besichtigt unter Ausschluss jeder Sachmängelhaftung"), valid except for fraud or an express guarantee**; Landgericht, selbständiges Beweisverfahren common, 1–3 yrs. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **ES Spain** — saneamiento por vicios o defectos ocultos, Código Civil art. 1484; the acción de saneamiento is extinguished in **6 months counted from DELIVERY (entrega), NOT from discovery**, and it is a plazo de caducidad (non-interruptible, raised by the court ex officio) — brutally short for a building, so buyers often instead frame incumplimiento / aliud pro alio / dolo / error under the general 5-year prescription (art. 1964) or saneamiento por evicción for title loss; burden on buyer (existed + hidden + grave); remedy art. 1486 (acción redhibitoria = rescission/price back OR quanti minoris/estimatoria = price reduction; damages only with the redhibitoria AND a seller who knew); hidden servidumbres, usufructo, derecho de uso/habitación, censos, derecho de tanteo/retracto — notario + Registro de la Propiedad strong for registered charges (fe pública registral), residual = unregistered servidumbres, urbanistic infractions, afección fiscal / community-fee debts; "vendido como cuerpo cierto / en el estado en que se encuentra" clauses + 6-month caducidad make the practical window far shorter than buyers expect (route via dolo/incumplimiento if seller concealed); Juzgado de Primera Instancia, 1.5–3 yrs, pericial needed. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **PT Portugal** — venda de coisa defeituosa / cumprimento defeituoso, Código Civil art. 913 (+ art. 905 ff venda de bens onerados); layered and buyer-hostile — buyer must **denounce within 1 year of knowledge (art. 916)** AND then file the action within the caducidade of art. 917 (immovables: action caduca 1 year after the denúncia, with an absolute long-stop tied to delivery) — net effect denounce ≤ 1 yr of discovery, sue ≤ 1 yr of denouncing, all within ~5 yrs of delivery; burden buyer-hostile — buyer must show the defect existed and (dominant TRC/TRP case law) that it was not detectable on a normal viewing ("defeito oculto" read narrowly); remedy by remission — anulação (art. 905/913), redução, repair/substitution (art. 914), indemnização (art. 915); hidden servidões, usufruto, uso e habitação, direito de preferência, undisclosed mortgages/penhoras — Conservatória do Registo Predial + notary/CASA PRONTA good but historically incomplete in rural parishes (prédios omissos/não descritos + unregistered servidões the trap); exclusion clauses common in older-property sales; Tribunal Judicial, slow, perito needed. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (precise immovable denúncia/caducidade durations per dominant Relação case law — verify exact months against arts. 916/917 CC for the specific contract).
- **UK England & Wales** — **caveat emptor** is the default: NO implied warranty that the property is sound/fit/free of latent defects in the conveyance of an existing dwelling, seller has no general positive duty to volunteer defects; protections are (1) the seller's answers on the Law Society TA6 (and TA7/TA10) — actionable if asked and answered untruthfully/misleadingly; (2) the Misrepresentation Act 1967 (rescission/damages, s.2(1) reverses burden onto the misrepresentor); (3) the CPUT 2008 (estate-agent/seller misleading omissions); (4) half-truth or active concealment ("painting over damp") defeats caveat emptor; Misrepresentation/contract claim generally **6 years from breach/contract** (Limitation Act 1980 s.5); latent-damage tort separate 6yr/3yr-from-knowledge + 15yr long-stop; burden on buyer (misrepresentation + reliance, then s.2(1) shifts disproof of reasonable belief to seller); remedy rescission/damages for misrepresentation, **no remedy at all for a genuinely latent defect the seller did not know about and was not asked about**; undisclosed easements, restrictive covenants, overriding interests, chancel repair, unregistered rights — Land Registry official copies + searches, title largely guaranteed but overriding interests the residual gap; County/High Court, loser-pays, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **IE Ireland** — **caveat emptor**, materially the same as E&W: no statutory implied term of fitness/soundness in a conveyance of land, absent an express warranty or actionable misrepresentation the vendor has no duty to ensure the house is sound/habitable; protections are pre-contract replies to requisitions on title + the Law Society contract, misrepresentation, conveyancer's searches; newly-built homes differ (HomeBond/structural-guarantee + Building Control Amendment Regs) but **second-hand homes are caveat-emptor**; contract/misrepresentation generally **6 years** (Statute of Limitations 1957, as amended); burden on buyer (misrepresentation/breach of express term); remedy rescission/damages for misrepresentation, no remedy for a latent defect the vendor neither knew nor warranted; undisclosed easements, burdens on the Land Registry/Registry of Deeds folio, rights of way, (rural) commonage/turbary — conveyancer's title investigation + searches the safeguard; Circuit/High Court, loser-pays, slow. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **NL Netherlands** — non-conformiteit, BW art. 7:17 (the property must possess the qualities the buyer could reasonably expect for normal use); seller's mededelingsplicht (disclose known serious defects hindering normal use that the buyer cannot see on a normal viewing) outweighs the buyer's onderzoeksplicht (no destructive/technical survey required); buyer must complain "within reasonable time after discovery" (BW art. 7:23 lid 1 — bekwame tijd; non-consumer ~2 months working rule) and the claim then prescribes **2 years after that complaint** (art. 7:23 lid 2); burden on buyer (property lacks a quality needed for normal use; for the disclosure route, seller knew); remedy nakoming/herstel, vermindering, partial/total ontbinding, or damages; hidden erfdienstbaarheden, vruchtgebruik, recht van gebruik/bewoning, kwalitatieve verplichtingen, opstal/erfpacht — notaris + Kadaster strong for registered charges; the standard NVM/koopakte ouderdomsclausule + "as is"/niet-bewoningsclausule shift age-appropriate defect risk to the buyer but do NOT override the seller's mededelingsplicht for known serious defects and must be specific to be effective; Rechtbank, moderate, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **BE Belgium** — garantie des vices cachés, (old) Code civil art. 1641–1649 (new Civil Code Book 7 on sales being phased in — verify which regime applies to the specific deed date; 2025–26 resale conveyancing still largely runs on the art. 1641 ff line); action must be brought **"à bref délai" (art. 1648)** — Belgium did NOT adopt France's fixed 2-year rule, "bref délai" is judicially assessed case-by-case from discovery; burden on buyer (hidden + serious + anterior); remedy action rédhibitoire or estimatoire/quanti minoris + damages if seller knew; hidden servitudes, usufruit, emphytéose, droit de superficie, droit de préemption — notaire runs the recherches hypothécaires + urbanistic certificates (the sol pollué attestation, esp. Flanders OVAM/Brussels/Wallonia, a key disclosure layer); exclusion clauses ("vendu dans l'état, sans garantie des vices cachés") valid, strictly construed, but DO bind a good-faith private seller — combined with the elastic "bref délai", BE post-deed recourse is weaker than the fixed FR window suggests; Tribunal de première instance, expertise judiciaire, slow/costly. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (flag the Book 7 transition for the deed date).
- **AT Austria** — Gewährleistung, ABGB § 922 ff; § 928 — no warranty for offenkundige (obvious) Mängel noticeable with ordinary diligence unless the seller expressly assured the missing quality or fraudulently concealed; **3 years for unbewegliche Sachen (immovables) from delivery (§ 933)**; burden on buyer (the Mangel; obvious-defect bar § 928 shifts practical onus to having inspected); remedy § 932 — primarily Verbesserung/Austausch, then Preisminderung or Wandlung for non-trivial defects, Schadenersatz where seller at fault; hidden Servitut/Dienstbarkeit, Fruchtgenuss (Nießbrauch), Wohnungsgebrauchsrecht, Reallast, Vorkaufsrecht — Notar/Anwalt + the reliable Grundbuch protect registered burdens, residual = ersessene (acquired-by-prescription) unregistered servitudes + public-law restrictions; **Gewährleistungsausschluss standard in private Liegenschaftskauf, valid except for fraud / express assurance** — the headline 3-year guarantee is routinely contracted away; Bezirks-/Landesgericht, 1–2.5 yrs. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **CH Switzerland** — Sachgewährleistung, Obligationenrecht OR art. 197 ff; the warranty action for immovables **prescribes 5 years from transfer of possession (OR art. 219 + art. 210)**, shortened by immediate notice (Rügepflicht) once a defect is discovered — late notice forfeits the claim; burden on buyer (defect existed at transfer + non-apparent + prompt notice); remedy art. 205 (Wandelung = rescission OR Minderung = price reduction) + damages where seller at fault / assured the quality; hidden Dienstbarkeiten, Nutzniessung, Wohnrecht, Grundlast, Vorkaufsrecht, Baurecht — Notar/Grundbuchamt + the federally reliable Grundbuch protect registered charges well (cantonal Lex-Koller foreign-ownership restriction a separate title risk); the **Freizeichnungsklausel / "wie besichtigt unter Ausschluss jeder Gewährleistung" is standard in resale, valid except for fraudulently concealed defects** and cannot exclude a specifically-warranted quality; the Rügepflicht (notify immediately on discovery) traps slow foreign buyers; cantonal courts, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (confirm the precise 5-yr immovable trigger/running point in OR art. 210/219 against current OR text for the deed date).
- **SE Sweden** — fel i fastighet, Jordabalken 4 kap. 19 §; seller liable for a dolt fel the buyer could not have discovered through the investigation reasonably warranted given age/condition — a strong buyer undersökningsplikt means anything a competent inspection should have revealed is NOT a hidden defect (buyer-hostile on visibility); claim must be raised within reasonable time after the buyer noticed/should have noticed (reklamation — case-law ~1–3 months once aware) and seller liability **prescribes 10 years from the buyer taking possession (tillträde) (JB 4:19b)** — the 10-year long-stop is buyer-friendly versus the rest of W1, the heavy undersökningsplikt the offsetting buyer-hostile element; burden on buyer (defect + pre-dated sale + not discoverable by the warranted investigation); remedy prisavdrag, hävning (material breach only), damages if seller's utfästelse/negligence shown; hidden servitut, nyttjanderätt, ledningsrätt, rätt till väg — Lantmäteriet/Fastighetsregistret reliable, unregistered/old servitut + gemensamhetsanläggning obligations the trap; many contracts add a friskrivningsklausel (valid if specific); tingsrätt, loser-pays, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **NO Norway** — avhendingslova (1992): seller liable where the property is in significantly worse condition than the buyer could expect, the seller failed to disclose material facts he knew/ought to have known, or gave incorrect information; the **2022 reform abolished the blanket "solgt som den er / as is" clause for consumer-to-consumer sales** (a generic "as is" no longer caps liability; a tilstandsrapport regime now drives expectations); buyer must complain within reasonable time of discovery (reklamasjon) with an absolute **5-year long-stop from takeover (avhl. § 4-19)**, extended where the seller acted grossly negligently/in bad faith; burden on buyer (defect/withheld information materially affected the bargain); remedy retting, prisavslag, heving (material breach), erstatning; hidden servitutter, bruksrett, forkjøpsrett, veirett — Kartverket/grunnboka reliable for registered heftelser, unregistered rights + konsesjon/odel claims the residual title trap; post-2022 framework comparatively buyer-friendly but the reklamasjon "within reasonable time" + 5-year long-stop still bind and a deductible-style materiality threshold applies before a price reduction; tingrett, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (confirm exact § for the deductible/threshold).
- **FI Finland** — laatuvirhe (quality defect), Maakaari (Code of Real Estate 540/1995); seller liable for a defect making the property materially different from what the buyer could justifiably expect, for incorrect information, and for failure to disclose known material defects (buyer also has an investigation duty for what an inspection should reveal); buyer must notify within reasonable time of discovery with an absolute **5-year hard long-stop from the property being handed over (Maakaari)** — beyond 5 years the buyer loses the right unless the seller acted grossly negligently/contrary to good faith; burden on buyer (qualifying defect + timely notice); remedy hinnanalennus, kaupan purku (material defect), damages; hidden rasitteet, käyttöoikeudet, etuosto-oikeus (municipal pre-emption), road rights — Maanmittauslaitos/lainhuuto-ja kiinnitysrekisteri reliable for registered charges; the buyer's own investigation duty bars claims for what a reasonable inspection should have caught; käräjäoikeus, loser-pays, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (verify exact chapter/section for notice timing).
- **DK Denmark** — governed by the lov om forbrugerbeskyttelse ved erhvervelse af fast ejendom + the huseftersynsordning; default rule = seller liable ~10 years for hidden defects, BUT if the seller provides a tilstandsrapport + elinstallationsrapport + offers an ejerskifteforsikring and undertakes to pay ≥ half the premium, the seller is **statutorily exempt from liability for the building's physical condition/installations** (regardless of whether the buyer takes the insurance) — buyer's recourse then runs against the ejerskifteforsikring, not the seller (seller still answers for fraud + matters outside the reports, e.g. title/legality); window tied to the ejerskifteforsikring policy term (commonly 5 or 10 years) for covered physical defects, residual seller exposure follows ordinary limitation (3/10 yr, Danish Limitation Act); burden — buyer/insurer's process shows the defect is covered and not a noted/age-expected condition; remedy = insurance indemnity (repair cost), not rescission, for scheme-covered defects (ordinary damages/rescission only for residual seller liability); hidden servitutter, brugsrettigheder, forkøbsret — Tinglysning highly reliable for registered byrder, trap = non-tinglyste rights + public-law/zoning constraints not in the reports; **most resales transfer physical-defect risk to an insurance product, not the seller**; insurance claim first, byret for residual seller liability, loser-pays. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (confirm current policy-term defaults + precise residual-liability limitation).
- **IS Iceland** — fasteignakaupalög nr. 40/2002 (Act on Purchase of Real Estate): seller has a statutory duty to disclose all material information he knows/ought to know and may not withhold information of importance (incl. defects), also liable for incorrect information and for the property being materially worse than the buyer could expect; buyer must complain within reasonable time of discovery with a long-stop measured in years from handover (Nordic pattern); burden on buyer (qualifying defect/withheld information); remedy afsláttur, withholding part of the price, repair, rescission for material breach, damages; hidden servitudes/use-rights — Þjóðskrá/fasteignaskrá reliable for registered charges; disclosure-driven and relatively buyer-protective but the reasonable-time complaint rule + statutory long-stop still bind; héraðsdómur, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (precise §/long-stop period — data not fully verified in public English sources; verify in fasteignakaupalög nr. 40/2002 on island.is/Alþingi).
- **GR Greece** — hidden defects / lack of agreed qualities, Astikos Kodikas art. 534 ff (remedies from art. 540 — the first three available regardless of seller fault, damages requiring fault); claims relating to a defect of an immovable prescribe **5 years** (general CC rule for immovables under the warranty regime), running from delivery; burden on buyer (defect/missing quality existed at delivery); remedy art. 540 ff (meiosi timimatos = price reduction, anastrofi = rescission, repair/replacement, or damages with fault / agreed-quality assurance); hidden douleies, epikarpia (usufruct), oikisi, pre-emption — the Ktimatologio/Cadastre modernisation (replacing the old transcription system) is incomplete in some areas so undeclared/mis-mapped rights and afthaireta (unauthorised constructions) are the dominant practical trap; exclusion clauses common; Protodikeio, often 3–5 yrs, expert evidence. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (AK art. 534/540 framework confirmed; exact limitation article/period flagged — verify in AK arts. 554–558).
- **LU Luxembourg** — garantie des vices cachés, Code civil art. 1641 ff (text materially mirrors the French/Belgian Napoleonic line; Legilux publishes art. 1648); defect must be **denounced to the seller within a brief period of discovery AND the action filed within 1 year of that denunciation**, on pain of forfeiture (per Luxembourg practitioner sources interpreting art. 1648); burden on buyer (hidden + serious + anterior); remedy action rédhibitoire (rescission + price back + damages) or action estimatoire (keep + partial refund); hidden servitudes, usufruit, droit d'usage/habitation, droit de préemption — notaire runs the transcription/hypothec check (reliable register); the clause de non-garantie des vices cachés valid and common, defeated only by seller bad faith; Tribunal d'arrondissement, expertise, 1–2 yrs. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (precise denúncia/1-year mechanics per practitioner interpretation — verify against current Luxembourg Code civil art. 1648 text).
- **CY Cyprus** — predominantly **caveat emptor** (English-law-derived conveyancing); no general statutory implied warranty of soundness on resale, buyer protection runs through contract terms, misrepresentation, and the Department of Lands & Surveys title search (the historic "trapped buyers" / undelivered-title and developer-mortgage problem is a Cyprus-specific title risk that overshadows physical-defect recourse); misrepresentation/contract limitation (general 6-year limitation under Cyprus Limitation Law) — rescission/damages for actionable misrepresentation, little/no remedy for a true latent defect not warranted or concealed; dominant trap = undisclosed memos/mortgages on the seller's or developer's title, lack of separate title deed, easements; the real risk is title delivery, not vices. **Confidence**: LOW-MEDIUM (common-law caveat emptor confirmed; specific statutory citations not verified — verify in Cyprus Contract Law Cap.149 / Sale of Goods + Immovable Property law).

## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

Across the EU-CEE/Baltic set statutory hidden-defect warranties apply by default — this is not caveat emptor — and "as-is" clauses cannot launder concealed defects (exclusion void for seller bad faith). The headline buyer-hostile contrast is the spread in the seller-liability window from handover: RS's 6-month default (extendable only by contract) versus CZ/PL/HU's 5-year windows is the widest in the region — for a foreign buyer the practical difference between recourse and none; ME/BA/MK inherit the same ex-Yugoslav ZOO architecture, so RS art. 482's 6-month default is the best structural proxy for their likely default term pending jurisdiction-specific verification. Two cross-cutting structural points often matter more than the warranty length itself: the EU vs non-EU enforcement cliff (CZ/PL/HU/RO/HR/SI/SK/BG/EE/LT/LV enforce cross-border via Brussels I bis; RS/ME/BA/MK/AL/GE/MD/AM/AZ rely on bilateral treaty/reciprocity, materially slower/costlier), and the register-reliability spectrum (EE/GE-NAPR/LV/LT/SI strong digitised title registers → CZ/SK/PL/HU mature → RO/RS/MK/MD moderate → HR cadastre-vs-gruntovnica mismatch / BG deeds-based → AL/ME restitution+informality → BA highest off-register risk). A search-tooling hazard: a generic "Georgia Civil Code hidden defects" query returns the US State of Georgia (caveat-emptor/UCC), NOT the country Georgia (Sakartvelo) — folded into the GE entry.

## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

8 of 10 jurisdictions (all except IL and — partially — TR) are Egyptian-Sanhuri-derived civil codes; the hidden-defect ("ضمان العيوب الخفية" / daman al-'uyub al-khafiyya, the khiyar al-'ayb option) architecture is near-identical across QA/SA/JO/OM/BH/KW/AE: (a) seller warrants the thing free of old, hidden, material defects unknown to the buyer; (b) default remedy is the buyer's option — rescind (return + refund) OR keep at full price; price-reduction is generally NOT a free-standing remedy unless partial loss/restitution is impossible (contrast EU/Turkish bedel indirimi); (c) the warranty is a default rule that can be contractually excluded ("sold as-is" / bay' al-bara'a) EXCEPT where the seller deliberately concealed the defect (fraud destroys the exclusion everywhere — the single most reliable post-completion lever region-wide); (d) short prescription (commonly ~1 yr from delivery for the uyub claim) — separate from and much shorter than the 10-yr decennial construction liability (a different regime, owed by the contractor/engineer, NOT a recourse against a private re-seller; do not conflate — collapsing these materially overstates buyer recourse). The waqf (Islamic endowment) encumbrance is a region-specific hidden-title trap (SA/AE/QA/KW). IL resale (Sale Law 1968 §16) and TR (TBK art. 231/2 gross-fault) reach the same fraud-exception logic by different doctrinal routes.

## APAC + Central Asia + South Asia

### Regional pattern

A civil-law vs common-law split dominates this region: JP/KR/TW (Japanese-/German-modelled), TH/KH (codified Civil Codes), MO (Portuguese Civil Code), VN/CN (socialist civil codes), ID (Dutch KUHPerdata), PH (Spanish-derived Civil Code) all have a statutory implied warranty against hidden defects (Roman actio redhibitoria / quanti minoris lineage), while SG/HK/MY/IN/LK are common-law caveat-emptor jurisdictions where the seller has NO general duty to disclose physical defects — recourse runs through misrepresentation, fraud, statutory new-build defect-liability periods, or (IN only) a statutory disclosure duty for latent defects; KZ/UZ are post-Soviet civil codes. Stating the regime plainly is the single most important output: a common-law "as is" sale gives the buyer almost nothing for a hidden physical defect, a civil-law sale gives a short-fuse but real remedy. Short discovery/delivery clocks are the dominant practical risk for an overseas buyer who finds the defect on a later visit — KR 6 mo from knowledge, PH 6 mo from delivery, TH 1 yr from discovery, TW 6 mo from notice / 5 yr longstop, JP 1 yr from knowledge — pre-completion inspection is worth far more than post-completion litigation across the entire region. Foreign-ownership voids the warranty question entirely in TH/ID/VN/PH/KH/MV (and conditionally MY/LK/IN-agricultural/TW-reciprocity): a void acquisition (nominee/anti-dummy/Sharia-restricted/category-barred) has no valid title and therefore no defect warranty to enforce, so the title-validity/ownership-structure check logically precedes any hidden-defect analysis.

### Country one-liners

- **JP Japan** — 契約不適合責任 keiyaku-futekigō-sekinin (liability for non-conformity to contract), Civil Code 民法 art. 562–566, in force 1 Apr 2020 — the 2020 reform **abolished the prior 瑕疵担保責任 kashi-tanpo-sekinin latent-defect warranty**; pre-2020 sources citing "瑕疵担保責任 art. 570" for resale are superseded — for contracts on/after 1 Apr 2020 the governing concept is 契約不適合 under art. 562–566; for NEW dwellings 住宅品質確保法 imposes a mandatory 10-year liability on the seller/builder for structural members and rain-penetration; where the non-conformity is of type/quality the buyer must **notify within 1 year of becoming aware (art. 566)** or lose the remedy (the 1-year notice rule does NOT apply if the seller knew or was grossly negligent in not knowing at delivery), general 10-year / 5-year-from-knowledge prescription (art. 166) still caps the underlying claim; burden on buyer (delivered property does not conform to the contract — contract terms define conformity, so the statutory 重要事項説明書 disclosure under 宅建業法 materially shapes what counts as conforming); remedy 追完請求 cure/repair (art. 562) → 代金減額請求 price reduction (art. 563) → 損害賠償 damages + 契約解除 termination (art. 564); hidden 抵当権, 地上権, 地役権, 借地権, 賃借権 appear on the 登記事項証明書 — recourse for an undisclosed registered encumbrance is non-conformity, for an unregistered possessory right (tenant in occupation) also non-conformity but the registry will not have warned; District Court civil suit ~12–24 months, filing fee scaled to claim value + Japanese counsel (sellers usually JP-resident, service straightforward); no foreign-ownership restriction (full freehold); 契約不適合責任 applies by default, in 個人間 private sales the parties may validly contract-out (免責特約) but the exclusion is void as to defects the seller knew and concealed (art. 572), where the seller is a 宅建業者 selling to a consumer 宅建業法 art. 40 prohibits shortening the notice period below 2 years from delivery. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **KR South Korea** — 하자담보책임 haja-dambo-chaegim, Civil Act 민법 art. 580 (specific goods, applying art. 575(1) mutatis mutandis), art. 581 (sale by kind), art. 582 (exercise period); for multi-unit/strata buildings the 집합건물법 overlays a separate developer warranty for common/structural elements; rights under art. 580/581 must be exercised **within 6 months of the buyer becoming aware of the defect (art. 582) — one of the shortest fuses in the region**, the underlying claim also subject to the 10-year general extinctive prescription; 집합건물법 developer warranties tiered by element (commonly 2 / 3 / 5 / 10 years — verify the current 집합건물법 시행령 schedule, amended several times); burden on buyer (a defect existed and the buyer neither knew of it nor was negligent in not knowing — art. 580 excludes liability where buyer knew/negligently unaware); remedy 계약해제 rescission where the defect frustrates the contract's purpose, otherwise 손해배상 damages (art. 580→575(1)), for by-kind sales demand defect-free substitute (art. 581(2)) — no standalone statutory price-reduction action for specific-goods defects; hidden 저당권, **전세권 (jeonse — a registered deposit-lease real right that survives transfer)**, 지상권, 임차권, 가등기 appear on the 등기부등본, art. 575–576 govern seller liability where encumbered by a third party's right or 저당권 is exercised and the buyer loses ownership — a 전세권/임대차 tenant in occupation the classic hidden-encumbrance trap (a 대항력 lease can bind the new owner even if unregistered); District Court ~12–18 months (the 6-month discovery clock the practical killer for an overseas buyer); foreigners may own (notification + restricted military-zone permission); applies by default, exclusion does not cover defects the seller knew and did not disclose (art. 584). **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **TW Taiwan** — 物之瑕疵擔保責任, Civil Code 民法 art. 354 (seller warrants no defect impairing value/utility at risk-transfer + any warranted quality), art. 359 (remedies), art. 365 (limitation), art. 364 (substitute for fungibles); rescission/price-reduction rights lapse **6 months after the buyer notifies the seller of the defect, OR 5 years from delivery, whichever is earlier (art. 365)** — the 6-month bar does NOT apply where the seller intentionally concealed (art. 365 proviso), buyer also has an art. 356–357 prompt-inspect-and-notify duty (failure forfeits the warranty unless concealment); burden on buyer (the defect impaired value/fitness at the time risk passed; seller's intentional concealment defeats the limitation/notice defences); remedy 解除契約 rescission OR 減少價金 price reduction (art. 359) — if rescission would be "obviously unfair" the buyer is limited to price reduction, damages where the seller warranted a quality or fraudulently concealed (art. 360); 權利瑕疵擔保 (warranty against defects of rights, art. 349–353) — hidden 抵押權, 地上權, 不動產役權, 典權 (diǎn — a redeemable possessory right), unregistered tenancy (Land Registration constitutive, so the higher risk is an unregistered occupancy right or a 三七五減租 farm-tenancy); District Court ~12–24 months (5-year longstop from delivery a hard ceiling); **foreign-ownership reciprocity rule (土地法 art. 18)** — nationals of countries that bar Taiwanese from owning land are themselves barred (verify nationality eligibility, a void acquisition has no warranty to enforce); applies by default, exclusion valid except for fraudulently concealed defects (art. 366). **Confidence**: HIGH (foreign-ownership reciprocity flagged for verify).
- **HK Hong Kong** — **strong caveat emptor** — the vendor has NO general duty to disclose physical (patent or latent) defects, the buyer relies on their own surveyor + solicitor's requisitions on title, the vendor's duty limited to proving good title (typically a 15-year chain under the Conveyancing and Property Ordinance Cap. 219) and not actively misrepresenting; - **TH Thailand** — civil-law statutory defect warranty, Civil and Commercial Code (CCC) s. 472 — in case of any defect impairing value or fitness for ordinary purposes or for the contract's purposes the seller is liable, **whether or not the seller knew**, s. 473 exceptions, s. 474 limitation; **no defect action later than 1 year after discovery of the defect (s. 474) — short fuse**; burden on buyer (a value/fitness-impairing defect; seller liable irrespective of knowledge — strict — subject to s. 473 carve-outs); exceptions (s. 473): no liability if (i) buyer knew or would have known with ordinary care, (ii) the defect was apparent at delivery and buyer accepted without reservation, or (iii) the property was sold by **public auction** (the auction carve-out matters for distressed/foreclosed Thai property); remedy damages, rescission where the defect defeats the contract's purpose, repair/price-adjustment negotiated under general default-and-remedies; warranty against eviction / defects of right (CCC s. 475 ff), hidden jamnong (mortgage), sidhi-kep-kin (usufruct), sidhi-asai (habitation), phara-jum-yom (servitude), registered/unregistered lease on the Land Department title deed (Chanote / Nor Sor 3 Gor); **foreigners cannot own land** — lawful routes condominium unit (≤49% foreign quota per building) or registered leasehold (≤30 years), the single biggest TH post-completion shock is a **nominee structure (Thai-company landholding to circumvent the foreign-land ban) being void/illegal** — a title-validity issue that destroys the asset (a void acquisition has no s. 472 warranty); Thai courts, Thai-language, ~12–24+ months (1-year-from-discovery bar tight); s. 472 applies by default, excludable by agreement (s. 483) but the exclusion does not cover a defect the seller knew of and concealed (s. 485). **Confidence**: HIGH.

for new builds **RERA 2016 s. 14(3)** imposes a 5-year structural/workmanship Defect Liability Period from date of possession, builder to rectify within 30 days at no cost, non-waivable by contract; a fraud/non-disclosure suit runs under the Limitation Act 1963 generally **3 years** from when the fraud/defect was or could with reasonable diligence have been discovered (Art. 59 / s. 17 fraud-postponement), RERA structural DLP = 5 years from possession; burden on buyer (the defect was material, latent, known to the seller, and undisclosed — heavier than a civil-law strict warranty but real); remedy rescission + damages for fraudulent non-disclosure, statutory rectification/compensation under RERA for new-build structural defects, the seller's charge for unpaid price / buyer's charge for prepaid price under s. 55(4)(b)/55(6)(b); India largely uses deeds-registration not Torrens (presumptive, not indefeasible title) — undisclosed prior mortgage/charge, unregistered prior agreement to sell, benami holdings, pending litigation (lis pendens, s. 52 TPA), tenancy, easement, and ancestral/HUF coparcenary claims are common hidden encumbrances, s. 55(1)(a)'s "defect in the seller's title" duty squarely covers undisclosed encumbrances the seller knew of (a 30-year title search + encumbrance certificate the standard protection); non-residents/foreign nationals face FEMA restrictions (OCI/PIO and NRI rules differ; agricultural/plantation land generally barred — a FEMA-non-compliant acquisition is challengeable); civil suit notoriously slow (often 5–10+ years) so practical recourse value is low even though the substantive right is strong, RERA Authority/Appellate Tribunal faster for new-build DLP claims; caveat emptor for patent defects, statutory s. 55(1)(a) disclosure duty for latent + title defects non-excludable by an "as is" clause for what the seller knew. **Confidence**: HIGH.
- **LK Sri Lanka** — common law is Roman-Dutch; for sale of goods/movables the Sale of Goods Ordinance applies caveat emptor (no implied quality warranty except misrepresentation / reliance on seller's expertise) but for **immovable property Roman-Dutch law recognises the aedilitian remedies (actio redhibitoria / actio quanti minoris)** — a seller is liable for latent (hidden) defects materially affecting the property even absent fraud, and in contractual damages if the defect was fraudulently concealed or a quality warranted, so LK sits between pure caveat-emptor common-law and civil-law: caveat emptor for patent defects, aedilitian latent-defect liability for immovables; **the exact LK prescription period for the aedilitian action on immovables (the Roman-Dutch actio redhibitoria historically runs ~6 months / quanti minoris ~1 year, but the period as applied by Sri Lankan courts under the Prescription Ordinance — typically a longer contractual period — is NOT independently verified here; verify against the Prescription Ordinance + LK case law before relying)**; burden on buyer (a latent defect not discoverable on reasonable inspection materially impairing the property and existing at sale; fraud/concealment escalates to full damages); remedy redhibitoria (rescission + price recovery) or quanti minoris (price abatement), damages where the seller knew/warranted; deeds-registration (not Torrens, though Bim Saviya/title-registration rolling out parcel-by-parcel) — undisclosed mortgage, lease, servitude, fideicommissum (a Roman-Dutch entail still encountered in older LK titles — a genuine hidden-encumbrance trap), partition-action interests, ancestral co-ownership common; **foreigners generally barred from outright land freehold** (Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act) — lawful routes long leases (up to 99 years) or condominium units (a barred freehold acquisition is void); civil courts, slow, weak practical recourse for an overseas plaintiff; caveat emptor (patent) + aedilitian latent-defect liability (immovables) under Roman-Dutch common law, modifiable by contract subject to fraud limits. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (Roman-Dutch regime + aedilitian-remedy applicability verified; LK-specific prescription period NOT verified — flagged).
- **MV Maldives** — the post-completion hidden-defect question is largely moot for foreign buyers because **there is NO foreign freehold land ownership in the Maldives — all land is State-owned and foreign nationals access property only via leasehold (up to 99 years, typically tourism/resort or development concessions)**; for a foreign buyer the operative instrument is a lease/head-lease/sub-lease + the lease covenants, not a sale-warranty regime — recourse for a hidden physical defect runs through the lease's express repair/condition covenants, the head-lessor's obligations, and contractual misrepresentation, not a Civil-Code defect warranty; the Maldives is an Islamic-law (Sharia) + statutory jurisdiction, no developed civil-code hidden-defect doctrine of the JP/TH type for real property accessible to foreigners; **specific statutory defect-recourse for Maldivian-citizen freehold sales — data not publicly available in this pass; verify against Maldivian statute / Sharia contract principles (the khiyar al-'ayb option-for-defect under Islamic sale law is the doctrinal analogue, but its codified application in Maldivian law is not verified here)**; the dominant risk is lease-term, head-lease validity, and government-concession conditions/revocation (a title/tenure risk, not a quality defect); Maldivian courts/arbitration per the lease's dispute clause, small jurisdiction, slow, foreign-investor disputes often routed to contractual arbitration; lease-covenant + misrepresentation driven — no foreign freehold means no foreign-buyer sale-warranty regime to invoke. **Confidence**: LOW (foreign-leasehold-only structure verified; statutory/Sharia hidden-defect recourse for citizen freehold NOT verified — flagged honestly).
- **PK Pakistan** — **common-law-based** (English-common-law legacy) overlaid with **Islamic law** — a mixed system (2026-06-13 playbook), conveyancing governed by the **Transfer of Property Act 1882** + the **Registration Act 1908** (applied per province); by the common-law baseline (cf. IN/LK) the seller has no general statutory duty to volunteer physical defects and post-completion recourse for a hidden physical defect runs chiefly through fraud / misrepresentation / active concealment or an express contractual warranty — **BUT the precise PK hidden-defect-disclosure provision (whether the inherited TPA s.55-style latent-defect/title disclosure duty is applied as in India) and the limitation period are NOT stated in the playbook — do NOT import India's TPA s.55(1)(a) treatment as if pinned for PK; data not publicly available in this pass — verify primary TPA 1882 text per province**; the **dominant risk is title-validity, not a quality defect** — the operative ownership record is a **presumptive Record of Rights (the mutation / *intiqal* entry in the *jamabandi* / *fard*) plus a registered deed, NOT a Torrens guaranteed title**, and land records are **provincial (no national cadastre — Punjab most digitised via PLRA / Arazi Record Centres, Sindh/KP/Balochistan materially less so)**, so undisclosed prior mutation/charge, a forged/overlapping *fard*, an unregistered prior agreement, and disputed *khasra* boundaries are the real hidden-encumbrance vectors (a full mutation-chain trace + encumbrance check the standard protection); foreign access is gated — **non-origin foreigners need prior Ministry-of-Interior approval (1980 Order under s.3 Foreigners Act 1946) + cantonment/border-zone security clearance**, while the practical foreign market is the **diaspora via the SBP Roshan Digital Account → Roshan Apna Ghar** (NRP/POC); final court for civil/property matters the **Supreme Court of Pakistan** (constitutional jurisdiction moved to the new Federal Constitutional Court by the 27th Amendment, signed 13 Nov 2025 — verify operative Article 175E), civil litigation notoriously slow + PKR exchange controls (SBP Foreign Exchange Manual) constrain repatriation of any recovery; presumptive-title due diligence the real safeguard, not a post-completion defect remedy. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (mixed common-law/Islamic system + presumptive Record-of-Rights / provincial-cadastre / mutation-chain + foreign-access gates + Supreme Court apex sourced to the dated playbook; the PK-specific hidden-defect statute/disclosure duty + limitation period NOT pinned — flagged honestly).
- **VU Vanuatu** — the post-completion hidden-defect question is largely **lease-driven** rather than a sale-warranty regime, because **there is NO freehold — full stop**: the 1980 Constitution (Ch. 12, Arts 73–75) abolished freehold and vested ALL land permanently in the indigenous custom owners, so a foreigner (and any non-custom-owner ni-Vanuatu) holds land ONLY via a **registered lease under the Land Leases Act [Cap 163]** (≤75-yr ceiling) — a "purchase" is an **assignment of an existing lease with N years remaining** + an annual ground rent to the custom-owner lessor, and **years-remaining is the #1 diligence item**; recourse for a hidden physical defect therefore runs through the lease's express condition/repair covenants, the lessor's obligations, and contractual misrepresentation, **not** a JP/TH-style Civil-Code defect warranty; the legal system is **mixed (English common law + French civil-law influences + customary land law)**, so no single clean statutory hidden-defect regime is citable, and **the operative defect-recourse mechanics + any limitation period are NOT stated in the playbook — data not publicly available in this pass — verify with a Vanuatu conveyancing lawyer / at the [Department of Lands, Survey & Registry](https://mol.gov.vu/index.php/lands-dept)** (do NOT assert a vices-cachés article or a caveat-emptor rule); the dominant hidden risk is the lease itself — **years-remaining, registered caveats/charges, the custom-owner lessor's identity/consent (Lessor's Consent to Transfer + Minister of Lands consent on certain leases), and ground-rent arrears** — surfacing on the Lands Dept extract (apartment buyers hold a strata lot under the **Strata Titles Act 2000** sitting over the same head-lease — confirm head-lease years-remaining); final court the **Court of Appeal of Vanuatu** (republic since 1980, no UK Privy Council link); **ZERO direct tax** (no income/CGT/inheritance/wealth tax) narrows the post-completion downside, but the lease-tenure/years-remaining/custom-owner-dispute layer is the real exposure, not a defect warranty. **Confidence**: LOW (no-freehold / leasehold-only structure + Land Leases Act [Cap 163] + Court of Appeal sourced to the dated playbook; the lease-defect-recourse mechanics + any limitation period NOT verified — flagged honestly).

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

Several civil-law families are at play: (a) Bello/Chilean code (CL → CO → EC, near-identical article numbering, acción redhibitoria / quanti minoris, 1yr inmueble); (b) Mexican CCF (vicios ocultos, very short 6-mo window); (c) Brazilian CC 2002 (vícios redibitórios, 1yr imóvel); (d) Argentine CCyC 2015 (defectos ocultos, 3yr caducidad + 1yr prescripción); (e) Peruvian CC 1984 (shortest at 6-mo inmueble); (f) Uruguay/Paraguay (Vélez-derived); (g) Caribbean common law (BS/JM/BB/BZ — caveat emptor default, no statutory hidden-defect warranty, recourse only via fraud/misrepresentation/express warranty). Across the entire civil-law group these statutory windows are SHORT and run from delivery/possession, NOT from discovery of the defect (AR is the partial exception via its 60-day-from-manifestation denuncia + 3yr caducidad + 1yr prescripción; BR allows discovery-based counting but caps at 1yr for imóveis) — by the time a foreign buyer notices a problem the acción redhibitoria is frequently already time-barred, so the operative protections are a pre-purchase technical survey, an express contractual warranty/saneamiento clause read carefully (sellers routinely waive but a waiver does not cover defects the seller knew and concealed in MX/AR/CL/CO and most of the group), and a registry/title study (a contractually distinct, often stronger route than the physical-defect acción redhibitoria). DO ≠ DM: DO = Dominican Republic (French-Napoleonic civil-law, Torrens registered title), covered here; DM = Dominica (Eastern-Caribbean common-law island), out of scope and NOT covered.

- **BR Brazil** — vícios redibitórios, Código Civil 2002 art. 441–446; window **1 year for imóvel counted from entrega efetiva; if buyer was already in posse the term runs from alienação and is halved (art. 445 caput)**, for defects that by nature only manifest later the term runs from ciência (knowledge) up to a maximum of 1 year for imóveis (art. 445 §1) — these are decadência periods (extinguish the right, no interruption/suspension like prescription, Enunciado 28, I Jornada de Direito Civil); burden on buyer (the defect is oculto + pre-existing, existed at delivery); remedy ação redibitória (rescission, return price) OR ação estimatória / abatimento no preço (quanti minoris, art. 442), damages: if the alienante knew the defect → restitui + perdas e danos, if did NOT know → restitui price + contract costs only (art. 443); a 2025 Código de Normas change permits unifying alodial + marinha land in one matrícula) — for coastal BR property verify SPU status (CAT/RIP, foreiro vs ocupante, laudêmio due, foro arrears) before closing; - **CL Chile** — saneamiento por vicios redhibitorios, Código Civil art. 1857–1870; requisites (art. 1858, copulative): defect (a) existed at time of sale, (b) grave (impide uso natural / would not have bought or bought for much less), (c) oculto (buyer did not and could not easily know it; art. 1858 §3 — a buyer who could have known by their profession is excluded); window **acción redhibitoria prescribes in 1 year for inmuebles (6 mo muebles) from entrega real (art. 1866); the quanti minoris (acción estimatoria) prescribes in 18 months for inmuebles (1 yr muebles) (art. 1869)**, parties may lengthen or shorten by stipulation; burden on buyer (the three art. 1858 requisites); remedy buyer elects acción redhibitoria (rescission) OR acción quanti minoris/estimatoria (price reduction, art. 1860), if seller knew the defect and did not disclose also damages (art. 1861); hipoteca, usufructo, servidumbre, censo, prohibición de enajenar, embargo — verify the certificado de dominio vigente + certificado de gravámenes, prohibiciones e interdicciones + certificado de hipotecas at the competent Conservador de Bienes Raíces (CBR jurisdiction-specific; a 10-yr title study standard); ~1–2.5 yr est. civil litigation (the 1-yr inmueble clock from delivery the binding constraint); warranty by law (art. 1857), may be waived but a waiver does not cover defects the seller knew and concealed (art. 1859). **Confidence**: HIGH (CC art. 1857/1858/1860/1866 sourced; art. 1869 18-mo quanti-minoris for inmueble per secondary legal commentary on the code).

## Africa + Anglo non-EU

### Regional pattern

In every one of these jurisdictions the dominant post-completion risk for a foreign buyer is hidden TITLE / encumbrance, not physical defect — un-titled/customary land (MA melkia, TN non immatriculé, NG Omonile/stool, GH stool, EG urfi), foreign-ownership tenure caps (KE 99-yr, GH 50-yr leasehold, SC/RW sanction/leasehold), and registry gaps. The physical hidden-defect regime (voetstoots / vices cachés / caveat-emptor-plus-disclosure) is the secondary layer; conveyancer/notaire registry searches and (in CA/US/AU/NZ) title insurance / Torrens indefeasibility are the practical mitigations. US / CA / AU disclosure + limitation are sub-national — exemplar states/provinces named, not over-generalised (CA's Quebec is carved out as civil-law CCQ garantie de qualité, far more buyer-protective than the common-law caveat-emptor of the other provinces; the US has no single national rule; AU and NZ statutory citations are corrected below). EG figures carry an inflation caveat.

### Country one-liners

- **ZA South Africa** — common-law warranty against latent defects (Roman-Dutch actio redhibitoria / actio quanti minoris) routinely excluded by a **voetstoots** ("as-is, with all faults") clause in the deed of sale; the Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019 (in force 1 Feb 2022) makes a mandatory written disclosure form compulsory (must be completed/signed by the seller and attached to the offer to purchase; absent the form the agreement is read as if no defects were disclosed) — but the **PPA disclosure form does NOT abolish or override voetstoots; s.67 PPA does not repeal the common-law voetstoots principle**, a voetstoots seller who innocently did not know of a latent defect remains shielded even after the form regime; extinctive prescription **3 years** from when the debt is due / defect reasonably discoverable (Prescription Act 68 of 1969 s.11(d)); burden hard on the buyer — to defeat voetstoots the buyer must prove the seller (a) knew of the latent defect AND (b) deliberately concealed it / fraudulently failed to disclose with dolus (mere non-disclosure of an unknown defect is not enough; the disclosure form is not a warranty but a false entry is strong evidence of the concealment the buyer must otherwise prove); remedy actio redhibitoria (rescission + restitution) for material defects, actio quanti minoris (price-reduction / damages) for lesser ones (CPA 68/2008 overlay generally does NOT apply to private once-off seller sales but can apply where the seller sells "in the ordinary course of business" — developers, flippers); High Court action, ~est. 18–36 months + material legal cost, a foreign plaintiff may be ordered to furnish security for costs; ZA is voetstoots-default — virtually every standard deed of sale carries the clause, the PPA form is a disclosure tool not a warranty and does not shift the burden off the buyer. **Confidence**: HIGH (statute + multiple SA conveyancing-firm sources; ## 7 universal mitigations

1. **Read the contract for the warranty waiver before you sign** — locate the *clause de non-garantie* / *Gewählleistungsausschluss* / as-is / *voetstoots* clause and price or negotiate it; assume the statutory warranty is waived unless the contract says otherwise.
2. **Get the independent pre-offer survey** (cross-ref `--surveyor`) — the cheapest defence is finding the defect before completion, because post-completion recourse is short, buyer-hostile, and often waived.
3. **Commission an independent title/encumbrance search beyond the notary's** — specifically for usufruct, *enfiteusi*, servitudes, life interests, leasehold (IL ILA), and *terreno de marinha*/*laudêmio* on coastal property; these are the encumbrances the standard conveyance can miss.
4. **Diarise the recourse clock from the DELIVERY date, not discovery** — and the short notice deadlines (IT 8-day *denuncia*) — because the window may already be running on completion.
5. **Obtain written seller representations + a property information form** where one exists (UK TA6) — a documented misrepresentation is often the only viable route in caveat-emptor jurisdictions and defeats an as-is clause if the seller knew.
6. **Model the cost of enforcement from abroad before relying on the remedy** — local counsel, sworn translation, and judgment enforcement frequently exceed the defect value; treat the statutory remedy as weak unless the defect is large.
7. **For GCC/MENA, separate the seller *uyub* clock from the contractor decennial** — confirm in writing which obligor you have recourse against, and that the as-is exclusion is void only against deliberate concealment.
