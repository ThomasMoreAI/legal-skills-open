# Universal `--squatter` Section — Occupation Risk · Eviction Timeline · Adverse Possession for Absentee Owners

Foreign-buyer **squatter / occupation** layer: the risk that an absentee or second-home owner finds an occupied unit slow, expensive and legally constrained to clear — and, on unattended land, the risk that adverse possession / *usucapione* extinguishes title outright. Covers the occupation-risk types (squatting/*okupación* vs non-paying-tenant overstay vs adverse possession of land), the eviction timeline and process, anti-squatter fast-track eligibility, the self-help prohibition and owner liability, absentee-foreign-owner exposure, the adverse-possession/*usucapión* period, and seasonal eviction moratoria. Distinct from `--crime` (burglary/theft stats — this is lawful-but-adverse occupation), `--scams` (this is not fraud), `--rental` (yield/STR-zoning, not eviction-process risk), and `--notary`. This is the *can I actually get my property back, and can someone take title to it while I'm away* check.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *occupation + adverse-possession* layer — the country-specific eviction timeline, anti-squatter fast-track eligibility, absentee-owner monitoring/insurance, and adverse-possession periods for land and buildings. It does **not** restate crime statistics (`--crime`) or transaction fraud (`--scams`). A foreign buyer typically needs three pieces of intel before the offer: (1) how long and how costly is eviction here if an occupier moves in, (2) is the property disproportionately exposed because it will sit empty, (3) can adverse possession extinguish title on unattended land and within what period.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Occupation-risk types** present in the jurisdiction — squatting / *okupación* / informal occupation vs non-paying-tenant overstay vs adverse possession of unfenced land.
2. **Eviction timeline + process + realistic duration/cost** (ES desahucio/juicio verbal/okupa · IT sfratto · FR expulsion + commandement · UK Interim Possession Order · DE Räumungsklage · ZA PIE Act court order · US state UD).
3. **Anti-squatter fast-track + eligibility + status/date** (ES Ley Orgánica 1/2025 criminal juicio rápido · FR loi 2023-668 + CPCE L412-6 · UK LASPO 2012 s.144 residential-only · NL Kraakverbod 2010 · US 2024-25 state reforms e.g. FL HB 621).
4. **Self-help prohibition + owner liability** for lock-change / utility cut / belonging removal.
5. **Absentee/foreign-owner exposure** (empty second homes targeted; non-resident can't react in-country) + monitoring/insurance options.
6. **Adverse possession / usucapión / prescription period** for land + building (years; good/bad faith; registered vs unregistered; Torrens immunity where it applies).
7. **Seasonal moratorium** (FR trêve hivernale 1 Nov–31 Mar · PL okres ochronny · HU 15 Nov–30 Apr) + **Confidence** HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **Self-help is illegal almost everywhere — changing the locks or cutting utilities makes the OWNER liable.** Across every jurisdiction researched the route is the courts, not direct action; ZA PIE Act makes self-help eviction contempt, US lockout statutes penalise it, and an owner who acts unilaterally typically loses standing and incurs liability.
2. **Absentee and empty second homes are disproportionately targeted, and you cannot react in-country.** This is the structural foreign-owner exposure — the very profile this skill serves is the highest-risk one, and the mitigation (monitoring, caretakers, anti-okupa insurance) is contractual and pre-emptive, not reactive.
3. **This is lawful-but-adverse, not a crime stat and not fraud.** It is orthogonal to `--crime` (burglary) and `--scams` (fraud); the metric that actually matters is the *eviction timeline*, which ranges from weeks to several years and is the decision variable.
4. **Anti-squatter fast-tracks exist but are narrowly conditional.** Spain's Ley Orgánica 1/2025 (in force 3 Apr 2025) routes *allanamiento*/*usurpación* to a criminal *juicio rápido* but does NOT cover *inquiokupación* (defaulting tenants, still the slow civil track); France's loi 2023-668 and the 48-hour préfet route turn on the property being a *domicile*. Eligibility conditions decide everything.
5. **Adverse possession / *usucapione* can extinguish your title on unattended land.** India 12-year (Limitation Act 1963, s.27 extinguishes the owner's *right* itself — the classic NRI loss); Italy 20-year; South Africa 30-year; Brazil 5/10/15-year *usucapião*; and the clock frequently resets only on owner action (Kenya). Unfenced rural land bought and left is the acute case.
6. **Torrens / land-titles registration immunises many parcels from adverse possession — know whether yours is registered-title.** Singapore (LTA 1993), Malaysia (NLC — never recognised), registered land in Australia/New Zealand, Land Titles parcels in parts of Canada, and Torrens-registered Dominican Republic land are substantially immune; the residual exposure is old-system / unregistered land.
7. **Seasonal eviction moratoria suspend enforcement for months.** France's *trêve hivernale* (1 Nov–31 Mar), Poland's *okres ochronny* (1 Nov–31 Mar), Hungary's winter moratorium (15 Nov–30 Apr) — though true squatters (vs defaulting tenants) are often carved out, materially changing the absentee-owner calculus.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

Lawful-but-adverse occupation risk for absentee/foreign owners splits into two distinct legal channels almost everywhere here: a fast-tracked criminal/administrative route against true squatters (entry by *voie de fait* / *okupación* / *kraken*) versus a slow civil route against defaulting or holding-over tenants — and 2023-2025 reforms (ES LO 1/2025, FR loi 2023-668, IT D.L. 48/2025→L. 80/2025) accelerate only the squatter side, never the defaulting-tenant side. Self-help (lock-change, utility cut, removing belongings) is barred in every jurisdiction and exposes the owner to criminal and civil liability. A *violent or clandestine* occupier generally cannot found acquisitive prescription/usucapión — the period runs only once possession becomes public, peaceful and owner-like — so the real absentee adverse-possession risk is unattended, unfenced, undeclared inherited land (acute in GR, material in IE, register-constrained in DE/AT/CH/Nordics), not active urban squats; empty urban 2nd homes are an eviction-cost/delay problem, not usually a title-loss problem. Only FR and LU operate a winter *trêve hivernale* (squatters of a *domicile* statutorily excluded in FR).

### Country one-liners

- **ES Spain** — Risk: two distinct tracks — (a) *okupación* of empty property: criminal *allanamiento de morada* (CP art. 202, if it's someone's dwelling) vs *usurpación* (CP art. 245, non-dwelling/empty 2nd home — minor offence, historically slow); (b) *inquiokupa* — non-paying or overstaying tenant (purely civil, NOT covered by the 2025 fast-track). Eviction: pre-reform *desahucio* / *juicio verbal* routinely **6 months–2+ years** (court backlog + vulnerability suspensions). Fast-track: **Ley Orgánica 1/2025, de 2 de enero** (de medidas en materia de eficiencia del Servicio Público de Justicia), in force **3 April 2025** — adds *allanamiento de morada* and *usurpación* to the *juicio rápido* (fast criminal trial) catalogue: accused before judge within 72h, trial within ~15 days, ruling within 3 days, eviction immediately after; burden shifts to occupant to prove a legal right; **post-judgment vulnerability re-assessment that previously stalled physical eviction is eliminated for this fast track**. The common premise "Spain's 2025 anti-okupa law lets you evict any squatter or non-paying tenant in 15 days" is wrong — LO 1/2025 does NOT speed up *inquiokupación* (defaulting/overstaying tenants), which stays in the slow civil *juicio verbal*; reform is criminal-track only. If a within-48h flagrancy report is made, police can remove squatters without a court order (flagrante delito) — but the 48h window is narrow and contested in practice (🟡 verify case-specific). Self-help: barred — lock-change / utility cut on an occupant exposes owner to *coacciones* (CP art. 172) and civil liability. Absentee: empty coastal/2nd homes are the canonical target; a private *seguro anti-okupa* / alarm-monitoring market exists (⚠️ insurer marketing, scope varies — read policy). Usucapión (Código Civil): for real property **10 years** with *buena fe* + *justo título* between present parties / **20 years** without title / **30 years** *extraordinaria*; an *okupa* with no title and clandestine/violent entry generally cannot found usucapión (possession not "pública, pacífica y en concepto de dueño"). Moratorium: no general winter truce, but vulnerable-occupant eviction suspensions have been repeatedly extended by RD-ley (status fluid — verify current RD-ley at boe.es). Sources: boe.es (LO 1/2025), Código Penal arts. 202/245, Código Civil arts. 1940-1959. **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **FR France** — Risk: *squat* (occupation sans droit ni titre — entry by *voie de fait*) vs *locataire défaillant* (defaulting/overstaying tenant — covered by an *expulsion* via *commandement de quitter les lieux* + *concours de la force publique*). Eviction (tenant route): *commandement de payer* → *assignation* → judgment → *commandement de quitter* → bailiff + préfet force — realistically **~12–24 months** including trêve hivernale. Squat route is now fast: *procédure administrative accélérée d'expulsion* — owner (or tenant) files with the **préfet**, who must rule within **48h**; if granted, forces de l'ordre evict **without a court judgment** (CCH; strengthened by **loi n° 2023-668 du 27 juillet 2023** "visant à protéger les logements contre l'occupation illicite", not 2022). loi 2023-668 also: criminalised squatting penalties up to **3 years + €45,000** (occupying another's domicile), created an offence for *maintien* after the squat, and reduced protections for defaulting tenants (*clause de résiliation de plein droit* mandatory, shorter délais). Self-help: strictly barred — *expulsion par voie de fait* by the owner is itself an offence (*violation de domicile* can run against the owner); utility cut / lock-change → owner criminal + civil liability. Absentee: a *résidence secondaire* left empty is the textbook squat target; the 48h préfet route was designed precisely for absentee owners (still requires proving ownership + that occupation is without title). Adverse possession: *prescription acquisitive* (Code civil) — **30 years** ordinary / **10 years** abrégée with *juste titre* + *bonne foi* (immovables); squatter possession that is clandestine/violent does not run (*possession utile* required: continue, paisible, publique, non équivoque). Moratorium: **trêve hivernale 1 Nov – 31 Mar** suspends tenant evictions — BUT **squatters are statutorily excluded**: **CPCE art. L412-6** (as amended by loi n° 2023-668) provides the sursis "ne s'applique pas lorsque la mesure d'expulsion a été prononcée en raison d'une introduction sans droit ni titre dans le domicile d'autrui à l'aide de manœuvres, de menaces, de voies de fait ou de contrainte" — i.e. a squat of a *domicile* can be cleared 1 Nov–31 Mar; the trêve still fully protects defaulting *tenants*. (2025-26 trêve: 1 Nov 2025 – 31 Mar 2026.) Sources: legifrance.gouv.fr (loi n° 2023-668 du 27 juillet 2023; CPCE art. L412-1/L412-6 LEGIARTI000047900053; CCH; Code civil arts. 2258 sqq.). **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **IT Italy** — Risk: *occupazione arbitraria* of a residential property (now a dedicated crime — **art. 634-bis c.p.**, "Occupazione arbitraria di immobile destinato a domicilio altrui", **2–7 years** imprisonment) vs *sfratto per morosità* (defaulting tenant — civil, slow) and *sfratto per finita locazione*. Eviction (tenant): *intimazione di sfratto* + *convalida* → if uncontested **~2–4 months**; contested / *graduazione* / officer-availability delays push to **6–12+ months or years**, with *proroga* of the executive date common. Fast-track: **D.L. 11 April 2025 n. 48 ("decreto sicurezza"), converted by Legge 9 June 2025 n. 80** — inserted **art. 634-bis c.p.** (in force 12 April 2025 on publication) AND **art. 321-bis c.p.p.** (art. 10 c.3 D.L. 48/2025), an accelerated reintegration-in-possession / sgombero route allowing rapid removal once occupation is reported. This is a **criminal**-track instrument tied to art. 634-bis (occupation of *altrui domicilio* by violence/threat/without title) — it is **not** a blanket 15-day civil eviction for any defaulting tenant; the separate 2026 *DDL rilascio immobili* aiming at a 15-day judicial release is **proposed, NOT yet law** (🟡 do NOT cite as in force). Self-help: barred — owner who re-enters by force / cuts utilities risks *esercizio arbitrario delle proprie ragioni* (art. 392 c.p.) + civil liability. Absentee: empty 2nd homes / inherited *immobili* are prime targets, esp. urban; private anti-occupation monitoring exists (⚠️ marketing). Usucapione (c.c. art. 1158 sqq.): real property **20 years** ordinary; **10 years** *abbreviata* with title transcribed + good faith; agricultural/mountain land shorter regimes. An *occupante abusivo* whose possession is *violenta o clandestina* cannot usucapt — the period runs only from when violence/clandestinity ceases and possession becomes public/peaceful (c.c. art. 1163). Moratorium: no fixed winter truce; the *graduazione degli sfratti* / prefect-set staggered enforcement calendar and periodic *blocco sfratti* decrees have historically delayed enforcement (status fluid — verify). Sources: normattiva.it (D.L. 11 Apr 2025 n.48; L. 9 Jun 2025 n.80; c.p. art. 634-bis; c.p.p. art. 321-bis; c.c. arts. 1158-1167), idealista.it / ecnews.it (⚠️ secondary, cross-checked). **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **UK United Kingdom** — Risk: residential vs commercial squatting differ sharply; also *trespasser* vs holding-over tenant. Eviction: **squatting in a residential building is a criminal offence — s.144 Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 (LASPO), in force 1 Sept 2012** (max 51 weeks' imprisonment / level-5 fine) — police can arrest and remove; owner does NOT need a civil possession order for *residential* squats. **s.144 LASPO is residential-only** — squatting *commercial* premises (or land) remains a **civil** matter; the owner uses an *Interim Possession Order (IPO)* (CPR Part 55 II) — once served, squatters get 24h to leave and committing/remaining becomes a criminal offence pending the full hearing; standard possession claims otherwise take weeks–months. (Scotland: separate regime — no LASPO s.144 equivalent; squatting handled under Trespass (Scotland) Act 1865 + civil removing.) Self-help: owner may use reasonable force to *displace a trespasser* only where lawful and without breaching s.6 Criminal Law Act 1977 (using/threatening violence to enter premises where someone opposing entry is present is itself an offence) — in practice owners should use the IPO/police route, not self-help, to avoid liability. Absentee: empty homes targeted; "guardianship" (property-guardian) companies are the UK absentee-owner mitigation market (⚠️ commercial; licence-not-tenancy status itself litigated). Adverse possession: **Land Registration Act 2002, Sch. 6** — for **registered land**, a squatter must apply after **10 years** adverse possession; registered owner is notified and can object (defeats most claims) — squatter only succeeds in narrow conditions (e.g. reasonable belief of ownership for 10 years — *Brown v Ridley* [2025] UKSC clarified the 10-year reasonable-belief period need not be the 10 years immediately before application); **unregistered land** retains the old **12-year** Limitation Act 1980 regime. *Best v Chief Land Registrar* [2014] confirmed criminal squatting under s.144 does not by itself bar an adverse-possession claim. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: legislation.gov.uk (LASPO s.144; LRA 2002 Sch.6; Limitation Act 1980; Criminal Law Act 1977 s.6), Parliament research briefing SN00355. **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **NL Netherlands** — Risk: *kraken* (squatting) — criminalised nationwide. Eviction: under the **Wet Kraken en Leegstand** ("Kraakverbod"), in force **1 October 2010**, squatting is a criminal offence (up to **1 year** imprisonment; more with violence) — passed from civil to criminal courts; OM (prosecution) can clear, though courts have required a short notice/kort geding window before clearance in some cases (⚠️ verify current practice). A 2021 amendment (in force ~2022) further accelerated clearance (faster eviction timeline — verify exact date/instrument at wetten.overheid.nl). Squatters no longer acquire *bewoonersrecht* (right of habitation) after a year — that pre-2010 protection ended with the Kraakverbod. Self-help: barred — owner must use the criminal/judicial route, not force. Absentee: empty buildings are the target; the *antikraak* (anti-squat / property-guardian, *bruikleen*) market is large and the standard Dutch absentee-owner mitigation — guardians hold a *bruikleenovereenkomst*, not a lease (weaker occupant rights, but also a vacancy-deterrent for owners). Adverse possession (*verjaring*, BW): acquisitive prescription **10 years** with good faith (*te goeder trouw*, art. 3:99 BW) / **20 years** extinctive route (art. 3:105 BW after the owner's claim has prescribed at 20 yrs) — Dutch Supreme Court (*Gemeente Heusden* line) allows a bad-faith possessor to acquire via the 20-year route but exposes them to damages/restitution to the dispossessed owner. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: wetten.overheid.nl (Wet Kraken en Leegstand; BW arts. 3:99/3:105/3:306), Wikipedia (Dutch squatting ban — ⚠️ secondary, cross-checked). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **DE Germany** — Risk: *Hausbesetzung* (squatting — entering empty property is *Hausfriedensbruch*, StGB §123, prosecuted on owner complaint within 3 months) vs *Mietnomaden* (defaulting tenant who exploits civil eviction delay — informal term, not a separate legal category) vs holding-over tenant. Eviction: no self-help (`§ 858 BGB` *verbotene Eigenmacht*) — owner must file *Räumungsklage*; default judgment for non-paying non-responding tenant realistically **~4–6 months** (Cologne AG benchmark); contested / *Räumungsschutz* (§765a ZPO hardship) / bailiff scheduling stretch to **3–9+ months**, occasionally longer. The *Berliner Räumung* (eviction limited to handing over possession, leaving belongings) is the cost-saving enforcement variant. Squats: police can act faster on a fresh *Hausfriedensbruch* complaint if the occupation is recent and the owner complains promptly; an established occupation still typically needs the civil route. Self-help: strictly barred — lock-change/utility cut = *verbotene Eigenmacht* (§858/§859 BGB), owner liable to be put back in possession + damages. Absentee: empty inherited / 2nd properties targeted; private monitoring market (⚠️ marketing). Ersitzung: registered land — **Buchersitzung** (§900 BGB) **30 years** entered in Grundbuch as owner + possession; movables **10 years** (§937 BGB). Practically, adverse possession of titled real property against the Grundbuch owner is very hard (the register controls) — squatters do not acquire title in practice. Moratorium: no statutory winter truce (court may grant *Räumungsfrist*/Vollstreckungsschutz on hardship — discretionary, esp. winter/health, not a fixed calendar bar). Sources: gesetze-im-internet.de (BGB §§858/859/900/937; StGB §123; ZPO §765a), Tieben/MTH (⚠️ law-firm secondary, cross-checked). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **AT Austria** — Risk: defaulting/overstaying tenant (*Räumungsklage*) and squatting (rarer; *Besitzstörung* / *Hausfriedensbruch* StGB §109). Eviction: *Räumungsklage* before the *Bezirksgericht* then *Exekution* via *Gerichtsvollzieher* — uncontested ~ a few months, contested longer (~6–12 months est., court-dependent). A summary *Besitzstörungsklage* (ABGB §339, 30-day window from learning of the disturbance) is the fast tool against a fresh squat — it restores possession without deciding ownership. Self-help: barred — *eigenmächtige* re-entry exposes the owner to a *Besitzstörungsklage* by the occupant. Absentee: empty 2nd homes (Alpine holiday property) the target; monitoring market modest (⚠️). Ersitzung (ABGB §1460 sqq.): real property **30 years** ordinary / **3 years** for movables; *uneigentliche Ersitzung* (no title/good-faith) **30 years**; against the *Grundbuch* register practically very hard. Moratorium: no fixed winter truce; court may grant an *Aufschub der Exekution* on hardship. Sources: ris.bka.gv.at (ABGB §§339/1460-1477; StGB §109). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **CH Switzerland** — Risk: *Hausbesetzung* (squatting — historically tolerated in some cantons e.g. Geneva, now mostly cleared; entry = *Hausfriedensbruch*, StGB Art. 186) vs defaulting tenant (*Ausweisung/Exmission*). Eviction: where the lease has clearly ended and facts are undisputed, landlord uses the **summary procedure (*Verfahren in klaren Fällen*, ZPO Art. 257)** at the single judge — fast (weeks–a few months); disputed cases go to ordinary procedure (longer). Squatter clearance is cantonal — some cantons act quickly on owner complaint + clear *Hausfriedensbruch*. Self-help: barred — owner uses the *Ausweisung* / cantonal police route, not force. Absentee: empty chalets/2nd homes; Lex Koller restricts foreign 2nd-home ownership in the first place, so the absentee-foreign-owner squat exposure is structurally smaller. Ersitzung (ZGB): titled real property is *grundbuchlich* — **ordentliche Ersitzung Art. 661 ZGB 10 years** where someone is wrongly entered in the *Grundbuch*; **ausserordentliche Ersitzung Art. 662 ZGB 30 years** for unregistered land/owner-unknown via court; movables 5 years (Art. 728). Against a correctly registered owner, adverse possession is effectively unavailable. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: fedlex.admin.ch (ZGB Art. 661/662/728; ZPO Art. 257; StGB Art. 186). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **PT Portugal** — Risk: *ocupação ilegal/abusiva* (empty property — penalties for illegal occupation were aggravated by a law approved by Parliament **July 2025** — verify exact Lei n./date at dre.pt) vs *arrendatário* in default / holding over (civil). Eviction: the **Procedimento Especial de Despejo (PED)** via the **Balcão Nacional do Arrendamento (BNA)** — for rent default the BNA route averages **~2–6 months**; a contested court *ação de despejo* runs **6 months–2 years** (contestation, police involvement, suspension periods). Self-help: barred — owner must use the PED/judicial route; force/utility-cut exposes owner to liability. Absentee: empty inherited rural and Algarve/Lisbon 2nd homes targeted; the 2025 penalty increase responds to publicised absentee-owner cases. Usucapião (Código Civil arts. 1287 sqq.): immovables with registered title + good faith **10 years**; title without registration / no good faith up to **15** then **20 years**; possession that is *violenta ou oculta* only counts from when it becomes peaceful/public (art. 1297). Moratorium: no fixed winter truce; periodic legislative eviction-suspension measures have occurred (status fluid — verify at dre.pt). Sources: dre.pt / bna.mj.pt (RAU/NRAU PED; Código Civil arts. 1287-1300), idealista.pt (⚠️ secondary, cross-checked). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **SE Sweden** — Risk: defaulting/overstaying tenant or unlawful occupant — eviction (*avhysning*) is enforced by the **Kronofogden** (Enforcement Authority) on application; self-help eviction by the owner is illegal. Squatting as an organised movement is marginal; an unlawful occupant is removed via Kronofogden after a *betalningsföreläggande/handräckning* or court order — timeline typically a few months (court/agency-dependent, verify). Self-help: barred (Kronofogden monopoly on enforced eviction). Absentee: low squat exposure relative to S. Europe; empty *fritidshus* rarely occupied adversely. Adverse possession (*hävd* / *urminnes hävd*; Jordabalken): acquisitive prescription is restrictive — Sweden largely abolished classic adverse possession of registered land; historical *hävd* periods existed (cf. Nordic *hævd* 20-yr tradition) but modern registered title is strongly protected — practical squatter title acquisition is effectively unavailable; precise statutory position `data not fully publicly verified — verify at riksdagen.se Jordabalken`. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: kronofogden.se, government.se, Jordabalken (to verify). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **FI Finland** — Risk: defaulting/overstaying tenant; eviction (*häätö*) via the District Court then enforcement by the *ulosottoviranomainen* (enforcement authority/bailiff). Timeline typically a few months for an uncontested default (court/agency-dependent — verify). Self-help: barred — bailiff-enforced only. Absentee: low adverse-occupation exposure; empty *mökki* (summer cottages) rarely squatted. Adverse possession: Finland does **not** have classic adverse possession of registered land in the common-law sense — the land register/title system controls; historical *nautinta*/usucaption concepts are not a practical route to defeat a registered owner; `process direction HIGH; adverse-possession specifics not fully publicly verified — verify at finlex.fi (Maakaari)`. Moratorium: no seasonal truce; court/enforcement can grant a short postponement on hardship. Sources: oikeus.fi (enforcement), finlex.fi (Maakaari — to verify). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **NO Norway** — Risk: defaulting/overstaying tenant (*utkastelse*) via the *namsmann* (enforcement officer) under *tvangsfullbyrdelsesloven*; squatting marginal. Timeline a few months typical for uncontested (verify). Self-help: barred — *namsmann* enforced. Absentee: low; empty *hytte* (cabins) rarely occupied adversely. Adverse possession (*hevd*, *hevdsloven* 1966): real property **20 years** in good faith (*aktsom god tro*); shorter (10 yr) for some limited rights / longer where claimant should have known; the Norwegian Code 1688 already contained a *hævd* provision (Nordic tradition). A possessor whose entry was clearly unlawful/known generally fails the good-faith test. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: lovdata.no (hevdsloven; tvangsfullbyrdelsesloven), Wikipedia (Nordic hævd — ⚠️ secondary). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **DK Denmark** — Risk: defaulting/overstaying tenant (*udsættelse*) via the *fogedret* (bailiff's court); the *fogedforbud* (interim prohibitory injunction) is the fast tool to stop an ongoing unlawful occupation/disturbance. Squatting marginal (historical *Ungdomshuset* aside). Timeline: *fogedret* eviction typically weeks–a few months; *fogedforbud* faster (interim). Self-help: barred — *fogedret* enforced. Absentee: low; *sommerhus* (summer houses) have separate ownership/residency rules but adverse occupation is rare. Adverse possession (*hævd*, Danske Lov 5-5-1, tradition from Jyske Lov 1241, standardised to **20 years** in 1547 and carried into the Danske Lov 1683, still in force): real property **20 years** continuous, unchallenged, owner-like possession (*alderstidshævd* for longer/immemorial). A clandestine/violent or clearly-mala-fide occupant's possession is not "uforstyrret" and generally does not found *hævd*. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: retsinformation.dk (Danske Lov 5-5-1), Wikipedia (Danish hævd history — ⚠️ secondary, cross-checked against Danske Lov tradition). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **IS Iceland** — Risk: defaulting/overstaying tenant; eviction enforced by the *sýslumaður* (district commissioner) / *fógeti* (bailiff). Squatting essentially negligible (small population, low vacancy in occupied areas). Timeline: a few months typical (verify). Self-help: barred — bailiff-enforced. Absentee: very low adverse-occupation exposure. Adverse possession (*hefð*, Lög um hefð nr. 46/1905): real property **20 years** continuous good-faith possession; movables 10 years. A mala-fide/clandestine occupant generally cannot acquire by *hefð*. Moratorium: no seasonal truce. Sources: althingi.is (Lög um hefð nr. 46/1905). **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **IE Ireland** — Risk: *trespasser* (squatter in empty property) vs overholding tenant. ## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

The universal post-socialist baseline: self-help eviction (lock-change, utility cut-off, removing belongings) is unlawful everywhere — the owner must obtain a court title and enforce via a state bailiff/court-executor, and a self-helping landlord risks criminal liability (coercion / unlawful interference with possession) plus civil damages. In every civil-law jurisdiction below a tenant/lessee holds derived (precarious) possession and cannot run the adverse-possession clock against the landlord — the clock is a risk from trespassers / colour-of-title holders / boundary encroachers, not from your own tenant. Acquisitive-prescription periods are precisely confirmed for the EU members (PL 20/30, CZ/SK/SI/LT/RS 10, HR 10/20, HU 15/5, BG 5/10, RO 10/30) but UNCONFIRMED — and explicitly not fabricated — for EE, LV, ME, BA (entity-split), MK, AL, GE (US-state collision), MD, AM, AZ. Only PL (1 Nov–31 Mar) and HU (15 Nov–30 Apr, with a squatter carve-out) have explicit statutory winter eviction bans; for every other country a found "none" is recorded as `data not publicly available — verify` rather than asserted. The strongest at-signing absentee mitigants are CZ/RO/PL lease structuring (notarial enforceable deed, registered fixed-term lease = titlu executoriu, occasional/institutional lease) and HR's "urgent" classification of ownership eviction claims.

- **HU Hungary** — Risk: (a) holdover after fixed-term lease expiry, (b) *önkényes lakásfoglaló* (arbitrary occupant — moved into an empty unit with no title), (c) *elbirtoklás* by a possessor. ## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

In the GCC and most of MENA the "squatter" risk a foreign/absentee buyer actually faces is almost never a stranger-trespasser on titled property — it is a non-paying or holdover tenant who will not leave, resolved through a rent-disputes committee or enforcement court, not the civil/criminal trespass route familiar in the West. Titled land in the GCC and Israel sits on Torrens-style registries (UAE Land Department / Tabu; Israel Tabu) where adverse possession against registered land is effectively impossible — the single most reassuring fact for an absentee buyer of titled property here; Türkiye is the main exception (a 20-year acquisitive-prescription mechanism exists but is barred against registered land save a narrow defective-registry exception). Self-help eviction (lock-changing, utility cut-off, removing belongings) is illegal across every jurisdiction in this set and exposes the owner to damages/criminal liability — a uniform finding — and seasonal eviction moratoria of the EU type do not exist anywhere in this region; the binding constraint is court/committee speed, not a calendar ban. Lebanon is in active legal/economic crisis (rent-law overhaul ping-ponged through Parliament and the Constitutional Council mid-2025) and is flagged LOW throughout.

### Country one-liners

- **TR Türkiye** — Risk types: dominant flavour is **non-paying or holdover tenant** (TR tenancy law is structurally pro-tenant); secondary risk is occupation of *unregistered* land. Eviction process: two routes — (a) **tahliye davası** (eviction lawsuit) through the *sulh hukuk mahkemesi*, slow: **~2 years typical, up to 3 years with appeal** (Yargıtay/Court-of-Cassation stage); (b) for rent default, the faster **icra (enforcement) route** — landlord initiates combined rent-collection + eviction at the *İcra Dairesi* (enforcement office); after the payment order is served the tenant has **30 days to pay or 7 days to object**; absent objection/payment the enforcement office proceeds to forced eviction without a full trial (Turkish Code of Obligations Art. 315 default mechanic; istanbullawyerfirm.com, akkaslaw.com, ozansoylu.av.tr — ⚠️ aggregator law-firm, cross-checked across 4 firms). Fast-track: **tahliye taahhütnamesi** (eviction undertaking, TCO Art. 352) — a wet-signed written promise by the tenant to vacate on a fixed date; if breached, the landlord may file for eviction **within 1 month** of that date OR go straight to the enforcement office — the single most effective tool, routinely signed at lease start. Self-help: prohibited; eviction only via court + enforcement office; forcible removal is a state act. Absentee exposure: a non-resident landlord who cannot attend hearings is materially exposed to the multi-year *tahliye davası* timeline — mitigant is (i) always take a notarised/dated *tahliye taahhütnamesi*, (ii) appoint a Turkish lawyer with vekâletname (PoA), (iii) monitor rent arrears monthly. Adverse possession: *olağanüstü kazandırıcı zamanaşımı*, **Turkish Civil Code (TMK) Art. 713** — **20 years** uninterrupted, unchallenged possession "as owner" (*malik sıfatıyla*); the general rule is that adverse possession **cannot run against registered (tapulu) land** — TMK 713/2 allows it ONLY where the registry is so defective the owner cannot be identified (or owner long-dead/whereabouts unknown), so a registered, identifiable foreign owner of *tapulu* land is **effectively immune**; requires a court action (*tapu iptali ve tescili davası*), not automatic (TMK Art. 713; istanbulattorneys.com, dergipark.org.tr academic). Seasonal moratorium: none (temporary COVID-era and rent-cap measures existed but no seasonal moratorium). **Confidence**: HIGH (statute-anchored on adverse possession & undertaking; timelines aggregator-sourced but consistent across 4 firms).

- **AE United Arab Emirates** — Risk types: trespass/squatting on *titled* property is rare (strong title registry, residency-gated occupation); the real risk is a **holdover or non-paying tenant**, plus **off-plan/empty-unit informal occupation** in low-supervision developments. Eviction process (Dubai): governed by **Law No. 26 of 2007 as amended by Law No. 33 of 2008**; disputes go to the **Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC / "RDC")** under Dubai Land Department — not ordinary courts. Notice rules: eviction *during* a lease term is limited to enumerated breaches (non-payment: tenant gets a **30-day** notice to remedy after default before an eviction claim, Art. 25(1)); eviction at/after expiry for **owner sale or personal/first-degree-relative use** requires **12 months' prior notice served via Notary Public or registered mail** (Art. 25(2)). The landlord files at RDSC with Ejari-registered contract + title deed + the served notice; the tenant may contest within 15 days. Timeline (specific): first hearing **~2-3 weeks** after filing; if mediation fails, First Instance Circuit ruling **typically within ~30 days of the first hearing**; **straightforward eviction filing-to-first-instance ruling ~1-3 months**. Appeal must be lodged **within 15 days** of the ruling — but for claims **under AED 50,000 the first-instance judgment is final / not appealable** (a meaningful fast-finality threshold). Post-judgment, if the tenant refuses, the landlord applies to the **Execution Court** (Dubai Police assist physical removal) — **~2-6 additional weeks** (rdc.gov.ae FAQ; tamimi.com; cross-checked notaryindubai.com — ⚠️ aggregator). Fast-track: the RDSC itself is the expedited venue; Ejari registration + a clean 12-month notarised notice + a sub-AED-50k claim where applicable (final at first instance) is the practical fast path. Self-help: prohibited — utility disconnection, lock changes and forced removal are unlawful and expose the landlord to damages (Dubai Land Department guidance; tamimi.com). Absentee exposure: empty/holiday units carry low *squat* risk but high *holdover-tenant* risk; non-resident landlords should (i) keep Ejari current, (ii) serve the 12-month notice early and correctly notarised (defective service is the #1 reason RDSC rejects an eviction), (iii) use a managing agent with PoA. Abu Dhabi runs an analogous regime via the **Abu Dhabi Rental Disputes** mechanism / Tamm; other emirates have rent committees. Adverse possession: against **registered land — immune**; UAE Civil Code prescription concepts do not defeat a registered Land-Department title; titled real property in Dubai/AD is protected by the registry (Dubai Land Department title system; UAE Civil Code Federal Law 5/1985 — possession does not transfer registered title). Seasonal moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH (Dubai statute + RDSC procedure well-documented; Abu Dhabi/other-emirate detail MEDIUM).

- **IL Israel** — Risk types: genuine **stranger-trespasser (פולש)** squatting is a recognised, distinct scenario here (more so than the GCC), alongside **holdover/non-paying tenant**; public-land squatting handled separately. Eviction process: civil **תביעת פינוי / "mesira rechusha"** (delivery-of-possession / eviction claim) — an **expedited track**, typically **~3-6 months** filing-to-enforcement, materially faster than ordinary civil suits; enforcement of the judgment is carried out through the **הוצאה לפועל (Enforcement & Collection Authority)**, not by the owner. Public/state land squatting is handled under the **Public Land (Eviction of Squatters) Law, 5741-1981** (administrative eviction order route for Israel-land/local-authority land). Fast-track / self-help window: the **Land Law, 5729-1969** §18 gives the lawful holder a **narrow self-help right** — reasonable force to *prevent* a trespass, and to remove a trespasser **within 30 days of the unlawful seizure**; **after 30 days, or against a former tenant, self-help is barred** and the only remedy is the court (Land Law §§18-19; versa.cardozo.yu.edu; knesset.gov.il statute PDF) — this 30-day window is the single most important operational fact for an absentee owner (discover an intrusion late and you lose the self-help option entirely). Self-help (general): changing locks / cutting utilities against a tenant is **illegal**; landlords doing so are liable in damages — court authorisation is mandatory. Absentee exposure: high — an overseas owner is unlikely to detect a seizure inside the 30-day §18 window, so the practical posture is (i) local property manager doing physical checks, (ii) immediate counsel on discovery, (iii) the expedited *mesira rechusha* track. (The politically-charged Absentees' Property Law / East-Jerusalem disputes are a separate sovereignty matter, deliberately excluded from this squatter analysis — mentioned only to disambiguate it from §18 self-help and the *mesira rechusha* track.) Adverse possession / prescription: governed by the **Prescription Law, 5718-1958** interacting with the Land Law; for **registered (mukar / Tabu-registered) land under the title-registration (Torrens) system, acquisitive prescription is effectively unavailable** — registered title is not defeated by possession; prescription historically bites mainly on **unregistered/settlement-pending land**, and post-1969 the regime sharply curtails prescriptive acquisition. A registered foreign owner of Tabu-registered land is **protected**; verify the parcel's registration status (Tabu vs. unsettled) on due diligence. Seasonal moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on §18 30-day rule and expedited track; MEDIUM on precise prescription periods for edge-case unregistered parcels — verify per parcel at the Land Registry.

- **SA Saudi Arabia** — Risk types: predominantly **non-paying / holdover tenant**; stranger-squatting on titled (Sakk) land is uncommon; foreign freehold ownership is itself narrowly permitted, so most absentee exposure is via leased units. Eviction process: a lease documented and authenticated on the **Ejar** platform (REGA / Ministry of Municipalities & Housing) is a self-enforcing **executive instrument** — the landlord goes **directly to the Enforcement Court (Tanfeedh / محكمة التنفيذ)** without first filing a substantive lawsuit; an **undocumented** lease has no such shortcut and requires a full general-court action first. Standard Ejar terms give a **~15-day grace period** on non-payment before formal action; the landlord must serve **written notice** before eviction proceedings. Timeline: **~1-3 months** depending on complexity and whether the tenant contests (elaqatlaw.com; trowers.com; saudigazette.com.sa — ⚠️ law-firm/press, cross-checked). Fast-track: the Ejar→Tanfeedh path *is* the fast-track; **Rental Dispute Resolution Committees** under the Ministry handle contested matters; the Enforcement Court can compel compliance by **suspending the tenant's government & banking services and imposing a travel ban** — an unusually strong enforcement lever. A 2025 framework (REGA) further streamlined this and introduced Riyadh-area rent regulation. Self-help: explicitly **illegal** — Saudi Gazette guidance confirms landlords may **not** cut electricity/water/gas or remove belongings to force payment. Absentee exposure: register every lease on Ejar (the decisive protective step), keep notices documented; non-resident owners benefit from the strong Tanfeedh enforcement once an executive instrument exists. Adverse possession: Sharia-based *hiyaza* (long undisturbed possession) can found a claim over **unregistered** land, but **registered Sakk title is not displaced by possession** and the Real Estate Registration Law programme further hardens registered title — a registered owner of titled property is **protected**; unregistered/edge parcels carry residual *hiyaza* risk. Seasonal moratorium: none. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (Ejar/Tanfeedh mechanics well-attested across multiple firms + Saudi Gazette; exact statutory adverse-possession periods under Sharia/Real-Estate-Registration-Law are not cleanly codified in a single citable English source — verify with Saudi counsel / REGA).

- **QA Qatar** — Risk types: **non-paying / holdover tenant** dominant; foreign ownership is zone-restricted so most exposure is leasehold; stranger-squatting on titled land uncommon. Eviction process: **Law No. (4) of 2008 on Property Leasing** (Al Meezan legal portal, almeezan.qa — primary); non-payment is a ground under **Art. 19** — landlord serves a **legal notice demanding payment within a set deadline**, then files with the **Rental Disputes Resolution Committee** at the Ministry of Justice, which has exclusive jurisdiction over tenancy disputes and is described as **free and relatively efficient**; committee decisions are enforced per Council-of-Ministers procedure. Timeline: not published as a fixed statutory number — committee practice ~weeks to a few months; treat as `est.` pending committee-specific data — **data not publicly available — verify at [almeezan.qa](https://www.almeezan.qa/LawView.aspx?opt=&LawID=3982&language=en)**. Fast-track: the Rental Disputes Committee is itself the specialised expedited venue (no ordinary-court queue). Self-help: prohibited (Law 4/2008 routes all eviction through the Committee; no self-help remedy exists). Absentee exposure: keep the tenancy and notices documented; non-resident landlords act through the Committee with local counsel/PoA; empty-unit squat risk low given strong title registry and residency-gated occupancy. Adverse possession: against **registered title — not available**; Qatari registered real-property title is protected by the registry — possession does not transfer a registered title (no clean English statutory citation for the prescription period — treat unregistered-land edge cases as `data not publicly available — verify with Qatari counsel / Ministry of Justice`). - **OM Oman** — Risk types: **non-paying / holdover tenant** dominant; foreign ownership largely confined to designated ITC zones, so absentee exposure is mostly leasehold; stranger-squatting on titled land uncommon. Timeline: a submitted case must be **passed to the Committee within 7 days**; the **Committee must decide within 90 days** of referral (RD 12/2025 procedural provisions); rent-payment delay now triggers faster action than under the old RD 6/89 regime. Fast-track: the new Committee replaces the slower court route — that *is* the fast-track (and the 90-day decision cap is a hard statutory ceiling). Self-help: prohibited — eviction requires the formal Committee process; no self-help remedy. Absentee exposure: register the tenancy (registration is required for Committee access, mirroring the GCC pattern), act through the Committee with local counsel; ITC-zone units have low squat risk given title registry. Adverse possession: against **registered title — not available** under the Omani land-registration system; unregistered-land edge cases — **data not publicly available — verify with Omani counsel / Ministry of Housing & Urban Planning**. Seasonal moratorium: none. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (RD 12/2025 reform + 7-day/90-day timelines primary-sourced and recent; adverse-possession period not authoritatively published — honestly flagged).

- **BH Bahrain** — Risk types: **non-paying / holdover tenant** dominant; stranger-squatting on titled land uncommon. Eviction process: **Real Estate Lease Law No. 27 of 2014** (bahrainbusinesslaws.com — primary); non-payment ground: a landlord may evict for **non-payment for 2 consecutive months** (the general restriction on eviction does not apply once rent is 2 months unpaid, unless the Committee finds good reason). The tenant must be **notified, with 3 months' notice for relevant termination dates, by registered mail with acknowledgment of receipt** — defective service can sink the eviction. Disputes go to the **Lease Disputes Committee** (Ministry of Justice). Critical gating fact: the Committee **will not hear disputes on leases not registered** with the Property Lease Agreement Registration office — and leases must be registered within **1 month of signing** — an unregistered lease leaves the landlord with **no committee remedy at all**. Timeline: not published as a fixed number — **data not publicly available — verify at [bahrainbusinesslaws.com](https://bahrainbusinesslaws.com/laws/Property-Rent-Law)** / Lease Disputes Committee. Fast-track: the Lease Disputes Committee is the specialised venue, conditional on registration. Self-help: prohibited (all eviction routed through the Committee). Absentee exposure: **register the lease within 1 month** — the single decisive step for a non-resident landlord; without it the entire Committee enforcement track is unavailable. Adverse possession: against **registered (Survey & Land Registration Bureau) title — not available**; possession does not defeat a registered Bahraini title; unregistered-land edge cases **data not publicly available — verify with Bahraini counsel / SLRB**. Seasonal moratorium: none. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (Law 27/2014 substantive grounds + registration gating primary-sourced; timeline and adverse-possession period not published — honestly flagged).

- **KW Kuwait** — Risk types: **non-paying / holdover tenant** dominant; foreign freehold ownership is highly restricted, so absentee exposure is overwhelmingly leasehold; stranger-squatting on titled land uncommon. Eviction process: **Law No. 35 of 1978 on real-estate leasing** (as amended, incl. **Law No. 15 of 2020** COVID-era amendments); non-payment: under **Art. 10** the tenant must pay per the written agreement; tenant gets a **3-day grace period** after the due date before the landlord may file; the landlord then files an eviction case in the Kuwait courts. Timeline: not published as a fixed number; the dedicated rental division is intended to be faster than ordinary civil queues — treat as `est.` — **data not publicly available — verify with Kuwaiti counsel / Ministry of Justice**. Fast-track: the dedicated single-judge rental division *is* the expedited mechanism. Self-help: prohibited (eviction is a judicial act through the rental division). Absentee exposure: non-resident landlords need local counsel with PoA to run the rental-division case; document the written lease and arrears. Adverse possession: against **registered title — not available**; registered Kuwaiti real-property title is protected — possession does not transfer it; unregistered-land edge cases **data not publicly available — verify with Kuwaiti counsel**. Seasonal moratorium: none (the 2020 Law 15 amendments were a COVID caseload-management measure, not a seasonal eviction ban). **Confidence**: MEDIUM (Law 35/1978 + single-judge rental division + 3-day grace primary-attested across firms; timeline and adverse-possession period not published — honestly flagged).

- **JO Jordan** — Risk types: **non-paying / holdover tenant** dominant; foreign ownership permitted with approval, but absentee exposure is mostly leasehold; stranger-squatting on titled land uncommon. Timeline: not published as a fixed number — court-driven, treat as `est.` — **data not publicly available — verify with Jordanian counsel**. Fast-track: no separate administrative committee analogous to the GCC; the route is the ordinary competent court — slower posture than UAE/SA/OM. Self-help: prohibited (eviction is a judicial order enforced by authorities). Absentee exposure: higher relative to GCC committee jurisdictions because the route is full court litigation — non-resident landlords need local counsel with PoA and documented notice/arrears. - **LB Lebanon** — Risk types: **non-paying / holdover tenant** dominant, *severely aggravated by active economic and legal crisis*; a large legacy stock of **old controlled-rent (pre-23 July 1992) tenancies** where the occupier pays a near-worthless legacy rent and is functionally entrenched — the closest thing in this set to a structural squatting problem, and the one most likely to ambush a foreign buyer of an older building. Contracts signed **after 23 July 1992** are freely-agreed and treated neutrally by the law. Eviction process: a post-1992 tenant **may be evicted for non-payment after 2 months' default, following legal notice**; but the courts are **slow — commonly ~2 years to evict a non-paying tenant** (globalpropertyguide.com — ⚠️ aggregator; lcps-lebanon.org policy monitor). The **old controlled-rent regime** has been the subject of a destabilising legislative saga: a Non-Residential Rent Law was adopted Dec 2023, withdrawn, re-published 3 Apr 2025, **struck down by the Constitutional Council 20 May 2025**, re-passed by Parliament **12 June 2025**, and approved by the Constitutional Council **22 July 2025**, with the rent-liberalisation timeline extended to **5-8 years** (klfi.net; today.lorientlejour.com; ministryinfo.gov.lb — press + firm; the dates ping-pong by source so treat each as `est.` and re-verify). Fast-track: none functioning reliably — courts are slow and the legal framework itself is in flux; **no dependable expedited route exists in 2025-26**. Self-help: prohibited in law (eviction is judicial), but enforcement institutions are weak amid the crisis. Absentee exposure: **highest in this set** — a foreign buyer of an older Lebanese building must specifically due-diligence whether any unit carries a pre-1992 controlled tenancy (massively value-impairing and slow to clear) and must price in the slow, unstable judicial route; do not rely on the new-law timeline until it has actually run. Adverse possession: registered-land title under the Lebanese Land Registry is, as a rule, not defeated by possession; but with registry/court institutional stress, treat as **data not publicly available — verify with Lebanese counsel / Land Registry** — do not assert immunity as confidently as for the GCC/IL Torrens systems. Seasonal moratorium: none (the issue is systemic court paralysis and legislative flux, not a calendar ban). **Confidence**: LOW (active crisis; rent-law status changed multiple times in 2025 and dates differ by source; eviction timelines aggregator-sourced; verify everything with current Lebanese counsel before relying).

### Confidence

Self-help prohibition and the absence of any EU-style seasonal/winter eviction moratorium are uniform across all 10 countries — the highest-confidence cross-cutting findings in this batch. HIGH: TR (statute-anchored TMK Art. 713 + TCO Art. 352 undertaking; timelines aggregator-sourced but consistent across 4 firms) and AE (Dubai statute + RDSC procedure well-documented; Abu Dhabi/other-emirate detail MEDIUM); IL HIGH on §18 30-day rule + expedited *mesira rechusha* track. MEDIUM: SA, QA, OM, BH, KW, JO — substantive grounds/committee jurisdiction primary-sourced, but published fixed eviction timelines do not exist for QA/KW/BH/JO (all `data not publicly available — verify`; do not invent a number) and exact prescription periods for *unregistered/unsettled* parcels were not cleanly citable in English (flagged `verify with local counsel`, not asserted). Adverse-possession immunity against registered title is a structural inference from the Torrens/parcel-registration model — robust as a directional statement for AE/IL/SA/QA/OM/BH/KW/JO. LOW: LB — every datapoint crisis-degraded; the 2023-2025 rent-law saga dates differ by source (each `est.`); the reliable takeaway is "slow, unstable, value-impairing for old-stock buildings", not any specific date.

## APAC + Central Asia + South Asia

### Regional pattern

An absentee/foreign owner discovers an occupied unit (overstaying ex-tenant, informal land occupier, or adverse-possessor accruing time) and finds clearing it slow, costly, or — worst case — finds title eroding. The recurring theme: **India's 12-year adverse possession is the single most notorious NRI absentee trap on the planet** (with s.27 extinguishing the owner's *right*, not just the remedy), with LK Sri Lanka (10-yr Prescription Ordinance) and HK Hong Kong (12-yr private land) the secondary headline traps — these three should drive the `--squatter` verdict-band for absentee/NRI/diaspora buyers. JP/KR/SG/MY are slow-but-title-safe (court-only eviction strongly protects an in-place occupier but title is not at risk); SG and MY are the two clean no-title-risk jurisdictions (SG abolished adverse possession for registered land 1 Mar 1994, MY never recognised it under Torrens). VN/CN are State-regime jurisdictions (no private adverse possession but State-recovery risk). No jurisdiction in this region has a seasonal eviction moratorium of the European/COVID type as a standing rule.

### Country one-liners

- **IN India** — Risk: (a) overstaying tenant under rent-control regimes + (b) informal occupant + (c) **adverse possession — the classic NRI absentee disaster**. Eviction: ejectment / suit for possession in civil court; rent-controlled tenancies (state Rent Control Acts) are extremely slow — multi-year is normal, appeals stretch it further; a tenant whose entry was lawful **cannot** claim adverse possession (Bajaj Finserv; nrilegalservices.com). Adverse possession: **12 years for private land (Limitation Act 1963, Schedule Art. 65), 30 years against government (Art. 112); s.27 is the unusual provision that extinguishes BOTH the remedy AND the owner's *right* to the property** (not merely the right to sue) — open, continuous, hostile, exclusive possession ripens into title; the owner's clock resets only on a successful interruption (legal notice/entry/suit) (primary: Limitation Act 1963, indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1565/5/A1963-36.pdf — Art. 65 & s.27; corroborated bajajfinserv.in, crypticproperty.com). Fast-track: none uniform; some states have summary eviction for specific tenancy classes — verify per state Rent Control Act. Self-help: barred — possession must be recovered through court; forcible re-entry exposes owner to criminal/civil liability. Absentee: **VERY HIGH** — empty NRI flats and ancestral/agricultural land left with a "caretaker" are the textbook adverse-possession loss; mitigations: registered caretaker agreement (acknowledges owner's title, defeats hostility), periodic documented inspections, up-to-date mutation/property-tax records in owner's name, periodic legal notice. Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on the 12yr/30yr Limitation Act figures and self-help bar; MEDIUM on per-state eviction timelines (vary widely by state Rent Control Act).

- **JP Japan** — Risk: overstaying non-paying tenant (the dominant risk; squatting/informal land occupation is rare). Eviction: court-only via building-surrender suit (建物明渡訴訟 / 明渡訴訟); the tenant is strongly protected by the **Act on Land and Building Leases (借地借家法 / Shakuchi Shakuya Hō)** plus the Civil Code — landlord must prove "just cause" (正当事由) and follow strict procedure; non-payment is a serious enough breach to justify termination. Timeline: from arrears to vacated possession **~4–6 months** typically — certified demand → suit → uncontested judgment in ~60–90 days → enforcement (forced surrender) a further ~30–50 days (legaladvicejapan.com; PLAZA HOMES). Fast-track: none specific; rent-guarantee companies (家賃保証会社) drive the process in practice but the court track is the same. Self-help: prohibited — no lock-out / utility cut-off / removal of belongings; landlord doing so faces liability. Absentee: MODERATE — strong tenant protection means a non-paying occupier stays for months even with a clean case, but title is **not** at risk on residential tenancies; engage a 管理会社 (management company) for absentee monitoring. Adverse possession: 取得時効 (acquisitive prescription) — **20 years (Civil Code Art. 162) generally, reduced to 10 years only if the possessor began in good faith and without negligence**; a lawful tenant's possession is not hostile (en.wikibooks.org Japanese Property Law; verify Civil Code Art. 162 at japaneselawtranslation.go.jp). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on 借地借家法 protection + 10/20yr prescription; MEDIUM on the 4–6mo aggregate (practitioner estimate, varies with contest/court load).

- **PH Philippines** — Risk: (a) informal settler families (ISFs) + (b) professional squatters/syndicates + (c) overstaying occupant. Eviction: **summary procedure under Rule 70, Rules of Court** — *forcible entry* (entry by force/stealth) or *unlawful detainer* (lawful entry, possession withheld after demand); must be filed within 1 year. The **criminal Anti-Squatting Law was PD 772, repealed by RA 8368 (1997)** — squatting per se is no longer a crime against most occupants (dhsud.gov.ph RA 8368; chanrobles.com; housinginteractive.com.ph). RA 7279 (UDHA 1992, "Lina Law", as amended by RA 10884) is **not** repealed and is the regime that *protects* informal settlers / regulates eviction of ISFs: ≥30-day notice, LGU consultation, humane relocation/financial assistance before demolition — making clearance slow and politically fraught. Fast-track: **professional squatters and squatting syndicates have NO relocation rights and can be evicted on court order after a short (≈5-day) notice** (E.O. 129); proving an occupant is a "professional squatter" is the key accelerator. Self-help: barred — eviction requires a court order and sheriff execution; LGU/NHA handle ISF relocation within ~45 days of final judgment. Absentee: **HIGH** — vacant land or untended provincial lots attract ISF settlement; absentee foreign owners (who in any case cannot own land — only condos / long lease) face slow ejectment plus relocation politics. Adverse possession: yes — Civil Code acquisitive prescription (ordinary 10 yr with just title + good faith; extraordinary 30 yr without) applies to **unregistered** land; Torrens-registered land is generally protected (verify Civil Code Arts. 1117–1138 at lawphil.net). Moratorium: none statutory; demolitions sometimes suspended around weather events / by LGU policy. **Confidence**: HIGH on RA 7279 / PD 772-repeal / Rule 70; MEDIUM on prescription nuances for registered vs unregistered land.

- **TH Thailand** — Risk: (a) overstaying tenant + (b) informal land squatting + (c) adverse possession against **titled** land. Eviction: civil suit for eviction; owner should immediately report unauthorised occupiers to police and/or file an eviction complaint with the court; courts will enforce eviction for non-payment but may allow staged repayment of arrears (Bangkok Post; thailandlawonline.com CCC s.537–571). Timeline: data not publicly available as a single national figure — verify with a Thai litigation firm; contested possession suits commonly run 1+ year. Fast-track: none specific. Self-help: not advisable — repossession should go through police complaint / court; foreign owners especially should avoid self-help given limited standing on land. Absentee: HIGH for **land** (foreigners generally cannot own land — Land Code s.86 — so exposure is via long lease / condo / structures), MODERATE for condos. **Adverse possession: 10 years of peaceful, open, continuous possession with intent to own (Civil & Commercial Code s.1382) — CRITICAL caveat: this applies only to fully titled land (Chanote/Nor Sor 4 Jor)**; possession of lesser/un-deeded land does not ripen the same way, and the 10-yr clock and a court judgment are required to transfer title (Tilleke & Gibbins; library.siam-legal.com CCC s.1367–1386). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on the s.1382 10-yr rule + title-deed caveat + foreign land bar; LOW on a national eviction-timeline figure (not published).

- **HK Hong Kong** — Risk: (a) overstaying tenant + (b) adverse possession of land/lots (a live, litigated issue in HK, esp. NT village land and absentee-owner lots). Eviction: landlords apply for possession to the **Lands Tribunal** (under the Landlord and Tenant (Consolidation) Ordinance Cap. 7) or the courts at common law (judiciary.hk; elegislation.gov.hk Cap. 7). Timeline: data not publicly available as a single figure — verify with a HK solicitor; uncontested possession is relatively orderly, contested adverse-possession defences run long. Fast-track: none specific beyond the Lands Tribunal's streamlined jurisdiction. Self-help: not advisable; possession via court/Tribunal order. Absentee: **HIGH for vacant lots/land** — adverse possession is a documented HK land-grab risk against absentee owners, particularly New Territories land. Adverse possession (Limitation Ordinance Cap. 347): **12 years vs a private owner where possession began on/after 1 Jul 1991; 20 years where it began before 1 Jul 1991; 60 years against Government land**; on expiry the paper owner's title is extinguished and the burden of proof is on the possessor (info.gov.hk LCQ7 2024; devb.gov.hk; hkreform.gov.hk). A 2013 Law Reform Commission report exists; reform proposed but **no abolition enacted as of May 2026 — verify current status at hkreform.gov.hk**. Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on the 12/20/60yr Limitation Ordinance structure; MEDIUM that no reform has yet changed it (re-verify).

- **KR South Korea** — Risk: overstaying non-paying tenant (dominant); informal squatting rare. Eviction: court-only eviction suit (명도소송 / myeongdo-sosong); the **Housing Lease Protection Act (주택임대차보호법)** strongly protects residential tenants — guaranteed 2-year term + one renewal request for a further 2 years, and **a landlord may not self-evict without a court ruling even after the term ends** (elaw.klri.re.kr Housing Lease Protection Act; globalpropertyguide.com). Jeonse/deposit structures complicate recovery (deposit priority on registration). Timeline: data not publicly available as a single figure — verify with a Korean firm; contested 명도소송 commonly runs many months to ~1 yr+. Fast-track: none specific. Self-help: prohibited — court ruling + official enforcement required. Absentee: MODERATE — tenant protection slows recovery but title is not at risk on a residential lease; engage a property manager for absentee oversight; jeonse deposit return is the bigger absentee cash-flow risk. Adverse possession: 점유취득시효 (acquisitive prescription) — Civil Act **Art. 245** has two limbs: (1) **20 years** of peaceful open possession with intent to own → acquire ownership *by registration*; (2) Art. 245(2) — a registered possessor who has possessed for **10 years in good faith and without negligence** acquires ownership (the two-limb structure is well-corroborated by secondary legal sources but the precise statutory wording must be re-verified at elaw.klri.re.kr (Civil Act, Art. 245) — the KLRI English statute page is JS-rendered and did not expose the exact Art. 245 string in this pass). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on Housing Lease Protection Act + court-only eviction; MEDIUM on the 20/10yr prescription cite (Civil Act Art. 245 — re-verify exact wording).

- **SG Singapore** — Risk: overstaying tenant (dominant); squatting/adverse possession effectively eliminated. Eviction: written termination notice → apply to the State Courts for a possession order + **Writ of Possession**; Sheriff/Bailiff executes on the Notice of Eviction date. Timeline: **typically ~2–6 months total; court proceedings ~1–2 months** when uncontested (judiciary.gov.sg; singaporelegaladvice.com). For non-payment of rent, **s.18A Conveyancing and Law of Property Act gives the tenant 4 weeks to pay all arrears** (relief against forfeiture). Fast-track: the writ-of-possession route is itself relatively quick for uncontested cases; no separate expedited track. Self-help: prohibited — Sheriff/Bailiff executes possession; landlord lock-outs are unlawful. Absentee: **LOW–MODERATE** — orderly, fast, title-safe; main absentee risk is the months of process for a contesting tenant, not loss of title. **Adverse possession: ABOLISHED for registered land by the Land Titles Act 1993 (in force 1 Mar 1994)** — only possessory claims fully accrued (12 yrs) before 1 Mar 1994 are preserved (s.177(3) LTA); post-1994 squatting cannot ripen into title (sso.agc.gov.sg LTA 1993; singaporelawwatch.sg Ch.29). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH across the board (statutory + judiciary primary sources).

- **TW Taiwan** — Risk: overstaying tenant + adverse possession of **unregistered** land. Eviction: civil suit for return of possession (Civil Code / Code of Civil Procedure); statutory tenant protections apply (e.g. the Land Act / 土地法 rent provisions, and the 租賃住宅市場發展及管理條例 residential-rental act). Timeline: data not publicly available as a single figure — verify with a Taiwanese firm. Fast-track: none specific. Self-help: not advisable; court order required. Absentee: MODERATE — registered land is well protected (registration is conclusive — Land Act Art. 43; Civil Code Art. 758), so adverse possession bites mainly **unregistered** estates / superficies. **Adverse possession: Civil Code Arts. 769–770 — 20 years (or 10 years if possession began in good faith and without negligence) of peaceful continuous possession of *unregistered* land → may apply to be registered as owner** (Articles 769/770 expressly do NOT apply to registered estates) (law.moj.gov.tw Civil Code; Constitutional Court Interpretation No. 107; lexology.com). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on the registered-vs-unregistered split + Arts. 769/770; MEDIUM on the 20/10yr split (re-verify wording) and LOW on eviction-timeline.

- **MY Malaysia** — Risk: (a) overstaying tenant + (b) squatting on state/private land. **Adverse possession is NOT recognised — Torrens system; National Land Code s.341 protects the registered proprietor against any squatter claim however long the occupation** (fareezlaw.com; thestar.com.my; koochinnam.com). Unlawful occupation of State land is itself a statutory offence (NLC s.425 — fine ≤ RM10,000 and/or ≤1 yr). Eviction: two routes — (1) writ of summons for trespass + eviction, or (2) **Order 89 Rules of Court 2012 summary originating-summons proceedings to recover possession**; after a possession order the owner files a writ of possession **within 3 months** (e-perundangan.pkns.gov.my; mahwengkwai.com). The Emergency (Clearance of Squatters) Regulations 1969 permit local-authority forced clearance in some cases. Fast-track: **Order 89 summary proceedings IS the fast-track for clear-cut trespass/no-tenancy occupiers**. Self-help: not advisable — use Order 89 / writ; do not self-evict. Absentee: **LOW on title risk** (no adverse possession), MODERATE on nuisance (squatting on absentee lots still requires a court process to clear). Foreign buyers: state-level minimum-price thresholds and consent apply — verify per state. Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on the no-adverse-possession / NLC s.341 / Order 89 framework.

- **ID Indonesia** — Risk: (a) land grabbing / "land mafia" syndicates (penyerobotan tanah) + (b) overstaying occupant + (c) overlapping-certificate disputes. Eviction: civil land-dispute litigation; **enforcement is the core problem — physical repossession after a court win is routinely resisted, with BPN (National Land Agency) record-correction delays and social/political friction** (kusumalawfirm.com; medium.com/@neighbours.agency). Multiple certificates over the same plot (weak BPN/LGU coordination + outdated mapping) force lengthy litigation. Adverse possession: Indonesia's UUPA/registration system does **not** grant title by mere long occupation in the common-law sense; rights flow from registered titles (Hak Milik / HGB / Hak Pakai) — but long undisputed occupation can feed competing-claim litigation in practice. Fast-track: none reliable; criminal complaint for penyerobotan possible where forgery/fraud present. Self-help: not advisable. Absentee: **HIGH** — foreigners cannot own land (only Hak Pakai / strata / leasehold via PT PMA); absentee/nominee structures plus weak enforcement make a contested occupier slow and costly to remove. Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: MEDIUM — strong on the enforcement-weakness and foreign-ownership facts; the precise statutory mechanics of long-occupation claims are not cleanly documented in English — verify with an Indonesian land lawyer / BPN.

- **VN Vietnam** — Risk: overstaying occupant / occupier of a land-use-right parcel; **no private land ownership exists — land is "owned by the entire people", State-administered; users hold Land Use Rights (LUR) with a LURC certificate** (foreigners 50-yr LURC; locals indefinite) (vietnam-briefing.com; Land Law 2024 No. 31/2024/QH15). Eviction: civil/administrative process to recover possession of the LUR parcel; the dominant repossession risk in Vietnam is the **State** reclaiming land for "socio-economic development / defence", not private adverse possession. Adverse possession: the common-law concept does not apply — title cannot accrue by occupation against the State land regime; disputes are over LUR allocation/recognition. Fast-track: none specific. Self-help: not advisable. Absentee: **MODERATE-HIGH** — process opacity + State-recovery powers mean an absentee LUR holder can face both slow occupier-removal and administrative reclamation; English-language primary law on private-occupier eviction timelines is sparse. Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: MEDIUM — high on the no-private-ownership / LUR structure; LOW on private-occupier eviction mechanics/timelines (not publicly documented in English — verify with a Vietnamese firm).

- **CN China** — Risk: overstaying tenant; informal occupation. **No private land ownership — urban land is State-owned, rural land collectively owned; buyers hold land-use rights / building ownership** (land-use term typically 70 yrs residential — verify current renewal rules). Eviction: civil suit; the Civil Code (2021) governs leases and possession claims. Adverse possession: **China does NOT recognise common-law adverse possession** — there is no acquisitive-prescription route to land title; the Civil Code's acquisitive-prescription provisions do not transfer real-property title by occupation (verify Civil Code Book II at npc.gov.cn — data not publicly available in a single English primary cite, verify with a PRC firm). Fast-track: none specific; some courts offer simplified procedure for clear cases. Self-help: not advisable — court enforcement required. Absentee: **MODERATE** — title not erodible by occupation, but court enforcement of possession against a resisting occupier can be slow and locally variable; foreign individuals face residence/eligibility restrictions on purchase (verify per city). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: MEDIUM — high on "no private land ownership / no adverse possession"; LOW on eviction-timeline specifics (verify with a PRC firm — not searched in depth this pass).

- **LK Sri Lanka** — Risk: (a) overstaying tenant + (b) **prescriptive possession (the local adverse-possession analogue) — a real absentee-owner risk**. Eviction: declaration-of-title / ejectment action in civil court; rent-controlled premises (Rent Act) slow the process. **Prescription: 10 years of undisturbed, uninterrupted possession by a title adverse to / independent of the owner extinguishes the owner's title and vests it in the possessor — Prescription Ordinance (Cap. 81), Ord. No. 22 of 1871, s.3** (commonlii.org Cap.81; srilankalaw.lk; lawnet.gov.lk). A tenant's possession is derivative/not adverse (does not ripen against the landlord); an unsuccessful action against the possessor does **not** interrupt the running of prescription. Fast-track: none specific. Self-help: not advisable — title declaration via court. Absentee: **HIGH** — empty/ancestral land with no documented possession is the textbook 10-yr prescription loss for diaspora owners; mitigations mirror India (caretaker agreement acknowledging owner's title, documented periodic possession, tax records in owner's name, timely action to interrupt). Moratorium: none. **Confidence**: HIGH on the 10-yr Prescription Ordinance rule + tenant-not-adverse + no-interruption-by-failed-action; LOW on eviction-timeline specifics.

- **PK Pakistan** *(mixed common-law + Islamic-law jurisdiction; the **land-record layer is provincial — there is no national cadastre**, records sit with each province's Board of Revenue + ICT, Punjab the most digitised (PLRA); title is a **Record of Rights** — the mutation/*intiqal* entry in the *jamabandi*/*fard* + a registered deed under the Registration Act 1908 — which is **presumptive, NOT a Torrens guaranteed title**; the practical foreign-buyer market is the **diaspora** (NRPs / POC holders via the SBP Roshan Digital Account → Roshan Apna Ghar), with non-origin foreigners needing Ministry-of-Interior approval under the 1980 Order; final court for civil/property = Supreme Court of Pakistan.)* Risk: the structural exposure is **title-dispute / contested-possession risk rather than a clean codified squatter regime** — because the Record of Rights is *presumptive not conclusive*, a foreign/diaspora owner of an unwatched plot faces mutation-record manipulation, overlapping claims and informal occupation, and there is **no single authoritative parcel register to settle it the way a Torrens register would** (per the PK playbook). Eviction / process / realistic duration: **`data not publicly available — verify`** — the eviction route and any timeline were **not captured at a primary source this pass; verify with a Pakistani property attorney / the relevant provincial Board of Revenue / [supremecourt.gov.pk](https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk/)**. Fast-track: none identified — verify locally. Self-help: not advisable; recover possession through the courts. Absentee exposure: **HIGH for the diaspora profile by construction** — NRP/POC owners are by definition absentee and the presumptive-record system rewards continuous documented possession; the practical controls are an up-to-date mutation/*fard* in the owner's name, a registered caretaker arrangement, and periodic documented inspection (the same discipline the IN/LK entries prescribe). Adverse possession / limitation period: Pakistan shares the colonial-era **Limitation Act** lineage with pre-partition India (where 12-yr adverse possession of private land is the rule under the Limitation Act 1963), but the **exact Pakistani statutory period and instrument are NOT captured at a primary source this pass — `data not publicly available — verify` with the Limitation Act 1908 (Pakistan) / a local attorney**; do **not** import the Indian 12-yr figure as a confirmed PK number. Moratorium: none documented. Sources: PK playbook (primary-sourced: PLRA punjab-zameen.gov.pk + PLRA Act 2017; Registration Act 1908 framing; Foreigners Act 1946 / 1980 Order at fia.gov.pk; SBP RDA / Roshan Apna Ghar; Supreme Court of Pakistan). **Confidence**: MEDIUM on the presumptive-record / no-national-cadastre / diaspora-absentee framing (playbook-grounded); LOW on the adverse-possession period + eviction timeline (genuinely not captured for PK — flagged, not slot-filled).

- **KH Cambodia** — data not publicly available — verify Cambodia 2001 Land Law adverse-possession / informal-settlement regime with primary source (Ministry of Land Management). **Confidence**: LOW.

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

Self-help eviction is illegal everywhere below (criminal liability for the owner) unless explicitly noted. Adverse possession ("usucapião"/"usucapión"/"prescripción adquisitiva"/"adverse possession") is the dominant LATAM absentee-owner trap — vacant land and unwatched 2nd homes are the prime targets; a documented caretaker plus dated possession evidence (utility bills, photos, ronda/vigilancia) is the only practical mitigant. Most jurisdictions offer an expedited route only for *fresh* intrusions (BR's 1-yr-and-1-day possessory rite; CO's ≤30-day police *lanzamiento*; criminal *usurpación/despojo/invasión* with anticipated restitution; CL's Ley 21.461 monitorio) — miss the short window and it reverts to a slow ordinary civil suit. The single most powerful Caribbean/Torrens mitigant is registered title: in DR there is **no acquisitive prescription against a Torrens-registered (saneado) parcel** regardless of occupation length. Pandemic-era eviction moratoria across the region have expired; none current as of 2026-05-15 — if a country reintroduces one, treat as a Tier-1/2 regulatory-watch trigger.

## Africa + Anglo non-EU

### Regional pattern

In the US, Canada, and Australia eviction procedure, the 2024-25 squatter reforms, and the adverse-possession period are state/province-level — exemplars are named (FL/GA/NY; NSW/Vic/SA; Ontario), not generalised; the 2024-25 US fast-track laws (FL HB 621, GA Squatter Reform Act, NY FY2025 budget) target scam/holdover-removal speed, NOT the centuries-old adverse-possession doctrine (which they were drafted not to alter). In civil-law Africa (MA/TN/EG/CV/MU/SC) the operative concept is acquisitive prescription / usucaption / *usucapião* / *hiyaza*, not common-law "adverse possession", and there is generally a registered-land carve-out (Torrens-style *titre foncier* / land registration defeats prescriptive claims). Self-help eviction is unlawful everywhere here — the ZA position is the sharpest: under the PIE Act 19/1998 + constitutional s26(3), any removal of an occupier from a *home* without a court order is unlawful and exposes the owner to a spoliation order and contempt, even where the occupier never had any right. The headline absentee exposure is vacant/undeveloped land left unmonitored (ZA, NG, KE, GH, the Caribbean-style Anglo jurisdictions) where the 12-30-year prescription clock resets only if the owner sues, retakes possession, or formally acts before expiry.

### Country one-liners

- **ZA South Africa** — Risk: unlawful occupier of a built dwelling **and** vacant-land/2nd-home invasion — the highest-profile absentee-owner exposure in this group; informal-settlement formation on under-watched land. Eviction: governed by the **Prevention of Illegal Eviction from and Unlawful Occupation of Land Act 19 of 1998 (PIE Act)** — a court order is **mandatory** (Constitution s26(3): no eviction from a home without a court order made after considering all relevant circumstances); court applies a **"just and equitable"** test, must serve ≥14 days' written notice on both the occupier *and* the municipality, and PIE **expressly excludes** the common-law *rei vindicatio* / spoliation self-help remedies for vindicating ownership; timeline is slow (months to well over a year when occupiers contest and the municipality must report on alternative accommodation). Fast-track: none as a statutory expedited track; an **urgent application under PIE s5** is the closest, but courts apply it narrowly — the applicant must show (a) a **real and imminent danger of substantial injury/damage** to a person or property if the occupier is not evicted forthwith, (b) the **hardship balance** favours urgent relief, and (c) **no other effective remedy** is available in time; an absentee owner can bring it via a mandatary / power of attorney, but must assemble a tight evidence pack (occupation/damage photos, security reports, managing-agent affidavits) — exactly what a remote owner is least able to produce quickly, so s5 is not a reliable absentee escape hatch. Self-help: absolute prohibition — any removal of an occupier from a *home* without a court order is unlawful and exposes the owner to a spoliation order and **contempt**, even where the occupier never had any right; this is *the* operative point for ZA absentee owners. - **US United States** — Risk: holdover tenant (former lawful tenant who won't leave — a contract/UD problem, *not* squatting) **and** true squatter (entered with no colour of right); out-of-state and foreign owners of vacant units are explicitly targeted in the 2023-24 squatting-scam wave. ## 7 universal mitigations

1. **Never self-help** — changing locks / cutting utilities / removing belongings makes the owner the liable party; use the statutory eviction route from day one.
2. **Treat an empty property as a monitored asset** — appoint a local caretaker / property manager (cross-ref `--property-management`), schedule physical inspections, and keep documentary evidence of possession (the antidote to adverse-possession claims).
3. **Buy anti-occupation / legal-expenses insurance where the market exists** (ES anti-okupa policies, UK/EU legal-expenses cover) and confirm it covers eviction legal costs, not just damage.
4. **Verify the eviction timeline before the offer, not after** — in slow jurisdictions (ES inquiokupa, IT sfratto, IN civil suit) the carrying cost of a months-to-years eviction is a material line in the decision.
5. **Fence, register, and physically mark unattended land** — adverse possession turns on un-interrupted hostile possession; a registered title, fencing, and periodic re-entry reset or defeat the clock.
6. **Confirm whether the parcel is registered (Torrens / Land Titles)** — registered title is largely immune to adverse possession; old-system/unregistered land is the exposed case and changes the diligence weight entirely.
7. **For tenanted purchases, confirm the occupant's legal status in writing pre-completion** — a "sitting" non-paying tenant or protected legacy tenant (CY/MT/LB) can be functionally equivalent to a squatter and must be priced or excluded before the deed.
