# Universal `--property-management` Section — Owner-Hires-Manager · Commission · Licensing · Tax-Collector Role

Foreign-**owner** property-management layer: once you own the property and live abroad, who manages it, on what commission, under what licensing regime, and — critically — whether the manager becomes a statutory tax collector who withholds on your gross rent. Covers the long-let vs short-let manager distinction, commission bands, the property-manager licensing/registration regime (or its absence), owner-portal/reporting norms, the manager's non-resident-owner tax-collector role, and the recurring opacity/bypass red-flags. Distinct from `--rental` (income, yield, regulation, rental regime — the *asset's* economics) and `--agent` (buyer-side agency at *purchase*). This section is about the *ongoing operate-from-abroad relationship*.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *owner-hires-a-manager* layer — the commission structure, the licensing/registration regime the foreign owner verifies before signing a mandate, the manager's role as a non-resident-owner withholding agent, client-money protection, and the opacity patterns to guard against. It does **not** restate rental yield/regime (`--rental`) or buyer-side purchase agency (`--agent`). A foreign owner typically needs four pieces of intel: (1) the all-in commission for long-let vs short-let, (2) whether "property manager" is a regulated/protected title here and how to verify the licence, (3) whether the manager must withhold tax on the owner's gross rent and what election avoids it, (4) whether client money is protected or commingled.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Long-let vs short-let manager distinction** — are these separate businesses/licences, and is short-let increasingly permit-gated (AE DET holiday-home operator permit, TR Law 7464 owner-only tourism-rental permit, EU municipal STR registration)?
2. **Commission bands** — long-let % of gross (+ tenant-find/leasing/renewal fees), short-let % of gross (+ cleaning/booking fees) or flat/hybrid. Date-stamped, source-tier tagged. Commission is never statutorily capped — tag `(market-norm, cross-check)` unless a regulator caps it; `data not publicly available` rather than estimate where unsourced.
3. **Licensing / regulatory regime for property managers** — the actual mandate, named with its statute (FR carte G Loi Hoguet n°70-9; ES administrador de fincas + Colegio; IT amministratore + L.220/2012; PT IMPIC; UK ARLA Propertymark + mandatory redress + Client Money Protection from 1 Apr 2019; IE PSRA; DE §34c GewO; MY BOVAEP Act 242; ZA PPRA Fidelity Fund Certificate; JP 賃貸住宅管理業 registration; US/CA/AU state/province broker licence). State explicitly where it is **deregulated/unlicensed** (PL since 2014; NZ residential PM — Bill discharged Jun 2024; much of CEE).
4. **Owner-portal / reporting norm** — is owner-statement reporting regulated (FR compte rendu de gestion) or market practice; named platforms in use.
5. **Tax-collector role** — does the manager withhold/remit non-resident-owner tax, and what election avoids gross withholding? (US 30% gross + 1042-S unless W-8ECI net election §871(d); CA 25% Part XIII unless NR6 + §216; UK NRL 20% unless approved; IE NLWT collection-agent; IT cedolare 21%/26% intermediary withholding; ES Modelo 210; PT Modelo 30; JP 20.42% non-resident withholding). Name the form/scheme.
6. **Red-flag patterns** — financial-breakdown opacity, manager-bypass (locals/tenants contacting the owner directly to cut commission), commingling where no client-money protection, short-let-permit non-compliance shifting the fine to the owner.
7. **Confidence**: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **The non-resident-owner withholding trap: the manager may be a statutory tax collector who withholds on GROSS rent.** US property managers withhold **30% of gross** + file Form 1042-S unless the owner files **W-8ECI** to elect net taxation under IRC § 871(d); Canada's resident agent withholds **25% Part XIII on gross** unless **Form NR6** + a § 216 return; the UK Non-Resident Landlord Scheme withholds 20% unless HMRC-approved; Ireland's NLWT and Italy's cedolare-secca intermediary withholding (21%/26%) are analogous. A foreign owner who does not file the election loses a quarter to a third of gross rent at source and must reclaim it. This is the highest-impact, least-known trap in the section.
2. **Commission is never statutorily capped — the headline % hides the all-in cost.** No jurisdiction caps property-management fees. The real cost is long-let % + tenant-find/leasing fee + renewal commission + maintenance markup, or short-let % + cleaning + booking + linen fees. Always compare all-in on a realistic occupancy, not the headline rate.
3. **Client-money protection is patchy — commingling is the structural risk where it is absent.** Mandatory client-money protection exists only in the UK and Ireland; France requires a statutory *garantie financière* (Loi Hoguet); Australia and South Africa run trust-account audit regimes. It is absent across Germany, Austria, Switzerland, the Nordics, the Netherlands, most of CEE and LATAM — there the owner's rent and tenant deposit sit in the manager's general account with no ring-fence.
4. **"Property manager" is a regulated, protected title in some markets and an unregulated free-for-all in others.** Licensed/protected: FR carte G, ES administrador de fincas, IE PSRA, MY BOVAEP (Act 242), ZA PPRA Fidelity Fund Certificate, US/CA/AU state-or-province broker licence. Deregulated/unlicensed: Poland since 2014, New Zealand residential PM (the Residential Property Managers Bill was discharged June 2024), much of CEE. Verify the licence against the named public register before signing a mandate — and where none exists, recourse is civil-claim only.
5. **Short-let management is increasingly licence-gated and the permit liability sits with the OWNER.** AE requires a DET holiday-home operator permit; Türkiye's Law 7464 tourism-rental permit can be applied for only by the owner, not the manager; EU municipalities increasingly require STR registration. A manager operating without the permit exposes the owner — not the manager — to the fine and the listing takedown.
6. **The condominium/building administrator is a different role from the foreign owner's letting manager.** Italy's *amministratore di condominio*, Spain's *administrador de fincas*, Belgium's *syndic* administer the common parts of the building on behalf of all co-owners — they are not the individual owner's single-unit letting agent. Conflating the two (and assuming the building administrator is "managing your flat") is a common and costly scoping error.
7. **Manager-bypass and reporting opacity are the recurring operate-from-abroad complaints.** Locals or tenants contacting the owner directly to cut the manager's commission (a documented Cyprus pattern); financial-breakdown opacity (reported for Madeira lettings and Bali villa management); owner-portal reporting is unregulated almost everywhere. These are 🟡 anecdotal but recurrent across owner forums — the mitigation is contractual (itemised monthly statements + audit rights), not regulatory.

## Regional patterns + country one-liners

The six regional blocks below give the commission band, licensing regime, tax-collector role and red-flag pattern per country, with anti-hallucination flags surfaced honestly. Commission bands are market-norm/aggregator-tier (no jurisdiction caps them); licensing claims are tied to a named statute or marked unlicensed/deregulated; manager-bypass/opacity patterns are tagged 🟡 anecdotal and never a verdict basis.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

A foreign owner living abroad hires a manager for a long-let (`gestion locative` / standing tenancy) or short-let (`conciergerie` / holiday-let) property; this is distinct from `--rental` (yield/regime) and `--agent` (buyer-side). No W1 jurisdiction statutorily caps property-management fees — what is regulated is *who may hold client money / arrange tenancies* (FR garantie financière, UK CMP, IE PSRA client account, IT/ES/PT/BE/MT licensing), so commission % is contractually free everywhere and always `market-norm, cross-check`. Only the UK and Ireland make the manager a statutory tax-collector for the non-resident owner's rental income (UK NRL 20% / IE collection-agent + NLWT 20%); Italy makes the *short-let intermediary* a cedolare-secca withholding agent; France/Spain/Portugal/Germany/Nordics/Benelux have the owner self-file. Client-money protection is patchy — mandatory & schemed in UK (CMP) and IE (PSRA compensation fund), statutory financial guarantee in FR and MC/régie, but no national CMP equivalent in DE, AT, CH, the Nordics, NL, GR, CY, or the microstates, where commingling is the structural red flag. The manager-bypass pattern (locals/tenants contacting the absentee owner directly to undercut commission, cash-in-hand) is reported most for CY and holiday-let markets generally — 🟡 anecdotal/forum-tier, never a verdict basis.

### Country one-liners

- **FR France** — Split: **gestion locative** (long-let: lease drafting, rent collection, repairs, *compte rendu de gestion*) vs **conciergerie** (short-let / saisonnier: check-in, cleaning, listing). Commission: long-let ~6–10% of gross rent + a separate tenant-find/`mise en location` fee historically ~0.75–1 month rent (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let conciergerie ~15–25% of gross stay revenue (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licence: a manager holding/handling funds needs the **carte professionnelle mention "Gestion immobilière" (carte G)** under **Loi Hoguet n°70-9 of 2 Jan 1970** (reinforced by Loi ALUR 2014) — requires a **garantie financière** (statutory minimum €110,000 per activity; €30,000 in first 2 years) + **RCP** (professional indemnity) + CCI-issued card — NOT the carte T (transactions). Reporting: the **compte rendu de gestion** is a *regulated* periodic account owed to the mandant. Tax role: FR has **no general agent-withholding scheme** for non-resident landlords — the non-resident files French income tax themselves; furnished lets fall under **LMNP/LMP** regimes; **prélèvements sociaux** apply to French-source rental income; the manager is not a statutory withholding agent (contrast UK NRL). Red flag: opaque `compte rendu` line items / commingled trust accounts where the garantie financière is absent (an unlicensed "conciergerie" handling rent without a carte G — common in holiday-let micro-operators). **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **IT Italy** — Split: long-let via a **property manager / agenzia** (the **amministratore di condominio** is a building-level co-ownership administrator — a distinct role, not the owner's letting manager); short-let via **property manager / gestione affitti brevi**. Commission: long-let ~8–12% gross + finder fee (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let ~18–25% gross or hybrid flat+% (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licensing: the **amministratore di condominio** is governed by **Legge n. 220/2012 art. 71-bis** — mandatory initial + ongoing periodic training (grandfather clause: ≥1 year administering in the 3 years pre-reform exempts the qualification but not the recurring-training duty); a short-let property manager who *mediates* contracts also touches **mediazione/agente immobiliare** rules (Legge 39/1989, CCIAA roll). Tax role: **YES — material.** Short-let cedolare secca is **21% for a single property** but **26% from 1 Jan 2024 where >1 property** is let (taxpayer may elect 21% on one); statute chain DL 50/2017 (intermediary withholding) + 2024 Budget Law rate change; Airbnb operationalised intermediary withholding/remittance for Italian-sourced ≤30-day stays in **2024** (Agenzia delle Entrate Circolare 10/E of 10 May 2024). Reporting: market platforms (Lodgify/Guesty/local PMS) — no statutory owner-account form analogous to FR. Red flag: intermediary failing to withhold/remit cedolare (liability flows to owner); CIN (Codice Identificativo Nazionale, 2024–25) non-compliance shifting fines to the owner. **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **ES Spain** — Split: long-let via **gestor/agencia de alquiler** (the **administrador de fincas** is primarily community/building administration — a distinct regulated profession, not the owner's letting manager); short-let via **gestión de alquiler turístico / conserjería**. Commission: long-let ~5–10% gross + ~1 month tenant-find (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let ~18–25% gross or hybrid (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licensing: the **administrador de fincas** is a collegiate profession (historically **Decreto 693/1968**, organised through the **Colegio Oficial de Administradores de Fincas**); ordinary letting agents/inmobiliarias are **not nationally licensed** (some autonomous communities — e.g. Catalonia's AICAT register — impose regional registration). Tax role: **NO statutory agent-withholding on rent**, but the non-resident owner must self-file **Modelo 210** (IRNR) — **for income accrued from 2024 the quarterly grouping option was removed**; income may be grouped annually or filed per accrual; EU/EEA (incl. IS/NO/LI) residents taxed at 19% with deductible expenses, non-EU at 24% without deductions; holiday lets may also trigger quarterly local indirect tax (e.g. Canary Islands IGIC Form 420). Red flag: manager not surfacing the Modelo 210 obligation (owner assumes it is withheld — it is not); regional short-let registration (e.g. Andalusia VFT, Catalonia HUT) non-compliance attributed to the owner; commingling — no statutory client-money protection scheme. **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **PT Portugal** — Split: long-let via **mediação imobiliária / gestão de arrendamento**; short-let via **gestão de Alojamento Local (AL)** operator. Commission: long-let ~8–12% gross + finder fee (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let ~15–25% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licensing: anyone carrying on **mediação imobiliária** (incl. arranging tenancies) needs an **IMPIC licence** (Instituto dos Mercados Públicos, do Imobiliário e da Construção) under the real-estate-mediation legal regime (Lei n.º 15/2013); pure caretaking/cleaning of an AL unit is not itself mediation. Tax role: non-resident owner's Portuguese rental income is **IRS category F** (long-let) or category B (AL business); a Portuguese payer/intermediary paying income to a non-resident files **Modelo 30** and withholding may apply (commissions to non-resident entities reported via Modelo 30; resident-payer withholding on professional income is 25%); practically the AL operator does not act as final tax-collector for the owner's rental profit — the owner files IRS (or via fiscal representative). Reporting: market PMS (Guesty/Lodgify/local); no statutory owner-account form. Red flag: unlicensed "gestão" entity arranging tenancies without IMPIC; AL registration (Registo Nacional de Alojamento Local) lapses / 2024 reforms compliance shifting liability to owner; no national client-money protection. **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **UK United Kingdom** — Split: long-let via **letting/managing agent** (full management vs let-only/tenant-find); short-let via **holiday-let management company / Sykes-type agency**. Commission: long-let full management ~10–15% of gross rent (inc VAT, region-dependent — London lower %, regions higher) + a separate tenant-find/set-up fee where let-only (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let ~15–25% of gross or hybrid (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licensing/regulation: no single national agent licence, but letting agents handling client money **must belong to a government-approved Client Money Protection (CMP) scheme — mandatory since 1 April 2019** (Client Money Protection Schemes for Property Agents (Approval and Designation of Schemes) Regulations 2018, but the duty commenced 1 Apr 2019) **AND** a redress scheme (The Property Ombudsman or Property Redress Scheme); ARLA Propertymark membership is *voluntary* (Scotland/Wales have separate letting-agent registration regimes). Tax role: **YES — the Non-Resident Landlord Scheme (since 1996).** A UK letting agent (or, absent an agent, the tenant) **must deduct 20% tax** from a non-resident landlord's rental income and remit to HMRC, *unless* the landlord has HMRC approval to receive rent gross (apply on **form NRL1**); default is 20% agent/tenant withholding. Reporting: agent issues annual NRL6 certificate; market PMS for short-let (Sykes/Hostfully). Red flag: agent handling client money without CMP membership (post-2019 illegality); failure to operate NRL deduction → HMRC liability lands on agent/owner; opaque "management + maintenance markup" stacking. **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **DE Germany** — Split: long-let via **Hausverwaltung / Mietverwaltung** (WEG/condominium administration is the related **WEG-Verwalter** role, distinct); short-let via **Ferienwohnung-Verwaltung / Agentur**. Commission: long-let Mietverwaltung commonly a per-unit monthly flat fee (~€20–35/unit/month) rather than % — or ~3–8% of cold rent for single units (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let ~18–25% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licensing: **§ 34c Gewerbeordnung (GewO) Gewerbeerlaubnis** is required for commercial residential-property management (Wohnimmobilienverwalter) — since the 2018 amendment a **proof-of-competence (Sachkundenachweis)** / IHK route applies, but there is no national licensing exam analogous to a bar; it is a trade permission. Tax role: **no agent-withholding scheme** — non-resident landlord files German income tax (beschränkte Steuerpflicht); the manager is not a statutory withholding agent for rent. Reporting: market norm; no statutory owner-account form. Red flag: unlicensed Verwalter operating without §34c permission; commingled client accounts (no UK-style CMP). **Confidence**: HIGH.

- **AT Austria** — Split: long-let via **Hausverwaltung / Immobilienverwalter**; short-let via **Ferienwohnungs-Agentur**. Licensing: **Immobilienverwalter** is a **regulated trade (reglementiertes Gewerbe)** under the **Gewerbeordnung 1994** requiring a Befähigungsnachweis (qualification proof) — Austria *does* gate the profession (more than DE's §34c). Commission: long-let Verwaltung often a per-unit flat (e.g. ~€4–8/m²/year of usable area or per-unit monthly) (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let ~18–25% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Tax role: non-resident files Austrian income tax; no general rent-withholding agent scheme. Reporting: market norm. Red flag: unlicensed operator; commingling — no national CMP scheme. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (commission bands inferred from regional norm — `est.`, cross-check).

- **CH Switzerland** — Split: long-let via **Liegenschaftsverwaltung / régie** (cantonal-language dependent); short-let via specialist holiday-let agency (notably **Interhome**, operating since 1965, full-management or listing-only). Commission: long-let Verwaltung ~4–6% of gross rental income (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025); short-let holiday-let agencies commonly **~20–40% of gross** (Interhome-type full service) or commission-only models (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025). Licensing: **no federal licence** for property managers — the profession is **self-regulated via SVIT** (Schweizerischer Verband der Immobilienwirtschaft) certification; short-let permits/registration are **cantonal/municipal** (vary widely; some resorts cap or register STR). Tax role: non-resident owners of Swiss property file via the cantonal system; **no nationwide agent rent-withholding scheme** for residents-abroad rental income (distinct from the wage Quellensteuer). Reporting: agency portals (Interhome owner portal); market norm. Red flag: cantonal STR-registration non-compliance attributed to owner; commission opacity in holiday-let "all-in" models; no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (federal vs cantonal split is HIGH; commission bands `est.` cross-check).

- **SE Sweden** — Split: long-let via **fastighetsförvaltning / hyresförvaltning**; short-let via holiday-let agency. Licensing: there is **no licence for property managers**; only the **fastighetsmäklare (estate agent)** is statutorily licensed and registered with **Fastighetsmäklarinspektionen (FMI)** under the Estate Agents Act (Fastighetsmäklarlagen) — *brokerage*, not letting management. Commission: long-let ~ flat or ~5–10% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~15–25% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`). Tax role: non-resident owner files Swedish income tax (SINK/ordinary); no agent rent-withholding scheme. Note: Sweden's rent for private dwellings must be "reasonable" (utility-value system) — constrains long-let pricing. Red flag: commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (regulatory split HIGH; commission `est.`).

- **FI Finland** — Split: long-let via **isännöitsijä / vuokranvälitys** (note: *isännöitsijä* = housing-company manager, distinct from the owner's letting agent); short-let via agency. Licensing: estate agents/letting brokerages need authorisation under the **Act on Real Estate Agencies and Rental Apartment Agencies (Laki kiinteistönvälitysliikkeistä ja vuokrahuoneiston välitysliikkeistä 1075/2000)** — rental-apartment brokerage *is* a registered activity (regional state administrative agency register); pure property management of a single owner's unit is lighter-touch. Commission: long-let ~ first-month-rent finder + management fee, or ~5–10% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~15–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: non-resident files Finnish tax; no general agent rent-withholding. Red flag: unregistered rental brokerage; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM.

- **NO Norway** — Split: long-let via **eiendomsforvaltning / utleiemegling**; short-let via holiday-let agency. Licensing: **estate agency (eiendomsmegling)** is licensed/supervised by **Finanstilsynet** under the Estate Agency Act (eiendomsmeglingsloven) — *brokerage*; standalone letting management is lighter-touch. Tax role: **rental agents have a third-party reporting duty** — the Norwegian Tax Administration (Skatteetaten) requires rental agents/intermediaries to submit third-party information on rental of real property (so the manager *reports* but does not generally *withhold* the non-resident owner's tax); commission charged to the owner must be explicitly stated per period or in aggregate. Commission: long-let ~5–10% gross (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~15–25% gross (`est.`). Reporting: regulated third-party data submission to Skatteetaten + market portals. Red flag: agent commission not separately disclosed (Skatteetaten guidance requires it); commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (reporting duty sourced HIGH; commission `est.`).

- **DK Denmark** — Split: long-let via **ejendomsadministration**; short-let via holiday-let agency (Sommerhus-udlejning bureaus dominate the coastal market). Licensing: **ejendomsmægler (estate agent)** is statutorily titled/registered (Lov om formidling af fast ejendom m.v.) — *brokerage*; letting administration is lighter-touch. Commission: long-let ejendomsadministration commonly per-unit flat fee (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); **summer-house letting bureaus typically retain a high share (~ 40–50% of gross)** for full marketing+booking+handover service (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`, well-known Danish sommerhus model). Tax role: non-resident files Danish tax; the summer-house bureau model has specific tax-deduction rules for owners (bureau-let bundfradrag) — bureau reports turnover; no general agent rent-withholding of the owner's net. Red flag: very high bureau commission vs private-let; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (sommerhus high-commission model is well-attested but %`est.`).

- **NL Netherlands** — Split: long-let via **verhuurmakelaar / vastgoedbeheer**; short-let via agency. Licensing: **unlicensed market** — "makelaar" is *not* a protected title; quality is via **voluntary trade bodies NVM (largest, ~4,400 members), VBO (~1,100), VastgoedPro** with their own training/conduct codes; municipal short-let rules (e.g. Amsterdam 30-night cap + registration) are strict and separate. Commission: long-let ~ first-month-rent finder + ~5–10% management (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~15–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: non-resident owner is in **Box 3** (deemed-return wealth tax on Dutch property, not actual rent for long-let) — no agent rent-withholding scheme; manager is not a tax-collector. Red flag: no statutory client-money protection (NVM members have a guarantee fund — non-members none); municipal STR-licence non-compliance attributed to owner. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (unlicensed-market + Box 3 HIGH; commission `est.`).

- **BE Belgium** — Split: long-let via **rentmeester / syndic-gestion locative** (the **syndic** administers co-ownership — a distinct role, not the owner's letting manager); short-let via agency. Licensing: the **vastgoedmakelaar / agent immobilier** is a **regulated profession via the BIV/IPI** (Beroepsinstituut van Vastgoedmakelaars / Institut professionnel des agents immobiliers) — *rentmeester* (property-management) and *bemiddelaar* (brokerage) columns are both IPI-regulated. Commission: long-let ~ first-month-rent finder + ~5–12% management (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~18–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: non-resident files Belgian non-resident income tax; no general agent rent-withholding. Red flag: operating rentmeester activity without IPI registration; commingling. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (IPI regulation HIGH; commission `est.`).

- **IE Ireland** — Split: long-let via **letting agent / property management agent**; short-let via holiday-let / STR management company. Licensing: **statutory licence required** — anyone providing property services (estate agent, letting agent, **management agent**) must hold a **PSRA licence** under the **Property Services (Regulation) Act 2011**; unlicensed provision is an offence (up to 5 years / unlimited fine); the PSRA mandates a **Client Account / Professional Indemnity Insurance / Compensation Fund** regime. Commission: long-let ~ first-month-rent finder + ~5–12% management (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~18–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: a **non-resident landlord with an Irish collection agent** — the collection agent is **accountable for the tax** (files an Irish return for the landlord); alternatively from 1 Jul 2023 a tenant paying a non-resident landlord directly must withhold 20% and remit via the **Non-Resident Landlord Withholding Tax (NLWT)** system (Revenue) — so the manager *can* be the tax-accountable party. Reporting: PSRA-regulated client-account standards. Red flag: unlicensed STR "management" entity; collection-agent not declaring; new STR register (Fáilte Ireland STRSS) non-compliance. **Confidence**: HIGH (PSRA + NLWT sourced; commission `est.`).

- **GR Greece** — Split: long-let via **diacheirisi akiniton (property management)**; short-let via Airbnb-management agency (large market — Athens/islands). Licensing: real-estate agents (mesitis) are registered (Law 4072/2012 / GEMI broker registry); standalone property management is lighter-touch. Commission: long-let ~8–12% gross + finder (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~18–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: non-resident files Greek tax (rental income taxed at 15/35/45% bands); short-let platform/registry (AMA short-let registry number) compliance; no general agent rent-withholding of owner net. Red flag: AMA short-let-number non-compliance shifting fines to owner; manager bypass (owner contacted directly to drop commission); commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-LOW (regulatory detail `est.`/cross-check).

- **LU Luxembourg** — Split: long-let via **gestion locative / régie**; short-let via agency. Licensing: real-estate agent/manager activity requires a **business permit (autorisation d'établissement)** from the Ministry of the Economy with a relevant **agent immobilier / administrateur de biens** qualification — it is gated. Commission: long-let ~ first-month finder + ~5% management (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~18–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: non-resident files Luxembourg non-resident income tax; no general agent rent-withholding. Red flag: operating without autorisation d'établissement; commingling. **Confidence**: LOW (commission + detail `est.`, thin public sourcing for management specifically).

- **CY Cyprus** — Split: long-let via **property management company** (large expat-landlord market — Paphos/Limassol); short-let via villa-management agency. Licensing: **estate agents are licensed** under the Estate Agents Law (Law 71(I)/2010) via the Cyprus Real Estate Agents Registration Council; standalone management is lighter-touch. Commission: long-let ~8–12% gross + finder (`market-norm, cross-check`, 2025 — `est.`); short-let ~18–25% gross (`est.`). Tax role: non-resident files Cyprus tax; no general agent rent-withholding. Red flag: **manager-bypass pattern is well-known here** — local handymen/contacts approach the absentee owner directly to undercut the agent's commission, and tenants pay cash to bypass reporting (🟡 anecdotal/forum-tier, flag, not a regulator finding); commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: LOW (estate-agent law HIGH; bypass pattern 🟡 anecdotal).

- **MT Malta** — Split: long-let via **letting/management agency**; short-let via holiday-let agency. ## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

A foreign owner living abroad hires a manager for a long-let or short-let unit; distinct from `--rental` (yield/regime) and `--agent` (buyer-side). No CEE/Balkan/Baltic/Caucasus state caps private property-management commission, so every commission band is a practitioner-norm inference flagged `est.` 🔴 `(market-norm, cross-check)` and must be replaced with 2+ live local-firm quotes before any verdict relies on it. Across W2 no country was confirmed to impose *automatic at-source withholding by a residential property manager* on an individual non-resident landlord — where withholding exists it is generally triggered when the **payer is a legal entity/enterprise** (BG, RS, AZ, possibly SK/LT/LV/CZ `srážková daň` to non-residents), so a management *company* acting as payer may inherit that obligation but the "Tax role" line stays "verify with <national tax authority>". Higher-confidence sourced elements include PL 2014 deregulation, CZ 39/2020 scope + Airbnb exclusion, HR 2025 2/3-co-owner STR consent, HU 2025–26 Budapest STR moratorium, and the various flat-rate/deduction PIT regimes; the 7 Western-Balkan/Caucasus sparse markets carry LOW confidence and explicit data-gap markers.

## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

A foreign owner living abroad (the default in GCC freehold zones) appoints a manager for a long-let or short-let unit; distinct from `--rental` (let economics) and `--agent` (sale-side broker). Region-wide structural fact: the GCC has no personal income tax on individuals as of 2025 — UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman all levy zero PIT on rental income for individuals ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/), 2025) — but municipal/tourism short-let levies still apply (UAE Tourism Dirham, etc.) and **Oman ends its zero-PIT status on 1 Jan 2028** (Royal Decree No. 56/2025, 5% PIT on annual income above OMR 42,000). Long-let PM commission bands are NOT published as regulated figures for TR, IL, QA, SA, JO, OM, BH, KW — only AE has aggregator-level published bands (themselves market-norm, not regulated); all short-let "~15-25%"/"~20-25%" figures outside AE are `est.` regional-norm extrapolations to be calibrated against the appointed operator's contract. No commission figure is presented as a regulated fact unless a regulator publishes it (none in this region do for PM commission); the "non-resident owner relies on word-of-mouth referral and gets burned" pattern is real and recurrent (esp. UAE) but anecdotal 🟡, used only to motivate verify-the-licence, never as a quantified claim.

### Country one-liners

- **AE United Arab Emirates (Dubai-weighted)** — Split is sharp and licence-gated: a long-let manager (RERA-licensed real-estate management) is a different regulated activity from a short-let **holiday-home operator** (DET permit per unit). Commission: long-let **~5-10% of annual rent**, with ~5% the volume norm and fixed annual packages for single units ([Grosvenor RE 2025 guide](https://grosvenor-re.com/understanding-property-management-fees-in-dubai-2025-guide/), aggregator ⚠️ — cross-check, market-norm not regulated); short-let **~15-25% of gross booking revenue managed** (15-20% commission-only, 12-15% hybrid + service fees) ([Luxury Lettings 2026](https://luxurylettings.ae/2026/01/02/dubai-holiday-home-management-fees/) / [Homevy](https://homevy.com/blog/short-term-rental-management-fees-dubai), aggregator ⚠️ market-norm). Licence/regulatory: Dubai RERA (under DLD) licenses property-management firms and mandates Ejari long-let contract registration; short-let governed by the **Dept of Economy & Tourism (DET, formerly DTCM** — Nov 2021 merger of DTCM with Dept of Economic Development; some portals still use `dtcm.gov.ae`**)** holiday-home permit — per-unit permit + operator licence, title deed/Ejari + NOC from owners-association/developer, AED 1,500 one-time application fee, mandatory smoke detectors/extinguishers; fines AED 5,000 → AED 100,000 for unpermitted operation ([DET Holiday-Homes Guide PDF](https://hhpermits.det.gov.ae/HolidayHomes/pdfs/Holiday-Homes-Guide.pdf), primary regulator ✅). Common-area governance: **Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 on Jointly Owned Property** (an **Emirate of Dubai law**, in force 18 Nov 2019, repealing Law 27/2007 — NOT a Federal Law; the standalone Owners Association legal entity was abolished, a developer must appoint a RERA-approved managing agent, owners sit on a non-legal Owners Committee (max 9, RERA-selected), disputes go to RDSC — the foreign owner does not control the building manager). **Abu Dhabi runs a parallel, emirate-specific regime under the Department of Culture & Tourism (DCT), not DET** — DCT Circular No. 8/2025 + the new Holiday Homes System (launched 2 Dec 2025) make a valid licence mandatory before listing on any platform from 1 Jan 2026; shared rooms/beds banned; platforms must remove unlicensed listings within 30 days. Reporting: Guesty/Hostaway dominate GCC short-let back-ends; owner statements a market norm, not a regulated disclosure. Tax role: **no personal income tax** on the owner's rental income (2025); individual real-estate investment income excluded from the AED-1m corporate-tax turnover test ([PwC UAE](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-arab-emirates/individual/income-determination), 2025); the manager DOES collect/remit the **Tourism Dirham** on short-let (AED ~10-15 per occupied bedroom per night, remitted monthly). Red flag: non-resident owners selecting a manager by word-of-mouth rather than RERA-licence verification — unlicensed firms can't enforce leases or do Ejari, and short-let-permit non-compliance shifts the AED 5k-100k fine to the owner (e.g. AED 18,000 unpaid rent via an unlicensed firm — 🟡 anecdotal, never a verdict basis). **Confidence**: HIGH (primary DET + RERA/DLD; commission bands aggregator-sourced, flagged).

- **TR Türkiye** — **No PM-specific licence exists.** Anyone brokering/managing as a business falls under the **Regulation on Immovable Property Commerce** (Official Gazette No. 30442, 5 Jun 2018) — the *emlak yetki belgesi* / authorisation certificate, requiring an MYK Level-5 (supervising) or Level-4 real-estate consultant competency certificate ([CTR / MYK](https://www.myk.gov.tr/), primary ✅) — that regulates *agency*, not property management as a distinct profession; there is no Turkish property-manager registry. Short-let is the load-bearing distinction: **Law No. 7464** (Official Gazette Nov 2023, in force **1 Jan 2024**) requires a Tourism-Purpose Rental Permit from the **Ministry of Culture & Tourism** (via e-Devlet) for any let under 100 days, and the permit can be applied for **only by the property owner — NOT a tenant, agent, or property manager** ([Mondaq summary of Law 7464](https://www.mondaq.com/turkey/data-protection/1422698/), secondary ⚠️) — a foreign owner cannot delegate the *permit* to a manager (only the booking operations); unanimous building co-owner consent required; one owner capped at 25% of independent units; fines TRY 100,000 (1st) → 500,000 → 1,000,000; a local-council *pansiyon* licence may also apply. Commission: long-let management not standardised — `data not publicly aggregated for foreign-owner long-let PM — verify with the appointed firm`; short-let management commonly **~20-25% of gross** (est., aligns with regional norm, ⚠️ no Turkish regulated figure — calibrate against the booked operator's contract). Reporting: market-driven, Guesty/local PMS; unregulated. Tax role: **stopaj withholding** — where a residential let's tenant is a corporate entity/business it must withhold 20% of gross rent and remit it; the owner still files an annual return and credits the withheld amount ([PwC Turkey](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/turkey/individual/income-determination); [GİB non-resident rental guidebook 2025](https://intvrg.gib.gov.tr/hazirbeyan/assets/pdf/2025THEGUIDEBOOKTAXPAYERS.pdf), primary ✅); non-residents taxed on Turkey-source rental only; residential exemption (TRY 47,000 for 2025) does NOT apply to commercial lets and a manager-paid arrangement does not extinguish the owner's filing duty. Red flag: foreign owners assuming the short-let permit can be held by/through their manager (it cannot — owner-only); assuming stopaj withholding closes the tax loop (it does not — annual return still due). **Confidence**: HIGH (primary 2018 Regulation + Law 7464 + GİB guide; commission est. flagged).

- **IL Israel** — **No PM-specific licence.** Real-estate brokerage is regulated by the **Real Estate Brokers Law, 5756-1996** (Hebrew year 5756 = 1996; the commonly-cited "5752-1992" is incorrect — no such 1992 brokerage law) administered by the Ministry of Justice registrar (exam, annual renewal, disciplinary regime) ([Barnea / Lexology](https://barlaw.co.il/practice_areas/litigation/real-estate-litigation/client_updates/), secondary ⚠️) — that law governs **sale brokerage only**; property management of a rented unit is **not a separately licensed activity** in Israel, managers operate under ordinary contract/agency law. Commission: long-let PM `data not publicly aggregated as a regulated band — verify with the appointed firm`; short-let management est. **~15-25% gross** (regional norm, ⚠️ no Israeli regulated figure). Reporting: market-driven, unregulated. Tax role — the key foreign-owner trap: the favourable residential exemption (monthly rent up to **NIS 5,654 in 2025**) and the 10% flat-rate route are **denied to non-Israeli-resident owners** — a non-resident's Israeli rental income is taxable as Israeli-source income with no small-landlord exemption ([PwC Israel](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/israel/individual/income-determination); [y-tax.co.il](https://y-tax.co.il/en/tax-on-rental-income/), secondary ⚠️); a manager is not a statutory tax collector — the non-resident owner must file. Red flag: a manager (or owner) applying the resident exemption / 10% track to a non-resident's income, creating an undisclosed liability; opacity of net-vs-gross statements. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (tax treatment well-sourced; commission bands not publicly aggregated — flagged).

- **QA Qatar** — Two distinct regimes. Long-let: a property-management company must be licensed by the municipal authority (historically the Ministry of Municipality/MME) and lease agreements registered with the municipality at an annual fee of ~1% of rent ([ARAB MLS summary](https://arabmls.org/what-are-the-regulations-for-property-management-companies-in-qatar/), aggregator ⚠️ — verify with Aqarat/[Real Estate Regulatory Authority](https://www.aqarat.gov.qa/en/)); real-estate sector now overseen by **Aqarat (Real Estate Regulatory Authority)** + a new Real Estate Registration Dept under the Ministry of Justice (implementing regs issued June 2025, primary ✅ context). Short-let: a **Qatar Tourism Holiday Homes licence** is mandatory — owner or authorised tenant applies via Qatar Tourism e-Services, inspection + Classification Manual compliance, **5-year licence validity**, fine **QR 200,000** for unlicensed operation ([Qatar Tourism Holiday Homes](https://www.qatartourism.com/en/licensing-e-services/e-services/holiday-homes), primary ✅). Commission: `data not publicly aggregated for foreign-owner PM — verify with the appointed firm`; short-let est. ~20-25% gross (regional norm, ⚠️). Reporting: market-driven. Tax role: **no personal income tax** on individual rental income (2025, [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/)); manager's only collection role is the ~1% municipal lease-registration fee on long-let and Qatar Tourism fees on short-let. Red flag: short-let-permit non-compliance shifting the QR 200,000 fine to a non-resident owner; commission/breakdown opacity given no regulated disclosure standard. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (short-let primary-sourced; long-let PM commission not publicly aggregated).

- **SA Saudi Arabia** — Strongly regulated and platform-mediated. All rental contracts must be registered on **Ejar**, the national digital tenancy platform; the sector is supervised by **REGA (Real Estate General Authority)** ([REGA / Ejar](https://rega.gov.sa/en/rega-services/platforms/ejar/), primary ✅). A person/firm performing brokerage or technical real-estate management must hold a **FAL licence** (REGA, via Saudi Real Estate Institute accredited training; fee schedule not published by REGA on the FAL page — data not publicly available, verify current FAL fees at REGA) ([REGA FAL](https://rega.gov.sa/en/rega-services/platforms/fal-real-estate-brokerage/), primary ✅); REGA also issues a licence to an establishment to "manage the technical affairs of a real estate." 2025 framework additions: unified tenancy contract + **Riyadh 5-year rent freeze from 25 Sep 2025** (residential & commercial, urban Riyadh — material to a foreign owner's long-let revenue model) ([Elaqat Law analysis](https://www.elaqatlaw.com/post/how-do-landlord-tenant-regulations-work-in-saudi-arabia-under-ejar-rega-and-the-new-2025-framework), secondary ⚠️ on a primary 2025 reform). Commission: `not publicly aggregated as a regulated band — verify with the FAL-licensed firm`; brokerage commission is contractual under the Brokerage Law; short-let licensing handled via the tourism authority (verify current Saudi tourism short-let permit at REGA/Saudi Tourism — not aggregated here). Reporting: Ejar gives a regulated contract/payment record (strong owner protection — Ejar-authenticated leases are executive documents enforceable in execution courts). Tax role: **no personal income tax** for individuals (2025, [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/saudi-arabia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)). Red flag: appointing a non-FAL-licensed manager (can't legally transact / register on Ejar); the Riyadh rent freeze invalidating pro-forma rent-escalation assumptions sold to foreign buyers. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (Ejar/REGA/FAL primary-sourced; PM commission not publicly aggregated).

- **JO Jordan** — No PM-specific national licence; rental relationship governed by **Landlord and Tenant Law No. 11 of 1994 (as amended)** + Civil Code ([Expat Focus / global guides](https://www.expatfocus.com/jordan/guide/jordan-property-letting), secondary ⚠️); property-manager registration/notification is municipality-driven (e.g. Greater Amman Municipality) — `confirm landlord-notification requirement with the relevant municipality`. Commission: not publicly aggregated; sale-side agent commission norm is ~2-3% — PM long-let band `data not publicly available — verify with the appointed firm`. Reporting: market-driven, unregulated. Tax role: unlike the GCC, **Jordan DOES tax rental income** — leasing income included in taxable income, progressive PIT 5%-30%, plus a 1% national contribution above JOD 200,000 (2025); non-residents taxed on Jordan-source income only ([PwC Jordan](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/jordan/individual/income-determination), primary ✅); a non-resident owner typically appoints a local representative via power of attorney; the manager is not a statutory withholding agent for residential rent — owner files. Red flag: assuming "Middle East = no tax" (false for Jordan); non-resident reliance on an informal local representative without a clear written mandate. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (tax primary-sourced; PM commission not aggregated).

- **OM Oman** — Foreign (non-GCC) freehold ownership is confined to **Integrated Tourism Complexes (ITCs)**, where the master developer / ITC operator typically runs an in-house management & rental pool (Al Mouj, Jebel Sifah, Muscat Bay, AIDA, etc.) — so the foreign-owner PM choice is often ITC-operator-tied rather than an open market ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/foreign-ownership-of-real-estate-in-oman/), primary-firm ⚠️). Commission: not publicly aggregated as a regulated band; sale-side agent ~3% is the common cited figure — long-let/short-let PM `data not publicly available — verify with the ITC operator's rental-pool agreement`. Reporting: ITC-operator pool statements (private); unregulated disclosure standard. Tax role: **no personal income tax in 2025 — BUT Oman introduces a 5% personal income tax from 1 January 2028** (Royal Decree No. 56/2025, on annual income above OMR 42,000) ([PwC Oman](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/), 2025); a foreign owner modelling a multi-year hold MUST factor this calendar-actionable change (any Oman "no income tax" statement must carry the sunset; re-stamp before 2028). Red flag: ITC rental-pool agreements with opaque blended-pool revenue allocation (your unit's nights vs pooled distribution); assuming permanent zero-tax beyond 2027. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (foreign-ownership structure + 2028 PIT primary-sourced; PM commission not aggregated).

- **BH Bahrain** — The clearest PM-specific licensing regime in the GCC. Under **Law No. 27 of 2017 (Real Estate Sector Regulation)**, *all property managers operating in Bahrain must be licensed by RERA Bahrain*; a full PM licence requires the **RERA Certificate in Property Management** (≈20-hour course + exam), with a 12-month transitional pathway for <3-years-experience applicants, and managers bound by the RERA Code of Conduct ([RERA Bahrain — Property Managers](https://www.rera.gov.bh/en/property-managers), primary ✅) — making "is the manager RERA-licensed?" a hard, verifiable gate for a foreign owner. Long-let vs short-let: short-let/holiday-let permitting is comparatively less codified than Dubai/Qatar — `confirm current Bahrain short-let permit status with RERA / Bahrain Tourism`. Commission: not publicly aggregated as a regulated band — `verify with the RERA-licensed firm`. Reporting: licensed-PM Code-of-Conduct obligations apply; statement granularity still contractual. Tax role: **no personal income tax** for individuals (2025, [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/)). Red flag: appointing an unlicensed manager (a real, checkable RERA breach here, unlike most of the region); commission/expense opacity within the contracted statement. **Confidence**: MEDIUM-HIGH (Law 27/2017 + RERA PM-licence primary-sourced; commission not aggregated).

- **KW Kuwait** — The foreign-owner PM scenario is structurally narrow. Non-GCC foreigners are generally barred from owning residential real estate; exceptions require ~10 years' residency + Council of Ministers approval, or are limited to KDIPA-licensed entities / listed companies / licensed real-estate funds under **Decree No. 195 of 2025** ([GLA & Co](https://www.glaco.com/blog/kuwaiti-decree-no-195-of-2025-concerning-foreign-ownership-of-real-estate-by-companies-and-investment-funds-in-the-state-of-kuwait/), secondary ⚠️ on a primary 2025 decree) — so the typical "foreign individual abroad hiring a manager" persona barely exists for KW residential; where a permitted foreign entity holds property, PM is municipality-licensed and lets are governed by the **1978 Lease Law as amended by Decree-Law No. 95 of 2024**. Commission: `not publicly aggregated — verify with the appointed firm`. Reporting: market-driven. Tax role: **no personal income tax** for individuals (2025, [PwC Kuwait](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kuwait/individual/taxes-on-personal-income)). Red flag: a "foreign owner" structure that is itself non-compliant with Kuwaiti ownership restrictions — the PM question is moot until ownership eligibility is confirmed. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (ownership restriction + 2024/2025 reforms sourced; PM market data not aggregated, largely N/A by ownership rules).

- **LB Lebanon** — **Active financial/currency crisis — treat as high-risk, low-data.** There is no functioning regulated PM licensing regime equivalent to RERA/REGA; rental is split between the pre-1992 rent-controlled "old law" and post-1992 Civil-Code 3-year contracts ([LCPS](https://www.lcps-lebanon.org/en/articles/details/4977/), policy-research ⚠️); post-2024-conflict displacement + a collapsed banking system mean rents demanded in USD cash or LBP at the parallel-market rate, dollarised maintenance costs that can exceed LBP rents (driving deliberate vacancy), landlords explicitly targeting foreign / single tenants who can pay USD cash ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1264546/), [Housing Monitor](https://housingmonitor.org/en/content/rental-market-trends-amid-crisis), reportage ⚠️ — directional, not statistical). Commission: no reliable public figure — `data not publicly available; any quoted band is unverifiable in current conditions`. Reporting: no regulated standard; cash-economy opacity is the baseline. Tax role: rental income is taxable in principle but enforcement is weak/inconsistent amid the crisis — `verify current treatment with a Lebanese tax adviser; do not rely on pre-2019 guidance`. Red flag: the ENTIRE engagement is a red flag for a remote foreign owner — currency-conversion disputes, cash-only flows, no escrow, no enforceable PM accountability, parallel-rate skimming on every remittance; do not benchmark against pre-crisis data. **Confidence**: LOW (crisis conditions; no reliable PM market or regulatory data — stated honestly).

### Confidence

HIGH for AE and TR (primary DET/RERA/DLD + 2018 Regulation/Law 7464/GİB), MEDIUM-HIGH for SA and BH (Ejar/REGA/FAL; Law 27/2017 + RERA PM-licence), MEDIUM for IL/QA/JO/OM/KW (tax or structure primary-sourced but PM commission not publicly aggregated), and LOW for LB (active financial crisis — no reliable PM or regulatory data, stated honestly). Long-let PM commission bands are not regulated figures anywhere in the region; the only published bands (AE) are aggregator/market-norm, and all non-AE short-let percentages are `est.` regional-norm extrapolations to calibrate against the appointed operator's contract.

## APAC + Central Asia + South Asia

### Regional pattern

A foreign owner living abroad hires a manager for a long-let or short-let property; distinct from `--rental` (yield/demand) and `--agent` (transaction broker). Every commission % in W4 is market/aggregator/inference-tier unless tied to a regulated scale (MY BOVAEP scale; KR/TW brokerage caps are *concepts* whose exact current schedules must be verified at the regulator) — no commission figure is a statutory fact and all are calibration-flagged. Several markets impose a manager-as-withholding-agent or payer-withholding regime on non-resident owners (JP 20.42%, TW 20%, SG 24% YA2024, TH 15%, ID PPh26 20%, PH 25% final NRA-NETB, CN 20% IIT, KH 14%, IN §195 30%+) while others (HK 15% Property Tax on the owner, MO 10% imposto predial) put the burden on the owner directly. Foreign-ownership structure is the recurring high-risk theme — TH (49% condo cap / leasehold villas), ID (unconstitutional nominee freehold), VN (50-yr term), PH (60/40 rule), KH (70%/above-ground strata), LK/MV (leasehold only) — a manager may be operating an asset inside a structure the foreigner does not fully control; ID/TH/MV villa management is the highest-opacity sub-segment (hidden fees, 25-50% cost mark-ups, commingled payouts — 🟡 forum/aggregator, a real diligence flag, never a verdict basis). KZ/UZ/KH/LK/MV have no professionalised foreign-owner PM industry comparable to JP/SG/TH/MY/ID; KZ rate flagged for re-verification post the 18 Jul 2025 Tax Code (effective 2026).

### Country one-liners

- **JP Japan** — Split: 賃貸管理 (chintai kanri, long-let) is a distinct regulated activity from 民泊 minpaku short-let operation (Housing Accommodation Business Act / 住宅宿泊事業法). Commission: long-let ~5% gross/month is the widely-quoted market norm (🔴 est. — no statutory cap; cross-check per agreement); minpaku/STR full-service ~15-20% gross (🔴 est., aggregator-derived). Licence: two PM-specific regimes — (a) 宅地建物取引業免許 (Building Lots & Buildings Transaction Business licence) for brokerage, and (b) **賃貸住宅管理業 Rental Housing Management Business registration with MLIT — mandatory since 15 June 2021 for firms managing >200 rental units** (≤200 = recommended, not compelled); 5-yr renewal; registration number must appear in the management contract (✅ MLIT via PLAZA HOMES summary of the Act on Optimization of Rental Housing Management Business). Reporting: written 重要事項説明 + contract delivery legally required before signing; owner-portal firm-by-firm, not mandated. Tax role: ✅ when rent is paid to a non-resident owner, the payer (tenant if a corporation, OR the property manager acting as payer) **must withhold 20.42% of gross rent** (income tax + 2.1% special reconstruction surtax) and remit by the 10th of the following month — NTA No.12014; KEY CARVE-OUT: an **individual** tenant renting for own/relative residence is NOT required to withhold; a **corporate** tenant or a **PM-collected** stream always must. Red flag: owner abroad relying on PM to discharge the 20.42% withholding — confirm in writing the PM is the designated payer and files, else the owner carries the liability; confirm the firm's MLIT registration number if portfolio >200 units. **Confidence**: HIGH (NTA + MLIT primary-tier).

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

This is the `--property-management` section — a foreign owner living abroad hiring a manager for a long-let or short-let; distinct from `--rental` (yield/demand) and `--agent` (buy-side broker). Foreign-owner absentee management is structurally large in MX (Riviera Maya/Baja), CR (Pacific/Caribbean coast), PA, and the Caribbean islands — the densest entries. Almost no LATAM/Caribbean state fixes PM commission by law, so commission bands are market-norm industry figures unless a statute fixes them. Licensing is sharply split: mandatory broker/manager licensing in PA (Decreto Ley 6/1999 + Junta Técnica), BS (Real Estate (Brokers and Salesmen) Act 1995 / BREA, definition expressly includes *management* for compensation), and JM (REDDA / Real Estate Board, with a documented gov-reported manager shortage), plus a colegio/registry regime for brokers in UY, AR and a corredor licence statute in EC and BR (Lei 6.530/1978 CRECI), versus genuinely unlicensed PM in CR, CO, CL, PY, BZ. The non-resident tax-collector role is material and well-documented in several (MX platform-retention, BR 15% IRRF, CR 13% VAT + 15/15, PE 5% non-domiciled SUNAT, EC 25%, DO 27% + 18% ITBIS); BS has no income tax (a key positive differentiator). The documented Caribbean developer-fund-opacity / "manager bypass" pattern (informal manager pocketing the withholding, opaque cost deductions) applies to DO/BS/JM by analogy 🟡 — DM (Dominica) is OUT OF SCOPE; any source attributing a Dominica program/visa/tax to "DR/DO" is the known DR↔DM conflation error and is rejected.

### Country one-liners

- **MX Mexico** — Split is sharp: *arrendamiento* (long-let, often a local *administrador* or inmobiliaria) vs *vacation-rental management* (huge foreign-owner business, Riviera Maya / Los Cabos / PV). Commission: long-let ~8–10% gross; short-let ~20–30% of gross managed (full-service), often hybrid (base % + per-clean / per-turnover pass-through) — market-norm, cross-check ⚠️. Licence: **no national PM or realty licence**; real-estate practice is regulated *state-by-state* — Quintana Roo (Tulum/Playa/Cancún), CDMX, Jalisco, Nuevo León among the handful with formal state registration/licence + annual CE (~30 hrs in QRoo) (2026, ⚠️ industry/legal); AMPI membership is the dominant *voluntary* national credential. Tax role: **material** — under the **2020 ISR + IVA reform for digital platforms (in force 1 Jun 2020)** Airbnb/Vrbo retain tax at source for non-residents; **no RFC supplied → platform withholds 20% income tax + 100% of the 16% IVA, treated as final** (gov reform, ⚠️ accountant-explained mechanism); a non-resident renting *off-platform* relies on a property-management company / RFC-registered representative to remit ISR (non-resident 25% gross, no deductions) + IVA. Red flag: foreigner-targeted overcharge; opaque per-turnover cost pass-throughs bundled into "management"; informal *administradores* with no fideicomiso/RFC trail and no recourse 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on tax-platform mechanism + state-by-state licence; MEDIUM on commission bands (market-norm, no statutory anchor).

- **BR Brazil** — Long-let *administração de imóveis* (typically via a CRECI-registered *imobiliária / administradora*) vs short-let / temporada vacation management. Real-estate brokerage AND property administration are regulated by **Lei nº 6.530/1978** (regulamentada por Decreto 81.871/1978); the *corretor de imóveis* and *imobiliária* must be **registered with the regional CRECI** (Conselho Regional de Corretores de Imóveis, federal system COFECI) — practising without CRECI registration is an offence; *administração de imóveis* is customarily performed by a CRECI-registered firm; pure facilities/condo management (síndico/administradora de condomínio) is a separate, less-regulated activity — do not conflate (gov statute, planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l6530.htm). Commission: long-let *taxa de administração* ~8–10% of monthly rent (market-norm, ⚠️); short-let/temporada management higher, ~20–30% (⚠️). Tax role: non-resident owner's rental income is subject to **IRRF withholding at 15%**; in practice the *administradora* / appointed *procurador* (CPF + local representative effectively required for a non-resident) collects rent, applies permitted deductions (e.g. IPTU/municipal taxes), withholds and remits (gov, taxsummaries.pwc.com/brazil/individual; the 15%-on-net-base nuance is aggregator-explained ⚠️ — verify with Receita Federal / a Brazilian accountant). Red flag: opaque deduction base on the IRRF calc; non-CRECI "administradora" exposing the owner to unenforceable contracts 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on Lei 6.530/1978 + CRECI + 15% IRRF; MEDIUM on deduction-base mechanics and commission bands.

- **CR Costa Rica** — Vacation-rental management of foreign-owned villas is one of the largest absentee-owner service businesses in the country (Guanacaste/Papagayo, Pacific coast, Caribbean side); long-let vs short-let management clearly split. Commission: short-let full-service ~20–30% of gross (Vacasa ~30%, Special Places ~20%; owner-sourced/long-90-day stays ~10%) — ⚠️ company-published; long-let ~10% market-norm ⚠️. Licence: **real-estate brokering is an UNregulated industry — no government licence required**; the **Cámara Costarricense de Corredores de Bienes Raíces (CCCBR)** is a *private non-profit (founded 1974), membership voluntary*, not a regulator (reg/⚠️ camara.cr) — PM is entirely unlicensed; vetting falls on the owner. Tax role: since 2019 rental income is subject to income tax + **13% VAT (IVA)**; the common regime is "15/15" (15% deemed-expense, 15% rate on the 85% base, monthly form D-125); short-let tourist accommodation is VAT-liable; a non-resident owner relies on the property manager / fiscal representative to file D-125 and remit (gov DGT regime, ⚠️ accountant-explained; a 2026 ~12.75% Airbnb-tax change is industry-reported — verify at Hacienda/DGT). Red flag: unlicensed-manager landscape → commingling and "fee plus undisclosed markups on cleaning/maintenance" is a common foreign-owner complaint pattern 🟡; developer-controlled rental-pool opacity on resort condos 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on no-licence/CCCBR-voluntary + 13% VAT + 15/15; MEDIUM on the 2026 12.75% figure (industry-reported, unverified at source).

- **PA Panama** — Long-let administration vs short-let (Panama City, Bocas, Coronado, beach towns). Commission: long-let ~10% market-norm; short-let ~20–25% full-service ⚠️ (no statutory rate). Licence: **a real-estate broker LICENCE is mandatory** — **Decreto Ley Nº 6 de 8 de julio de 1999** regulates the profession and creates the **Junta Técnica de Bienes Raíces** within the MICI (Ministerio de Comercio e Industrias); anyone receiving consideration for intermediation must be licensed; eligibility is Panamanian citizens or foreigners resident 5+ years, exam-based, licence indefinite (gov, mici.gob.pa/jtbr-marco-legal; statute PDF mici.gob.pa/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/ley-6-8-071999.pdf); whether *pure rental management* falls within the licensed "corretaje" definition vs facilities management is the grey zone — leasing/management of land for compensation is within Decreto Ley 6 scope, so a compliant manager should be (or work under) a licensed *corredor* — verify the manager's JTBR licence number. Tax role: rental income is Panama-source and taxable; a non-resident relies on the manager / local representative to handle ITBMS (7% VAT, where applicable to short-term tourist lodging) and income tax remittance — exact non-resident withholding mechanism: data not exhaustively public here, verify with DGI (dgi.mef.gob.pa). Red flag: unlicensed "managers" operating outside Decreto Ley 6; foreigner overcharge; developer rental-pool opacity in tower condos 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on Decreto Ley 6/1999 + Junta Técnica licence mandate; MEDIUM on whether all PM is captured by the licence; LOW on the non-resident tax-remittance mechanic (verify DGI).

- **CO Colombia** — Long-let *administración de inmuebles* (inmobiliaria) vs short-let (Medellín, Cartagena, Bogotá). Commission: long-let customary **~10% of monthly rent + IVA** — *no law fixes it*, but the ~10% is recognised as a *costumbre mercantil* certified by the Cámara de Comercio de Medellín for Antioquia (reg/⚠️); short-let full-service higher ~20–25% ⚠️. Licence: **no PM or realty licence** in Colombia — the activity is not licensed (the *lonja* membership is voluntary). Tax role: payments abroad to a non-resident for rentals are subject to **~20% withholding (retención en la fuente para pagos al exterior)** vs the standard ~3.5% resident rental withholding; the local payer/agent withholds (gov DIAN concept; ⚠️ tax-advisory explained — confirm current rate with DIAN, estatuto tributario art. on rentas de fuente nacional / pagos al exterior). Red flag: opaque "cuota de administración" vs the building's condominium *administración* fee (two different "administración" charges easily conflated to a foreign owner); informal Medellín STR managers 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on no-licence + 10% costumbre; MEDIUM on the 20% non-resident withholding (rate confirm at DIAN).

- **UY Uruguay** — Long-let vs short-let (Punta del Este seasonal, Montevideo). Licence: real-estate *rematadores/corredores* operate under a long-standing colegiado/registry framework — brokers/auctioneers have a registration/colegio regime; pure rental PM is not separately licensed; new **Ley 20.352 (Sept 2024)** requires tourist-lodging operators renting under 120 days to register with the Ministerio de Turismo (gov, ⚠️ industry-summarised — verify at gub.uy/ministerio-turismo). Tax role: non-resident landlords pay a **flat 12% on rental income under IRNR**; the local manager/representative typically handles the IRNR retention and DGI compliance (gov IRNR; ⚠️ advisory-explained). Red flag: seasonal-only managers, off-season vacancy opacity; informal Punta del Este peso-cash deals 🟡. **Confidence**: MEDIUM — 12% IRNR and Ley 20.352 are solid; broker-registration nuance and commission bands LOW (verify).

- **CL Chile** — Long-let vs short-let (Santiago, coast, Pucón). Commission: long-let ~5–10% market-norm; short-let higher ⚠️ (no statutory rate). Licence: **no licence — *corredor de propiedades* is an unregulated free profession**; no professional title required, only a Chilean RUT + SII activity registration + municipal *patente comercial* to operate commercially (gov SII registration requirement; ⚠️ industry/SERNAC-explained — sernac.cl notes the lack of specific regulation); PM equally unlicensed. - **AR Argentina** — Long-let vs short-let (Buenos Aires Palermo/Recoleta, Bariloche, Mendoza). **Inflation/peso caveat: any ARS figure here is indicative only and decays fast; commissions are quoted as % so they track, but absolute caps/fees in pesos are not stated for that reason.** Commission: long-let *administración* customarily ~5–8% of monthly rent + the leasing/*comisión* (post-2023 *Ley de Alquileres* reforms / DNU 70/2023 deregulation moved leasing-commission norms — verify current local custom); short-let higher ⚠️. Tax role: rental income broadly taxed; a **non-resident must appoint a local representative / responsable sustituto** who handles the withholding and AFIP (now ARCA) compliance — effective burden cited ~ a flat rate on rental earnings regardless of residency (⚠️ aggregator; verify current Ganancias/beneficiarios del exterior mechanism at the tax authority). - **PE Peru** — Long-let vs short-let (Lima Miraflores/Barranco, Cusco). Commission: long-let ~8–10% market-norm; short-let higher ⚠️ (no statutory rate; data thin). Licence: **no mandatory realty/PM licence** in Peru (the agent registry / RVA is voluntary). Tax role: **clear and material** — for a *non-domiciliado* owner, first-category (rental) income carries a **5% withholding on gross**; a domiciled tenant/payer is *obliged to withhold 5%* and remit to SUNAT (gov, nodomiciliados.sunat.gob.pe + taxsummaries.pwc.com/peru); a property manager handling a non-domiciled owner typically operates this 5% retention. Red flag: informal-manager no-recourse; opacity on the 5% being correctly remitted (owner should see SUNAT proof) 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on the 5% non-domiciled withholding (SUNAT primary); MEDIUM on no-licence; LOW on commission bands (market-norm, thin).

- **EC Ecuador** — Long-let vs short-let (Cuenca expat market, Quito, coast). Commission: long-let ~8–10% market-norm; short-let higher ⚠️ (no statutory rate; thin). Licence: Ecuador HAS a *corredor de bienes raíces* licence regime under the **Ley de Corredores de Bienes Raíces (Ley 173 / Decreto Supremo)** — only licensed *corredores* may legally mediate real-estate operations (gov statute, vlex/ACBIR; acbir.com.ec/docs/Ley-de-Corredores-de-Bienes-Raices.pdf); whether pure rental management is captured vs intermediation is a grey zone — verify the manager's *credencial*. Red flag: informal managers operating outside the Ley de Corredores; foreigner overcharge in expat hubs 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on the corredor licence statute existing + 25% non-resident rate; MEDIUM on PM-vs-intermediation scope.

- **PY Paraguay** — Long-let vs short-let (Asunción; small foreign-investor market growing). Commission: long-let ~8–10% of monthly rent market-norm ⚠️ (no statutory rate; thin data). Licence: **no real-estate or PM licence requirement** — foreign ownership and management are open; managers operate as local companies (⚠️ industry/relocation-firm sources). Tax role: rental income from Paraguayan property is taxed *domestically as "rentas de capital" at a flat 8% under Ley 6380/2019* (it is local-source, not "foreign" income); a RUC-registered manager/representative files (gov statute Ley 6380/2019; ⚠️ aggregator-explained — verify at SET, set.gov.py). Red flag: thin professional landscape, informal management, no recourse 🟡; small market = few reputable absentee-owner managers. **Confidence**: MEDIUM — no-licence + Ley 6380/2019 8% are reasonably solid; commission bands and the practical remittance flow LOW (sparse public data — verify SET).

- **BS Bahamas** — Major foreign-owner second-home / vacation-rental market (Nassau/Paradise Island, Exuma, Abaco); long-let vs short-let split is central; vacation-rental management of foreign-owned villas is a large business. Commission: short-let full-service management ~20–30% market-norm ⚠️; long-let ~8–10% ⚠️; **sale-side** commission (context, not PM): 6% improved / 10% vacant land + businesses (reg, BREA). Licence: **mandatory — the Real Estate (Brokers and Salesmen) Act 1995** empowers the **Bahamas Real Estate Association (BREA)** as the statutory regulator/licensor; the Act's definition of "practice of real estate business" **expressly includes the *management* or lease of land for compensation** — a paid rental manager falls within the licensing requirement (gov statute, laws.bahamas.gov.bs .../1995-0033.pdf; BREA breabahamas.com). Tax role: **the Bahamas has no income tax / no rental-income tax** — so there is *no* non-resident rental-income withholding for a manager to operate (a key positive differentiator vs every LATAM peer here); VAT (currently 10%) can apply to vacation-rental accommodation services (gov; verify current VAT treatment at inlandrevenue.finance.gov.bs). Red flag: developer-controlled rental-pool opacity on branded resort condos; unlicensed "villa managers" outside the 1995 Act; opaque expense pass-throughs to absentee owners 🟡 (the documented Caribbean developer-fund-opacity pattern applies in spirit). **Confidence**: HIGH on the 1995 Act + BREA + no-income-tax; MEDIUM on commission bands + current VAT treatment.

- **DO Dominican Republic** — (NOT Dominica/DM.) Large foreign-owner STR/villa market (Punta Cana, Las Terrenas, Sosúa/Cabarete); long-let vs short-let split central; villa/condo vacation management of foreign-owned units is a big business. Commission: short-let full-service ~20–30% market-norm ⚠️; long-let ~8–10% ⚠️ (no statutory rate). Licence: **no mandatory national real-estate or PM licence** (AEI / professional-association membership is voluntary; the *Colegio Dominicano de Corredores* exists but is not a hard government gate) — verify; PM is effectively unregulated. Tax role: **material and well-documented** — non-resident rental income is subject to **27% withholding**; the property-management company customarily collects rent, deducts its fee + operating costs, **withholds the 27% and remits it to the DGII** before wiring net to the owner; short-term tourist-accommodation rentals also carry **18% ITBIS (VAT)** (gov DGII rate; ⚠️ aggregator/Airbnb-tax-guide explained mechanism — verify at dgii.gov.do; Airbnb 2024 DR tax guide). Red flag: **strong** — informal managers pocketing the "withheld" 27% without remitting (owner left liable), opaque fee/cost deductions before the net wire, foreigner-targeted overcharge, developer rental-pool opacity in Punta Cana resort condos 🟡 (the documented Caribbean developer-fund-opacity / "manager bypass" pattern applies directly — demand DGII remittance receipts). **Confidence**: HIGH on 27% non-resident withholding + 18% ITBIS + manager-as-collector mechanism; MEDIUM on the licence statement (no hard gate, verify); the red-flag pattern is anecdotal-but-recurring 🟡.

- **JM Jamaica** — Long-let vs short-let (Kingston long-let; Montego Bay/Negril/Ocho Rios villa STR). Commission: short-let full-service ~20–30% ⚠️; long-let ~8–10% ⚠️ (no statutory rate). Licence: **mandatory — the Real Estate (Dealers and Developers) Act (REDDA)** requires a person engaged as a *dealer* (incl. property management for compensation) to be **licensed by the Real Estate Board (REBCSC)**; a property manager must be licensed to engage in dealings, and the Act expressly captures management activity; ~202 licensed property managers nationally vs ~2,000 strata complexes (a documented *shortage*, gov-reported, not just anecdotal), 4–5 yrs training to licence (gov statute, laws.moj.gov.jm; reg, reb.gov.jm; Jamaica Observer 2025 "Property managers scarce"). Tax role: rental income is taxable in Jamaica; a non-resident relies on the manager/representative for income-tax + GCT (15% where applicable to commercial/tourist accommodation) compliance — exact non-resident withholding mechanism: data not exhaustively public here, verify at TAJ (jamaicatax.gov.jm). Red flag: licensed-manager *shortage* → many strata/villas managed by unlicensed operators (structural, gov-reported); developer/strata-fund opacity 🟡. **Confidence**: HIGH on REDDA + Real Estate Board licence + the documented manager shortage; LOW on the non-resident tax mechanism (verify TAJ).

- **BB Barbados** — Long-let vs short-let (west/south coast villa STR is a large foreign-owner market). Commission: long-let leasing fee customarily ~one month's rent (paid only on a found tenant); ongoing management % not statutorily fixed; sale-side unofficial ~5% + VAT (⚠️ industry — context); short-let full-service management higher ⚠️. Licence: real-estate brokerage in Barbados is **not subject to a mandatory government licence** (no statutory licensing board comparable to BS/JM — ⚠️ industry-sourced; verify) BUT **short-term-rental properties must be registered/licensed as tourist accommodation with the Barbados Tourism Authority (BTA)**, licence valid to Dec 31, annual renewal — the *person offering the accommodation (owner or manager) must be licensed* (gov BTA registration regime; ⚠️ industry-summarised — verify at barbados.org/visit-barbados / BTA). Red flag: STR-registration non-compliance by informal managers; villa-management expense opacity to absentee owners 🟡. **Confidence**: MEDIUM — BTA STR-registration mandate solid; broker "no mandatory licence" is industry-sourced (verify); tax mechanism LOW.

- **BZ Belize** — Long-let vs short-let (Ambergris Caye / San Pedro, Placencia — heavily foreign-owned STR). - **KY Cayman Islands** — (British Overseas Territory; USD-pegged CI$.) Long-let via a **property-management company / managing agent** vs short-let via **villa / vacation-rental management** (Seven Mile Beach condo corridor is the dense absentee-owner market). Commission: long-let ~10–15% of gross + tenant-find/leasing fee `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let villa/condo full-service ~25–35% of gross `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)` — no Cayman dataset; verify with 2+ CIREBA-member firm quotes. Licence: **no statutory property-manager licence found** — real-estate practice is organised through **[CIREBA (Cayman Islands Real Estate Brokers Association, founded 1987)](https://www.cireba.com/)**, a **voluntary trade body / broker-MLS** with mandatory course + 3-segment exam + code of ethics **for its members** (it self-describes as quasi-regulatory but is **not a statutory licensor** — no Loi-Hoguet-style carte-G equivalent confirmed; standalone property management is unlicensed). Reporting: market PMS; CIREBA member standards only; no statutory owner-account/CMP regime. Red flag: substantial beachfront strata/HOA fees (the dominant reason gross ~6–7% yield collapses to ~2% net — pull the strata budget); STR managers operating without a Hotel Licensing Board licence shifting the fine to the owner; opaque cleaning/turnover markups bundled into "management"; commingling — no national client-money-protection scheme. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (no-tax + 13% TAT + CIREBA-voluntary HIGH; commission bands `est.`/`market-norm`; "no statutory PM licence" is a verified-as-negative against practitioner sources — confirm absence at gov.ky).

- **BM Bermuda** — (Self-governing British Overseas Territory, North Atlantic — NOT Caribbean; USD-par BMD.) Commission: long-let ~10–15% of gross + tenant-find `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let ~25–35% `est.` ⚠️ — thin public data; verify with 2+ Bermuda agent quotes. Licence: **YES — statutory, the [Real Estate Brokers' Licensing Act 2017](http://www.commonlii.org/bm/legis/num_act/rebla2017281.pdf)** (in force 2 Oct 2017, replacing the Real Estate Agents' Licensing Act 1976) makes real-estate **brokers + agents** licensed by the **Superintendent of Real Estate** (broker fee ~$635 / agent ~$105; criminal-background check + FIA registration; pro-active inspection + penalties; advised by the Real Estate Brokers' Advisory Board) — a FATF-driven AML regime ([gov.bm — real estate licensing](https://www.gov.bm/articles/real-estate-licensing)). **Whether the 2017 Act's licensed activity captures pure single-owner property *management* (vs brokerage) is not confirmed on the primary text — verify the Act's scope with the Superintendent before treating a manager as "licensed".** Tax role: **MINIMAL on rental income — Bermuda has no income tax, no capital gains tax**, so there is **no non-resident-owner rent withholding**; the manager is not a rental-income tax-collector. Reporting: market practice + Superintendent inspection of licensees; no statutory owner-CMP. Red flag: **letting without the Minister's permission breaches the ALHL condition** (liability on the owner); Land-Tax (up to ~55% marginal on high ARV) + hurricane insurance + rainwater-tank upkeep crush net yield; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (no-income-tax + the 2017 Act existence + Ministerial-letting-permission HIGH; commission `est.`; PM-vs-brokerage licence scope unverified).

Commission: long-let ~10–15% of gross `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let villa/managed-pool full-service ~25–35% of gross `est.` ⚠️ — no public dataset; verify with 2+ licensed TCI firm quotes. - **VG British Virgin Islands** — (British Overseas Territory; USD sole legal tender. Distinct from the **US** Virgin Islands — do not import USVI's property-manager-licensing rules, which are a different jurisdiction.) Long-let vs short-let villa management (small, illiquid, high-end villa + resort-condo market — Tortola, Virgin Gorda). Commission: long-let ~10–15% `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let full-service villa ~25–35% of gross `est.` ⚠️ — no public dataset; verify with 2+ local firm quotes. Licence: **no statutory real-estate-agent or property-manager licence — "no regulatory regime for real estate agents other than the general trade licensing regime applicable to all business"** ([O'Neal Webster, BVI law firm](https://onealwebster.com/across-the-divide-real-estate-representation-in-the-virgin-islands/), practitioner/secondary). A manager operates under a general **Trade Licence**; PM is unregulated. Tax role: **MINIMAL — the BVI has no income tax, no capital gains tax** (payroll tax exists on employment, irrelevant to passive ownership), so **no non-resident-owner rent withholding**. The owner's standing real-estate taxes are a **modest annual land tax + a house tax of 1.5% of notional annual rental value** (holding taxes, not rent withholding — see `--tax`). **KEY structural trap: the right to short-let can be a *condition of the owner's NBLHL* — some Non-Belongers Land Holding Licences require a Trade Licence *before* the property may be rented at all**, so STR is not an automatic right and the manager cannot cure it ([bvi.gov.vg](https://www.bvi.gov.vg/), practitioner-flagged). Reporting: market practice; no statutory owner-CMP. Red flag: managing/marketing a unit for rent where the NBLHL forbids letting (owner liable); 12% non-Belonger stamp duty + ~3-yr development commitment already loaded into the owner's basis; expensive/scarce hurricane insurance crushes net; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (no-income-tax + unregulated-PM (practitioner-sourced) + NBLHL-letting-condition HIGH; commission `est.`).

- **KN St. Kitts & Nevis** — (Independent Commonwealth realm; XCD pegged EC$2.70 = US$1.) Long-let vs short-let — but the market is overwhelmingly **CBI-condo units placed in developer rental-management / hotel pools** (Frigate Bay, Christophe Harbour, Nevis); the open long-let market is thin (small population). Commission: long-let ~8–12% `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let villa/pool full-service ~25–35% of gross `est.` ⚠️ — no public dataset; verify with 2+ local firm quotes (developer-pool terms are seller-controlled). Licence: **no statutory general property-manager / estate-agent licence found** outside the CBI context (the CBI **Authorised-Agent** regime gates *citizenship-application filing*, not ordinary letting management); standalone PM is unlicensed — verify with the Ministry of Justice & Legal Affairs. (Note: the Aliens Land Holding Licence is an **ownership** licence for the foreign buyer, not a manager's licence.) Tax role: **MINIMAL on rental income — St. Kitts & Nevis has no personal income tax, no capital gains tax and no inheritance tax** (the headline CBI draw, IRD-verified), so **no non-resident-owner rent withholding** for a manager to operate. (Owner's standing tax is a **modest annual property tax** — see `--tax`.) Corporate income tax applies to an SKN entity if the owner structures through one — verify scope at [IRD (sknird.com)](https://www.sknird.com/). Reporting: developer-pool operator statements (seller-controlled) + market PMS; no statutory owner-CMP. Red flag: **CBI developer rental-pool projections are seller-controlled** — occupancy + return numbers are `est., developer projection — request actuals`; the bundled-citizenship premium in the purchase price depresses true yield; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (no-personal-income-tax HIGH (IRD); "no statutory PM licence" verify-as-negative; commission `est.`).

- **AG Antigua & Barbuda** — (Independent Commonwealth realm; XCD pegged EC$2.70 = US$1.) Long-let vs short-let — branded CBI developments (Tamarind Hills, Hodges Bay, Nonsuch Bay) regulate management + STR in their **HOA/development rules** (read them before modelling yield). Commission: long-let ~8–12% `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let villa full-service ~25–35% of gross `est.` ⚠️ — no public dataset; verify with 2+ local firm quotes. Licence: **no statutory property-manager / estate-agent licence found** — licensed *property agents* who do management + rentals exist but operate without a dedicated statutory PM licence (the CBI **Authorised-Agent** licence gates citizenship filing only; the **NCLHL** is the foreign buyer's *ownership* licence under CAP. 293, not a manager's licence) — verify at [legalaffairs.gov.ag](https://legalaffairs.gov.ag/). Tax role: **MATERIAL — unlike its no-tax BOT neighbours.** Although **personal income tax was abolished in April 2016**, a **25% levy on NET rental income applies to non-residents** (multiple 2025–26 tax guides ⚠️ — verify at IRD); a non-resident landlord needs a **local tax ID**, and **short-term-rental operators must register for ABST (the VAT-equivalent) — ~17% on STR / 12.5% hotel-tourist accommodation** + remit the **Tourism Guest Levy** ([Antigua Observer — IRD STR ABST notice](https://antiguaobserver.com/inland-revenue-urges-short-term-rental-operators-to-register-and-file-sales-tax/); aggregator-explained mechanism ⚠️ — verify at IRD). A property manager handling a non-resident owner therefore touches both the 25% non-resident rental charge and ABST/Tourism-Levy collection — **but whether the manager is the *statutory withholding/collection agent* (vs the owner self-filing) is not confirmed — verify at IRD before relying.** Reporting: market practice + development HOA rules; no statutory owner-CMP. Red flag: STR operator unregistered for ABST/Tourism Levy → fine on owner; manager not surfacing the 25% non-resident rental levy + local-TIN duty (owner assumes "no income tax" covers it — it does not for non-resident rental); commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (25% non-resident rental levy + ABST-on-STR sourced (aggregator/IRD ⚠️); "no statutory PM licence" verify-as-negative; commission `est.`; manager-as-collector role unverified).

- **LC Saint Lucia** — (Independent Commonwealth realm, OECS/CARICOM; XCD pegged EC$2.70 = US$1.) Long-let vs short-let — condominium/branded-residence developments + CBI-approved projects are the foreign-owner core. Commission: long-let ~8–12% `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let villa full-service ~25–35% of gross `est.` ⚠️ — no public dataset; verify with 2+ local firm quotes. Licence: **no statutory property-manager / estate-agent licence in force** — the **[Real Estate Association of St. Lucia (est. 2009)](https://realtystlucia.com/st-lucia-realtors-association/)** is a **voluntary body still campaigning to *secure* regulatory legislation** (i.e. none yet exists); PM is unregulated. (The **Alien Landholding Licence** under the Aliens (Licensing) Act Cap. 15.37 is the foreign buyer's *ownership* licence, not a manager's.) Tax role: **POTENTIALLY MATERIAL — Saint Lucia taxes non-residents on St-Lucian-source income.** Withholding tax on payments to non-residents runs at **25% (non-resident) / 15% (CARICOM regional) / 10% (local)**, and a non-resident's rental income from a source other than a PE is liable to WHT ([PwC — Saint Lucia individual taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/saint-lucia/individual/taxes-on-personal-income); **the IRD A-Z of Taxes is offline behind a site-wide maintenance page — `no primary source currently carries the 25% non-resident WHT; verify at the Inland Revenue Department, Saint Lucia`**) — **so a St-Lucia payer/manager can fall within a 25% non-resident WHT obligation; the exact mechanism for a property manager withholding on an individual owner's rent is NOT pinned on a primary IRD procedure — verify at IRD before relying.** Separately, **VAT** applies to short-term-rental income above the registration threshold (sector rate possible — verify rate/threshold at IRD). Standing tax: annual property tax 0.25% residential of OMV; note a foreign *seller* faces a flat 10% vendor stamp duty (exit, not rent — see `--tax`). Reporting: market practice; no statutory owner-CMP. Red flag: manager not surfacing the non-resident WHT + VAT-on-STR obligations (owner left exposed); "no capital gains tax" widely cited but **UNVERIFIED at IRD — flag**; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (25%/15%/10% non-resident WHT framework + VAT-on-STR sourced; manager-as-withholding-agent mechanism unverified; "no statutory PM licence" sourced to the association's own reform campaign; commission `est.`).

- **GD Grenada** — (Independent Commonwealth realm, OECS/CARICOM; XCD pegged EC$2.70 = US$1.) Long-let vs short-let — CBI-approved projects + south-peninsula (St. George's/SGU) villas are the foreign-owner core. Commission: long-let ~8–12% `est.` ⚠️ `(market-norm, cross-check)`; short-let villa full-service ~20–30% of gross (the GD playbook's own management band) `est.` ⚠️ — no official index; verify with a local letting agent's actuals. Licence: **no statutory property-manager / estate-agent licence found** — verify at [Laws of Grenada (laws.gov.gd)](https://www.laws.gov.gd) (the **Alien Landholding Licence** is the foreign buyer's *ownership* licence, not a manager's); PM is effectively unregulated. Tax role: **MATERIAL and the most clearly-pinned of the eight — Grenada's IRD operates a 15% withholding tax on payments to non-residents, expressly including rental income.** The person remitting (the **property manager / payer**) **must deduct the 15% and pay it to the Inland Revenue Division within 7 days, must furnish the non-resident a WHT certificate, and is *personally liable* for any tax they fail to deduct** ([IRD — withholding tax](https://ird.gd/index.php/taxes/withholding-tax)) — i.e. **the GD manager is a genuine statutory tax-collector for the absentee owner**, the strongest tax-collector role in this Caribbean cluster. **VAT (15%)** may also apply to short-stay tourist accommodation above the registration threshold (verify at IRD). Standing tax: low annual property tax (~0.2% land / 0.3% building residential); no CGT, no inheritance tax. Reporting: market practice; no statutory owner-CMP. Red flag: **demand the IRD WHT-remittance receipts** — an informal manager who deducts the "15%" but fails to remit leaves the owner exposed (the deductor's personal liability is the owner's protection only if traceable); high GRENLEC power cost + hurricane insurance + the ~20–30% management load crush net yield; commingling — no national CMP. **Confidence**: HIGH on the 15% non-resident WHT + manager-deduct-and-remit-within-7-days mechanism (IRD primary); MEDIUM on VAT-on-STR (verify); "no statutory PM licence" verify-as-negative; commission `est.`.

- **TT Trinidad & Tobago** — (Twin-island English common-law republic; **final appeal to the UK Judicial Committee of the Privy Council — NOT the CCJ's appellate jurisdiction**; **dual land-title system — ~75–80% Old-Law deeds (no state title guarantee) vs RPA/Torrens Certificate of Title**, *est. per Fitzwilliam Stone*; TTD managed float.) Long-let (corporate/diplomatic/energy-expat West Trinidad — Westmoorings/Maraval/One Woodbrook Place, often USD-denominated for tenants) vs short-let (Tobago villa/guesthouse). Commission: **`data not publicly available — "confirm per agency" (no verified standard rate captured)`** — the TT playbook explicitly carries no agent/management % (do not fabricate one). Licence: **no statutory property-manager / estate-agent licence found in the playbook** — attorneys + **real-estate agents ARE subject to T&T's FIU-supervised AML/CFT framework** (source-of-funds duties), but that is an AML hook, not a PM competence-licence; `dedicated PM/estate-agent licensing regime — data not publicly available — verify at the relevant authority`; PM is effectively unregulated. Tax role: **NOT a PM-collected non-resident withholding** — residential rental income is **VAT-EXEMPT** and **net rental income is taxed at the individual income-tax bands (25%/30%, TT$90,000 allowance)** which the owner self-files (BIR); the **NEW Landlord Business Surcharge (Finance Act 2025 = Act No. 14 of 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026) — 2.5% on the first TT$20,000 of QUARTERLY gross rent / 3.5% above — is CREDITABLE against the landlord's annual income tax (an advance-payment mechanism, NOT a permanent tax or a flat yield haircut for a compliant landlord)**, with a one-time **TT$2,500 BIR registration (deadline 30 May 2026)** — and it is **landlord self-registration, not a manager-operated withholding on a non-resident's gross**. CGT applies only to disposals **within 12 months**. Reporting: market practice; no statutory owner-CMP. STR: **STR-specific licensing not verified at a primary source — verify short-let/accommodation-licensing with Tourism Trinidad / the Tobago Tourism Agency** (do not assert "no rules"). Red flag: **the dominant absentee-owner risk is FX, not a tax-collector — there is no legal capital-repatriation bar, but a genuine multi-year USD shortage means authorised dealers RATION forex**, so converting accumulated net TTD rent to USD for remittance is slow/uncertain ("even from USD accounts held in T&T") — **the single biggest practical exit/operate-from-abroad risk** (EUROCHAMTT / IMF Article IV / Chamber Jan 2025); a manager cannot cure the FX queue. Also: most land is Old-Law deeds with **no single authoritative title register** — an attorney must trace the chain of title before a manager is even engaged; commingling — no national CMP; wire/impersonation fraud against international buyers (verify wire instructions verbally). **Confidence**: MEDIUM (Privy-Council apex + FX-rationing-exit-risk + residential-rent-VAT-exempt + Landlord-Business-Surcharge-creditable + CGT-only-<12-months HIGH from the playbook; "no statutory PM licence" verify-as-negative; commission `data not publicly available`; STR licensing an open gap).

### Confidence

HIGH for MX/BR/CR/PA/CO/CL/PE/EC/BS/DO/JM on their primary-sourced anchors (state-by-state or statutory licensing, plus the non-resident tax mechanism where pinned — Lei 6.530/1978, Decreto Ley 6/1999, CCCBR-voluntary + 13% VAT, 5% SUNAT, 25% EC, 27%+18% DO, REDDA shortage, BS 1995 Act + no income tax); MEDIUM for UY/PY/BB/BZ/TT (solid on licence-status or one tax anchor, thin on commission/remittance mechanics — TT: Privy-Council apex + USD-rationing-exit-risk + residential-rent-VAT-exempt + creditable-Landlord-Business-Surcharge from the playbook, but PM licensing a verify-as-negative and commission `data not publicly available`); commission bands are market-norm everywhere (almost no state fixes PM commission by law) and several non-resident withholding mechanisms (PA/CL/JM/BB/BZ) are marked `data not publicly available — verify at <DGI/SII/TAJ/BRA/Belize Tax Service>`. AR's peso figures are suppressed by design (inflation/FX + 2023–25 reform churn); the DO/BS/JM manager-bypass red flag is recurring-anecdotal 🟡; DM (Dominica) is excluded and any DR↔DM conflation rejected.

## Africa + Anglo non-EU

### Regional pattern

A foreign owner living abroad hires a manager for long-let or short-let; distinct from `--rental` (tenant-side market) and `--agent` (transaction broker). The Anglo bloc has strong but sub-national licensing — US PM licensing is state-by-state (CA DRE, FL DBPR cited; some states exempt), CA is provincial (BC BCFSA licenses rental PM; ON RECO has no separate PM licence; AB RECA licenses PM), AU is state-by-state real-estate-agent licence + mandatory trust-account audit (NSW *Property and Stock Agents Act 2002* cited; VIC/QLD parallel) — and the single most important foreign-owner risk fact is that **NZ residential property management is NOT a licensed activity** (REAA 2008 carves it out; the Residential Property Managers Bill was not proceeded with 30 Apr 2024 and discharged June 2024 — the gap persists, civil-claim-only recourse). The major foreign-owner tax trap is gross-withholding-by-default: US 30% FDAP (W-8ECI/§871(d) election for net), CA 25% Part XIII (NR6/§216 election for net); AU/NZ have no PM rent-withholding (AU instead faces 15% foreign-resident CGT-on-sale + state land-tax surcharges). The Africa bloc has licensing regimes but enforcement-variable: ZA's Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019 (in force 1 Feb 2022, PPRA) expressly includes property/portfolio managers (no Fidelity Fund Certificate = no lawful remuneration); KE (EARB/Cap 533), NG (ESVARBON/NIESV), MU (REAA Act 2020) are primary-sourced but PM-scope or enforcement is thinner, while MA/EG/TN/GH/CV/SC/RW are intentionally terse and honest with `data not publicly available` markers. Commission bands are aggregator/market-norm throughout, tagged `(market-norm, cross-check ⚠️)`; FX repatriation friction is a recurring red flag (NG/EG/TN/GH/CV).

### Country one-liners

- **US United States** — Split: long-let residential PM vs short-let/STR (vacation-rental) management — distinct firms, distinct fee logic. Commission: long-let ~8-12% of monthly rent + a one-off leasing/tenant-placement fee (often 50-100% of one month's rent or a flat fee); short-let ~15-40% of rental revenue, ~25-35% common for full-service (Vacasa range ~18-35%) `(2025-26 market-norm, cross-check ⚠️ — Baselane/Vacasa/Evolve aggregators)`. Licence: real-estate broker / property-manager licence is **STATE-BY-STATE** — most states require the PM to hold a real-estate broker or salesperson licence (e.g. **California DRE**, **Florida DBPR/FREC**); a handful exempt or have limited PM-specific permits (some states require no licence for pure rent collection); trust/escrow ("client trust account") rules are also state-level — name 1-2 exemplars only, do not generalise to all 50 states. Reporting: AppFolio / Buildium / Yardi (long-let), Vacasa / Evolve own-portal (short-let); owner statements monthly; trust-account reconciliation is state-mandated where licensing applies. Tax role (major foreign-owner trap): a US property manager paying rent to a non-resident alien (NRA) owner is a **withholding agent** and must withhold **30% of GROSS rent** as FDAP tax and file **Form 1042-S** — UNLESS the owner gives the PM a completed **Form W-8ECI** electing under **IRC §871(d)** to treat the rental as effectively-connected income taxed on a **net** basis (then file 1040-NR); tenants/PMs are personally liable for un-withheld tax (IRS Pub 515; Williams Parker). Red flag: foreign owners blindsided by 30%-of-gross withholding when no W-8ECI/§871(d) election filed; PM commingling in states without a trust-audit regime; opaque short-let "host fee" stacking on top of commission. **Confidence**: HIGH (IRS primary; commission aggregator-tagged).

- **CA Canada** — Split: long-let residential PM vs short-let/cottage management (the latter increasingly squeezed by municipal STR bans, e.g. BC). Commission: long-let ~8-12% of monthly rent + tenant-placement (often 50-100% of one month's rent); short-let ~15-30% `(2025 market-norm, cross-check ⚠️)`. Licence: **PROVINCIAL** — **BC**: residential rental PM is licensed (BCFSA — formerly RECBC — under the *Real Estate Services Act*; separate rental-PM licensing tier); **Ontario**: real-estate agents are licensed under RECO (*TRESA 2002/REBBA*) but there is **no separate residential-property-manager licence** — pure PM can be unlicensed; **Alberta**: RECA licenses property managers — name BC/ON/AB as exemplars, do not generalise nationally. Reporting: owner statements monthly; trust-account rules provincial (BC/AB strong). Tax role: rent paid to a non-resident owner is subject to **25% Part XIII withholding on GROSS rent** (CRA), remitted by the Canadian-resident agent/PM by the 15th of the following month, NR4 slip issued — UNLESS the owner + agent file **Form NR6** (CRA-approved) to withhold 25% on **NET** rent, then the owner files a **§216 return** (CRA T4144); agent is liable + interest/penalty if it fails to withhold. Red flag: NR6 not filed → 25%-of-gross drag; ON owners assuming PM is licensed when no PM licence exists there; STR municipal-ban exposure mid-management. **Confidence**: HIGH (CRA primary; commission aggregator-tagged).

- **AU Australia** — Split: long-let residential PM vs short-let/holiday-let management (Airbnb concierge). Commission: long-let ~5-9% of weekly rent (national avg ~7.5%; CBD/high-density 5-8%, regional higher) + a one-off **letting fee** ~1-2 weeks' rent (avg ~1.4 weeks; up to ~4 weeks regional NSW); short-let ~15-25% `(2025-26 market-norm, cross-check ⚠️ — LocalAgentFinder/Houst)`. Licence: **STATE-BY-STATE** real-estate-agent / property-manager licence + mandatory **trust account with annual external audit** — **NSW**: *Property and Stock Agents Act 2002* (licensee-in-charge authorises all trust withdrawals; audit period ends 30 June, lodged with NSW Fair Trading by 30 September, s.115 qualified auditor); VIC (Consumer Affairs Victoria / *Estate Agents Act 1980*), QLD (*Property Occupations Act 2014*) parallel — name NSW as exemplar. Reporting: PropertyMe / Console / Property Tree dominant; trust-account-audit-driven owner statements. Tax role: **no PM rent-withholding** on resident or non-resident owners — BUT foreign owners face foreign-resident **CGT withholding on sale** (now 15% of price, no threshold, 2025 rules) and state **land-tax / absentee-owner surcharges** (e.g. VIC absentee surcharge, NSW surcharge land tax); the PM does not collect these but they shape net yield. Red flag: foreign owner conflating "no PM withholding" with "no tax" (CGT/land-tax surcharge surprise); using an unlicensed "letting agent" outside the trust-audit regime → commingling exposure. **Confidence**: HIGH (NSW legislation primary; commission aggregator-tagged).

- **NZ New Zealand** — Split: long-let residential PM vs short-let/holiday-home management. Commission: long-let ~7.5-9% of rent collected + a one-off letting fee (often ~1 week's rent + GST); short-let ~15-25% `(2025 market-norm, cross-check ⚠️)`. Licence: **residential property management is NOT a licensed activity in New Zealand** — the common claim that PMs must hold a Real Estate Agent licence under the **Real Estate Agents Act 2008 (REAA)** is **wrong for pure residential property management**: "real estate agency work" in REAA s.4 is transaction-bringing-about work and does NOT capture standalone residential property management, so residential PMs do **not** need an REA licence and are **not** overseen by the Real Estate Authority (REA); the **Residential Property Managers Bill** (introduced Aug 2023) would have created licensing/standards but the Government announced on **30 April 2024** (Housing Minister Chris Bishop) it would **not proceed**, and the Bill was **discharged in June 2024** — HUD still describes the regime as merely *proposed*, so the regulatory gap **persists** (HUD; Real Estate Authority; The Law Association; REAA 2008 s.4). Reporting: owner statements + trust-account practice is **voluntary/contractual**, not statutorily audited as in AU/ZA. Tax role: **no PM rent-withholding**; non-resident owner files an IR3NR / pays NZ tax on net rental income; no foreign-owner CGT regime (bright-line test applies to short holding periods only). Red flag (high): **no statutory licensing, no trust-account audit, no industry ombud for unlicensed PMs** → if a residential PM commingles, absconds with rent/bond, or is incompetent, a foreign owner has **limited regulatory recourse** (civil claim only); verify the PM is a member of a voluntary body (e.g. RPMA / REINZ) and audits its trust account contractually. **Confidence**: HIGH (REAA + HUD primary; the unlicensed status is the key correction).

- **ZA South Africa** — Split: long-let residential PM vs short-let (Airbnb) management; both fall under one regulator. Commission: long-let ~8-10% of monthly rent + a procurement/letting fee (often ~one month's rent); short-let ~15-25% `(2025 market-norm, cross-check ⚠️)`. Licence: the **Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019 (PPA)** came into force **1 February 2022** (replacing the Estate Agency Affairs Board with the **Property Practitioners Regulatory Authority, PPRA**) and its broadened "property practitioner" definition **expressly includes anyone who, for gain, manages property on another's behalf** — i.e. residential and commercial property/portfolio managers; such managers must hold a valid **Fidelity Fund Certificate (FFC)** (3-year validity) plus tax-clearance and BBBEE certificates; **no FFC = not legally entitled to any remuneration/commission**, and a payment can be reclaimed (PPRA; SAFLII PPA 2019). Reporting: PPRA-mandated trust-account + annual audit underpins owner statements; platforms vary (WeconnectU / Payprop common locally ⚠️). Tax role: **no automatic PM withholding** on non-residents — but a non-resident owner's SA-source rental income is **taxable in SA** (residence-based system; SA-source rule), the owner must register with SARS as a non-resident landlord, file the ITR12, and is generally a **provisional taxpayer** (two payments, Aug & Feb); DTA relief may apply (SARS). Red flag: PM operating **without a valid FFC** (illegal, no Fidelity Fund cover if rent is misappropriated); commingling where trust-audit lapsed; non-resident owner unaware of SARS provisional-tax registration duty. **Confidence**: HIGH (PPA/PPRA + SARS primary; commission aggregator-tagged).

- **KE Kenya** — Split: long-let PM vs short-let (Airbnb/serviced-apartment) management; short-let largely unregulated specialist firms. Commission: long-let ~8-12% of monthly rent collected + a letting fee `(market-norm, cross-check ⚠️ 🟡 — limited primary)`; short-let ~15-25% `(market-norm 🟡)`. Licence: estate agents (which by EARB practice covers property managers acting for a fee) must be **registered with the Estate Agents Registration Board (EARB)** under the **Estate Agents Act, Cap 533** (operationalised 1987); registration typically requires a relevant qualification + full membership of the Institution of Surveyors of Kenya (ISK) Valuation & Estate Management chapter (EARB; Kenya Law Cap 533); enforcement against unregistered operators is **weak in practice** ⚠️. Reporting: owner statements contractual; no statutory trust-audit regime as robust as ZA/AU — verify the firm is ISK/EARB-registered. Tax role: no formal PM rent-withholding mandate for non-residents in primary sources reviewed — non-resident rental income is taxable in Kenya (KRA); `withholding-agent treatment of PMs — data not publicly available — verify at kra.go.ke`. Red flag: unregistered "agents" outside EARB; no Fidelity-Fund-style cover → commingling/absconding risk; opacity for an absentee owner. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (EARB/Cap 533 primary; commission + PM-tax thin).

- **NG Nigeria** — Split: long-let PM vs short-let/shortlet (a large informal Lagos/Abuja "shortlet" market). Commission: long-let ~10% of annual rent + agency/agreement fees (commonly 10% agency + 10% legal at letting, market practice) `(market-norm, cross-check ⚠️ 🟡)`; short-let ~15-25% `(market-norm 🟡)`. Licence: estate surveying & valuation (the chartered profession covering property/facility management for a fee) is regulated by the **Estate Surveyors and Valuers Registration Board of Nigeria (ESVARBON)**, with the **Nigerian Institution of Estate Surveyors and Valuers (NIESV)** as the professional body — only registered ESV firms may practise valuation/management for reward (ESVARBON; NIESV); in practice a very large pool of unregistered "agents" operates the residential lettings/shortlet market. Reporting: contractual; no statutory trust-audit regime equivalent to ZA/AU — `owner-portal norm — data not publicly available`. Tax role: rental income is taxable (NRS, the Nigeria Revenue Service formerly FIRS / state IRS for individuals); `PM withholding-agent treatment for non-resident owners — data not publicly available — verify at nrs.gov.ng`. Red flag: unregistered agents, double/triple-agent fee stacking, FX repatriation friction for a foreign owner (CBN), high absconding/commingling risk outside ESVARBON firms. **Confidence**: MEDIUM (ESVARBON/NIESV primary; commission market-practice; tax thin).

- **MU Mauritius** — Split: long-let PM vs short-let (villa/IRS-ERS-PDS scheme) management; managed-estate (Smart City / PDS) often has a captive in-scheme manager. Commission: long-let ~8-10% of monthly rent + letting fee `(market-norm, cross-check ⚠️ 🟡)`; short-let ~20-25% `(market-norm 🟡)`. Licence: real-estate agents must **register with the Real Estate Agent Authority** under the **Real Estate Agent Authority Act 2020** (in force 1 Nov 2020) — registration requires a diploma or 5 years' experience **plus a financial security** (cash/bank bond/insurance/mortgage) callable to satisfy a judgment debt; whether *pure property management* (vs agency/transactions) is squarely caught is **less clearly delineated** in the Act ⚠️ — verify scope. Reporting: scheme-manager statements where in a PDS estate; otherwise contractual. ## 7 universal mitigations

Apply across all jurisdictions, strongest in unlicensed-PM and no-client-money-protection markets:

1. **File the non-resident-owner tax election before the first rent is collected** — W-8ECI (US) / NR6 + § 216 (CA) / NRL approval (UK) / the local equivalent — so the manager does not withhold on gross. This is the single highest-value action in the section.
2. **Get the full fee schedule in writing, all-in, modelled on realistic occupancy** — long-let % + every ancillary fee, or short-let % + cleaning/booking/linen — not the headline rate.
3. **Verify the manager's licence/registration against the named public register** (or confirm explicitly that the activity is unlicensed here) **and confirm client-money protection / trust-account audit** before signing.
4. **Sign a written management mandate with monthly itemised owner statements, audit rights, a defined fee cap, and a clean termination clause** — the contract is the only protection where no CMP regime exists.
5. **For short-let, confirm WHO legally holds the operating permit and where the non-compliance liability lands** — assume it lands on the owner unless the contract and the statute say otherwise.
6. **Keep the letting manager separate from the condominium/building administrator** — confirm exactly which property (your unit vs the common parts) each one manages.
7. **Cross-reference `--rental` (regime/yield reality) and `--scams` (manager-fraud register)** before committing — the management relationship is where a clean purchase quietly leaks return.

## Remote strata / condo governance exposure (absentee foreign owner)

If you buy into a building/scheme run by an owners' assembly (copropriété, condominio, Wohnungseigentümergemeinschaft, owners corporation, condominium corporation, HOA, MCST), you are bound by majority votes taken at meetings you will almost never physically attend. The four ways an absentee gets hurt: (1) your **proxy is invalid or your e-vote isn't offered**, so your share simply doesn't count; (2) a **low/no second-call quorum** lets a small attending minority pass binding resolutions including spending; (3) there is **almost never a statutory ceiling on special assessments** — a simple/qualified majority can levy an unlimited derrama / appel de fonds / special levy against your lot; (4) a **mandated reserve/sinking-fund study** either protects you (forced saving, priced-in) or its absence means a deferred-maintenance bomb lands as a lump-sum levy. Verify all four before offer — they are not in the listing.

Per-jurisdiction:

Universal remote-participation checklist — run these before offer; each maps to a sliver above and none appears in a listing:

1. **Pull the last 2-3 AGM/EGM minutes + the proxy form** from the syndic / managing agent / strata manager / property manager. Confirm the form is the current statutory form and that a non-resident may appoint a proxy (and whether e-voting/visioconférence/correspondence vote is actually offered, not just legally possible). No minutes provided = walk-away signal.
2. **Read the second-call / adjourned-meeting quorum rule for the jurisdiction** (ES/PT/DE/IT/BC/SG/NSW: a minority — sometimes one owner — can bind you). Assume any resolution can pass without you; budget for it.
3. **Ask in writing for any approved or pending special assessment / derrama / appel de fonds / special levy** and the funding resolution. Remember: in nearly every jurisdiction here there is **no statutory ceiling** (only US-CA caps board-only special assessments at 5% of budget without a member vote). Get the number, not a reassurance.
4. **Obtain the reserve-fund / sinking-fund study or depreciation report** (ON every 3 yrs, BC every 5 yrs, FL SIRS every 10 yrs, FR fonds de travaux + DTG, IT special fund per-works, CA every 3 yrs). Absence or "waived" status = deferred-maintenance liability that will arrive as a lump-sum levy on your lot.
5. **Check the reserve-fund balance vs. the study's recommended funding** and the building's age / next milestone or structural inspection date (esp. FL ≥3 storeys, BC, ON). An underfunded reserve on an ageing building is a priced-in future special assessment — negotiate it off the price.
6. **Confirm how meeting notices and votes will physically reach you abroad** (postal vs. portal vs. email; statutory notice periods are short — ES 6 days ordinary, SG proxy lodged ≥48h before). Set up a local proxy-holder or managing-agent standing instruction so your vote is never lost by default.
7. **Read the by-laws/règlement/regolamento for any restriction on proxy delegation count** (FR ≤3 / IT ≤1/5 / NSW ≤5% / SG ≤2) — if the building is proxy-farmed by a developer or block voter, an absentee minority owner has effectively no governance voice; treat as a red flag.
