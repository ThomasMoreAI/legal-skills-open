# Universal `--finance` Section

Foreign-buyer mortgage availability, LTV caps for non-residents, current rate environment, FX-loan rules across all **126 supported countries**. This is the universal `--finance` overview; the per-country tables below are authoritative (where a country has no domestic non-resident mortgage market the row says so honestly — "cash market" / "data not publicly available — verify with lender").

**Snapshot**: the original 87 rows are dated Apr–May 2026; the 23 post-103 jurisdictions (Crown Deps + DK territories + NL Caribbean + Caribbean UK-OT/CBI + na/bw/pk/tt/vu) were added **2026-06-13** from their playbooks. Rates move monthly — treat as point-in-time.

**Anti-hallucination**: every claim should be cross-checkable against the cited primary source. Where this section's value is not specific enough for a transaction, the per-country playbook + bank's published tariff is authoritative.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:
1. **Foreign-buyer access**: yes / restricted / effectively cash-only / banned
2. **LTV cap (non-resident)**: % vs resident benchmark + deposit needed
3. **Indicative 10-yr fixed rate** (Apr 2026)
4. **FX-loan rules** (post-2008 CHF crisis legacy where applicable)
5. **Banks with non-resident desks**
6. **Key caveats**: DTI cap, age limits, tax-ID requirement, min income, processing time
7. **Confidence**: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW

## Regional patterns

### Western Europe (FR, IT, ES, PT, DE, AT, CH, NL, BE, LU, IE, UK, GR, MT, CY)
Mortgages broadly available to non-residents but LTV caps drop 10–30 ppts vs residents. Eurozone 10-yr fixed clusters in **3.0–4.5%** band Apr 2026 (ES at low end ~2.55–4.0%, DE 3.5–5%, FR 3.3–3.8%). UK BoE base **3.75%** (held since Dec 2025 after Iran-conflict energy shock); UK expat residential ~4.0–6.0%. Switzerland is the outlier — SNB policy rate **0.00%** Mar 2026; 10-yr CHF mortgages **~1.4–2.0%** but non-resident LTV capped 50–65%.

### Western European microstates (LI, MC, AD, SM)
Three distinct regimes, all small markets where private banking dominates over retail mortgage products. **LI** (Liechtenstein) operates **GVG Lex Liechtenstein (LR 214.11) — non-residents are effectively banned from acquiring real estate**, with only ~5–10 special dispensations granted per year on demonstrable Liechtenstein-purpose grounds; CHF currency union with Switzerland since **1923 Customs Treaty** means CHF mortgage rates apply (LLB, VP Bank, Liechtensteinische Landesbank, Volksbank AG mortgages typically **1.5–2.5%** Apr 2026); Tragbarkeit calculations follow Swiss FINMA-style imputed 5% theoretical rate. **MC** (Monaco) is **open to all nationalities without restriction** — residency requires ~€500k bank deposit (practitioner bar, no statute), **no annual property tax / no CGT / no PIT** (French nationals carved out via [1963 Convention fiscale franco-monégasque](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000312026)); private-banking mortgages from CFM Indosuez, Compagnie Monégasque de Banque, Barclays Monaco, J. Safra Sarasin, EFG Monaco — **typical LTV 50–70% for HNW non-resident, EUR-denominated, 1.5–3.0%** Apr 2026. **AD** (Andorra) **Llei 36/2024 òmnibus law applies a ~10% non-resident real-estate surcharge** (per [BOPA Llei 36/2024](https://www.bopa.ad/)); residency Activa requires ~€600k investment OR €350k + €50k AFA deposit; mortgages from Andbank, MoraBanc, Crèdit Andorrà, BPA — typical **LTV 60–70% for resident foreigners, 50–60% for pure non-residents at 2.5–4.0% EUR** Apr 2026; INAF (Institut Nacional Andorrà de Finances) supervises.

### CEE / Baltics (CZ, SK, PL, HU, RO, BG, SI, EE, LV, LT, HR)
All EU. EU nationals near-resident terms with local income; non-EU pure-non-residents face 50–70% LTV. **FX-loan history is dominant policy memory**: PL CHF ban + ~700k Frankowicze, HU 2014 forced conversion + retail FX ban since 2010, HR Sept 2015 forced CHF→EUR conversion (now eurozone since Jan 2023), RO Oct 2016 forced CHF conversion at issuance-date FX. SK and CZ never had material CHF retail crisis. Rates **3.5–7.5%** for foreigners; HU highest (6–9%).

### Nordics (SE, FI, NO, DK, IS)
Mortgages available but strongly biased toward local-employment income. SE legal LTV cap 85% but banks internally apply 60–80% non-resident. NO 60–75% non-resident vs 85–90% resident. DK has best-in-world mortgage-bond system but secondary-residence and bopælspligt (residence requirement) constrain non-EU buyers. IS post-2017 capital-controls lifted; ISK volatility means foreign-currency-indexed structures common.

### Western Balkans (RS, ME, BA, MK, AL)
Non-resident lending case-by-case, harder, more expensive than EU. **Most foreign buyers pay cash.** Serbia NBS rules limit non-residents (Banca Intesa/Raiffeisen/OTP cited as most foreigner-friendly). ME has CKB, Hipotekarna, Lovćen Banka (dedicated non-resident product), Universal Capital Bank. BA limited specialist desks. AL has explicit deposit minimums: 15% lek primary residence, 25% euro loan.

### Anglo non-EU (CA, AU, NZ)
All three have **explicit foreign-buyer purchase restrictions**, not just lending caps. CA Prohibition Act extended through **1 Jan 2027**. AU **temporary ban on established/pre-owned dwellings through 31 Mar 2027** plus FIRB fees from AUD 15,100 plus state surcharges. NZ Overseas Investment Act 2018 bans most non-residents from existing homes; AU + SG citizens/PRs exempt; new builds OK.

### Latin America (MX, BR, AR, CR, PA, CO, UY, CL, PE, EC, PY)

### Caribbean (DO, JM, BS, BB, BZ)
Mortgages exist but expensive and paperwork-heavy → practical reality is **cash market** for most international buyers. MX 9% MXN, 30–50% deposit non-resident. BR 9.5–14.5% (Selic **14.75%** Mar 2026, cuts paused after Iran shock); CPF + permanent residency (CRNM) effectively required. AR rates 25–38% UVA peso → properties priced in USD, foreigners pay cash. CR 6–9% USD/7–10% CRC, up to 65% LTV. PA USD-dollarized, 60–70% LTV non-resident. **DO** USD-priced in tourist zones (Punta Cana, Las Terrenas) but DOP-denominated mortgages dominant — non-residents typically cash; Scotiabank DR/Banco Popular offer USD products at 7–9% to qualified buyers. **CO** mortgages denominated in **UVR** (CPI-indexed unit, BanRep) or fixed COP; BanRep policy rate **9.25%** Apr 2026; non-residents need CC/cédula extranjera + tax-ID NIT, LTV 50–70%. **UY** mortgages historically in **UI** (BCU CPI-indexed unit ~UYU 6.6 mid-2026); BHU + ANV state lenders are resident-only; foreigners use BROU/Itaú/Santander UY at 5–7% real over UI for non-VIS. **CL** prices/mortgages quoted in **UF** (Unidad de Fomento, BCCh-set daily inflation unit); CMF-regulated banks (Banco de Chile, BCI, Santander Chile, Itaú Chile, Scotiabank Chile) offer mortgages 4–6% real over UF to residents; non-residents face 50–70% LTV and additional documentation. **PE** (Peru) — **Border Zone 50km BANNED to non-citizens** under Constitution Art. 71 (terrestrial AND maritime borders); BCP, BBVA Continental, Interbank, Scotiabank Peru cap **non-resident LTV at 50–60%** (residents 70–80%); rates **7–10% PEN / 5.5–8% USD** Apr 2026; MiVivienda state subsidy is resident-only; USD pricing dominant ~30% of Lima/coast listings; PEN is managed-float (BCRP — Banco Central de Reserva del Perú) with minimal capital controls; notary mandatory (escribano público) ~0.5–1%; **SUNARP partida registral system** (national property registry — unique). **EC** (Ecuador) — **OPEN to foreigners** but Border Zone 50km + Galápagos require SECOB authorization; Banco Pichincha, Produbanco, BIESS cap **non-resident LTV at 50%** (residents 70%); rates **8–12% USD** Apr 2026; **CGT is 0% if held >5 years (UNIQUE)**; **USD officially adopted Sep 2000 — NO FX risk (UNIQUE)** but ISD 5% outbound capital tax; notary mandatory + Registro per Municipio (no national cadastre); **2024 Internal Armed Conflict Declaration** (Decreto Ejecutivo 110) raises security risk. **PY** (Paraguay) — **Border Zone Ley 2.532/2005 RURAL only** (urban Ciudad del Este / Encarnación are OPEN — common misconception that all border land is restricted); BNF, Itaú PY, Banco Continental, Banco Atlas, Banco Familiar offer mortgages — **non-resident LTV 50%** (residents 70%); rates **8–15% PYG / 6–9% USD** Apr 2026; **CGT effective ~2.4%** under Ley 6.380/2019 (30% taxable design with low effective rate — among LOWEST in South America); IVA 10% / 5% low-income housing; **territorial-source PIT** (foreign passive income generally NOT taxed); PYG is managed-float (BCP — Banco Central del Paraguay), relatively stable; USD informal pricing common; **Resolución SET 1186/2023 reformed RBI** — previously the cheapest formal RBI in South America (~USD 5,200) now requires **USD 70k SUACE business investment** (note "SUACE" = Sistema Unificado de Apertura y Cierre de Empresas).

### North America (US, CA)
**US** has the world's deepest mortgage market for non-residents — "Foreign National" loan products from non-bank specialists (Chase International is gone post-2024 retrenchment; HSBC, RBC City National, BMO Bank of Montreal Private, plus DSCR specialists like Lima One / Kiavi / Visio) offer **65–75% LTV for foreign nationals on US residential**, no SSN required (ITIN or passport), reserves 6–12 months PITI, rates roughly **+0.75 to +1.50 ppts above conforming 30-yr** (Freddie Mac PMMS 30-yr **6.75%** week of 2026-04-24, see [Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). Mortgage interest deduction cap **$750k acquisition debt** made permanent under OBBBA (PL 119-21, 4 Jul 2025). State-level non-resident lender variation high (FL/CA/TX/NY foreign-buyer-friendliest). CA Prohibition Act extended through **1 Jan 2027** (covered in the existing Anglo non-EU table).

### South Asia (IN, LK, MV)
**IN** (India) — residential property is **OPEN to NRIs (Non-Resident Indians) and OCIs (Overseas Citizens of India)** under FEMA 1999 + RBI Master Direction on Acquisition and Transfer of Immovable Property; **foreign nationals (non-NRI/non-OCI) are effectively BANNED** from acquiring residential property except via long-term residency under Section 6(5)/Citizenship Act and case-by-case RBI approval (rarely granted); agricultural / plantation / farmhouse land BANNED to NRIs/OCIs (Section 6 FEMA Notification 21/2000-RB). **Cadastre is DECENTRALIZED across 28 states + 8 Union Territories** (no national cadastre — UNIQUE) — Karnataka uses **Bhoomi**, Maharashtra uses **MahaBhulekh / 7-12 extracts**, Tamil Nadu uses **Patta-Chitta**, Telangana uses **Bhu Bharati** (successor to Dharani, Act No. 1 of 2025), Andhra Pradesh uses **Meebhoomi**, etc.; sub-registrar offices under state Registration Acts handle deed-registration. **SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Bank of Baroda + HFCs (LIC Housing Finance, PNB Housing, Tata Capital, Bajaj Housing Finance)** offer **resident LTV 90% (≤INR 30 lakh) / 80% (INR 30L–75L) / 75% (>INR 75 lakh)** per RBI Master Direction on Housing Finance; rates **8–12% INR** Apr 2026 (RBI policy repo **6.00%** Apr 2026 [verify]); **NRI LTV typically 75–80%** with proof of overseas income + NRE/NRO account; **foreign nationals (non-NRI/non-OCI) effectively NO mortgage market**. INR is **managed-float** by RBI with **capital controls under FEMA + LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) USD 250k/yr cap per resident**; NRIs repatriate sale proceeds in USD up to USD 1M/yr per individual via RBI scheme. **Stamp Duty 4–8% state-specific** (Maharashtra 6%, Delhi 6%, Karnataka 5%, Tamil Nadu 7%, Kerala 8%) + **registration 1%** = **5–10% all-in transfer cost**. Notary NOT mandatory in most states (registration via Sub-Registrar suffices); RBI macroprudential rules tighten cyclically on >INR 75 lakh tickets.

### MENA / Gulf (AE, IL, MA, EG, QA, SA, TN, JO, OM, BH, KW, LB)
Heterogeneous. **AE** has mature non-resident mortgage market post-CBUAE Mortgage Regulation Circular 31/2013 (revised) — first-property non-resident **LTV cap 75% on properties ≤AED 5M; 65% above AED 5M; second property 65/60%**; EIBOR-linked variable dominant (3M EIBOR ~4.0–4.4% Apr 2026 per CBUAE), 5-yr fixed available. **IL** Bank of Israel Banking Supervision Directive 329 caps **non-resident LTV at 50%** (vs 75% Israeli first home, 50% Israeli investor); BoI policy rate **4.50%** May 2026 (held through Iran-conflict shock); ILS dominant but USD/EUR mortgages available at some banks. **MA** Bank Al-Maghrib policy rate **2.50%** as of late 2025; CIH/Attijariwafa/BMCE/BMCI/SGMA offer non-resident MAD mortgages 60–70% LTV; **funds must enter via banque agréée intermédiaire with avis d'opération referencing Office des Changes Instruction 02** for repatriation rights. **EG** mortgages effectively unavailable to foreign non-residents — CBE Mortgage Finance Initiative subsidies are resident-and-citizen-only; **foreign-buyer market is USD-cash, gated by Form 4 inflow paperwork** (without it, future USD repatriation of sale proceeds is blocked). **QA** (Qatar) — foreign ownership is restricted to **9 freehold zones + 16 leasehold-99-yr zones** under [Law 16/2018](https://www.almeezan.qa/) (the 2018 freehold reform); QCB caps **non-resident LTV at 60–70%**, nationals 75% (per QCB instructions to banks); rates **~5.5–7.0% QAR** Apr 2026; QAR pegged to USD at **3.64 since 2001** (QCB fixed peg) → effectively zero USD-FX risk; QIB, QNB, Doha Bank, Commercial Bank of Qatar (CBQ), Ahli Bank Qatar (ABQ) all have non-resident desks; **NO recurring property tax / NO PIT / NO VAT yet** (UNIQUE — Qatar has not introduced VAT despite GCC framework agreement); transfer cost very low — **0.25% REA transfer fee + 1–2% notarial = LOW total** (under 3%); PR Law 10/2018 ≥QAR 1M (~USD 275k, capped ~100/yr); Investor Residency Law 21/2018 ≥QAR 730k. **SA** (Saudi Arabia) — **Law on Non-Saudis Ownership of Real Estate, Royal Decree M/14 of 14 Jul 2025, in force 22 Jan 2026** opens broader access (verify implementing regulations); SAMA caps Saudi LTV at 90% (with REDF subsidies), **expat-with-Iqama LTV 60–70%**; rates **5–9% SAR** Apr 2026; SAR pegged to USD at **3.75 since 1986** (SAMA fixed peg); **Mecca and Medina BANNED to non-Muslims** (constitutional/religious — UNIQUE); **NO recurring property tax (UNIQUE)** but **White Land + Vacant Property Fees up to 10%/yr banded, on idle urban land ≥5,000 m²**; RETT 5%; VAT 15%; **NO PIT** but Zakat 2.5% applies to Muslims; Premium Residency: **SAR 800k one-time / SAR 100k/yr renewable / Real-Estate-Owner SAR 4M tier**; Vision 2030 mega-projects (NEOM, Qiddiya, Diriyah, Red Sea, AlUla, Roshn). **TN** (Tunisia) — foreign acquisition outside ONTT-designated tourist zones requires **Governor's Authorization** (6–12 months, no statutory appeal — Code des Droits Réels 1957 + 1964 + 2024 update); agricultural land BANNED to foreigners; STB, BNA, Attijari Bank Tunisie, BIAT, BFPME offer **TND mortgages** (residents 70% LTV typical, foreigners typically need 30–50% deposit); rates **8–15% TND** Apr 2026; **FX-loans (EUR/USD) restricted post-2018** under BCT Circular 2018-04; TND is **managed-float SEMI-CONVERTIBLE** (NOT freely convertible — capital controls via BCT and Office des Changes); **IMF EFF was suspended Mar 2024** (Tunisia rejected the staff-level reform package). **JO** (Jordan) — foreign-buyer market is **OPEN under reciprocity** (Jordan Investment Law + Real Estate Ownership Law); **DLS (Department of Lands and Survey) under MoF — Tabu Torrens-style** registration; **HBTF (Housing Bank for Trade and Finance — largest), Arab Bank, Jordan Kuwait Bank, Cairo Amman Bank, Jordan Ahli Bank** offer **JOD mortgages — 75–80% LTV Jordanians / 50–65% non-residents**; rates **6–10% JOD** Apr 2026; **foreign USD-denominated 5–8% (limited availability)**; **JOD pegged to USD 0.7080 since 1995** (CBJ — Central Bank of Jordan — fixed peg) → effectively zero USD-FX risk for USD-priced sums; **Property Sales Tax 9% unified 2019** (was 4% buyer + 5% seller, [Income and Sales Tax Department](https://www.istd.gov.jo/)) + ~0.4–1% registration + ~0.5–1% notarial = **~10–12% all-in (HIGHEST in MENA)**; notary required + DLS registration. **OM** (Oman) — foreign ownership **restricted to Integrated Tourism Complexes (ITCs) under Royal Decree 12/2006** (non-GCC nationals); GCC nationals have broader access; **MHUP (Ministry of Housing and Urban Planning) Land Registry** + NSA (National Survey Authority); **Bank Muscat, Bank Dhofar, NBO (National Bank of Oman), Sohar International, Ahli Bank** offer **OMR mortgages — 70% nationals / 60% foreigners with residence**; rates **6–9% OMR** Apr 2026; **OMR pegged to USD 0.385 since 1986** (CBO — Central Bank of Oman — fixed peg) → effectively zero USD-FX risk; transfer **3% RD-Land + ~1% notarial = ~4–5% all-in**; notary required at MHUP for transfer registration. **BH** (Bahrain) — among **most open GCC for foreign buyers** under Law 2/2001 + RERA designated-areas list (extensive; verify at [RERA](https://www.rera.gov.bh/)); **SLRB (Survey and Land Registration Bureau)** records; **Ahli United Bank, BBK (Bank of Bahrain and Kuwait), NBB (National Bank of Bahrain), BisB (Bahrain Islamic Bank), Al Salam Bank, KFH Bahrain (Kuwait Finance House)** offer **BHD or USD mortgages — 80% nationals / 70% expats with CPR (Central Population Register card)**; rates **5–8% BHD/USD** Apr 2026; **BHD pegged to USD 0.376 since 2001** (CBB — Central Bank of Bahrain — fixed peg) → effectively zero USD-FX risk; **BHD has UNIQUE highest face-value globally (~USD 2.65/BHD)**; **SLRB Registration ~2% standard + 1.7% sliding additional = ~3–4% all-in**; foreign-buyer mortgages widely available (most open GCC); notary required at SLRB. **KW** (Kuwait) — **non-GCC foreigners CANNOT own real estate in Kuwait** (Decree-Law 74/1979 + amendments — only GCC nationals + naturalized Kuwaitis); some leasehold structures exist via specific commercial arrangements but practical foreign-buyer market essentially zero; **PACI (Public Authority for Civil Information) + MoJ (Ministry of Justice) Real Estate Registration**; **NBK (National Bank of Kuwait), KFH (Kuwait Finance House), Burgan Bank, Gulf Bank, KIB (Kuwait International Bank), ABK (Al Ahli Bank of Kuwait)** offer mortgages to Kuwaitis 70–80% LTV; effectively **NO mortgages for non-GCC foreigners** (cannot own); **KWD basket-managed since 20 May 2007** (was USD-pegged before; CBK — Central Bank of Kuwait — active); **KWD ≈ USD 3.25 — world's HIGHEST face-value currency**; transfer ~0.5–2% MoJ fee + ~0.5% notarial; notary required at MoJ. **LB** (Lebanon) — **POST-2019 BANKING CRISIS — banks effectively collapsed Oct 2019**; **BDL (Banque du Liban) "Lollar" (locked dollar accounts at official rate) crisis ongoing 2019–2026**; depositors faced effective haircuts; **mortgage market FROZEN** — pre-2019 banks Bank Audi, BLC Bank, Byblos Bank, Bank of Beirut, Fransabank, BLOM Bank, BBAC essentially do not extend new mortgages; **new mortgages limited to fresh USD entirely outside the formal banking system** (informal cash-based seller financing common); foreign buyers typically **pay CASH fresh USD**; **LBP HISTORICAL HYPERINFLATION** — LBP was **pegged USD 1,507.5 since 1997** → **decoupled Sep 2019** → black-market peak **USD 100,000+ in 2022** → unified 2023–24 around **USD 89,500–90,000** (verify daily on transaction); **General Directorate of Cadastre under MoF + Solidere BCD (Beirut Central District) separate regime**; **Registration 5.8% buyer + Notary ~0.6–1% + Stamp 0.4% = ~7–8% all-in**; notary required at General Directorate of Cadastre.

### East Asia (JP, SG, HK, KR, TW, MO, CN)
**JP** has the cheapest mortgage rates in the developed world but the strictest non-resident filters. Big-3 megabanks (MUFG, Mizuho, SMBC) generally refuse pure non-resident applications. The non-resident market = **Shinsei (now SBI Shinsei), Sony Bank, Tokyo Star, Aozora, Prestia (SMBC Trust)** — typically require **PR (永住権) or 5+ years JP income history**, with **50–60% LTV** on non-resident profiles, JP-resident guarantor. Variable rates **0.4–0.8%** for prime borrowers; 35-yr fixed **Flat 35** (JHF, [flat35.com](https://www.flat35.com/)) **1.84–2.06%** Apr 2026 for borrowers with PR/long-term-resident status. BoJ policy rate stepped from NIRP exit Mar 2024 → 0.25% Jul 2024 → 0.50% Jan 2025 → ~0.75% Dec 2025 ([BoJ MPM 251219](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2025/k251219a.pdf)) → 1.0% Jun 2026 → 1.25% effective 24 Sep 2026 ([BoJ MPM 260918](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k260918a.pdf)) — feeding gradually into variable-rate mortgages. **SG** has a deep mortgage market for non-residents but **MAS TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio) caps total debt service at 55% of gross monthly income** (MAS Notice 645), with **LTV at 75% / 45% / 35% for 1st / 2nd / 3rd housing loan** and tenor caps (max 30y HDB / 35y private; over-65 borrowers face shorter); **all new mortgages must reference SORA since 1 Jan 2025** (SOR/SIBOR retired) per [MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/notices/notice-645-computation-of-total-debt-servicing-ratio); ABSD foreigner stack 60% (residential) / 65% (entity) since Apr 2023 — buyers price the deposit gap in. **HK** **abolished ALL property cooling measures 28 Feb 2024** (Buyer's Stamp Duty + New Residential SD + Special SD cancelled per HKMA / IRD); **HKMA standard limits LTV ≤70% (≤HK$10M residential) / ≤60% (>HK$30M)** with stress-test rate of contracted-rate +200 bps and **DSR ≤50%** (HKMA Supervisory Policy Manual CR-G-3); 2047 New-Territories Crown-lease question is the structural question for any 30-yr mortgage horizon (most HK title deeds expire 30 Jun 2047 unless extended). **KR** Foreign Land Acquisition Reporting Act requires post-purchase notification within 60 days; **macroprudential DSR / LTV caps imposed by FSC + BoK** (Seoul/Gangnam/Gyeonggi LTV typically 40–50% in regulated speculative zones, lifted/relaxed cyclically); jeonse (전세) lump-sum-deposit market under stress 2022–24 led to HUG insurance reform; foreigners need ARC (Alien Registration Card) + Korean co-signer or guarantor at most domestic banks. **TW** **CBC (Central Bank Republic of China) selective credit controls Sep 2024** capped Taipei/New Taipei LTV at **60% on non-self-use** and **70% elsewhere** for natural-person second-home + corporate buyers (5th wave selective controls); Cross-Strait Act §69 restrictions on PRC nationals continue; foreign buyers (non-PRC) generally access non-self-use mortgages at 50–60% LTV via Cathay United, CTBC, Mega Bank, E.SUN. **MO** AMCM (Macao Monetary Authority) Notice 028/B/2017-AMCM caps **LTV at 70% (≤MOP 8M) / 60% (>MOP 8M)** for residential first-property; non-residents face an additional 10% NR-BSD (non-resident buyer stamp duty) on top of base 1–3% scale + 0.5% — total non-res stamp stack ~12–15%; mortgage market dominated by ICBC Macau, BNU, BCM, OCBC Macau.

### Southeast Asia (TH, ID, MY, VN, PH, KH)
Mostly **cash-market** for foreign buyers, with narrow exceptions. **TH** foreigners can buy condos (49% foreign-quota) but mortgage access is rare — UOB Thailand, ICBC Thailand, Bangkok Bank Singapore branch offer offshore-THB or SGD products to expats on work-permit (10-year visa); domestic Thai banks restrict to PR/work-permit holders. **ID** foreigners can hold Hak Pakai (right of use; up to 80 years cumulative on state / HPL land, max 30 yr renewable by deed over Hak Milik — PP 18/2021 Pasal 52) — domestic mortgages on Hak Pakai are scarce; HSBC Indonesia / DBS Indonesia / Standard Chartered provide some private-banking products to high-net-worth expats. **MY** has the most accessible foreign-buyer mortgage market in SE Asia — **BNM caps foreign-buyer LTV at 60–70%** per BNM credit guidelines; Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank, Hong Leong Bank, RHB all have non-resident desks; MM2H Gold/Platinum status marginally improves terms. **VN** 2024 Land Law (Decree 101/2024/ND-CP) **expanded foreign-owned credit institutions to act as mortgagees** (previously domestic-only), but practical foreign-buyer mortgage market still thin — most foreigners pay cash; HSBC VN, Shinhan VN, UOB VN offer limited products. **PH** BSP-regulated banks (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Security Bank, RCBC) offer foreign-buyer mortgages **at 60–70% LTV** to applicants with Philippine income or guarantor; rates **6.5–9% PHP** Apr 2026. Foreigners can own condos (40% foreign-quota) but **NOT land** (Philippine Constitution Art. XII §7).

### Eastern Europe non-EU (MD)
**MD** (Moldova) — residential property is **open to foreigners**, but **agricultural and forest land are banned** (Land Code Art. 6, only Moldovan citizens may own); flat **12% PIT and 6% CGT** (5-year primary-residence exemption) per [Fiscal Code](https://www.fisc.md/); mortgages thin and BNM (Banca Naţională a Moldovei) macroprudential rules apply; **Moldindconbank, Maib (Moldova Agroindbank), Victoriabank, Mobiasbanca-OTP, ProCredit Bank** offer MDL or EUR-indexed mortgages (typically **6–9% MDL, 5.5–7.5% EUR-indexed** Apr 2026); MDL is managed-float with severe volatility shock 2022–24 (Russia/Ukraine war + energy crisis). **Investor Residency Law 200/2010 €250k threshold → residency 5 years → naturalization at 8 years**; **CBI ENDED 16 May 2020 (Law 100/2020)**. Romanian-citizenship-by-descent path is the dominant EU-passport workaround. **Transnistria is OUT OF SCOPE** (no functioning property registry recognised by Chișinău).

### Central Asia (KZ, UZ)
**GE** has perhaps the most foreigner-friendly mortgage regime in the region — TBC Bank, Bank of Georgia, Liberty Bank, Credo Bank, Basisbank offer non-resident mortgages (50–70% LTV typical) on USD or EUR-indexed structures; GEL fixed-rate term loans rare beyond 5 years. **NBG (National Bank of Georgia)** macroprudential rules require loans under GEL 1,000,000 (unhedged-FX ceiling from 1 Jul 2026, NBG FSC) to be issued in lari unless the borrower's income is in the loan currency, with LTV capped at 90% GEL / 70% FX (80% for borrowers with income from abroad per NBG FSC Feb 2025; NBG's live PTI and LTV page and its FSR 2025 table disagree — confirm with the lender) — a meaningful restriction (see [NBG financial stability](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/larization-measures)). **TR** has near-zero foreign-buyer fixed-rate mortgage market in TRY because of CBRT's 2023–2025 rate ratchet (one-week repo peaked **50.0%** Mar 2024, down to **37%** since 23 Jan 2026 (held 10 Sep 2026) per TCMB); foreign buyers transact in **USD or EUR cash via FET-equivalent dekont** under MASAK AML rules; some banks (İş Bankası, Garanti BBVA, Akbank) offer USD-denominated mortgages 7–9% to citizenship-by-investment buyers above USD 400k. **AM** (Armenia) — **OPEN for residential** under Civil Code Art. 184 (no foreigner restriction); ### Sub-Saharan Africa (ZA, NG, KE, MU, CV, SC, GH, RW)
**ZA** has a mature mortgage market but a structural non-resident cap. **SARB Exchange Control Regulation A.1(B) and the FNB / Standard Bank / Absa / Nedbank / Investec foreign-investment frameworks** typically cap **non-resident LTV at 50%** without SA work visa; resident-foreigners with valid visa may access **70–90% LTV**. Repo rate **7.50%** Apr 2026 (SARB MPC); prime ~**11.0%**; 20-year ZAR mortgages priced prime-flat to prime+2.0 ppts non-resident. FICA source-of-funds + apostilled documentation adds 4–8 weeks; offshore-funds reverse-repatriation works via SARB endorsement at deed registration. **NG** (Nigeria) — **Land Use Act 1978 grants only LEASEHOLD via Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)** — there is NO freehold in Nigeria for any party (federal/state holds underlying title); **NO national cadastre** — fragmented across **LASRRA (Lagos State Resident Registration Agency) + LSLB (Lagos State Land Bureau) e-LandTitle** for Lagos + **AGIS (Abuja Geographic Information System)** for FCT + state Bureaux for other 35 states; **Access Bank, Zenith Bank, GTBank, UBA (United Bank for Africa), FBN (First Bank of Nigeria) + PMBs (Primary Mortgage Banks: FBN Mortgages, Imperial Homes, Aso Savings)** offer **NGN mortgages — 70–80% LTV** but rates **22–32% NGN** Apr 2026 (CBN MPR ~**27.50%** Apr 2026 [verify at CBN]) make NGN mortgages economically unviable for most buyers; **foreign-currency USD mortgages from Stanbic IBTC, Ecobank, Standard Chartered Nigeria — 50–60% LTV / 7–10% rates**; **NGN was unified + floated June 2023 → massive devaluation** (NGN ~460/USD pre-unification → 1,500+ peak black-market 2024–25 — verify daily); **informal USD pricing dominates prime real estate** (Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Banana Island, Lekki Phase 1, Asokoro, Maitama in Abuja); **State Consent Fee 8–15% Lagos / 8% FCT / 3–5% smaller states = MAJOR closing cost** (under Land Use Act §22 Governor's consent required for transfer); state law variance enormous; notary mandatory at deeds registry. **KE** (Kenya) — **DUAL legacy cadastre system** (RTA / RLA / GLA / LTA being **unified under LRA — Land Registration Act 2012**) — **Land Registry at Ardhi House (Nairobi) + County Registries**; **LIMS (Land Information Management System) digitization 2019–26 + Ardhi-Sasa portal** transitioning slowly; **KCB (Kenya Commercial Bank), Equity Bank, NCBA, Cooperative Bank, StanChart Kenya, Stanbic Kenya, Absa Kenya** offer **KES mortgages — 70–90% LTV** for residents; **foreign USD-denominated 50–65% LTV / 7–10% rates**; KES is **managed-float by CBK (Central Bank of Kenya)** — typical USD depreciation ~**2–5%/yr**; **informal USD pricing dominates prime Nairobi** (Karen, Lavington, Westlands, Kileleshwa, Runda, Muthaiga, Kitisuru); **Stamp Duty 4% urban / 2% rural** (Stamp Duty Act); **Affordable Housing Levy 1.5% gross** (Finance Act 2023 — under judicial review 2024–25, [verify final status at KRA](https://www.kra.go.ke/)); notary mandatory + **Land Control Board consent** required for agricultural-land transfers (LCB Act).

## Cross-cutting risks to flag

1. **FX-loan denomination vs income currency mismatch** — single biggest retail-borrower trap of last 20 years (CHF crisis). **Always cross-check `--finance` "FX-loan rules" against `--currency` "Currency code".**
2. **Foreign-buyer purchase bans dressed up as 'mortgages restricted'** — CA/AU/NZ rows make this explicit so a buyer doesn't conflate "70% LTV available" with "you can legally purchase". PH adds a Constitutional **land** ban (Art. XII §7) that does not block condo purchase but does block house+land — verify property type before quoting LTV.
3. **Eurozone accession transition (BG)** — contract repricing, dual-display deadlines, mortgage payment switchover are live April 2026 issues.
4. **Sanctions / PEP / AML overlay** in RS, AR, BR, BA, ME, EG, TR — non-resident mortgage approval depends on KYC/AML far more than rate. **EG Form 4** and **MA Office des Changes Instruction 02** are the two paperwork gates without which future repatriation of sale proceeds is structurally blocked — must be obtained at the moment foreign currency lands in-country.
5. **CPI-indexed mortgage units (UF/UI/UVR)** in CL/UY/CO are **not** simple fixed-rate loans — the principal escalates with inflation. A "5% real over UF" loan in a 4% CPI environment is a 9% nominal loan; modelling without the indexation adjustment understates payments. Always quote nominal CLP/UYU/COP at signing and re-state at each annual review.
6. **TRY hyperinflation** (Türkiye) makes any long-term TRY-denominated mortgage economically unviable for foreign buyers — the foreign-buyer market is **USD/EUR cash via FET-equivalent dekont**. Don't quote a TRY mortgage as a viable financing option.
7. **CBUAE LTV step at AED 5M** (75%/65%) for non-residents — buying a single property at AED 4.95M vs AED 5.05M shifts the deposit requirement by ~10 ppts of value (~AED 500k). Worth modelling at the deal level.
8. **JP Flat 35 ZEH discount** is materially valuable (−1.0 ppt for first 5 yr on a 35-yr fixed) — buyers should ask if the property qualifies before signing the variable-rate alternative. Tax deduction (住宅ローン控除) **0.7% × 13 yr** also stacks for ZEH/long-life housing.
9. **US estate-tax exposure** for non-domiciliary foreign individuals: only **$60,000 exemption** vs domestic $13.61M — a $1M property in personal name = $940k taxable estate at graduated rates to 40%. Mitigation via foreign blocker corporation, foreign trust, or US-Country estate-tax treaty (15 treaties exist) — cross-border-tax counsel before closing is non-optional.
