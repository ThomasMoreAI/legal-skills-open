# `--traffic` Extension — Transport-Noise Layer (Flight · Rail · Wind-Turbine Acoustic Exposure)

Where FLIGHT, RAIL, and WIND-TURBINE acoustic exposure is actually published per country — strategic noise maps (Lden/Lnight contours), the kind of public viewer that exists, and the wind-turbine dB statutes — and, more importantly, **which kind of noise-map system** the country runs, because that determines whether an address-precise contour exists at all. Loaded when `--traffic` is invoked.

**Snapshot**: June 2026.

**Scope**: this is the *acoustic-map* layer of `--traffic`. The core `--traffic` section already emits a single ROAD-noise verdict (🟢 silent / 🟡 light / 🟠 audible / 🔴 nuisance) **derived from vehicle counts** (TMJA / AADT / DTV / TGM / IMD) — count-inferred, road-only. This extension adds the **other three noise modes** — aircraft, rail, and wind turbine — read from **published strategic noise maps + statutes**, a higher-tier address-resolving primary artefact roads never had here. **Distinct from the road-count verdict** (that infers noise from a count; this reads an actual published Lden/Lnight dB band). **Distinct from `--risks`** (natural + technological hazards — gets a one-line cross-ref only). It pairs with `--traffic` (parent — the road verdict), `--amenities` (OSM proximity is the engine of the honest tail), and `--climate` (storm-season grid/airport disruption is a separate surface).

> **DECISION CAVEAT — read before quoting any number.** A strategic noise map is a **4.0 ± 0.2 m façade-height contour computed under CNOSSOS-EU** (END Annex II), *not* a measurement at this parcel. Report the map **BAND**, never a parcel-precise figure: write *"the address falls in the 55–59 Lden band on the `<map>` (strategic-noise-map contour, 4 m height — verify on visit)"*, never *"this house is at 58 Lden"*. This satisfies anti-hallucination Check 2 (no parcel-specific "is" statements) and the address-vs-commune trap — the contour is a road-segment / area-wide source, so the verb stays at map-band level.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **System type** (classify first — see the spine below): 🟢 national/regional PUBLIC address-zoomable viewer · 🟡 END-mandated maps exist but only via geoportal/WMS/open-data/per-agglomeration PDFs · 🟠 airport/rail-by-operator only (no national viewer) · 🔴 no public strategic noise map (OSM-proximity + WHO calibration only)
2. **The channel** — the named official viewer/portal + URL where one exists, OR the honest fallback (*"no national strategic noise map published — assess via `--amenities` OSM proximity + WHO 2018 thresholds; request operator contours; verify on visit"*). Never invent a viewer URL.
3. **Modes covered** — road / rail / aircraft / industry / wind, individually or combined — and which mode is **excluded** (e.g. UK Extrium excludes aircraft).
4. **Indicators** — Lden + Lnight (and the local label: CZ Ldvn/Ln, SI Ldvn/Lnoč, HU Lden/Léjjel, RO Lzsn/Lnoapte, FI Lden/Lyö — all the END Lden/Lnight pair; quote verbatim, then gloss). Switzerland uses Swiss **Lr** (LSV/OPB), **NOT** EU Lden — the dB bands are *not* comparable.
5. **Wind-turbine statute** (where confirmed) — the dB **limit** is a statute value, **citable as fact**; per-address exposure is **not** (that is a modelled prognosis, verify on consent conditions).
6. **The verdict** — the map BAND vs the WHO 2018 health threshold for that mode → 🟢🟡🟠🔴 (computed comparison, both numbers shown inline).
7. **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract.

## The system spine (classify first)

Whether an address-precise contour exists is set by the **public-access channel**, not by whether the maps legally exist. In the EU/EEA the maps are *always* mandated by law; the question is whether you can *see* them at a building footprint:

- **🟢 PUBLIC ADDRESS-ZOOMABLE VIEWER (road+rail+air)** — one government/regulator/public-body site lets a citizen zoom to a building footprint and read the Lden/Lnight band per mode, citable as the primary contour source. The confirmed-good set: **US (BTS), FR (Île-de-France — Bruitparif), DE (UBA+EBA), NL (Atlas Leefomgeving / RIVM), IE (EPA), AT (Lärminfo.at), ES (SICA/CEDEX), BE-Brussels (Geobru), DK (MiljøGIS), EE (Maa-amet Mürakaart), CZ (MZ geoportal), SI (ARSO Atlas okolja), HU (zajterkepek.hu), HR (HAOP/MINGOR), CH (SonBASE / map.geo.admin.ch — Swiss Lr, not Lden)**. UK-England (Extrium) sits here **with one exclusion**: road+rail only, **aircraft EXCLUDED**.
- **🟡 END-MANDATED, DATA EXISTS, BUT NO FRIENDLY NATIONAL VIEWER** — the EU END Directive *legally mandates* the maps and they are *published*, but public access is via geoportal/WMS/open-data/per-operator/per-agglomeration PDFs, not a single address-zoomable consumer viewer. **PT (APA PDFs), LU, MT, IT (per-comune), CY, PL, LT, LV, SK, FI, NO, SE, RO, BG, GR, IS, BE-Flanders/Wallonia.** The data exists — open the national geoportal / operator page and toggle the Lden/Lnight layer; the **EEA Noise Viewer ([noise.eea.europa.eu](https://noise.eea.europa.eu))** is the cross-EU/EEA fallback aggregating reported END contours. Never render any of these as "no map published".
- **🟠 AIRPORT/RAIL-BY-OPERATOR ONLY (no national viewer)** — outside the EU/US, aircraft (and sometimes rail) noise is published **per airport / per district plan**, not on a national viewer. **CA (Transport Canada NEF/NEP, airport-by-airport, voluntary), AU (ANEF charts per airport master plan + AS 2021:2015), NZ (NZS 6805:1992 ANB/OCB via district plans), UK-aircraft (DfT/CAA-ERCD + operator contours for Heathrow/Gatwick/Stansted).** Pull the contour for the **specific airport / district plan**; there is no national portal.
- **🔴 NO PUBLIC STRATEGIC NOISE MAP** — almost the entire non-EU / non-US / non-UK corpus (Caribbean, most of MENA, most of Asia, LatAm, Africa, Pacific) + the non-END microstates (AD/MC/SM/LI). No national strategic noise map and no citable national aircraft-noise standard confirmed. The honest output is OSM-proximity (`--amenities`) + WHO 2018 thresholds + "request operator-published contours where a major airport/rail/wind farm is within range; verify on visit" — **never a fabricated portal or a guessed dB band**.

## Verdict bands

Translate the **WHO 2018 Environmental Noise Guidelines for the European Region** ([who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789289053563](https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789289053563), released 30 Jan 2019; cross-confirmed by the EEA) into the skill's 🟢🟡🟠🔴 by mode. WHO recommends reducing each source **below** these levels to protect health — they are **health thresholds, NOT legal limits** (distinct from the citable wind *statutes* below). The verdict is a **computed comparison**: the map BAND minus the WHO value for that source. Show both numbers inline so the user can reverse the math.

**WHO 2018 thresholds** (recommend keeping exposure below):

| Source | Lden threshold | Lnight threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Road traffic | < 53 dB | < 45 dB |
| Railway | < 54 dB | < 44 dB |
| Aircraft | < 45 dB | < 40 dB |
| Wind turbine | < 45 dB (Lden) | *(no separate WHO Lnight value set)* |

> **Caveat on the wind 45 Lden figure**: the WHO landing page confirms wind turbine is one of the four covered sources, but the EEA page did not repeat the number — **re-quote the 45 Lden from the full WHO 2018 PDF before printing it as hard fact**. The road/rail/aircraft values are EEA-cross-confirmed and stand.

**Band mapping** (delta of the map BAND over the WHO threshold for that mode):

| Band | Trigger | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Map band is **below** the WHO threshold for that source | "Below the WHO health threshold for `<mode>` on the `<map>` contour — note as a strength. Still verify on visit." |
| 🟡 | Map band is **0–5 dB over** the WHO threshold | "Marginally above the WHO `<mode>` threshold (map band X vs WHO Y). Audible; monitor — glazing/orientation usually sufficient." |
| 🟠 | Map band is **5–10 dB over** the WHO threshold | "Meaningfully above WHO `<mode>` (band X vs Y). Plan/budget acoustic glazing + façade; pull the operator contour and assess on visit." |
| 🔴 | Map band is **>10 dB over** the WHO threshold, OR inside a statutory air-noise restriction zone (CA NEF >30, AU >20–25 ANEF, NZ inside ANB 65 dB Ldn) | "Significant — band X vs WHO Y, or inside a land-use noise-restriction contour. Aircraft Lnight especially drives sleep disturbance. Test/inspect; may walk away if unmitigatable." |

> **Lnight drives sleep disturbance more than Lden.** A 🟢 Lden can still hide a 🟠/🔴 Lnight — always read **both** indicators. Aircraft Lnight (WHO <40, the strictest threshold) is the one that most often flips an otherwise-quiet address.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **"There's a digital portal" ≠ "there's an address-precise dB you can cite at this parcel."** Every strategic noise map is a **4.0 ± 0.2 m façade-height contour** under CNOSSOS-EU (END Annex I/II) — an area-wide artefact, not a measurement at this house. The NL Atlas Leefomgeving says it itself: the map *"gives only a general picture and is NOT suitable for checking against legal standards such as façade noise load (gevelbelasting)"*. The US BTS viewer carries the same caveat: *"should not be used to evaluate noise levels in individual locations."* Quote the **band**; tag `(strategic-noise-map contour, 4 m height — verify on visit)`.
2. **The UK Extrium viewer EXCLUDES aircraft.** [extrium.co.uk/noiseviewer.html](http://www.extrium.co.uk/noiseviewer.html) maps England road + railway + Noise Important Areas only — **aircraft/aviation noise is published separately** by airport operators / DfT. Wherever aircraft noise is the buyer's concern (e.g. anywhere near Heathrow/Gatwick/Stansted), do **not** conclude "no aircraft noise" from a clean Extrium screen — point to the DfT/CAA-ERCD and operator-published contours instead.
3. **END maps are 4 m strategic contours, not a parcel certificate — and the "major" thresholds gate WHICH sources get a map.** END mandates maps only for agglomerations >100,000 inhabitants, major roads >3M veh/yr, major rail >30k trains/yr, major airports >50k movements/yr. **Below those thresholds, even an EU address may have NO mapped contour by design** — a rural dwelling outside the agglomeration/corridor collapses to the honest-tail template (OSM proximity + WHO). Don't over-promise the EU core.
4. **A wind statute LIMIT ≠ the measured exposure at this house.** DK 39/44, UK ETSU 35–40/43, DE TA Lärm 35, NL 47/41 are the *permitted ceilings* — citable as fact. Whether *this* dwelling actually sits at/under that ceiling is a modelled immission prognosis verified against the consent conditions, and France doesn't even use a fixed ceiling (it's an **emergence** rule — ambient-with-turbine minus background). Cite the statute as the limit; flag the actual exposure as `(modelled — verify on the consent/permit file)`.
5. **The `--traffic` road-count verdict ≠ an acoustic map — and Swiss Lr ≠ EU Lden.** The parent `--traffic` road verdict is *inferred from a vehicle count* (TMJA/AADT/DTV), road-only; this extension reads an *acoustic Lden/Lnight contour* for rail+air+road. Keep the two surfaces labelled. Separately, **Switzerland is the one non-END entry** — SonBASE / map.geo.admin.ch publishes Swiss **Lr (Beurteilungspegel, LSV/OPB)** day/night, **NOT** EU Lden — the dB bands are **not** comparable and must not be translated through EU/WHO Lden tables without flagging the metric difference.
6. **CA NEF / AU ANEF / NZ ANB-OCB are airport-specific land-use contours, not national viewers — and they carry their own restriction thresholds.** Canada (Transport Canada NEF: no new residential where NEF >30; complaints high >35) and Australia (ANEF: a site outside the **20 ANEF** contour generally needs no aircraft-specific construction protection, per AS 2021:2015) and New Zealand (NZS 6805:1992: **Air Noise Boundary 65 dB Ldn**, **Outer Control Boundary 55 dB Ldn** — noise-sensitive activities prohibited inside the ANB) are **per-airport / per-district-plan**, voluntary or council-discretion. Mark 🟠 and pull the contour for the *specific* airport / district plan. (NZ ANB/OCB values are from a SECONDARY reference — Environment Guide — pending the paywalled standard or a primary council district-plan rule.)
7. **Lnight drives sleep disturbance more than Lden — and the night band is the one most often omitted.** The Lden indicator carries a +5 dB evening / +10 dB night penalty already, but the *separate* Lnight layer (WHO aircraft <40, the strictest of all four sources) is what governs sleep. A coverage caveat compounds this: agglomeration-scoped maps (e.g. HU zajterkepek.hu, and many 🟡 geoportal layers) cover only cities >100k + major corridors — **a rural address outside those bands has no mapped Lnight contour at all**; the honest output there is the OSM-proximity + WHO template, not silence.

## Universal mitigations

Applicable across all 126 countries:

1. **Classify the system before trusting any map** — 🟢 address-zoomable viewer / 🟡 END-mandated-but-geoportal-only / 🟠 airport-by-operator / 🔴 no map. It decides whether you can cite a contour at all, or must fall back to OSM-proximity + WHO.
2. **Read BOTH indicators, and quote the BAND not a point** — Lden *and* Lnight (Lnight drives sleep disturbance; aircraft Lnight WHO <40 is the strictest). Always write the band + `(strategic-noise-map contour, 4 m height — verify on visit)`; never a parcel-precise dB.
3. **In 🟡 markets, open the geoportal / operator layer (or the EEA Noise Viewer)** — the data exists by law; toggle the Lden/Lnight WMS layer on the national geoportal, or use [noise.eea.europa.eu](https://noise.eea.europa.eu) as the cross-EU/EEA fallback that aggregates reported END contours.
4. **For aircraft, pull the airport's own contour** — UK Extrium excludes it; CA/AU/NZ are per-airport land-use contours (NEF / ANEF / ANB-OCB). Get the contour for the *specific* airport / district plan, and check the statutory restriction threshold (NEF >30, 20 ANEF, 65 dB Ldn ANB).
5. **In 🔴 markets, run `--amenities` OSM proximity + WHO calibration** — straight-line distance to the nearest (a) aeroway=aerodrome, (b) railway=rail mainline, (c) highway=motorway/trunk/primary, (d) power=generator + generator:source=wind. Distance + infrastructure class + WHO threshold gives a *directional* verdict ("likely above/below WHO road threshold"), NOT a dB number. Request operator/EIA contours; verify on visit.
6. **Treat a wind statute limit as the ceiling, not the exposure** — DK 39/44, UK ETSU 35–40/43, DE 35, NL 47/41 are permitted limits (citable as fact); FR uses an emergence rule (5 dB day / 3 dB night), not a ceiling. The actual exposure at this dwelling is a modelled prognosis — verify on the consent/permit file.
7. **Re-verify the viewer URL + the mapping round within 30 days of decision-day** — END maps refresh on a 5-yr cycle (latest round 2022; UK/IE Round 4 = 2021 data); viewers move (UBA path, BTS host, SE `/en/guidance/` path). URL-liveness ≠ data-still-current; a fresh round can shift a band.
