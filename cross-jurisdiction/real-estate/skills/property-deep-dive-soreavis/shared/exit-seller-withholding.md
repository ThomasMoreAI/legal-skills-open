# Universal `--exit` Section — Non-Resident-Seller Withholding & Tax-Clearance-Certificate Completion-Gate Extension

The property-country-side withholding / tax-clearance-certificate instrument a non-resident foreign owner must discharge before the sale can **complete**. Loaded when `--exit` is invoked alongside the per-country sell-side layer in `shared/exit.md`.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: the base `--exit` section covers sell-side *liquidity, time-on-market, agent commissions, sell-side closing costs*. This doc supplies the orthogonal **completion-gate** layer: the property-country (situs-side) non-resident-disposal **withholding-at-source** and/or **tax-clearance / compliance certificate** that legally gates the deed, the registry entry, or the release of proceeds. It is **not** the buyer's-residence-side tax (that is `--home-tax` — e.g. US §121/§988 on the seller's home-country return) and **not** the keep-or-sell-the-origin-home decision (that is the `--journey=foreign-buyer` sell-vs-rent extension). This is the transactional gate that must be cleared *before the title can transfer*.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Instrument** — the non-resident-disposal withholding and/or clearance/compliance certificate (named, with statute/form)
2. **Rate + basis** — rate(s), whether on **gross price** or **net gain**, threshold, holding-period bands (sourced + year, else `data not publicly available — verify at <tax authority>`)
3. **Who withholds / collects** — notary/escribano · buyer/payer-as-agent · self-assessment (this determines the failure mode)
4. **Gate point** — does it block the **deed authentication**, the **registry entry**, or only **proceeds release** — and the certificate lead-time if sourced
5. **Verdict band** — 🟢 / 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴 per the table
6. **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract

## The 3 structural models (the analytical spine)

Every jurisdiction's gate resolves to one of three — identifying which is the first task, because the failure mode differs:

- **Model A — Notary/escribano-collected retención that gates the escritura/registry.** Civil-law norm: the notary is the statutory withholding agent and *will not authenticate the deed* (or the registry will not inscribe) until the tax is collected/secured. Failure mode = the conveyance physically cannot complete. (e.g. RO, MX, PY, EC, AR, UY, IT <5yr, AT, CH, ID, PH eCAR.)
- **Model B — Buyer/payer-as-withholding-agent.** The purchaser (or their conveyancer) must withhold a fixed % and remit, on pain of becoming personally liable for the seller's tax. Failure mode = the buyer withholds (often over-withholds) and the seller chases a refund. (e.g. US FIRPTA, AU FRCGW, CA s.116, ES Modelo 211, IE s.980, NZ RLWT, MY RPGTA s.21B, JP, KR, IN s.195, ZA s.35A, CR, BR.)
- **Model C — No non-resident disposal CGT / self-assessment, transfer-tax gate only.** Either no CGT on disposal at all (the clean citable fact — most GCC, HK, SG, MO, several Caribbean) or the non-resident self-assesses post-sale with no completion gate (much of the Nordics, EE, HR, SI, PL, HU, UK NRCGT 60-day return). Failure mode = none at completion, but a later filing obligation + penalties; the *transfer tax / RETT / stamp duty* is a different instrument and must not be conflated with a seller-gains gate.

## Verdict bands

| Band | Trigger | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Model C — no non-resident disposal CGT, OR a fast routine clearance with no material lead-time | "Sale timeline unaffected; confirm the transfer-tax instrument, not a CGT gate." |
| 🟡 | Routine, predictable gate (notary-collected or standard buyer-withholding at a net or modest gross rate) | "Price the cash-flow timing + the certificate lead-time; standard." |
| 🟠 | Gross-basis over-withholding, OR a discretionary lower-rate/cost-basis certificate that must precede payment/deed, OR a multi-week clearance with refund-claim drag | "Mis-timing materially costs the seller; obtain the certificate before listing/payment." |
| 🔴 | Punitive default if a pre-payment/pre-deed certificate is missed, mandatory paid fiscal representative + long lead, or the gate can stall the conveyance for months | "Transaction-structuring issue — engage situs-country tax counsel before listing." |

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **The gate blocks COMPLETION, not just tax.** The deed/registry/proceeds are frozen until the certificate issues — AU ATO clearance certificate, IE CG50A, IL *ishur misim*, US Form 8288-B (~90 days), AR AFIP/ARCA certificate (~20 business days). Model the lead-time into the sale timeline, not the tax return.
2. **Resident exemptions usually do NOT apply to a non-resident.** "Primary-residence sales are CGT-free" is a *resident* rule — IL non-residents lose the principal-residence exemption; AU removed the main-residence exemption for foreign residents. Never carry a resident exemption into a non-resident sale.
3. **Withholding is often on GROSS price, not gain** — so it can exceed the real tax: US FIRPTA 15% of amount realized; ZA s.35A on price above threshold; CR 2.5% gross; VN flat 2% gross; PE punitive 30%-of-gross if the cost-basis certificate is not obtained *before any payment*. Over-withholding then needs a refund claim that can take a year.
4. **Who withholds determines the failure mode.** Model A (notary — conveyance can't complete), Model B (buyer liable if they don't), Model C (no gate but later penalties). Misidentifying the model mis-times the entire sale.
5. **A mandatory fiscal representative is a paid third party appointed BEFORE signing** — FR *représentant fiscal accrédité* (non-EU sellers, >€150k); equivalents elsewhere. A frequently-missed cost and lead-time that itself gates the deed.
6. **Transfer tax / RETT / stamp duty ≠ a seller-CGT gate.** Saudi RETT 5%, GCC registration fees, Caribbean stamp duty are transaction taxes, not non-resident-gains withholding. "No CGT" jurisdictions still have a transfer-tax gate — name the correct instrument; do not conflate.
7. **Cost-basis / lower-rate certificates are discretionary, time-boxed, and must precede payment or the deed.** PE SUNAT cost-basis certificate before any payment; IN Form 13 s.197; US 8288-B; AR ~20-business-day lead. Apply late and the punitive default applies irreversibly to that transaction.

## Universal mitigations

Applicable across all 126 countries:

1. **Identify the structural model (A/B/C) first** — it dictates whether the conveyance can stall, who is liable, and what to apply for.
2. **Apply for the clearance / cost-basis / lower-rate certificate before listing or before any payment**, never after — most are pre-deed or pre-payment gated and irreversible if late.
3. **Model the certificate lead-time into the sale timeline** (weeks to ~90 days) so the buyer's withholding obligation does not trigger by default.
4. **Confirm whether withholding is on gross or net** and, if gross, pre-plan the refund-claim path and its duration.
5. **Appoint any mandatory fiscal representative before signing** and price it.
6. **Separate the transfer-tax instrument from the seller-gains instrument** in the brief — both gate, but they are different liabilities.
7. **Engage situs-country tax counsel before listing** wherever the verdict is 🟠/🔴; this section flags the gate, it does not compute the liability.

## Regional patterns

### Western Europe + EFTA + micro-states + UK (R1)

- **ad** Andorra — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: *impost sobre les plusvàlues en les transmissions patrimonials immobiliàries* (**Llei 21/2006**, del 14 de desembre; text refós Decret legislatiu 29/04/2015; Reglament 17/01/2007). For a **non-resident transferor without permanent establishment**, a **5% retenció (pagament a compte)** of the total transfer value is operated as a guarantee at the deed; the excess over the tax actually due is refunded to the transferor within a maximum **6 months** of claim. (Residents get holding-period multiplicative coefficients; non-residents/legal entities do not, so non-residents are taxed on a less favourable base.) The notary handles the retention and the *Registre de la Propietat* inscription follows the plusvàlua liquidation — so it GATES completion. (Govern d'Andorra / Ministeri de Finances — Llei 21/2006, leslleis.com L2006021_TR1, 2015 text refós — confirm current rate at govern.ad.) Most binding completion-gate detail: 5% retention of total transfer value on the non-resident vendor at the deed, refundable within 6 months of claim. CORRECTION: framework statute is **Llei 21/2006** (not "21/2014" — 21/2014 is the Llei de bases de l'ordenament tributari, a different general procedural law); non-resident retention is a fixed **5%**, not a sliding-by-holding-period band.

- **at** Austria — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: real-estate gains are taxed under *Immobilienertragsteuer* (ImmoESt), §30/§30b EStG. The instrument is the **Parteienvertreter (Selbstberechnung)** — the notary/lawyer who draws the deed self-computes and remits ImmoESt at **30%** of the gain (special rate; reduced "Altvermögen" effective rates 4.2%/18% for pre-2002 acquisitions) to the Finanzamt, for residents *and* non-residents, at the latest by the 15th of the second month after the cash flows. Where the Parteienvertreter does not do the Selbstberechnung, a *besondere Vorauszahlung* of 30% is due. This is a withholding-at-completion that GATES the transaction (the Grundbuch *Selbstberechnungserklärung* is needed for registration). (BMF / Bundesministerium für Finanzen, EStG §30b, 2024 — may have changed; rate confirmed 30% special rate.) Most binding completion-gate detail: Grundbuch registration requires the Parteienvertreter ImmoESt self-assessment declaration.

- **be** Belgium — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: gains on Belgian immovable property held short-term by individuals fall under the *meerwaarde / plus-value* regime; the **non-resident income tax** (BNI/INR) return captures the gain, and from 2026 a general capital-gains regime applies. The notary collects all property-related tax debts at deed: the *notaire/notaris* requests fiscal **notification (Art. 433–435 Wetboek Inkomstenbelastingen / kennisgeving)** to the tax administration before passing the deed and must withhold from the price any tax debts the administration flags within the statutory response window — so it GATES completion. Headline non-resident rate cited at ~30.28% on certain gains (FPS Finance, 2026 — may have changed; cross-check, aggregator-influenced). Most binding completion-gate detail: notary's mandatory pre-deed fiscal notification + obligation to retain flagged tax debts from the sale price. (SPF Finances / FOD Financiën, fin.belgium.be.)

- **ch** Switzerland — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: real-estate gains are subject to cantonal **Grundstückgewinnsteuer / impôt sur les gains immobiliers** (purely cantonal/communal, no federal CGT). Same rules for non-residents as residents — tax tracks canton + holding period: ~30–40% for sales within 1–2 years, falling to ~10% after 20+ years' ownership. Crucially the **property itself is liable for the tax (gesetzliches Pfandrecht / statutory lien): if the seller does not pay, the cantonal authority can claim the unpaid gains tax from the BUYER** — so in practice the estimated tax is **deposited with the notary or municipality (Sicherstellung) out of the price** before the *Grundbuch/registre foncier* entry, which is when title legally transfers. This effectively GATES completion (buyer/notary will not release full proceeds nor proceed to registration without the gains-tax securitisation). (Cantonal tax administrations; cantonal statutes govern, e.g. ZH StG §216 ff.; comparis.ch / taxea.ch secondary corroboration, 2026 — confirm canton-specific rate at the cantonal Steueramt.) Most binding completion-gate detail: statutory lien on the property → buyer is secondarily liable, so estimated Grundstückgewinnsteuer is escrowed with notary/municipality before Grundbuch registration transfers title.

- **cy** Cyprus — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **Capital Gains Tax 20%** on the gain from disposal of Cyprus immovable property (Capital Gains Tax Law N.52/1980), applies to residents and non-residents alike (Cyprus-situs). The completion gate is the **Tax Clearance Certificate, Form N313**, stamped by the Tax Registrar of the Tax Department, plus separate municipal/communal and sewerage clearance certificates — the District Land Registry will NOT transfer/register title to the buyer until these are produced. The seller also pays a 0.40% levy to the Tax Department on the sale price at transfer. Lifetime exemption for all individuals, residents and non-residents alike: general €30,000 for disposals from 1 Jan 2026 (€17,086 to 31 Dec 2025); principal residence €150,000 subject to owner-occupation conditions; overall lifetime cap €150,000 (2026 data, source Cyprus Tax Department). This strictly GATES completion (Land Registry transfer is conditioned on N313). (Cyprus Tax Department, 2026 — may have changed; cross-check rate against Tax Department circular.) Most binding completion-gate detail: Land Registry refuses title transfer absent Form N313 CGT/IPT clearance.

- **de** Germany — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: a non-resident individual selling German real estate within the 10-year speculation period owes German *beschränkte Steuerpflicht* on the *privates Veräußerungsgeschäft* (§22/§23 EStG); after 10 years' holding (or qualifying own-use) it is **tax-free**. Germany has **no general statutory withholding at the notary on individual real-estate sale gains** and **no tax-clearance certificate gating the Grundbuch transfer** for the gain itself — the gain is self-assessed in the non-resident's German income-tax return. Practically, the *Notar* and *Grundbuchamt* do require the *steuerliche Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung* from the tax office for **Grunderwerbsteuer (transfer tax, a buyer-side liability)** before registering ownership — that clearance is the buyer/transaction gate, not a seller-CGT gate. So: no non-resident disposal-gain withholding; does NOT gate completion on the seller's gain. (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern / EStG §23, 2024 — may have changed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: no seller-gain clearance; Grunderwerbsteuer-Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung gates registration but is buyer-side.

- **dk** Denmark — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: a non-resident's gain on Danish real property is taxable under *begrænset skattepligt* / Ejendomsavancebeskatningsloven (the parcelhusreglen own-home exemption can apply). Denmark operates **no withholding-at-source on the buyer and no tax-clearance certificate gating tinglysning (Land Registry registration)** for the seller's property gain — the gain is self-assessed via the non-resident's Danish return to Skattestyrelsen. So it does NOT gate completion (the digital tinglysning system is not conditioned on a CGT clearance for the vendor's gain). Statute/rate detail for the non-resident band: data not publicly available in concise English primary form — verify at Skattestyrelsen (skat.dk) / Ejendomsavancebeskatningsloven. Most binding completion-gate detail: none — self-assessed; no statutory completion gate on the disposal gain.

- **es** Spain — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **Modelo 211 — 3% retención** of the agreed sale price, withheld by the BUYER from a non-resident vendor without permanent establishment and paid to AEAT within **one month** of the deed (IRNR, Real Decreto Legislativo 5/2004, art. 25.2 + RD 1776/2004). The buyer issues the non-resident the stamped "ejemplar para el transmitente no residente" copy of Modelo 211; without it the buyer is personally liable, so it functions as a **de facto completion gate** (notary records the retention). The seller then files **Modelo 210** within four months to settle final IRNR (general rate **19%** for EU/EEA, 24% otherwise) and reclaim any 3% over-withheld. The 3% is the binding mechanism. (AEAT — Agencia Tributaria, sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es, 2026 — may have changed; 3% and one-month confirmed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: buyer must withhold 3% and file Modelo 211 within one month or be personally liable for the vendor's IRNR.

- **fi** Finland — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: a non-resident's gain on Finnish real property is Finnish-source *luovutusvoitto* taxed as capital income (30% / 34% over €30,000 band; own-home exemption ≥2 years). Finland has **no buyer-side withholding and no tax-clearance certificate gating the registration of title (lainhuuto) at the National Land Survey** for the vendor's gain — it is self-assessed/declared to Verohallinto. So it does NOT gate completion. (Vero — Finnish Tax Administration, vero.fi "Capital gains in cross-border situations", 2024 — may have changed; rate 30%/34%.) Most binding completion-gate detail: none — self-assessed via Verohallinto; no statutory disposal-gain completion gate.

- **fr** France — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **prélèvement de l'art. 244 bis A CGI** on the non-resident's gain — flat **19%** (individuals) + social levies (7.5% solidarity for EEA/CH/UK-affiliated; **18.6%** standard for others from 1 Jan 2026 per LFSS 2026). The notary (*notaire*) computes, withholds and remits at the deed; for sellers resident outside the EU/EEA where price > **€150,000** a **représentant fiscal accrédité** must be appointed and the notary will NOT complete/release funds without the représentant's attestation. This is a hard withholding-at-deed that GATES completion. (impots.gouv.fr — DGFiP, CGI art. 244 bis A; 18.6% social rate 2026, LFSS 2026 — confirm current.) Most binding completion-gate detail: notary cannot pass the acte authentique without art. 244 bis A prélèvement settled and (non-EU, >€150k) a représentant fiscal accrédité in place.

- **gr** Greece — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: individual real-estate capital-gains tax (Art. 41 ITC, **15%**) is **SUSPENDED through 31 Dec 2026** (no CGT currently due for resident or non-resident individual sellers). However, the binding completion gate persists: the notary CANNOT draft/complete the transfer deed and the Land Registry/Κτηματολόγιο will not register it until the seller produces a **tax clearance / φορολογική ενημερότητα (no-debt certificate)** plus proof all ENFIA property tax (and any prior-year arrears/penalties) is settled and the property declared in E9; non-residents must keep a Greek AFM. So it GATES completion via the φορολογική ενημερότητα, even while the 15% CGT itself is suspended. (AADE — Independent Authority for Public Revenue, aade.gr "Property capital gains tax (Article 41)", 2026 — CGT suspension to 31 Dec 2026, confirm extension status.) Most binding completion-gate detail: notary/Land Registry require seller's tax-clearance certificate + ENFIA settlement; 15% CGT itself suspended.

- **ie** Ireland — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **Section 980 TCA 1997** — on disposal of Irish land/buildings the buyer MUST withhold **15%** of the consideration unless the vendor provides a **CG50A capital-gains-tax clearance certificate** from Revenue. Threshold: the withholding bites where consideration exceeds **€500,000** (commercial) or **€1,000,000** (residential); below that no withholding but CG50 still commonly used. A non-resident vendor obtains CG50A only by satisfying Revenue that no CGT arises or the CGT (33% rate) will be paid (often via solicitor undertaking to pay from proceeds). eCG50 issues within ~48 hours where straightforward; Revenue may take up to 35 working days. This GATES completion in practice (no CG50A → buyer withholds 15%). (Revenue.ie — CG50A guidance / Part 42-03-01a, 2025 — may have changed; thresholds €500k/€1m confirmed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: absent CG50A, buyer is legally obliged to withhold 15% of the price under s.980.

- **is** Iceland — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: a non-resident individual's gain on Icelandic real estate is Iceland-source and subject to **capital income tax (22%)**; own-residence relief can apply on reinvestment/holding conditions. The buyer/closing agent is generally required to **withhold tax on payments to non-residents** and the transfer is registered via Þjóðskrá/sýslumaður. Whether a formal seller tax-clearance certificate hard-gates the deed for individual real-estate gains: data not publicly available in concise English primary form — verify at Skatturinn / Iceland Revenue and Customs (skatturinn.is). Treat as: 22% capital-income tax on the Iceland-situs gain, non-resident withholding likely operated by payer — confirm gating mechanics with Skatturinn. Most binding completion-gate detail: non-resident gain is Iceland-taxable at 22%; payer withholding on non-resident — confirm registration-gate at Skatturinn.

- **it** Italy — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: residential-property gain is taxable only if sold **within 5 years** of acquisition (after 5 years fully exempt, all sellers). Within 5 years the vendor may elect at the deed for the **imposta sostitutiva 26%** (Art. 1 c.496 L.266/2005) which the **notaio withholds and remits** on the vendor's behalf — applies to non-residents on Italian-situs property; otherwise the gain goes into IRPEF (23–43%). The notarial election is the withholding mechanism; the rogito will state it. GATES completion only to the extent the notary will not finalise without the option exercised/tax paid where applicable. (Agenzia delle Entrate, L.266/2005 art.1 c.496; 26% confirmed, 2026 — confirm rate.) Most binding completion-gate detail: at the rogito the non-resident elects 26% imposta sostitutiva and the notaio withholds & pays it (only if sold within 5 years; else exempt).

- **li** Liechtenstein — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: real-estate gains are subject to the **Grundstücksgewinnsteuer** (Steuergesetz / SteG) on Liechtenstein-situs property, applying to non-residents. A clearance/payment of the Grundstücksgewinnsteuer to the Steuerverwaltung is required before the *Grundbuch* (Land Registry) entry of the transfer — so it GATES completion. Rate is progressive on the gain (degressive relief by holding period). Exact rate bands/threshold and the certificate name: data not publicly available in concise English primary form — verify at Steuerverwaltung Liechtenstein (stv.llv.li) / Steuergesetz Grundstücksgewinnsteuer. Most binding completion-gate detail: Grundbuch transfer conditioned on Grundstücksgewinnsteuer being settled with the Steuerverwaltung.

- **lu** Luxembourg — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: a non-resident individual's gain on Luxembourg real estate is Luxembourg-source (LIR / loi de l'impôt sur le revenu); speculative gain (held <2 years) taxed at progressive rates, longer holds at half the global rate with allowances. Luxembourg operates **no buyer-side withholding-at-source and no tax-clearance certificate gating the notarial transcription** for the individual seller's real-estate gain — it is assessed via the non-resident's Luxembourg return to the Administration des contributions directes (ACD). So it does NOT gate completion. Rate/holding-period detail: data not publicly available in concise English primary form — verify at Administration des contributions directes (impotsdirects.public.lu) / LIR. Most binding completion-gate detail: none — self-assessed via ACD; no statutory disposal-gain completion gate (notary does not withhold the seller's gain).

- **mc** Monaco — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Monaco levies **no personal income tax and no capital-gains tax on individuals' real-estate disposals** (no French-nationals exception aside) — there is therefore **no non-resident disposal-gain withholding and no CGT clearance certificate** gating the sale. The transfer is via a Monégasque notaire; the gate that exists is buyer-side registration/transfer duty (*droits de mutation*), not a seller capital-gains instrument. So: no seller-gain instrument; does NOT gate completion on a CGT basis. (Confirm with Direction des Services Fiscaux de Monaco, gouv.mc — no individual CGT, position stable but verify.) Most binding completion-gate detail: none on the seller's gain — Monaco has no individual real-estate CGT; only buyer-side mutation duty at the notaire.

- **mt** Malta — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **Property Transfer Tax (PTT)** — a **final withholding tax of 8%** of the transfer value (Income Tax Act, Art. 5A), **withheld by the NOTARY at the deed of transfer** and remitted to the Commissioner for Tax & Customs (CfR), for residents and non-residents (reduced rates 2%/5%/10%/12% in defined cases). A non-resident may, by producing to the notary a residence statement from their home tax authority, opt out of the older 12% basis into the relevant regime. Because the notary cannot publish the deed without computing and withholding the PTT, it strictly GATES completion. (PwC Malta / CfR — Income Tax Act Art. 5A; 8% standard PTT confirmed, 2026 — may have changed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: notary withholds 8% final PTT at the deed and cannot publish the transfer without it.

- **nl** Netherlands — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: the Netherlands taxes individuals' property under **Box 3** (deemed-return wealth tax), **NOT a realised capital-gains tax on disposal**, for non-business real estate; a non-resident owning Dutch property has limited Box 3 liability (rate 36% on deemed return, 2026; €59,357 exemption irrelevant to non-resident's Dutch-situs basis). There is **no disposal-gain withholding and no tax-clearance certificate gating the notarial *levering* / Kadaster registration** for an individual's sale gain. So: no realised-gain instrument; does NOT gate completion (transfer tax *overdrachtsbelasting* is buyer-side). (Belastingdienst — Box 3, 2026 — may have changed; pending Box 3 actual-return reform, confirm.) Most binding completion-gate detail: none — NL has no realised CGT on individual property; Box 3 deemed-return only; no completion gate on disposal.

- **no** Norway — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: gain on Norwegian real property is taxable at the flat **22%** general income rate for residents and non-residents (Skatteloven; own-home exemption if owned >1 year and used as residence ≥1 of last 2 years). Norway has **no buyer-side withholding-at-source and no tax-clearance certificate gating the tinglysing (Land Registry) transfer** for the seller's gain — it is self-assessed via the Norwegian tax return to Skatteetaten. So it does NOT gate completion. (Skatteetaten — Norwegian Tax Administration / Skatteloven; 22% confirmed via DLA Piper REALWORLD secondary, cross-check Skatteetaten, 2025 — may have changed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: none — self-assessed at 22%; no statutory disposal-gain completion gate.

- **pt** Portugal — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: real-estate gains by non-resident individuals are reported in the **IRS return Modelo 3 (Anexo G)**; since 1 Jan 2023 non-residents are assessed like residents — **50% of the gain** taxed at progressive IRS rates (up to 48% +solidarity), not the old flat 28%. Portugal has **no buyer-side 3%-type withholding at the deed and no CGT clearance certificate gating the predial registration** for the individual seller's gain — it is self-assessed. A **representante fiscal** is required for non-EU/EEA non-residents (EU/EEA optional). So it does NOT hard-gate completion on the gain (IMT/stamp duty is buyer-side). (Autoridade Tributária e Aduaneira — AT, portaldasfinancas.gov.pt; 50%-inclusion rule since 1 Jan 2023 confirmed, 2025 — may have changed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: none on the gain (self-assessed via Modelo 3 Anexo G); the binding seller obligation is appointing a representante fiscal if non-EU/EEA. CORRECTION: the widely-cited "flat 28% on the whole gain for non-residents" is STALE — repealed effective 1 Jan 2023; now 50% inclusion at progressive rates like residents.

- **se** Sweden — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: a non-resident individual's gain on Swedish real property / tenant-owner apartment is Swedish-source, taxed at an effective **22%** (22/30 of capital-gains tax base) (Inkomstskattelagen). Sweden has **no buyer-side withholding and no tax-clearance certificate gating the lagfart (title registration) at Lantmäteriet** for the seller's gain — it is self-assessed via the Swedish return to Skatteverket (deferral of gain on replacement home possible). So it does NOT gate completion. (Skatteverket — Swedish Tax Agency, skatteverket.se; 22% effective via PwC Tax Summaries secondary, cross-check Skatteverket, 2025 — may have changed.) Most binding completion-gate detail: none — self-assessed at ~22% effective; no statutory disposal-gain completion gate.

- **sm** San Marino — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: San Marino taxes capital gains on real-estate transfers under its general income tax law (Legge 16 dicembre 2013 n.166 IGR and amendments); a non-resident's gain on San Marino-situs property is San Marino-taxable. Whether a buyer/notary withholding or a seller tax-clearance certificate hard-gates the notarial transfer / registry: data not publicly available in concise English primary form — verify at Ufficio Tributario della Repubblica di San Marino (tributario.sm) / Legge n.166/2013. Treat as: real-estate gain taxable on SM-situs basis; gating mechanism (withholding vs self-assessment) unconfirmed. Most binding completion-gate detail: SM-situs real-estate gain is taxable; withholding/clearance-gate mechanics unconfirmed — verify at Ufficio Tributario.

- **uk** United Kingdom — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **Non-Resident Capital Gains Tax (NRCGT)** — a non-resident MUST file a **UK Property Disposal return and pay estimated CGT within 60 days of completion** (TCGA 1992 as amended; FA 2019 extended to all UK land), whether or not a gain or tax arises. Rates: 18%/24% residential, 18%/24% (or 20% historic) other (2026, confirm bands). The UK uses **no buyer-side withholding and no clearance certificate gating Land Registry registration** — enforcement is the mandatory 60-day return + penalties/interest, NOT a withholding or a completion gate. So it does NOT hard-gate completion (registration proceeds; HMRC enforces via the 60-day filing duty). (HMRC — GOV.UK "Capital Gains Tax for non-residents: UK residential property"; 60-day rule for completions on/after 27 Oct 2021 confirmed, 2026 — confirm rates.) Most binding completion-gate detail: no completion gate / no withholding — mandatory 60-day NRCGT return + payment is the binding obligation, even on a no-gain disposal.

(National Tax and Customs Administration (NAV) / PwC, 2026 — https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/hungary/individual/income-determination ; verify at NAV, https://nav.gov.hu). Most binding completion-gate detail: the notary is **legally required to collect the transfer income tax and the tax must be paid to the notary before the deed is authenticated** — no authentication → no Land Book registration. Hard, primary-confirmed registration gate.

- **kw** Kuwait — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: NO personal income tax and NO capital-gains tax on individuals in Kuwait; consequently NO non-resident-seller disposal withholding and NO tax-clearance certificate gating sale/transfer of real estate by an individual (note: foreign-property ownership by non-Kuwaitis is itself highly restricted under Kuwaiti law, a separate eligibility matter). Separate instrument (do not conflate): real-estate registration is handled via the Ministry of Justice Real Estate Registration & Documentation Department with registration formalities/fees — a registration duty, not a seller-gains withholding. GATES completion? NO (no seller-CGT/clearance gate exists for individuals) (PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Kuwait, 2025/2026, verify at https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kuwait; Kuwait Ministry of Justice, verify at https://www.moj.gov.kw). Most binding completion-gate detail: with no individual income/CGT regime there is no seller tax-clearance to file; registration formalities at the Ministry of Justice are the only completion condition and are administrative/transfer in nature, not a disposal-gains withholding.

- **lb** Lebanon — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: For a direct disposal of real estate by an individual NOT trading in real estate, there is generally NO capital-gains/profit tax in Lebanon — so no general non-resident disposal CGT-withholding on a private real-estate sale. EXCEPTION (do not conflate with direct sale): the 2022 Budget Law imposes a transfer tax on capital gains realised by individuals on disposal of SHARES in real-estate-holding companies at 3% (resident) / 5% (non-resident) — that is a share-disposal tax, not a direct-property withholding. Separate instrument: real-estate transfer/registration fees of roughly 5-6% (including a 5% transfer levy on fair value plus registration fee components, 0.4% stamp duty) are collected by the Directorate of Land Registration and Cadastre (Tabaris) and registration of the transfer cannot complete until these are paid — a transfer/registration duty borne on the transaction, not a non-resident disposal-gains withholding. GATES completion? Registration IS gated by payment of the ~5-6% transfer/registration fees at the Land Registry; there is NO separate non-resident disposal-gains clearance for a direct individual sale (share-route disposals carry the 5% non-resident tax) (Lebanon 2022 Budget Law; PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Lebanon Individual Other Taxes, 2025/2026, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/individual/other-taxes; Directorate of Land Registration & Cadastre, verify at https://www.lrc.gov.lb/en). Most binding completion-gate detail: a direct individual sale completes once the ~5-6% Land-Registry transfer/registration fees are paid (transfer duty, not a CGT withholding); only the SHARE-disposal route triggers a 5% non-resident gains tax.

- **om** Oman — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: NO personal income tax and NO capital-gains tax on individuals in Oman at present; consequently NO non-resident-seller disposal withholding and NO tax-clearance certificate gating the sale/transfer of real estate for individuals. (Oman has enacted a future personal income tax intended to commence 2028 — confirm scope/treatment of real-estate gains nearer the time; not in force for the disposal-gate analysis now.) Separate instrument (do not conflate): property-registration fees are collected by the Ministry of Housing & Urban Planning on registering the transfer — a registration duty, not a seller-gains withholding. GATES completion? NO (no seller-CGT/clearance gate for individuals under current law) (PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Oman, 2025/2026, verify at https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/oman; Oman Ministry of Housing & Urban Planning — the ministry's former `mhup.gov.om` host no longer resolves and `housing.gov.om` returns HTTP 403 to every client tried, so no primary page is citable: data not publicly available — verify with the Ministry of Housing & Urban Planning directly (2026-09-18 verified)). Most binding completion-gate detail: no individual CGT/income tax currently in force, so no seller tax-clearance exists; registration-fee payment at the Ministry of Housing is the only completion condition (transfer duty, not a disposal-gains withholding) — note the legislated 2028 personal-income-tax change to re-verify later.

- **qa** Qatar — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: NO personal income tax and NO capital-gains tax on individuals' real-estate sales in Qatar; consequently NO non-resident-seller disposal withholding and NO tax-clearance certificate gating sale/transfer of real estate for individuals. Separate instrument (do not conflate): a low property-registration fee (~0.25% on purchase) is collected by the Ministry of Justice Real Estate Registration Department on registering the transfer — a registration duty on the transaction, not a seller-gains withholding. GATES completion? NO (no seller-CGT/clearance gate exists for individuals) (PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Qatar Individual Income Determination, 2025/2026, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/qatar/individual/income-determination; Qatar Ministry of Justice Real Estate Registration, verify at https://www.moj.gov.qa). Most binding completion-gate detail: no individual income/CGT regime means no seller tax-clearance; the ~0.25% Ministry-of-Justice registration fee is the only completion condition and is a registration duty, not a non-resident disposal-gains withholding.

- **sa** Saudi Arabia — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: There is NO personal income tax / individual capital-gains tax on a natural person's real-estate disposal in Saudi Arabia. The applicable instrument is the Real Estate Transaction Tax (RETT) at 5% of the transaction value (not less than fair market value), under the RETT Law and Implementing Regulations effective 10 April 2025, administered by ZATCA — but RETT is a TRANSFER tax on the disposal transaction (primary liability on the seller/transferor, buyer jointly liable in avoidance cases), NOT a capital-gains/withholding on the non-resident's gain, and applies uniformly regardless of seller residency. RETT must be declared and paid to ZATCA and a RETT payment/exemption confirmation is required to complete notarisation/registration of the transfer — so RETT settlement (a transfer tax) does function as a registration gate, but it is not a non-resident disposal-gains withholding. GATES completion? Registration/notarisation IS gated by RETT settlement (a 5% transfer tax), but there is NO separate non-resident capital-gains withholding/clearance for individuals (Real Estate Transaction Tax Law & Implementing Regulations, effective 10 Apr 2025, ZATCA, https://zatca.gov.sa/en/RulesRegulations/Taxes/Pages/RETTRegulation.aspx; EY Tax Alert 2025, https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/saudi-arabia-issues-real-estate-transaction-tax-implementing-regulations). Most binding completion-gate detail: notarisation/registration of the transfer cannot complete without ZATCA RETT settlement/exemption confirmation (5% transfer tax) — a transaction transfer-tax gate, explicitly NOT a non-resident disposal-gains withholding; ZATCA may also reassess declared value within 3 years.

- **tr** Türkiye — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: YES on the gains side but NOT as a registration-blocking clearance — a non-resident individual selling Turkish real estate held LESS THAN 5 years is taxable on the value-increase gain ("değer artışı kazancı") under the Income Tax Law (Gelir Vergisi Kanunu No. 193); gains on property held 5+ years are EXEMPT. Taxable gains (above an annually-indexed exemption — 120,000 TRY for 2025) are taxed at progressive personal income-tax rates (the 2025 bracket span runs ~15% up to 40%), with the acquisition cost indexed to the PPI for inflation. The tax is SELF-DECLARED in an annual return (declared/paid the year following the sale, typically March), NOT withheld at source by the buyer. The title-deed transfer at the Land Registry (Tapu ve Kadastro) is conditioned on payment of the title-deed transfer fee ("tapu harcı", ~4% of declared value, shared seller/buyer) — that is a transfer duty, NOT the income-tax-gain clearance; the değer artışı gain is settled separately via the tax office, not at Tapu. GATES completion? The income-tax gain does NOT gate the tapu transfer (self-assessed post-sale); registration is gated only by the separate tapu harcı transfer fee (Income Tax Law No. 193; PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Türkiye Individual Income Determination, 2025/2026, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/turkey/individual/income-determination; Revenue Administration (Gelir İdaresi Başkanlığı), verify at https://www.gib.gov.tr; Tapu ve Kadastro Genel Müdürlüğü, https://www.tkgm.gov.tr). Most binding completion-gate detail: the non-resident değer artışı gain is self-declared to the tax office in the following year and does NOT block the tapu transfer; the only registration gate is payment of the ~4% tapu harcı (a transfer duty distinct from the disposal-gains tax), so a non-resident can complete the sale without a prior gains-clearance certificate.

### Asia–Pacific + Central & South Asia (R4)

- **au** Australia — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Foreign Resident Capital Gains Withholding (FRCGW) under Taxation Administration Act 1953 Sch 1 Subdiv 14-D. From 1 Jan 2025 the rate is **15%** of the contract price and the prior AUD 750,000 threshold is **REMOVED** — so it applies to ALL Australian real-property sales regardless of value. The PURCHASER must withhold 15% and pay it to the ATO at settlement UNLESS the vendor gives the buyer an ATO-issued **clearance certificate** (vendor proves Australian tax residency) before settlement; a foreign-resident vendor cannot get a clearance certificate but may apply for a **variation** to reduce the rate. (Australian Taxation Office, 2025, https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/foreign-residents-and-capital-gains-tax/foreign-resident-capital-gains-withholding/foreign-resident-capital-gains-withholding-overview). Clearance certificate est. processing: most issued automatically within days, up to 28 days if ATO review needed (ATO, 2025). Most binding completion-gate detail: no clearance certificate handed to buyer ⇒ buyer is legally obliged to withhold 15% of the FULL sale price at settlement (it is the buyer's liability, so conveyancers will not complete without it).

- **cn** China — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Individual Income Tax on property-transfer gains at a flat **20%** of the gain (Individual Income Tax Law); plus VAT, Land Appreciation Tax, deed tax and stamp duty settled at transfer. In practice the tax is collected at the local tax authority window and a tax-paid proof is required before the real-estate registration office (local 不动产登记 bureau) will register the title transfer; a separate **SAT departure tax-clearance** can apply to a foreign individual leaving China with outstanding IIT. (State Taxation Administration / PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, 2025, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/peoples-republic-of-china/individual/other-taxes). Est. processing time: data not publicly available — verify at State Taxation Administration (chinatax.gov.cn). Most binding completion-gate detail: title cannot be registered to the buyer until the transfer-related taxes (incl. 20% IIT on gain) are paid and the tax-paid certificate is presented to the property registration bureau.

- **hk** Hong Kong — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: NO general capital gains tax in Hong Kong (gains on a capital-asset disposal are not taxable; only if the IRD treats the disposal as a trade are they taxable under Profits Tax). Demand-side stamp duties (Buyer's Stamp Duty and Special Stamp Duty) were ABOLISHED for all residential transactions from 28 Feb 2024; only ad valorem stamp duty (Scale 2) remains. There is therefore NO non-resident-seller CGT withholding and NO seller tax-clearance certificate gating completion. (Inland Revenue Department / PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, 2025, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/hong-kong-sar/individual/other-taxes). Most binding completion-gate detail: completion is NOT gated by any seller CGT/withholding mechanism — a clean citable "no CGT" fact (the only seller-side tax risk is if the IRD recharacterises the gain as trading income under Profits Tax).

- **id** Indonesia — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Final income tax (PPh Final) on transfer of land/buildings at **2.5%** of the higher of transaction value or NJOP (Govt Reg PP 34/2016); for a foreign seller with no Indonesian tax number (NPWP) the rate can rise materially. The seller must pay the PPh Final BEFORE signing the Akta Jual Beli (AJB / sale-purchase deed), and the PPAT/Notary is legally PROHIBITED from signing the AJB until proof of payment (validated SSP) is produced; the validated SSP is then required for the BPN land-office title transfer. (Direktorat Jenderal Pajak, 2025, https://www.pajak.go.id/en/withholding-income-tax-article-42-corporate-taxpayer). Est. SSP validation: data not publicly available — verify at Direktorat Jenderal Pajak (pajak.go.id). Most binding completion-gate detail: notary cannot sign the AJB and BPN will not register transfer without the validated 2.5% PPh Final payment slip — a hard, statutory completion gate.

- **in** India — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: TDS under Income-tax Act 1961 s.195 — when a buyer purchases immovable property from a non-resident (NRI) the BUYER must deduct tax at source on the sale consideration / capital gain (long-term ~12.5% plus surcharge & cess; short-term at slab rate), and deposit it with quoting of buyer TAN. The non-resident seller can apply in **Form 13** to the Assessing Officer for a lower/nil-deduction certificate under s.197 so TDS is on the actual gain not gross consideration; issuance is discretionary and document-heavy. (Income Tax Department, 2025, https://www.incometax.gov.in / cleartax.in/s/section-195). Form 13 est. processing: officially up to ~1 month but commonly several weeks to a few months in practice (not an automated process) — treat as est., verify at Income Tax Department. Most binding completion-gate detail: without a Form 13 certificate the buyer is statutorily bound to withhold s.195 TDS on the GROSS consideration and is personally liable if he under-deducts — so buyers will not release full proceeds until TDS is settled.

- **jp** Japan — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Withholding under the Income Tax Act on a non-resident's disposal of Japanese real property — the PURCHASER must withhold **10.21%** of the gross sale price and remit to the tax office by the 10th of the following month, paying only 89.79% to the non-resident seller. Exemption only where price ≤ JPY 100,000,000 AND the buyer (individual) will use it as their own/relative's residence, or the seller is a Japanese resident. The non-resident reconciles via a final return (refund of any excess over true tax). (National Tax Agency, 2025, https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/individual/12014.htm). Est. refund via final return: data not publicly available — verify at National Tax Agency (nta.go.jp). Most binding completion-gate detail: there is no "clearance certificate" — the 10.21% gross withholding is automatic and the buyer's legal duty, so a non-resident seller always receives proceeds net of 10.21% unless a statutory exemption clearly applies.

- **kh** Cambodia — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Capital Gains Tax under **Prakas No. 1130 MEF.Prk.GDT (31 Dec 2025)** — which abrogated Prakas 346/2020 — at a flat **20%** on the gain (immovable property: deduct either an 80% standard deduction without documentation, or actual documented expenses); return filed and CGT paid to the GDT within 3 months of the gain being triggered. CGT on immovable property of physical persons has been DEFERRED multiple times — Prakas No. 1130 Art. 18(2) fixes immovable-property CGT for gains arising from 1 Jan 2027 onward (Art. 18(1) other capital assets from 1 Jan 2026; Art. 18(3) leaves indirect capital transfers to separate regulation); meanwhile the transfer of immovable property triggers a 4% property transfer (registration) tax payable before the cadastral/land office registers the new owner. (General Department of Taxation; KPMG Cambodia / DFDL summarising GDT, 2025, verify at https://www.tax.gov.kh). CGT est. payment window: within 3 months of the disposal (sourced). Most binding completion-gate detail: today the firmly-operative gate is the 4% transfer tax that must be paid before cadastre registration; the 20% CGT for immovable property is NOT yet in force (deferred to 1 Jan 2027 per latest guidance) — re-verify the effective date at GDT before relying on it.

- **kr** Korea (Republic of) — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Non-resident capital-gains on Korean real property is collected by buyer WITHHOLDING — the buyer (where required) withholds the LESSER of 11% of the gross sale price (incl. local surtax) or 22% of the net capital gain (incl. surtax) and remits to the tax authority, with the non-resident filing a CGT return to true-up. (National Tax Service, 2025, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-korea/individual/income-determination). Est. processing/refund: data not publicly available — verify at National Tax Service (nts.go.kr / Hometax). Most binding completion-gate detail: the buyer's withholding obligation (capped at 11% of price / 22% of gain) means the non-resident seller does not receive full proceeds at completion unless withholding is correctly discharged; confirm exact buyer-withholding trigger conditions at NTS.

- **kz** Kazakhstan — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Capital gains of a non-resident from Kazakhstan-situs property are subject to **15%** non-resident income tax (Tax Code); for individuals the gain is generally the excess of sale price over acquisition cost (a property held >1 year by a resident is often exempt, but the non-resident rule differs — re-verify). Whether the tax is collected by buyer-withholding vs. self-assessment, and whether a tax-clearance certificate gates the title transfer at the Justice/registration body: data not publicly available — verify at State Revenue Committee (kgd.gov.kz). (State Revenue Committee / PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, 2025, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kazakhstan/corporate/withholding-taxes). Most binding completion-gate detail: 15% non-resident rate on the gain is the headline; the collection mechanism (withholding vs filing) and any registration-office clearance must be confirmed at the State Revenue Committee before relying on it.

- **lk** Sri Lanka — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Capital Gains Tax under the Inland Revenue Act No. 24 of 2017 on the realisation of an investment asset (incl. land/buildings) — historically 10%; note Sri Lanka announced a CGT rate change effective 2026 (reported 15%) so re-verify the current rate. CGT is self-assessed and paid to the IRD; payment/clearance is in practice required for the notary attestation and subsequent registration of the deed. (Inland Revenue Department, 2025/2026, https://www.ird.gov.lk/en/Type%20of%20Taxes/SitePages/Capital%20Gain%20Tax%20(CGT).aspx Est. processing time: data not publicly available — verify at Inland Revenue Department (ird.gov.lk). Most binding completion-gate detail: CGT must be computed and paid on the gain (rate in transition — confirm 10% vs 15% at IRD for the disposal date) before the deed can be safely registered; no automatic buyer-withholding clearance-certificate regime confirmed.

- **mo** Macau (Macao SAR) — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Macau has NO general capital gains tax on the disposal of property by individuals (property-transaction gains are not subject to a dedicated CGT); a Stamp Duty on the property transfer (progressive, plus a special stamp duty / additional stamp duty regime on short-term resales and on non-resident/company acquirers) applies at transfer. There is therefore NO non-resident-seller CGT-withholding or seller tax-clearance certificate gating completion — the binding gate is stamp-duty payment at the Financial Services Bureau. (Financial Services Bureau (DSF) / PwC, 2025, verify at https://www.dsf.gov.mo). Most binding completion-gate detail: no seller CGT/withholding gate; transfer stamp duty must be paid at DSF before the registry will register the transfer — re-verify the special/additional stamp-duty position for non-resident parties at DSF.

- **mv** Maldives — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: The Maldives does not levy a personal capital gains tax on individuals on the sale of real property under the Income Tax Act (Law 25/2019); land ownership by foreigners is itself heavily restricted (foreigners generally cannot own freehold land), so non-resident "sellers" of real property are atypical and any disposal is usually of a leasehold/business interest taxed under business income rules. A specific non-resident-seller property withholding or tax-clearance completion gate: data not publicly available — verify at Maldives Inland Revenue Authority (mira.gov.mv). (Maldives Inland Revenue Authority, 2025, verify at https://www.mira.gov.mv). Most binding completion-gate detail: no individual property CGT regime identified; the operative constraint is foreign-ownership restriction on land rather than a seller tax-clearance gate — confirm any non-resident withholding on leasehold/asset disposals at MIRA.

- **my** Malaysia — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT) under the Real Property Gains Tax Act 1976. For a non-citizen/non-PR (Schedule 5 **Part III**) disposer the RPGT rate is **30%** on disposal within 5 years and **10%** in the 6th year and thereafter (unchanged since 1.1.2019), AND the acquirer must retain and remit part of the consideration to the **Director General of Inland Revenue within 60 days after the date of disposal** — **7%** for a Part III disposer (Part I 3%; Part II 5% within 3 years, 3% from the 4th year); failure adds a **10% increase charged to the acquirer**, and LHDN refunds any excess after assessment. **From the disposal of Year of Assessment 2026** the acquirer may instead remit an amount equal to the deemed-assessment amount, where the disposer notifies that amount before remittance, whichever is lower. (Lembaga Hasil Dalam Negeri / Inland Revenue Board — rates page last updated 23/06/2026, retention/remittance page last updated 25/05/2026; 2026-09-18 verified, https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/ckht/kadar-cukai-keuntungan-harta-tanah/ + https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/ckht/pegangan-dan-remitan-wang-oleh-pemeroleh/). Est. retention-refund: ~3–6 months (est., verify at LHDN). Most binding completion-gate detail: the buyer's solicitor is statutorily required to retain 7% of the price for a Schedule 5 Part III (foreign) seller and remit it to the DGIR within 60 days of the disposal — the seller never receives that 7% at completion until the RPGT assessment clears.

- **nz** New Zealand — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Residential Land Withholding Tax (RLWT) under the Income Tax Act 2007 — when an "offshore RLWT person" sells residential land and the bright-line test applies (2-year bright-line for sales on/after 1 Jul 2024), the VENDOR's conveyancer (or, failing that, the purchaser's conveyancer) must withhold RLWT = the LESSER of 33% (28% for a company) of the vendor's gain and 10% of the gross sale price, and pay it to Inland Revenue. The seller must complete an IR1101 RLWT declaration even where no deduction is ultimately required. (Inland Revenue, 2025, https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/buying-and-selling/residential-land-withholding-tax). Est. processing: data not publicly available — verify at Inland Revenue (ird.govt.nz). Most binding completion-gate detail: the conveyancer is the statutory withholder and must deduct RLWT before releasing settlement proceeds to an offshore vendor selling within the bright-line period — settlement funds flow net of RLWT.

- **pk** Pakistan — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: two stacked federal instruments under the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, both pegged to whether the seller is on the Active Taxpayer List (ATL/"filer"). (1) **Advance tax on the SALE under §236C — collected FROM THE SELLER at transfer by the registering authority** at **4.5% / 5% / 5.5%** of gross consideration (filer rate; ≤Rs.50m / Rs.50–100m / >Rs.100m bands), **11.5% flat for a non-filer**, **Finance Act 2025, eff. 1 July 2025** — and **§236C paid is adjustable against the final CGT**. (2) **Capital Gains Tax under §37(1A)**: for property acquired **on/after 1 July 2024 a flat 15% for filers regardless of holding period** (the holding-period concept was eliminated by Finance Act 2024; non-ATL → personal slab with a 15% minimum floor); property acquired on/before 30 June 2024 uses the legacy holding-period slabs (15% in yr-1 declining to 0% after ~6 years). **Diaspora relief (FBR primary)**: a **non-resident POC/NICOP holder** (present in Pakistan <183 days/yr) may access the **FILER §236C rate even without filing a return**, subject to FBR verification — material because the realistic non-resident vendor is an overseas Pakistani. Who collects: the **registering authority** collects §236C from the seller at the moment of transfer (so registration is the collection point), not a separate clearance certificate. Repatriation: RDA/Roshan Apna Ghar investments are "fully repatriable and subject to full and final taxation" (SBP primary); outside the RDA channel, repatriability depends on documented inward-remittance provenance — confirm the operative SBP FE-Manual clause for immovable property with the Authorised Dealer bank before relying. PKR is a managed float (not a peg); final court is the **Supreme Court of Pakistan** (no Privy Council). **Provincial stamp duty / registration fee / CVT / town-transfer are per-province and `data not publicly available — verify at the relevant provincial Board of Revenue / Excise, Taxation & Narcotics Control`.** ⚠️ FBR property slabs change with EVERY Finance Act — verify the current Finance Act. (Federal Board of Revenue — overseas FAQ (PRIMARY, eff. 1 Jul 2025), https://fbr.gov.pk/overseas-faqs/174240/174248 ; Income Tax Ordinance 2001 §236C / §37(1A) ; CGT rate cross-checked to PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Pakistan, Income determination; State Bank of Pakistan, https://www.sbp.org.pk/RDAC/Ghar.html.) est. processing time: §236C is collected at the time of transfer/registration (no separate certificate window). Most binding completion-gate detail: the registering authority collects §236C advance tax from the SELLER at transfer (filer 4.5/5/5.5% of consideration; non-filer 11.5%), creditable against the §37(1A) CGT (flat 15% filer for post-1-Jul-2024 acquisitions) — a non-resident POC/NICOP vendor can claim the filer rate without filing; verify the current Finance Act.

- **ph** Philippines — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: For real property held as a capital asset, Capital Gains Tax at **6%** of the higher of gross selling price or fair-market/zonal value (NIRC; BIR Form 1706), filed within 30 days of sale plus Documentary Stamp Tax. Critically, the BIR issues an **electronic Certificate Authorizing Registration (eCAR)** only after the CGT and DST are paid, and the Registry of Deeds will NOT issue a new title (TCT/CCT) to the buyer without the eCAR — this applies whether the seller is resident or non-resident. (Bureau of Internal Revenue, 2025, https://www.bir.gov.ph / RR 03-2019). eCAR est. processing: data not publicly available (varies by RDO) — verify at Bureau of Internal Revenue (bir.gov.ph). Most binding completion-gate detail: no eCAR ⇒ no title transfer at the Registry of Deeds — the eCAR (issued only post-payment of the 6% CGT + DST) is a hard, mandatory completion gate.

- **sg** Singapore — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Singapore has NO capital gains tax on property disposals (gains are not taxable unless IRAS treats the seller as trading in property, then taxed as income). The seller-side transaction tax is Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD) if sold within the holding period (3 years; 4 years for residential purchased from 4 Jul 2025), at 4–16% of price/value — but SSD is a stamp duty, not a CGT, and there is NO non-resident-seller CGT-withholding or seller tax-clearance certificate gating completion. (Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore, 2025, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property). Most binding completion-gate detail: completion is NOT gated by any seller CGT/withholding mechanism — a clean citable "no CGT" fact; only SSD (if within the holding period) and possible income-tax recharacterisation for traders apply.

- **th** Thailand — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Withholding tax on the seller's income from the property sale is collected by the LAND DEPARTMENT on the day of the title transfer (Revenue Code) — for an individual seller it is computed on the Land Department's appraised value with a holding-period deduction and progressive scale (companies a fixed 1%); plus transfer fee, specific business tax/stamp duty. For most individual non-resident sellers the tax is settled at the Land Office at transfer and that is effectively final. (Revenue Department / Land Department, 2025, verify at https://www.rd.go.th). Est. processing: settled same-day at the Land Office at transfer (sourced). Most binding completion-gate detail: the Land Department will not register the ownership transfer until the withholding tax and transfer fees are paid at the Land Office counter on transfer day — payment is a hard, in-person completion gate (no separate clearance certificate, but no transfer without payment).

- **tw** Taiwan — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: House and Land Transaction Income Tax (HLTI / 房地合一稅) under the Income Tax Act — a non-resident (no Taiwan residence permit / <183 days) seller is taxed at a FLAT **45%** if the property is held ≤2 years and **35%** if held >2 years, on the property-transaction income; Land Value Increment Tax is settled separately at transfer. A non-resident must file and pay the HLTI within 30 days of the transfer; tax payment is required before the transfer can be completed/registered. (Ministry of Finance / National Taxation Bureau, 2025, https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw/etwmain/en/announcement/alien-individual-income-tax/instructions). Est. processing: file/pay within 30 days of transfer (sourced). Most binding completion-gate detail: the non-resident seller's 45%/35% HLTI plus Land Value Increment Tax must be filed and paid in connection with the transfer — the very high flat non-resident rate (45%/35%) is the dominant economic gate; confirm the registry's payment-proof requirement at the National Taxation Bureau.

- **uz** Uzbekistan — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Capital gains of a NON-RESIDENT from the sale of Uzbekistan-situs immovable property are taxed at **20%** (Tax Code; non-resident "other income" rate). The tax is collected by WITHHOLDING at source — the obligation to withhold and remit is on the BUYER as tax agent (this is explicit for non-resident companies; non-resident individuals fall under the same withholding framework). Where the seller has no documents supporting the acquisition cost, the 20% is assessed on the GROSS sales price (no cost deduction). (Tax Committee; PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries citing the Uzbek Tax Code, 2025, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/individual/income-determination). Est. processing: withheld at the time of the transaction (sourced). Most binding completion-gate detail: the buyer is the tax agent and must withhold 20% (on the gain, or on the GROSS price if acquisition cost is undocumented) before paying the non-resident seller — the seller does not receive full proceeds at completion; confirm the individual-seller withholding mechanics at the Tax Committee (soliq.uz).

Verdict band: 🟢 (Model C — no non-resident disposal CGT; confirm the transfer-tax instrument, not a CGT gate). - **mx** Mexico — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Impuesto Sobre la Renta (ISR) on disposal of real estate by a non-resident (Ley del Impuesto sobre la Renta, art. 126 / Título V arts. 153 & 160 for residents abroad). Rate: a non-resident elects either **25% of the gross sale price** (no deductions) or **35% on the net gain** (with documented deductions / facturas); the non-resident cannot claim the residents' principal-residence exemption (no Mexican RFC / not resident). Threshold: no non-resident exemption; residents' one-off principal-residence exemption unavailable to non-residents. - **pa** Panama — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: on disposal of real estate the seller pays **2% Transfer Tax** plus a **3% advance income tax** on the higher of the transaction amount or cadastral value (Código Fiscal; the seller may elect the 3% advance as definitive, or be assessed **10% income tax on the actual capital gain** with the 3% advance credited and any excess refundable). Who collects: the **seller** is legally responsible; all taxes are paid through the **DGI eTax** platform — no escribano-withholding mechanism specific to non-residents. Clearance: an **eTax certificate for each tax (2% transfer + 3% advance income tax)** must be presented before the property title is transferred in the **Registro Público de Panamá**. GATES? - **pe** Peru — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Impuesto a la Renta on capital gain from disposal of real estate by a *no domiciliado* (Ley del Impuesto a la Renta, TUO D.S. 179-2004-EF, arts. 54 & 76). Threshold: the 5% favorable rate is conditional on obtaining the SUNAT cost-basis certificate before receiving proceeds. Who collects: the **domiciled buyer is the withholding agent** and withholds 5% (or 30% absent the certificate); the transaction needs the escritura pública and notification to SUNAT, with bancarized payment. Clearance: SUNAT **Certificado de Recuperación del Capital Invertido** issued before payment; buyer's withholding filing. GATES? Most binding completion-gate detail: the SUNAT cost-basis certificate (Recuperación del Capital Invertido) must be issued *before any payment* — otherwise the non-resident is taxed at 30% of gross market value instead of 5% of the gain.

- **py** Paraguay — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Impuesto a la Renta de No Residentes (**INR**) on disposal of real estate by a non-resident (Ley 6380/2019 "de Modernización y Simplificación del Sistema Tributario" and its regulations; Resolución General SET 42/2020). Rate: presumed net income of **30% of the sale price**, taxed at **15%**, i.e. an effective **~4.5% of the sale price** withheld at transfer (plus 100% of the VAT/IVA where applicable). Threshold: presumptive base — no cost-basis netting for non-residents; applies to physical persons, legal entities and other entities. Who collects: the **Escribano Público (notary)** withholds 100% of the IVA and INR on the non-resident's real-estate transfer (whether the non-resident acts directly or via attorney-in-fact), issues a Retention Receipt per tax, and submits authenticated copies to the **Dirección General de los Registros Públicos (DGRP)**. Clearance: notary-issued retention receipts filed with the DGRP. GATES? Yes — the DGRP will not process the public deed for a real-estate transfer without documentation evidencing payment of the corresponding IRP/INR. (Subsecretaría de Estado de Tributación (SET) / Dirección Nacional de Ingresos Tributarios (DNIT), 2026, https://www.set.gov.py ; Ley 6380/2019 ; RG SET 42/2020.) Most binding completion-gate detail: the escribano público withholds INR (~4.5% of sale price) and the DGRP will not process the public deed without proof of the IRP/INR payment.

- **tc** Turks and Caicos Islands — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: TCI has **NO income tax, NO capital gains tax, NO inheritance/estate tax, NO annual property/real-estate tax** — so there is **no non-resident-seller disposal-gain withholding and no CGT-clearance certificate** gating the sale; Model C. Gate point: the transfer is stamped (buyer-side) and registered — there is no CGT gate because there is no CGT. Verdict band: 🟢 (Model C — no non-resident disposal CGT; confirm the slab stamp-duty transfer instrument, not a CGT gate). - **tt** Trinidad and Tobago — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **CGT applies only to a disposal of a chargeable asset within 12 months of acquisition** — that gain is included with other income and taxed in the normal manner (individual bands **25%** up to TT$1,000,000 / **30%** above; TT$90,000 personal allowance), and a **disposal after 12 months is NOT subject to CGT** (PwC Income determination, reviewed 02 Jun 2026; statute frame Income Tax Act per the country playbook). There is **no buyer-withholding-on-disposal and no tax-clearance certificate** gating the conveyance for the seller's gain — T&T is English common-law (attorneys handle the Deed of Conveyance / RPA Certificate of Title; no civil-law notary), final appeals go to the **UK Privy Council (JCPC)**, and a <12-month gain is self-assessed via the income-tax return. The buyer-side transfer instrument is **stamp duty** (first-time buyer TT$0 on residential house+land up to TT$2,000,000; tiered above — **foreign-buyer eligibility for the first-time-buyer exemption is `data not publicly available — verify at IRD/BIR`**). **The dominant exit constraint is NOT a CGT gate but FX**: the TTD is a managed float (~6.74–6.78/USD, 2025-2026) and the **Exchange Control Act Chap. 79:50 was liberalised from April 1993 (no legal repatriation bar), but a genuine multi-year USD shortage means authorised dealers RATION forex** — a foreign vendor is paid in TTD and must source hard currency through a rationing authorised dealer to repatriate, which can be slow/uncertain (the single biggest practical exit risk; IMF 2026 Article IV + EUROCHAMTT). Who collects: BIR (stamp duty); Inland Revenue Division administers income tax/CGT. Gate point: no CGT-clearance gate on the deed — but proceeds *repatriation* (not the conveyance) is the binding friction. Verdict band: 🟡/🟠 — substantively Model C on a CGT gate (no withholding, no clearance certificate; CGT only on <12-month flips), but the USD-rationing repatriation drag means a foreign seller should price exit-FX timing and pre-arrange conversion with the authorised-dealer bank. Confidence: **HIGH** for the CGT <12-month rule + the FX-availability constraint (PwC + IMF Article IV + EUROCHAMTT; CGT band/allowance from PwC, gov tax portal stale on income tax — do not use it). (Inland Revenue Division / Board of Inland Revenue + PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries — Trinidad and Tobago, Income determination, reviewed 02 Jun 2026, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/trinidad-and-tobago/individual/income-determination ; Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago, https://www.central-bank.org.tt/ ; Foreign Investment Act Chap. 70:07 s.10(1); Exchange Control Act Chap. 79:50.) Most binding completion-gate detail: no CGT-clearance gate on the conveyance (CGT bites only on a <12-month disposal, folded into income tax 25%/30%) — the binding exit friction is FX: no legal repatriation bar, but USD rationing by authorised dealers can stall converting/remitting TTD sale proceeds.

- **uy** Uruguay — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: Impuesto a la Renta de los No Residentes (**IRNR**) on the capital gain from transfer of Uruguayan real estate by a non-resident (Título 8, Texto Ordenado 1996; IRPF Cat. - **vg** British Virgin Islands — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: the BVI is a **near-zero direct-tax jurisdiction — NO income tax, NO capital gains tax, NO inheritance/estate tax, NO corporation tax, NO VAT/GST, NO annual net-wealth tax** — so there is **no non-resident-seller disposal-gain withholding and no CGT-clearance certificate** gating the sale; Model C. - **bw** Botswana — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **NO separate flat CGT rate** — gains on Botswana-source immovable property are **folded into taxable income** and taxed at ordinary rates (individuals on the progressive PIT brackets; companies 22% CIT), with an **inflation-indexed cost base** (Income Tax Act; PwC Income determination). GATES: the disposal-gain side has no confirmed completion gate; the buyer-side transfer duty is the registration-linked charge — verify the disposal-gain registration mechanics at the Botswana Unified Revenue Service. - **na** Namibia — Non-resident-seller disposal gate: **NO capital gains tax on ordinary assets** — PwC verbatim "capital gains are not taxed in Namibia", which **includes residential property held as a capital asset** (exception: mining/petroleum licences; and property-trading profits can be taxed as ordinary *income* on a revenue-nature test). - **Barbados (bb) is a tax-clearance jurisdiction, not a CGT-withholding one.** No capital-gains tax; the binding completion-gate is the mandatory BRA Tax Clearance Certificate (statutory precondition to *any* conveyance, covering income/land/VAT/excise) plus Property Transfer Tax 2.5% + Stamp Duty 1%. Distinct from the LATAM escribano-retención pattern.

- **Argentina (ar) — ITI vs. Ganancias split is acquisition-date-dependent.** For property *acquired before 1 Jan 2018* the legacy ITI (Ley 23.905, 1.5% on transfer price) applies; for property *acquired on/after 1 Jan 2018* the 15% cedular Impuesto a las Ganancias on the gain applies. The bullet states both. Also: AFIP was renamed **ARCA** (Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero) in Oct 2024 — both names retained for traceability since statutes/URLs still resolve under afip.gob.ar.

- **Costa Rica (cr) — buyer withholds, NOT the notary.** Unlike the LATAM escribano-retención model in the grouping prompt, CR's non-resident mechanism is a **2.5% buyer withholding** on gross sale price (DGT resolution, Form 129 via TRIBU-CR within 15 days of the transaction month), not a notario-collected retención. The 2.25% election applies only to pre-1 Jul 2019 (gross-rate option) / pre-1 Jul 2020 (special withholding) acquired assets. Distinguished from CL/CO/AR/PE/EC/UY/PY.

- **Peru (pe) — the SUNAT cost-basis certificate is a hard economic gate, not a registry block.** Clarified that the 5% definitive rate for non-domiciled sellers is *conditional* on the Certificado de Recuperación del Capital Invertido being issued *before any payment*; absent it, the base is 30% of gross market value. This is the most consequential non-resident-specific trap among the 16 and is flagged as such rather than mis-stated as a notary gate.

- **Dominican Republic (do) — CGT headline rate flagged for primary-source verification.** Aggregator/DGII-reported guidance gives ~27% on the gain for foreign individuals (the standard top income-tax rate); sources conflate the headline income-tax rate with the non-resident CGT treatment. Bullet carries an explicit "verify the exact non-resident treatment with DGII" marker per the anti-hallucination contract. The 3% transfer tax + DGII/RNC registration are the firmer registry-level gates.

- **Mexico (mx) — primary SAT URL not directly retrieved; statute cited, source labelled.** The notario-as-withholding-agent rule and 25%-gross / 35%-net non-resident election are well-established (Ley del ISR arts. 126/153/160) but the supporting search hits were practitioner pages, not sat.gob.mx directly; the bullet cites the statute and labels the authority with a "verify at https://www.sat.gob.mx" qualifier rather than asserting a SAT-page URL.

- **Chile (cl) — gain tax is largely declared/withheld, not a registration-blocking certificate.** The notary/Conservador Formulario 2890 filing is an informational/procedural condition of the transfer; the non-domiciled 35% Additional Tax is assessed/withheld rather than enforced via a registry-blocking clearance certificate. Bullet marks the gate as "partial" to avoid over-stating it as an escritura block.

- **Colombia (co) — ganancia ocasional requires >2-year holding.** If the property is held <2 years the gain is ordinary income (not the flat 15% ganancia ocasional). The 15% source withholding for non-residents (no Colombian domicile) is distinct from the notario's retención en la fuente at the escritura; both are stated.

- **Ecuador (ec) — two-layer tax (municipal COOTAD + national LRTI).** The registry/notary gate is specifically the **municipal** utilidad/plusvalía receipt (COOTAD art. 556), not the national income tax — the bullet keeps these layers distinct because conflating them is a common error in aggregator sources.

- **Paraguay (py) — INR effective rate stated as derived, not asserted flat.** The ~4.5% figure is shown as a computed product (30% presumptive base × 15% rate) per RG SET 42/2020, with inputs visible inline, satisfying the "computed transparently" branch of the anti-hallucination contract rather than presenting 4.5% as a bare statutory rate.

- **Uruguay (uy) — escribano tax-control gate stated "in practice".** IRNR operates through source-withholding (buyer/payer); the escribano's role is tax-control + registration, so the registry gate is characterized as the escribano's compliance-control function rather than a discrete statutory clearance certificate, to avoid over-asserting the mechanism.
