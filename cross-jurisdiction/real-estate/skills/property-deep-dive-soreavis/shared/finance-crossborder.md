# Universal `--finance` Section — Cross-Border Mortgage & Alternative-Financing Extension

Non-resident foreign-buyer mortgage access, broker network, company/SPV purchase route and crypto-payment legality for the property's country. Loaded when `--finance` is invoked alongside the per-country mortgage layer in each playbook and the `shared/finance-banking.md` non-resident-bank-account extension.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: the base `--finance` section gives the per-country mortgage-availability / LTV / rate / FX-loan picture, and `shared/finance-banking.md` covers the parallel non-resident *bank-account* access layer. This doc supplies the orthogonal **cross-border financing** layer: (i) the realistic non-resident LTV ceiling and which lenders actually write to a foreign non-resident; (ii) the named cross-border mortgage-broker / expat-desk network; (iii) the company / SPV purchase route (where legal, the vehicle, and its key downside); (iv) crypto-payment legality for property. It does **not** duplicate the bank-account chain in `finance-banking.md`.

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Non-resident LTV ceiling** — the cap a foreign non-resident realistically gets (with primary source + year), distinct from the resident/owner-occupier cap
2. **Lender reality** — that only a subset of banks lend to non-residents (the "3 of 8 banks" pattern is anecdotal — never stated as a figure); name the banks/desks that do
3. **Named cross-border broker / expat desk** — real, verifiable firm with a non-resident desk, or "none located"
4. **Company / SPV purchase route** — legal? the vehicle (GmbH / SOPARFI / SL / BV / Ltd / LLC / trust), and the key tax/structural downside
5. **Crypto-payment legality** — legal / grey / prohibited per the financial regulator, plus any *verifiably real* developer/platform that accepts crypto for property
6. **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract

## Verdict bands

| Band | Trigger | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Non-resident mortgage market exists with a named LTV cap + ≥1 named non-res lender/broker; clear SPV route | "Financing feasible; underwrite at the stated non-res LTV, not the resident cap." |
| 🟡 | Non-resident lending exists but thin (few lenders, lower LTV, broker effectively required) OR SPV route carries a material tax downside | "Budget a larger cash component; engage a named broker pre-offer; price the SPV downside." |
| 🟠 | Non-resident mortgage near-absent (cash market in practice) OR currency-mismatch risk is structural (loan currency ≠ income currency) | "Assume cash purchase; if borrowing, model the FX-mismatch downside explicitly." |
| 🔴 | Foreign-buyer lending prohibited / foreign-buyer purchase itself restricted, OR crypto-for-property prohibited and mis-sold | "Financing thesis structurally void — verify the ownership-eligibility gate before anything else." |

The verdict turns on **realistic access**, not headline availability: a country can advertise mortgages yet, in practice, write none to a foreign non-resident.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **The resident LTV is not the non-resident LTV.** Headline LTV caps (regulator macroprudential limits) describe owner-occupiers/residents; the non-resident foreign-buyer cap is set by *bank credit policy* and is materially lower (commonly 50-70% vs 80-90%). Underwriting at the headline cap over-states leverage and under-states the cash requirement.
2. **Only a minority of banks lend to a foreign non-resident** — the rest decline at credit-policy, not pricing. The decisive question is *which named lender/desk will write the file at all*, before rate. Treat broad "mortgages available" claims as unverified until a specific lender/desk is named.
3. **Currency mismatch is the silent killer.** A EUR mortgage serviced from USD/GBP income (or a CHF/EUR loan, the 2008-2012 HU/PL/HR/RS lesson) can blow the debt-service ratio on FX alone, independent of rate. National FX-loan conversion statutes exist *because* of realised damage — model the mismatch, don't assume hedged.
4. **The SPV route trades a purchase problem for a tax problem.** DE vermögensverwaltende GmbH, LU SOPARFI, ES SL, NL BV, UK SPV Ltd, US LLC, ZA company/trust each solve an ownership/financing constraint but introduce annual filing, exit-tax, transfer-tax-on-shares, or anti-abuse exposure. "Buy through a company" is never free — price the downside.
5. **`fideicomiso` is not an SPV.** Mexico's restricted-zone bank trust is a *bare-trustee holding mechanism*, not a financing/holding company; conflating the two produces wrong tax and wrong financing conclusions.
6. **Crypto-for-property "acceptance" is usually marketing-tier.** Developer claims of "we accept Bitcoin" frequently mean fiat-conversion-at-signing via a third party, not on-chain settlement, and may sit in a regulatory grey zone or be outright prohibited (EG/MA/TN central-bank prohibitions). Verify legality at the financial regulator and the mechanics with the developer; never elevate a sales page to a citable fact.
7. **Broker marketing is listing-tier.** A broker's advertised "up to 80% for foreigners" is seller-controlled; the LTV that governs is the *lender's* published or regulator-confirmed non-resident policy. Cross-check every broker LTV against the bank/regulator before relying.

## Universal mitigations

Applicable across all 126 countries:

1. **Get the non-resident LTV in writing from a named lender before the offer**, not the headline macroprudential cap; size the deposit to that number.
2. **Engage a named cross-border broker/expat desk early** where the direct-to-bank path is thin — it is often the only route to a lender that will write the file.
3. **Match loan currency to income currency** or model the FX-mismatch debt-service stress explicitly (use the 2008-2012 CHF/EUR precedent as the downside case).
4. **Cost the SPV route fully** (formation + annual + exit + share-transfer tax + anti-abuse) with cross-border tax counsel before choosing it over direct ownership.
5. **Treat crypto-for-property as fiat-at-signing unless on-chain settlement is contractually explicit**, and confirm legality at the financial regulator first.
6. **Verify foreign-buyer ownership eligibility before financing** — a financing plan is moot if the purchase itself is restricted (AU established-dwelling ban, NZ regime, sectoral limits).
7. **Cross-check every advertised LTV/rate against the lender or central bank**; brokers and developers are listing-tier sources under the skill's ranking contract.

For **non-origin foreign nationals** no clear retail mortgage product surfaced — the realistic expectation is a **CASH purchase via SBP-approved inward remittance**, and they must first clear the **Ministry-of-Interior NOC** gate (1980 Order under s.3 Foreigners Act 1946) + security clearance for any cantonment/border/diplomatic-enclave parcel. Named non-res desk: **Roshan Apna Ghar via RDA-participating banks** is the named diaspora channel (the **House Building Finance Company / HBFC** is the state housing-finance institution; the domestic mortgage market is otherwise shallow). Company/SPV purchase route: **not addressed in the playbook this pass** — no residential-investment SPV/company-holding route for foreign nationals was researched; land records are **provincial** (no national cadastre) and agricultural/rural land is foreign-acquisition-restricted, so `data not publicly available — verify the company-holding route with a Pakistani lawyer + the relevant provincial Board of Revenue`. Crypto-payment for property: **not addressed in the playbook this pass** — no SBP/PVARA crypto-settlement authorisation was located and crypto regulation was not researched; treat as `data not publicly available — verify at State Bank of Pakistan ([sbp.org.pk](https://www.sbp.org.pk/))` and do not assume crypto is an accepted conveyancing channel (2026). **FX note (dominant financial risk)**: PKR is a **managed float (NOT a peg)** under an active IMF EFF with exchange-rate flexibility as the "primary shock absorber"; Pakistan operates **exchange controls** via the SBP FE Manual and the rupee is **not freely convertible** — repatriation is **clean only via the RDA / Roshan Apna Ghar channel** ("investments made through RDAs are fully repatriable"); **outside RDA, repatriation depends on documented inward-remittance provenance + SBP rules — get bank/SBP confirmation in writing before buying**. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (RDA diaspora channel + Interior-NOC gate + managed-float/exchange-controls primary-grounded; per-bank LTV/markup, company route and crypto not researched — hedged).

- **sg** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: available for private condos (foreigners cannot buy HDB; landed needs LDAU consent); **ABSD for foreigners is 60% (raised from 30%, Apr 2023; US persons & EFTA nationals get citizen-rate remission under FTAs)**; bank LTV regulatory cap 75% with TDSR 55%, in practice non-res offered ~60–75% (IRAS / MAS macroprudential framework — primary; PropertyGuru aggregator for the 60% ABSD, IRAS confirms). ### Latin America + Caribbean (R5)

- **ar** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: effectively **none** for true non-residents — Argentine retail mortgages (mostly UVA inflation-indexed) require Argentine residency + documented local income; ~near-100% of foreign purchases are **cash** (aggregator TheLatinvestor 2026; cross-check BCRA — no non-resident mortgage product published). - **ag** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: **largely a cash market** — most foreign + CBI buyers purchase cash or finance via a home-country lender against home-country security; regional banks (**Eastern Caribbean Amalgamated Bank (ECAB)**, **ACB Caribbean / Antigua Commercial Bank**, **Republic Bank**, **CIBC Caribbean**) lend to non-residents only case-by-case, at lower LTV + shorter tenor + larger deposit, with higher rates — **no published non-resident LTV cap** (data not publicly available — verify with the lender at quote stage; prudential framework set by the **Eastern Caribbean Central Bank ([ECCB](https://www.eccb-centralbank.org/))** for the XCD/EC$2.70-pegged OECS union). Named non-res broker/desk: **none located** with a dedicated non-resident mortgage desk. Company/SPV purchase route: a foreign-owned local company can hold land, but a non-citizen (whether buying directly or via a company) outside an approved CBI project still needs the **Non-Citizens Land Holding Licence (NCLHL)** under the Non-Citizens Land Holding Regulation Act CAP. 293 (commonly ~5% of value — *secondary; verify at [legalaffairs.gov.ag](https://legalaffairs.gov.ag/)*); a company route does **not** escape the NCLHL gate, and beneficial-ownership disclosure applies. ⚠️ **Barbuda land is historically held in COMMON (no individual freehold)** — do NOT assume a Barbuda parcel is freehold or financeable. Crypto-payment for property: **regulated but not legal tender** — Antigua & Barbuda has a **Digital Assets Business Act 2020** (FSRC-supervised VASP licensing) but **private crypto is not legal tender** and, as an ECCU member, the ECCB warns the public on crypto risk; conveyance settles in EC$/USD, no regulated property-settlement platform (verify at [FSRC](https://www.fsrc.gov.ag/) / [ECCB](https://www.eccb-centralbank.org/), 2025). **Confidence: MEDIUM** (lenders + CBI + NCLHL primary-grounded; non-res LTV + crypto-RE-settlement not publicly published — hedged).

NOTE: Bermuda is **North Atlantic, NOT Caribbean**. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (ALL anti-fronting + DABA primary-grounded; non-res LTV indicative/secondary — hedged).

Named non-res broker/desk: **Standard Chartered Kenya** (non-resident mortgages), **KCB** and **Equity Bank** diaspora centres. Company/SPV purchase route: legal — a Kenyan company holds property, but foreigners are constitutionally limited to **leasehold (max 99 years)**, not freehold, and an agricultural-land company with foreign control is restricted; downside is the leasehold cap + annual filing (verify with Ministry of Lands / Business Registration Service). Crypto-payment for property: **grey** — Central Bank of Kenya has cautioned against crypto and it is not legal tender; the 2025 VASP Bill is advancing but conveyancing settlement in crypto is not authorised; private-agreement only (verify at Central Bank of Kenya, 2025).

- **ma** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: available — four major banks lend to non-residents at up to **~70% LTV (≥30% down)**: **Attijariwafa Bank**, **Bank of Africa (ex-BMCE)**, **CIH Bank**, **Crédit du Maroc**; non-resident loans are typically in MAD and may require funds routed through a convertible-dirham account (aggregator Sands of Wealth Sept-2025 — cross-check the lender/Bank Al-Maghrib; **rates/figures may have changed**). Named non-res broker/desk: **Attijariwafa Bank** and **CIH Bank** have dedicated MRE/non-resident international desks. Company/SPV purchase route: legal — a Moroccan **SARL** holds property (foreigners may own urban property freely; **agricultural land is barred** to foreigners, and a company route does not bypass that); downside is annual filing + convertible-account/repatriation rules (verify with Office Marocain de la Propriété Industrielle / Office des Changes). Crypto-payment for property: **prohibited** — crypto transactions have been officially banned in Morocco since 2017 (Office des Changes / Bank Al-Maghrib joint notice); a regulatory bill is pending but crypto property settlement is currently unlawful (verify at Bank Al-Maghrib, bkam.ma, 2025).

- **mu** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: available **within approved schemes** — banks **MCB** and **SBM** (and others) finance non-resident purchases under government property schemes (**PDS / IRS / Smart City**, min ~USD 375,000 investment which also confers residence); standard non-resident LTV not authoritatively published (aggregator Habitaf 2025 — cross-check the lender; data not publicly available — verify at Bank of Mauritius, bom.mu). Named non-res broker/desk: **Mauritius Commercial Bank (MCB)** and **SBM** finance scheme purchases for non-residents. Company/SPV purchase route: legal and standard — a Mauritius company (often a GBC) holds the scheme unit; downside is non-resident freehold outside schemes is generally barred so the company must still buy *within* an approved scheme, plus annual substance/filing (verify with Economic Development Board Mauritius / Bank of Mauritius). Crypto-payment for property: **grey, regulated VASPs** — Mauritius licenses VASPs under the FSC (VAITOS Act 2021) but crypto is **not** legal tender and not authorised for conveyancing settlement; private-agreement only (verify at Bank of Mauritius / FSC Mauritius, 2025).

- **na** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: financing is available from the four principal banks (**Bank Windhoek**, **FNB Namibia**, **Standard Bank Namibia**, **Nedbank Namibia**); a non-resident is commonly cited as needing a **~20% deposit (est., advisory — NOT a Bank of Namibia rule)**, and **whether a non-resident needs BoN / Authorised-Dealer approval to borrow locally is not primary-confirmed — verify per-bank + at [BoN](https://www.bon.com.na/)**; no published statutory non-res LTV cap (the 2023 BoN amended LTV regulations introduced a deposit ladder for *additional* properties but the first-property/non-res figure was not re-verified this pass — re-fetch at BoN). Named non-res broker/desk: **none located** with a dedicated non-resident desk. Company/SPV purchase route: legal — property may be held via a **company / close corporation / trust**, but **entity transfer duty is a flat 12%** (vs the natural-person sliding scale topping ~11%) and, since the **Transfer Duty Amendment Act No. 6 of 2024 (eff. 1 Oct 2024)**, buying the **SPV that owns Namibian residential property** itself triggers transfer duty (share/member's/trust-interest deals are now caught) — verify with a Namibian conveyancer. Note **urban/residential freehold is OPEN to foreigners** (Constitution Art. 16(1); no enacted urban ban as of 2026-06-13) but **agricultural (commercial) land requires Ministerial consent + is subject to the State preferent right** (ACLRA 6/1995 s.58), and the not-yet-enacted consolidating **Land Bill** would prohibit foreign acquisition of communal AND commercial land if passed — watch it. Crypto-payment for property: **not addressed in the playbook this pass** — no BoN/NAMFISA crypto-settlement authorisation was located and crypto regulation was not researched; treat as `data not publicly available — verify at Bank of Namibia ([bon.com.na](https://www.bon.com.na/)) / NAMFISA` and do not assume crypto is an accepted conveyancing channel (2026). **FX note**: NAD is **pegged 1:1 to ZAR** inside the **Common Monetary Area** — flows **within the CMA** (NA↔ZA/LS/SZ) are **not** exchange-controlled, but flows **outside the CMA** are; route incoming funds through an Authorised Dealer and plan the repatriation channel before purchase. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (foreign-ownership scope + entity 12%/SPV-trigger + CMA peg primary-grounded; non-res deposit advisory-only, crypto not researched — hedged).

- **ng** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: available mainly as **diaspora** products — Access Bank's Diaspora Mortgage and similar (FCMB, Stanbic IBTC) lend to non-resident Nigerians on foreign-income/FX-denominated terms; standard non-resident LTV not authoritatively published (Access Bank / aggregator Habitaf 2025 — cross-check the lender; data not publicly available — verify at Central Bank of Nigeria, cbn.gov.ng). Named non-res broker/desk: **Access Bank** (Access Diaspora Mortgage), **Stanbic IBTC**, **FCMB** diaspora desks. Company/SPV purchase route: legal — a Nigerian company holds property, but the Land Use Act vests land in state Governors so what is held is a **statutory right of occupancy (≤99-yr lease)**, plus Governor's consent for transfers; downside is consent friction + annual CAC filing (verify with Corporate Affairs Commission / state Lands Bureau). Crypto-payment for property: **grey, recently re-opened** — CBN lifted its 2021 banking ban in Dec 2023 and the SEC now licenses VASPs (2024–25), but crypto is **not** legal tender and not authorised for conveyancing settlement; private-agreement only (verify at Central Bank of Nigeria / Nigerian SEC, 2025).

- **rw** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: **very limited** — Rwandan banks (Bank of Kigali, I&M, Equity Rwanda) lend mainly to residents; non-resident lending is case-by-case, no published LTV; foreign purchases predominantly cash (data not publicly available — verify at National Bank of Rwanda, bnr.rw). Named non-res broker/desk: **none located** as a dedicated non-resident desk. Company/SPV purchase route: legal — a Rwandan company holds land on a **leasehold** basis (foreigners and foreign-owned entities access land via leases, not freehold; investor leases via Rwanda Development Board); downside is the leasehold structure + RDB approval (verify with Rwanda Development Board / Rwanda Land Management Authority). Crypto-payment for property: **restricted** — the National Bank of Rwanda has warned the public against crypto and it is not legal tender or authorised for settlement; private-agreement only and discouraged (verify at National Bank of Rwanda, 2025).

- **sc** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: **very limited** — Seychelles banks lend mainly to residents; non-resident purchases require government **sanction (Immovable Property (Transfer Restriction) Act)** and are largely cash or developer-financed in approved villa/resort schemes; no published non-resident LTV (data not publicly available — verify at Central Bank of Seychelles, cbs.sc). Named non-res broker/desk: **none located** as a dedicated non-resident desk. Company/SPV purchase route: legal but **gated** — a Seychelles company can hold property, but a non-Seychellois (incl. a foreign-owned company) needs government sanction to acquire immovable property and pays a sanction fee; an IBC cannot hold local land; downside is the sanction process + substance rules (verify with Seychelles Financial Services Authority / Ministry of Lands). Crypto-payment for property: **grey** — Seychelles hosts many offshore VASPs but the FSA's VASP Act regulates them as exporters of service; crypto is **not** legal tender and not authorised for local conveyancing settlement (verify at Central Bank of Seychelles / FSA Seychelles, 2025).

- **tn** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: **very limited** — Tunisian banks lend mainly to residents; foreign (non-Tunisian) property purchase requires **Governor's authorisation** and is mostly cash; no published non-resident LTV (data not publicly available — verify at Banque Centrale de Tunisie, bct.gov.tn). Named non-res broker/desk: **none located**; non-resident Tunisians (TRE) get diaspora products from some banks — verify per bank. Company/SPV purchase route: legal but **gated** — foreigners need authorisation to acquire non-built land/agricultural land; a Tunisian company holding built urban property is feasible but still subject to the foreign-ownership authorisation regime; downside is approval friction + foreign-exchange repatriation rules (verify with Banque Centrale de Tunisie / Tunisian land registry). Crypto-payment for property: **prohibited/illegal** — Tunisia's exchange regulations bar crypto; the Central Bank has stated it is not legal and a regulatory framework is only being studied; crypto property settlement is unlawful (verify at Banque Centrale de Tunisie, 2025).

- **us** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: available — **Foreign National** programmes lend to non-resident aliens with no US credit, typically **~65–75% LTV (25–35% down)**; **DSCR (no personal income, qualifies on rent)** loans for investment property reach ~70–75% LTV, occasionally up to ~80% on strong profiles; assets often must season ~60 days (specialist-lender marketing — Foundation Mortgage / Angel Oak / America Mortgages 2025–26 — broker-tier, verify exact LTV with the named lender; **rates may have changed**). Named non-res broker/desk: **Foundation Mortgage**, **Angel Oak Mortgage Solutions**, **America Mortgages**, **Waltz**; for Canadians, **RBC Bank (US, Georgia)** lends up to **80% on primary/2nd-home, 75% on investment** using Canadian credit. Company/SPV purchase route: legal and **very common** — a US **LLC** holds the property (liability shield, estate-tax planning, privacy in some states); downside is potential loss of homestead/owner-occupant rates, FIRPTA 15% withholding on resale, and US estate-tax exposure for non-resident aliens above the low $60k exemption — get cross-border tax advice (verify with IRS FIRPTA guidance / state filing office). Crypto-payment for property: **legal/grey-practised** — crypto is legal property (no federal ban); private parties can transact, and platforms like **Propy** facilitate crypto-assisted US deals, but title companies typically require conversion to USD at closing and AML/source-of-funds checks apply (verify at IRS / FinCEN, 2025).

- **za** — Non-res foreign-buyer mortgage: **LTV effectively capped at 50%** of purchase price for true non-residents — Exchange Control Reg 3(1)(f) / the "50% borrowing rule" requires a non-resident to introduce ≥50% own foreign capital (1:1 local-credit-to-introduced-funds), administered by SARB FinSurv via Authorised Dealer banks; foreigners with a SA work/residence visa + SA income can exceed 50% subject to bank affordability (SARB Currency & Exchanges Manual / Polity / BusinessReport Jan-2026; **verify current SARB Manual**, resbank.co.za). Named non-res broker/desk: **Standard Bank**, **Absa**, **Nedbank**, **FNB**, and broker **ooba** all process non-resident bonds within the 50% rule. Company/SPV purchase route: legal — property held via a SA **company** or **trust**; but a non-resident-funded company is still bound by the exchange-control 50% local-borrowing limit and the entity needs SARB-approved foreign funding; downside is the borrowing cap carrying through + annual filing + transfer-duty/CGT on share-disposal anti-avoidance (verify with SARB FinSurv / CIPC). Crypto-payment for property: **grey** — SARB/FSCA treat crypto as a regulated financial product (FSCA declaration 2022) but **not** legal tender and not authorised for conveyancing settlement; cross-border crypto settlement is constrained by exchange control; private-agreement only (verify at South African Reserve Bank / FSCA, 2025).

## Corrections folded (research-surfaced)

- **"You can buy property directly with Bitcoin in Spain/Portugal/Greece"** (widely repeated by crypto-relocation marketing sites, e.g. astons.com): MISLEADING. Crypto is not legal tender in any EU/EEA state; the notarial deed settles in fiat (EUR). The accurate statement is crypto→EUR conversion via a regulated CASP, then a euro-denominated deed (MiCA fully applicable 30 Dec 2024, [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/digital-finance-and-innovation/markets-crypto-assets-regulation-mica)). Folded into every EU/EEA bullet as "grey — convert to EUR before the deed".
- **"France/Spain/Portugal cap non-residents at 80%+ LTV like residents"**: WRONG for non-residents. Non-residents are materially more constrained: ES ~60-70%, IT ~50-60%, PT ~60-70%; only FR is comparatively generous at ~70-85% (2025 sources cited inline; cross-check the lender/central bank — figures may have changed). Folded into the es/it/pt/fr bullets.
- **"Poland forced a CHF→PLN conversion like Hungary"**: WRONG. Poland passed no statutory conversion act; relief was litigation-driven via CJEU C-260/18. Hungary (Act LXXVII/2014) and Croatia (2015 amendment) did legislate; the relief mechanism differs by country ([EPRS briefing](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2021/689361/EPRS_BRI(2021)689361_EN.pdf)). Folded into hu/hr/pl/ro/rs bullets.
- **Studio Legale Metta is a law firm, not a mortgage broker** — useful for Italian non-res mortgage *parameters/legal advisory* but should not be cited as a "non-res broker"; corrected in the **it** bullet.
- **ae** "Damac/Emaar/Nakheel accept crypto" — widely repeated in aggregator/press but is *developer-marketing/press tier*, not a primary regulator statement. Corrected in-bullet: legality of crypto property settlement rests on the **VARA framework + DLD tokenisation pilot (primary)**; specific developer crypto channels must be verified against that developer's official sales contract before relying. Do not state developer crypto-acceptance as regulator-confirmed fact.
- **tr** Frequent aggregator claim that buyers "pay developers in crypto" in Turkey is wrong: **CBRT's 2021 Regulation on the Disuse of Crypto Assets in Payments prohibits using crypto for payments** — direct crypto property settlement is illegal; only fiat conversion then purchase is lawful. Folded into the tr bullet.
- **au** Aggregators still describe "foreign buyers getting Australian mortgages" generically — materially stale post-**1 Apr 2025 established-dwelling ban (FIRB, to 31 Mar 2027)**. Corrected in-bullet: established dwellings are off-limits to foreign persons (incl. foreign-owned companies — no SPV workaround); only new/off-the-plan with FIRB approval.
- **hk** Many older guides still cite a 15%/7.5% Buyer's Stamp Duty and lower LTV for non-permanent residents — **abolished 28 Feb 2024; HKMA uniform 70% LTV, Scale-2 AVD only** (HKMA, 2024). Folded into the hk bullet.
- **sg** Stale "30% ABSD for foreigners" persists in older sources — **raised to 60% (Apr 2023)**; entities pay 65% (IRAS). Folded into the sg bullet.
- **jp** "Foreigners can easily get a Japanese mortgage" is misleading for non-residents — **near-absent without Japan residency/PR**; SMBC Prestia/Suruga/SBI Shinsei lean to *residents*; corporate (KK/GK) loan is the real non-resident route. Folded into the jp bullet.
- **in / vn / id / kh** Generic "foreigners can get a mortgage" claims are wrong for pure foreign nationals — IN bars non-NRI/OCI residential purchase (FEMA), VN/ID/KH bar foreign land ownership and have effectively no non-resident mortgage market; nominee structures are illegal in ID/VN/KH/TH. Folded into each bullet.
- **Mexico — fideicomiso ≠ SPV (widely conflated).** Many listings and aggregators describe the *fideicomiso* as a "way to own through a trust/structure" and imply it is an asset-protection SPV. It is **not** a special-purpose vehicle: it is a *mandatory bank-trust title-holding mechanism* required for any foreigner buying inside the restricted zone (50 km coast / 100 km border). The genuine SPV route is a Mexican *sociedad* (S. de R.L./S.A.), which is only usable for non-residential or non-restricted-zone property without the trust. Folded into the **mx** bullet.
- **Canada — financing vs. eligibility conflation.** Cross-border-bank marketing ("Canadians can get a Canadian mortgage") frequently omits that the federal *Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians* (extended to **1 Jan 2027**) bars many non-resident foreigners from purchasing in-scope residential property *regardless of mortgage access*. A 35%-down mortgage offer does not equal legal eligibility. Folded into the **ca** bullet.
- **South Africa — "non-residents can get a normal 90% bond" (common aggregator overstatement).** Several blog/broker pages imply standard high-LTV bonds for foreigners; the binding constraint is Exchange Control Reg 3(1)(f) capping local credit at ~50% of price for true non-residents (visa+SA-income holders excepted). Folded into the **za** bullet.
- **Dominican Republic vs. Dominica.** Recurrent aggregator error attributes Dominica's (DM) digital-nomad/CBI-linked financing to the Dominican Republic (DO). DO has no such product; DM is out of scope. Flagged in the **do** bullet.
- **Brazil — "pay for BR property with crypto from abroad" overstatement.** Aggregators that say crypto is "legal and regulated in Brazil" omit that Banco Central do Brasil banned stablecoin/crypto settlement in *cross-border* (eFX) payments effective **1 Oct 2025** (Banco Central do Brasil / CoinDesk May-2026). Domestic private crypto transfer remains possible, but cross-border crypto settlement to fund a purchase is restricted. Folded into the **br** bullet.
- **Brazil/LatAm rural-land trap.** Listings frequently quote "no foreign-ownership restrictions in Brazil" — true for urban property but Law 5.709/71 imposes INCRA authorisation + municipal area caps on rural land and the 150 km border strip, and banks largely refuse to mortgage it to foreigners. Folded into the **br** bullet.
