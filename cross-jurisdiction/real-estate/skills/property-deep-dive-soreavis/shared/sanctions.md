# Universal `--sanctions` Section — Seller / UBO / PEP Sanctions-Screening at Deed Signing

Pre-completion screening of the *other side of the deed* — the seller, the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) behind any company / SPV / trust seller, and any politically-exposed-person (PEP) connection — against the four public sanctions lists, so a foreign buyer does not inadvertently transfer funds to (or acquire property from) a blocked person and find the funds frozen, the deal void, or themselves exposed to secondary sanctions. Loaded when `--sanctions` is invoked.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this is the *counterparty-screening* layer. It is **not** `--scams` (how transaction fraud happens), **not** `--notary` (the deed-execution process), **not** `--finance` / `--cross-border` (the buyer's OWN bank KYC + mortgage), **not** `--title-monitoring` (post-ownership title-fraud). `--sanctions` answers one orthogonal question: *is the party you are buying FROM — or their hidden owner — a sanctioned/blocked person, a PEP, or otherwise someone you must not fund; who is legally obliged to screen them; and can you (or your advisor) actually access the beneficial-ownership data to check?*

## Why this matters to a foreign buyer

Sanctions liability is **strict** in most regimes — you need not have known. If the seller (or the UBO of a company / trust seller) is a designated person, the transfer can be blocked, your funds frozen, the deal unwound, and you exposed to secondary sanctions. The risk concentrates where it is least visible: company / SPV / trust sellers, recently-naturalised Citizenship-by-Investment (CBI) passport holders, PEPs selling through nominees, and high-value markets that attract sanctioned wealth (the Gulf, CBI Caribbean, offshore-finance territories, prime EU / UK).

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Beneficial-ownership (BO) register + access tier** — named register + whether access is **PUBLIC** (buyer-searchable) · **legitimate-interest** · **authorities / obliged-entity-only** · **none / transitional**. (Post-CJEU, most EU registers are NOT public.)
2. **AML supervisor + FIU** — the body supervising real-estate AML + the financial-intelligence unit
3. **Gatekeeper screening duty** — who is the *regulated* screener at the deed (notary / lawyer / estate-agent), under what rule, OR `no DNFBP duty yet — buyer/conveyancer must self-screen`
4. **The realistic screening path** — who actually screens the seller / UBO in practice (almost never the buyer alone)
5. **Country sanctions / PEP note** — the one country-specific fact (FATF status WITH date, CBI exposure, a notable case)
6. **Confidence** — HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW per the skill's contract

## Classify first — the screening architecture

### The four lists (free, public — screen the seller AND the UBO against ALL of them)

- **US OFAC** — SDN List + Consolidated Sanctions List; free Sanctions List Search at `sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov` (fuzzy match, no login). Reaches non-US persons via secondary sanctions.
- **EU** — Consolidated Financial Sanctions List (the "FSF" database); free download (PDF / CSV / XML); the authentic text is the Official Journal. There is **no** OFAC-style interactive search box — do not imply parity.
- **UK** — the **FCDO UK Sanctions List** at `search-uk-sanctions-list.service.gov.uk`. The legacy **OFSI Consolidated List closed 28 Jan 2026** — do not cite it as the live source.
- **UN** — Security Council Consolidated List at `main.un.org/securitycouncil` (+ `search.sanctions.un.org`).

### The OFAC 50% Rule — why screening the named seller is not enough

An entity owned **50% or more, in aggregate, by one or more blocked persons is itself blocked** — even if not separately listed. A clean-looking company seller can be a blocked entity through its UBO. Screen the ownership chain, not just the name on the title.

### The gatekeeper model (FATF) — who is legally obliged to screen

- **Rec 22** — real-estate agents, notaries and lawyers (DNFBPs) must perform customer due diligence (identify + verify the customer + UBO; screen against sanctions / PEP lists) on property transactions.
- **Rec 12** — enhanced due diligence on PEPs (and their family / close associates).
- **Rec 24 / 25** — countries must hold adequate, accurate, current beneficial-ownership information.

Whether the *agent* specifically carries this duty varies — establish who the gatekeeper is before assuming one exists (e.g. AU only from 1 Jul 2026; in KR agents are not AML-reporting entities, the bank is).

### BO-register access tiers (the honesty axis)

The **CJEU struck down general-public access to EU beneficial-ownership registers on 22 Nov 2022** (Joined Cases C-37/20 & C-601/20). Most EU / EEA registers are now **legitimate-interest** access; some are **authorities-only**. Genuinely buyer-searchable PUBLIC registers are now the exception (UK PSC + Register of Overseas Entities; Gibraltar; Nigeria CAC). The 2024 EU AML package (Reg (EU) 2024/1624, the AMLR, applies 10 Jul 2027) re-bases access on legitimate interest by statute.

## Verdict bands

| Band | Trigger | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Strong DNFBP gatekeeper actively screening + clear sanctions framework + buyer/advisor can verify the UBO (public or legitimate-interest register) + low jurisdictional sanctions-risk | "Your notary/lawyer screens as a matter of law; the UBO is verifiable; proceed with a standard sanctions representation." |
| 🟡 | Gatekeeper duty exists but BO access is authorities-only (you rely on the gatekeeper's screen, can't self-verify) | "Get your regulated advisor to run + document the screen; you cannot self-check the UBO — make completion conditional on a clean result." |
| 🟠 | Gatekeeper duty thin / new / absent, OR elevated exposure (CBI state, offshore-finance centre, recent FATF grey-listing) | "Commission enhanced due diligence on the seller + UBO from an independent screener; do not rely on the agent alone." |
| 🔴 | No effective gatekeeper + opaque ownership + high sanctions-risk (active grey-list + nominee-heavy market) | "Treat counterparty screening as a deal-gating diligence item with specialist AML counsel; walk if the UBO can't be established." |

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **A BO register existing ≠ you can search it.** Since the CJEU's 22 Nov 2022 ruling most EU registers are legitimate-interest or authorities-only — the realistic screener is your *regulated notary / lawyer / agent*, who holds the access you don't. Never assume a buyer can self-serve the UBO.
2. **The OFAC 50% Rule hides the risk in the ownership chain.** A non-listed company / SPV seller that is ≥50% owned by a blocked person is itself blocked. Screen the UBO, not just the name on the deed.
3. **The gatekeeper duty may not exist yet — or may not sit with the agent.** Australia's real-estate AML duty starts only 1 Jul 2026; in Korea agents are not AML-reporting entities (the bank screens). Confirm who is obliged before relying on "the agent will check."
4. **CBI / golden-passport sellers carry elevated risk.** A recently-naturalised seller from a Citizenship-by-Investment state (CBI Caribbean; historic CY / MT) may be a PEP or someone who bought citizenship to obscure origin — treat as enhanced due diligence.
5. **FATF grey-list status is dated and moves.** UAE off 23 Feb 2024; Türkiye off 28 Jun 2024; South Africa + Nigeria off 24 Oct 2025; Lebanon *added* 25 Oct 2024; Bulgaria + Vietnam still listed. A current grey-listing signals weaker DNFBP enforcement — never assert "is grey-listed" without the date.
6. **A digital register is not a sanctions screen.** Highly digitised land / company registers rarely run list-matching; the screen is the gatekeeper's CDD, not the registry's existence.
7. **The US FinCEN Residential Real Estate Rule is contested.** Effective 1 Dec 2025 (reporting from 1 Mar 2026) but **vacated by the E.D. Texas court on 19 Mar 2026 and on appeal to the 5th Circuit — a circuit split** (a contrary M.D. Fla. ruling upheld it). Don't state the reporting obligation as settled; verify the current status.

## Universal mitigations

Applicable across all 126 countries:

1. **Screen the named seller AND the UBO against all four lists** (OFAC, EU, UK FCDO, UN) before any funds move — the public search tools are free.
2. **Have your regulated notary / lawyer / agent run + document the CDD / sanctions screen** — they hold the legitimate-interest BO-register access you don't; get it in writing.
3. **Apply OFAC 50% logic to any company / SPV / trust seller** — demand the full UBO chain; if it can't be established, treat that as the answer.
4. **Treat CBI-state sellers and any PEP connection as enhanced due diligence.**
5. **Check the jurisdiction's current (dated) FATF status** — a grey-listing means lean harder on independent screening.
6. **Put a sanctions / AML representation + condition precedent in the SPA** — make completion conditional on a clean screen of seller + UBO.
7. **Keep the screening evidence** — it protects you from secondary exposure and a frozen-funds / unwound-deal outcome if the counterparty is later designated.
