# Universal `--notary` Section — Forced-Heirship Sub-section Extension

**Forced-heirship / réserve héréditaire** layer for foreign property buyers — civil-law reserved-share regimes (réserve héréditaire / quota legittima / Pflichtteil / legítima / iryūbun / yuryubun / zachowek) vs common-law testamentary freedom + family-provision claims vs Sharia faraid + opt-out paths, EU Regulation 650/2012 (Brussels IV) nationality-law-choice mechanism, France's Aug 2021 prélèvement compensatoire carve-out (Art. 913 al. 4 Code civil — constitutionality and Brussels IV compatibility still pending in litigation as of May 2026), Switzerland's 1 Jan 2023 Pflichtteil reform, Liechtenstein's 1 Aug 2024 Erbrecht reform, Korean 2024 Constitutional Court ruling on sibling yuryubun, and the 2024-2026 reform calendar that re-shapes succession planning. Loaded when `--notary` is invoked alongside the existing transaction-completion logic in `shared/notary-process.md` and per-country playbook.

**Snapshot**: May 2026.

**Scope**: this doc supplies the *succession-law* layer — what fixed shares (if any) the law reserves for descendants, spouse, parents at the testator's death, and what planning vehicles (will registration, foreign-law choice via Brussels IV, lifetime gifts, trusts) survive the regime. The *transaction-completion* layer (notary-required vs solicitor, days-from-offer-to-deed, closing costs) lives in `shared/notary-process.md`. A foreign buyer often needs both: they need to close the purchase (transaction notary) AND they need to know whether their will from home country will be respected at death OR if local forced-heirship will override it (succession notary).

## Universal contract

For the property's country, return:

1. **Forced-heirship regime** — one of: **civil-law forced-share** (continental EU, LATAM, francophone Africa, JP/KR/TW), **common-law testamentary freedom + family-provision claims** (UK/IE/AU/CA/NZ/US), **Sharia faraid + opt-out paths** (GCC/MA/EG/JO/MY-Muslim/ID-Muslim/MV/KSA), **post-Soviet civil-code derivatives** (KZ/UZ/AM/AZ/GE/MD/CIS), or **pluralist** (LB 18 confessional regimes / IN Hindu+Muslim+Christian+Parsi / LK Roman-Dutch+Kandyan+Thesavalamai).
2. **Reserved portions** (cite Civil Code articles where available) — % to descendants / spouse / parents under the country's regime. Examples: FR 1/2 (1 kid) / 2/3 (2 kids) / 3/4 (3+ kids) per Code civil Art. 913; IT 1/2 (1 kid) / 2/3 (2+ kids) per Codice Civile Art. 537; ES 2/3 to descendants per Código Civil Art. 808 (1/3 estricta + 1/3 mejora) + REGIONAL FORALS; CH 1/2 (was 3/4 pre-2023) per ZGB 471 reform; JP iryūbun 1/2 of intestate share; KR yuryubun 1/2 (descendants) / 1/3 (parents/siblings — sibling held UNCONSTITUTIONAL Apr 2024).
3. **EU 650/2012 Brussels IV applicability** — does the country apply the regulation? In force EU-22 from 17 Aug 2015 (DK + IE opted out; UK no longer applies post-Brexit). Allows testator to choose nationality-law over residence-law via professio juris in a will. State whether the country APPLIES Brussels IV when foreign-resident-in-X dies owning property in X (i.e., does the country accept a Will-clause electing the testator's nationality law?). Note: **France's 24 Aug 2021 carve-out** (Loi 2021-1109 Article 24 + Art. 913 al. 4 Code civil + Decree 2021-1474) creates **prélèvement compensatoire** — forced-heirs can claim from FR-located property an amount equivalent to their forced-share even when foreign-law-choice excluded them (deaths from 1 Nov 2021).
4. **Foreign-buyer planning vehicles** — which mechanisms allow a foreign buyer to bypass forced-shares? Examples: **AE DIFC Wills Service Centre** + **Abu Dhabi Civil Family Court** for non-Muslim foreigners (post-1 Feb 2023 under Federal Decree-Law 41/2022); **TR civil-law nationality choice** under Code 5718 (Devletler Özel Hukuku) Art. 20; **MX fideicomiso** doubles as estate-planning; **PA/CR offshore-trust-friendly testamentary freedom**; **MU/SC trust structures** under Mauritius Trust Act 2001; **US revocable living trusts + LLC ownership**.

## ⚠️ 7 cross-cutting traps

1. **Brussels IV is not a universal escape valve.** The regulation applies only in EU-22 (DK + IE opted out, UK no longer applies post-Brexit), so testators with property in DK/IE/UK cannot use professio juris under Brussels IV — local forced-heirship applies. Even within Brussels IV jurisdictions, **France's 24 Aug 2021 prélèvement compensatoire** carves out FR-located property: forced-heirs (children) can claim from FR-property an amount equivalent to their forced-share even when the testator validly chose foreign nationality law. Conseil constitutionnel ruled the mechanism constitutional 9 Nov 2023 (Decision 2023-1059 QPC). Other CJEU cases pending (C-21/22, C-422/22). Buyers from common-law jurisdictions (UK/US/AU) who assume "I'll just write my own will" should verify Brussels IV status + any national carve-out before relying.
2. **Civil-law forced-shares are claims against the estate or against the property — not always both.** NL since 2003 BW 4 — legitieme portie is monetary claim against estate (heirs get money, not in-rem share). FR réserve héréditaire is in-rem (heirs get share of property). DE Pflichtteil is monetary claim. IT quota legittima is in-rem with azione di riduzione. The mechanism determines whether the foreign property itself can be reached or only its value via estate.
3. **Sharia inheritance opt-out is jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction.** AE allows non-Muslim foreigners to register a will at DIFC Wills Service Centre or Abu Dhabi Civil Family Court (post-Federal Decree-Law 41/2022, effective 1 Feb 2023). QA Article 12 Family Law allows non-Muslim foreigners to apply nationality law. SA + KW + OM have no comparable opt-out — Sharia faraid applies to all Saudi-located property regardless of testator nationality (foreigners largely barred from real estate ownership outside specific zones). MA + EG + JO are bench-discretion-driven if the deceased is Muslim. TR (1926 secular Civil Code) is the regional outlier — Civil-Code-derived forced-shares NOT Sharia.
4. **Religion at death determines regime in pluralist jurisdictions.** MY: Muslim citizen → state Syariah Court Faraid; non-Muslim → Distribution Act 1958. ID: Pengadilan Agama (religious) for Muslims vs Pengadilan Negeri (civil) for non-Muslims. LB: 18 confessional regimes (Sunni / Shia / Druze / 12 Christian / Jewish) — religion-of-deceased determines forum + applicable law. IN: Hindu Succession Act 1956 (amended 2005 — equal coparcenary for daughters) vs Muslim Personal Law vs Indian Succession Act 1925 (Christians/Parsis) vs Special Marriage Act 1954 (mixed). LK: General Inheritance Law (Roman-Dutch) + Kandyan Law (Sinhalese Buddhists) + Thesavalamai (Tamils) + Muslim Marriage and Divorce Act. A conversion at deathbed — or a divorce/remarriage — can flip the regime.
5. **Regional forals + state-Civil-Code variation matter for siting wills.** ES: Cataluña / Aragón / País Vasco / Navarra / Galicia / Baleares all have their own legítima rules — testator's residence at death controls regime, not citizenship (post-CJEU C-218/16 Kubicka). MX: 32 state Civil Codes diverge — federal testamentary freedom but state-level rules vary; CDMX + Yucatán + Quintana Roo most testamentary-free. CA: QC has légitime under CCQ Art. 684-695 even for foreign owners of QC-located property. US: Louisiana Civil Code Art. 1493+ forced-heirship vs 49 testamentary-free states + 11 community-property states. AU: state Family Provision Acts (NSW Succession Act 2006 / VIC Administration and Probate Act 1958 / etc.) vary on eligible-claimant categories + limitation periods.
6. **Lifetime gifts are clawed back into the estate.** Most civil-law jurisdictions calculate forced-shares including lifetime gifts (donations) made within a look-back period: **DE Pflichtteilsergänzungsanspruch** 10 years (BGB § 2325, with sliding scale of 10% per year); **FR rapport** unlimited for direct descendants; **BR doação inoficiosa** under CC Art. 549 voided at the moment of gifting (STJ 21 Jul 2023 timing test) with reduction action following the 10-year ordinary prescription of CC Art. 205; **BG запазена част calculation** clawback applies; **IT azione di riduzione** 10-year limitation from succession opening. A foreign buyer who gifts the property to children pre-death does not necessarily defeat forced-heirship — the gift may be "rapported" or "réintégrée" into the estate calculation. Trusts + lifetime structures need country-specific advice.
7. **EU 2024 AML6 + AMLA + DAC8 affect succession planning vehicles.** AMLA Frankfurt operational 1 Jul 2025 (Reg. 2024/1620). EU AMLR (2024/1624) directly applicable 10 Jul 2027 with EUR 10,000 cash-payment cap. DAC8 transposition deadline 31 Dec 2025 — extends automatic exchange of information to crypto-assets, life insurance, tax rulings. **For trust + foundation structures used to bypass forced-heirship**, beneficial-ownership reporting + AML CDD + cross-border income reporting now apply. The "I'll set up a Liechtenstein foundation" or "I'll trust the Mauritius offshore trust" route is more compliance-heavy than pre-2024.

## Western Europe + EFTA + microstates + UK

### Regional pattern

The 23-country region splits cleanly into three succession-law families. **Civil-law forced-shares dominate continental EU + EFTA + microstates** (AD, AT, BE, CH, DE, ES, FI, FR, GR, IS, IT, LI, LU, MC, MT, NL, NO, PT, SE, SM) — descendants and (in most regimes) spouse hold a non-disposable reserve fixed by Civil Code article, expressed either as a fraction of the estate (FR, IT, ES, BE, MC, LU, MT, PT, IS) or as a fraction of the intestate share (DE, AT, CH, LI, NL, GR, FI, SE, DK, NO). **Common-law testamentary freedom** survives only in **UK + IE**, both moderated by a discretionary court-jurisdiction over family provision (Inheritance Act 1975 / Succession Act 1965 ss. 111 + 117). **Hybrid civil/foral pluralism** in **ES** — six Comunidades Autónomas (Cataluña, Aragón, País Vasco, Navarra, Galicia, Baleares) override the Código Civil at the deceased's *vecindad civil*, producing legítima rates from 25% (Catalan pars valoris credit) to 50% (Aragón) to majority free disposition (Navarra natives, País Vasco collective legítima post Ley 5/2015).

**EU Regulation 650/2012 (Brussels IV)** — in force 17 Aug 2015 across the EU minus DK + IE; UK never opted in and exited via Brexit. Brussels IV lets the testator elect their nationality-law via *professio juris* in the will. CH/IS/LI/NO are non-EU but apply Brussels IV-compatible *professio juris* through bilateral treaty / domestic recognition (CH-IPRG Art. 90 ff., NO arveloven 2019 § 78). MC + AD + SM + MT + UK are outside the regulation but each has its own conflict-of-laws regime (MC Code IPRD 2017, MT Civil Code Title V, UK common-law domicile-of-deceased rule).

**FR Aug 2021 prélèvement compensatoire** is the region's most aggressive Brussels IV carve-out. Loi n° 2021-1109 du 24 août 2021 (Art. 24) added Code civil Art. 913 al. 4 (in force for deaths from 1 Nov 2021): if the deceased or any child was an EU citizen or EU-resident at death AND the elected foreign law has no forced-share, each child can claw back from FR-located property the equivalent of their FR forced-share. Practical effect: Brussels IV *professio juris* is partially neutralised against any FR property held by EU-nationals/residents. The provision's compatibility with Brussels IV is contested in doctrine but **no Conseil constitutionnel decision squarely upholding (or striking) Art. 913 al. 4 has been published as of May 2026** — pending QPC and CJEU referrals are the live monitoring item.

**CH 1 Jan 2023 reform** (revised Art. 470-478 ZGB) cut descendants' Pflichtteil from 3/4 to 1/2 of the intestate share, abolished the parents' Pflichtteil entirely, and held the spouse Pflichtteil at 1/2. **LI 1 Aug 2024 reform** (in force for deaths from 1 Oct 2024) mirrored CH/AT — abolished ascendants' Pflichtteil, retained descendants + spouse at 1/2 intestate share. **AT Erbrechts-Änderungsgesetz 2015/2017** abolished the Pflichtteil for parents and added a 5-year (extendable to 10) Stundung (deferred-payment) regime under §§ 765-767 ABGB to ease family-business transfers.

**ES regional foral choice-of-residence trap** is the single highest-impact planning variable in the region: a foreign retiree who acquires *vecindad civil* in Cataluña (after 10 years' residence + declaration) drops legítima from 2/3 (Código Civil) to 25% (Llibre IV CCCat) and switches it from co-ownership to a money credit. Wrong forum at death triggers wrong regime — and Brussels IV *professio juris* is interpreted at the *vecindad* level, not state level (Tribunal Supremo doctrine). **NL** is the only continental civil-law country where the legitieme portie is purely a money claim (BW 4:64, post-2003 reform) — heirs receive cash, never property.

### Country one-liners

**AD (Andorra)** — civil-law forced-share via Llei 46/2014 del 18 de desembre de la successió per causa de mort: llegítima for descendants + *quarta vidual* for surviving spouse with insufficient means; Andorra outside EU so Brussels IV does not apply (own conflict-of-laws regime); notarial will required for most planning vehicles; trap — Llei 30/2022 family-law reform reshaped spouse rank ahead of ascendants. Verify at [Llei 46/2014](https://leslleis.com/L2014046). **Confidence: MEDIUM** (official text in Catalan; English commentary thin).

**AT (Austria)** — Pflichtteil under §§ 757-770 ABGB: 1/2 of intestate share for descendants + spouse/registered partner; **parents lost Pflichtteil status in the 2017 reform** (Erbrechts-Änderungsgesetz, BGBl I 2015/87, in force 1 Jan 2017); Brussels IV applies — *Rechtswahl* by will to nationality law accepted; Stundung available 5 yrs (extendable to 10 by court) at 4% p.a. interest under §§ 765-767 ABGB to ease business/owner-occupied-home transfers. Verify at [BGBl I 2015/87 RIS](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/Dokumente/BgblAuth/BGBLA_2015_I_87/BGBLA_2015_I_87.pdfsig). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**BE (Belgium)** — Code civil Art. 913 (post-Loi du 31 juillet 2017, in force 1 Sep 2018): réserve = 1/2 of estate for ALL descendants combined regardless of count (was 3/4 with 3+ kids pre-reform); ascendants' réserve abolished; surviving-spouse réserve = usufruct on family home + furnishings; Brussels IV applies; trap — pre-1 Sep 2018 lifetime gifts can be re-anchored under reform via *déclaration de maintien*, missed window = old regime applies; 30-yr action limitation. Verify at [Justel - Loi 2017-07-31](http://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/cgi_loi/change_lg.pl?language=fr&la=F&cn=2017073125&table_name=loi). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**CH (Switzerland)** — revised ZGB Art. 470-478 (in force 1 Jan 2023): descendants' Pflichtteil cut from 3/4 to **1/2 of intestate share**; spouse held at 1/2; **parents lost Pflichtteil entirely**; non-EU so Brussels IV does not apply, but IPRG Art. 90 ff. allow foreign nationals resident in CH to elect nationality-law via testament (*professio juris* by treaty equivalent); trap — testaments drafted before 1 Jan 2023 default to OLD shares unless reformed; trap 2 — for FR-nationals owning FR property the FR Art. 913 al. 4 prélèvement still bites; cantonal succession-tax variance 0-50% layered on top. Verify at [Art. 471 ZGB swissrights](https://www.swissrights.ch/gesetze/Artikel-471-ZGB-2025-DE.php). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**DE (Germany)** — BGB §§ 2303-2338 Pflichtteil = **1/2 of intestate share** for spouse + descendants + parents (parents only if no descendants); Brussels IV applies but Pflichtteil is treated as German *ordre public* — heir who is German citizen cannot be effectively excluded by foreign-law choice, BGH IV ZR 110/19 (29 Jun 2022); Pflichtteilsergänzungsanspruch (§ 2325 BGB) claws back lifetime gifts on a 10-yr sliding scale (100% in yr 1, declining 10% per year, 0 after 10 yrs); spouse Zugewinnausgleich + Pflichtteil interaction. Verify at [§ 2303 BGB gesetze-im-internet](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__2303.html). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**DK (Denmark)** — Arveloven (LBK nr 1347 af 15/11/2023) §§ 5-9: **tvangsarv = 25% of intestate share** for spouse + biological children (capped per-child at DKK 1,470,000 / 2024 indexation); **DK opted OUT of Brussels IV** under the Lisbon-protocol JHA opt-out — Danish private international law (lex domicilii of deceased) governs ALL succession of DK-resident decedents, *professio juris* by foreign nationality is NOT enforceable for DK-located property; trap — foreign EU nationals owning DK property cannot rely on Brussels IV election. Verify at [Arveloven Retsinformation](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2023/1347). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**ES (Spain)** — Código Civil Art. 806-808: legítima = **2/3 to descendants** (1/3 *legítima estricta* + 1/3 *mejora* for unequal distribution among descendants); spouse usufruct 1/3 (with descendants) / 1/2 (no descendants); **OVERRIDDEN BY 6 REGIONAL FORALS at the testator's *vecindad civil* (10-yr residence + declaration)**: Cataluña 25% pars valoris money-credit (Llibre IV CCCat / Llei 10/2008), Aragón 50% to descendants only (DLeg 1/2011), País Vasco collective-legítima allowing exclusion of individual descendants (Ley 5/2015), Navarra effectively free disposition for natives (Ley 1/1973 Compilación), Galicia 25% (Lei 2/2006), Baleares partial; Brussels IV applies but *vecindad civil* (not nationality) determines internal regime. Verify at [BOE Leyes Civiles Forales](https://www.boe.es/biblioteca_juridica/codigos/codigo.php?id=48), [Ley 5/2015](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2015-8273). **Confidence: HIGH** for Código Civil; **MEDIUM** for foral interpretation by region.

**FI (Finland)** — Perintökaari (40/1965) Ch. 7: **lakiosa = 1/2 of intestate share** for descendants only (spouse has no lakiosa but intestate share + matrimonial-property regime); Brussels IV applies; 1934 Nordic Convention on private international law in succession matters runs IN PARALLEL with Brussels IV among DK/FI/IS/NO/SE — Brussels IV prevails between FI/SE and other EU MS, Nordic Convention prevails inter-Nordic. Verify at [Perintökaari Finlex](https://www.finlex.fi/en/legislation/1965/40). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**FR (France)** — Code civil Art. 912-917: réserve = **1/2 (1 child) / 2/3 (2 children) / 3/4 (3+ children)**; spouse réserve = 1/4 ONLY if no descendants; Brussels IV applies BUT carved out by **Art. 913 al. 4 *prélèvement compensatoire*** (Loi n° 2021-1109 du 24 août 2021 Art. 24, in force for deaths from 1 Nov 2021): if deceased or any child is EU-national/resident AND elected foreign law has no forced-share, each child can claw back FR-located property up to their FR forced-share equivalent; **trap** — the Conseil constitutionnel has NOT yet ruled directly on Art. 913 al. 4; doctrine widely expects QPC + CJEU referrals; notarial olographic + authentique testaments both valid; 10-yr action en réduction limitation from succession opening. Verify at [Art. 913 Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000043982288), [LOI 2021-1109 Art. 24](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/article_jo/JORFARTI000043964829). **Confidence: HIGH** for Civil Code; **MEDIUM** for Brussels IV carve-out durability.

**GR (Greece)** — Astikos Kodikas Art. 1825-1842: nόμιμη μοίρα = **1/2 of intestate share** for descendants + spouse + (in absence of descendants) ascendants; Brussels IV applies; trap — Greek courts treat νόμιμη μοίρα as *ordre public international* for Greece-located immovables, can override Brussels IV professio juris in practice for GR real estate; αγωγή μείωσης (reduction action) 2-yr limitation from learning-of-disposition, 20-yr long-stop. Verify at [Astikos Kodikas Art. 1825 (Greek MFA)](https://www.mfa.gr/usa/en/services/services-for-greeks/wills-and-inheritances.html). **Confidence: MEDIUM** (English commentary uneven; Greek primary text authoritative).

**IE (Ireland)** — common-law testamentary freedom moderated by Succession Act 1965: **Section 111 spouse legal right share = 1/2 (no children) / 1/3 (with children)**; **Section 117 children — NO automatic share, must apply to court within 6 MONTHS** of grant of probate proving testator failed in moral duty; **IE opted OUT of Brussels IV** — Irish private international law (lex situs for immovables, lex domicilii for movables) applies; trap — Section 111 is automatic and overrides any will, Section 117 is discretionary and strict 6-month limitation forecloses late claims. Verify at [Succession Act 1965 s.111](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1965/act/27/section/111/enacted/en/html), [s.117](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1965/act/27/section/117). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**IS (Iceland)** — Erfðalög nr. 8/1962: **skylduarfur = 1/3 of estate reserved for descendants**; 2/3 freely disposable by will; spouse intestate share + matrimonial-property regime; non-EU but Iceland recognises *professio juris* by foreign nationality for non-IS-resident decedents under domestic conflict-of-laws + 1934 Nordic Convention inter-Nordic; trap — IS skylduarfur is one of the most testator-friendly reserves in continental Europe (descendants get only 1/3, not 1/2). Verify at [Erfðalög Althingi](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/1962008.html). **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**IT (Italy)** — Codice Civile Art. 536-552: quota legittima = **1/2 (1 child) / 2/3 (2+ children combined)** for descendants; spouse legittima = **1/2 (no kids) / 1/3 (with 1 kid) / 1/4 (with 2+ kids)**; ascendants 1/3 (no descendants/spouse) / 1/4 (with spouse, no descendants); Brussels IV applies; trap — *azione di riduzione* (Art. 553-564) has **10-yr limitation from succession opening** (not from learning of disposition), so missed deadline forecloses claim entirely; trap 2 — donations made >10 yrs before death by donor STILL inside the *massa* fittizia for legittima calculation but *azione di restituzione* against third-party donee blocked after 20 yrs from donation. Verify at [Art. 536 Brocardi](https://www.brocardi.it/codice-civile/libro-secondo/titolo-i/capo-x/sezione-i/art536.html). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**LI (Liechtenstein)** — comprehensive reform in force 1 Aug 2024 (applies to deaths from 1 Oct 2024 onward), §§ 762-797 ABGB-LI: **Pflichtteil = 1/2 of intestate share** for descendants + spouse; **ascendants' Pflichtteil abolished**; new "care legacy" for unpaid carers; third-party wills require 3 simultaneously-present witnesses; non-EU so Brussels IV does not apply, but LI recognises professio juris by foreign nationality under domestic IPRG; trap — pre-1 Oct 2024 testaments default to PRE-reform shares for transitional cases (parents may still have claim if death pre-1 Oct 2024). Verify at [Erbrechtsreform LI BuA 123/2023](https://www.llv.li/serviceportal2/amtsstellen/stabstelle-regierungskanzlei/bua_123_2023_reform-erbrecht.pdf). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**LU (Luxembourg)** — Code civil Art. 913 (mirrors pre-reform FR): réserve = **1/2 (1 child) / 2/3 (2 children) / 3/4 (3+ children)** for descendants; ascendants no longer reserved (since 2007 reform); spouse not a forced heir but has matrimonial-regime + droit d'habitation on family home; Brussels IV applies; trap — LU has NOT enacted a FR-style prélèvement compensatoire, so foreign-law election via Brussels IV fully neutralises the LU réserve. Verify at [e-Justice LU restrictions](https://e-justice.europa.eu/topics/family-matters-inheritance/inheritance/restrictions-successions-special-rules/lu_en). **Confidence: HIGH**.

**MC (Monaco)** — Code civil mirrors pre-2007 FR: réserve = **1/2 (1 child) / 2/3 (2 children) / 3/4 (3+ children)** for descendants; ascendants 1/4 (no descendants); spouse NOT a reserved heir but Loi n° 1.481 added 1-year free-occupation right on family home for surviving spouse/partner; **MC outside EU** so Brussels IV does not apply, but MC's 2017 Code de droit international privé (Loi n° 1.448) accepts professio juris by foreign nationality; trap — MC's professio juris is more permissive than FR's prélèvement compensatoire, so MC residents owning FR property still trigger FR Art. 913 al. 4. **MT (Malta)** — Civil Code Cap 16 Art. 615-653: *rizerva* = **1/3 of estate (children ≤4) / 1/2 (children ≥5)**; surviving-spouse reserve = **1/4 (with children) / 1/3 (no children)** in full ownership; *rizerva* is a money-credit against the estate (not co-ownership), accrues interest from succession opening; Brussels IV applies; trap — 10-yr limitation on *azzjoni ta' tnaqqis* (reduction action). **SE (Sweden)** — Ärvdabalken (1958:637) Ch. 7 § 1: laglott = **1/2 of intestate share (arvslott) for biological children ONLY**; spouse has NO laglott but receives full intestate estate ahead of common children with deferred-inheritance for first-marriage children (efterarv); Brussels IV applies; 1934 Nordic Convention runs in parallel inter-Nordic; trap — disinherited child must contest will within **6 MONTHS** of being notified of will (klandertalan, ÄB 7:3) or laglott claim lapses. ## CEE + Western Balkans + Baltics + Caucasus

### Regional pattern

The region groups into four legal families with sharply different stress points for foreign property buyers. **EU-CEE (PL, CZ, HU, RO, HR, SI, SK, BG, EE, LV, LT, CY)** all apply Brussels IV (Reg. 650/2012) directly: succession follows the law of habitual residence at death unless the deceased made a `professio juris` for the law of nationality (Art. 22). For these states the foreign-buyer playbook is mostly procedural — execute a will with a clean Art. 22 election — but the *forced-heirship* layer of the chosen law still applies and can travel with you across borders.

**Ex-Yugoslav non-EU (BA, ME, MK, RS, plus EU-member HR/SI)** all derive their inheritance statutes from the SFRY Federal Law on Inheritance of 1955 (Zakon o nasljeđivanju SFRJ, "Sl. list FNRJ" 20/55). The five successor regimes differ only in revision history; the core *nužni dio* of 1/2 of the intestate share for descendants/spouse and 1/3 for ascendants/siblings is functionally identical across all five. BA is split (separate statutes for Federation BiH and Republika Srpska, plus Brčko) but with substantially the same fractions ([Paragraf BA — RS Zakon o nasljeđivanju](https://www.paragraf.ba/propisi/republika-srpska/zakon-o-nasljedjivanju.html)).

**Post-Soviet (AM, AZ, GE, MD)** civil codes were rebuilt 1995-2003 on the structural template of the 1994 Russian Federation Civil Code (Part III on succession enacted 2002), giving each a *обязательная доля / обовʼязкова частка / cota rezervată* fixed at **1/2 of the intestate share** for descendants/spouse/parents who at the time of opening the succession are minor or incapable of work. MD's 2018 Civil Code recodification (Law 133/2018, in force 1 Mar 2019) modernised the language and kept the 1/2 fraction; **the recodification renumbered Book IV and the pre-2019 "Art. 1505" no longer locates the provision — the current article number is not confirmable from a primary source reachable here (legis.md is behind a JavaScript/bot wall): `verify the article number and current text at [legis.md, the Ministry of Justice State Register of Legal Acts](https://www.legis.md/), opened in a browser`**. None of the four are in Brussels IV; cross-border practitioners use bilateral treaties or the deceased's national law via lex situs for immovables.

**Cyprus is the regional outlier.** Common-law jurisdiction layered with a statutory portion regime (Wills and Succession Law Cap. 195). Section 42 — which historically let those with UK/Commonwealth-domiciled fathers escape forced heirship — was repealed by Law 96(I)/2015 with effect for deaths on or after **3 July 2015** ([Mondaq — Cap. 195 § 42 repeal](https://www.mondaq.com/cyprus/wills-intestacy-estate-planning/1663676/cyprus-succession-law-chapter-195-repeal-of-section-42)). Brussels IV applies in CY, but for *immovables* in Cyprus the forced-heirship rules now bite regardless of testator's domicile or `professio juris`.

The CJEU's current line is *narrow but deferential to forum public policy*: **C-21/22 OP v Notariusz Gawlica (12 Oct 2023)** confirmed that a third-country national resident in an EU member state may choose the law of their third state under Art. 22 ([EUR-Lex 62022CJ0021](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0021)); **C-651/21 M.Ya.M. (30 Mar 2023)** clarified that succession waivers registered before one Member State court can be re-registered before the court of the deceased's habitual-residence Member State ([EUR-Lex 62021CJ0651](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0651)); **C-187/23 Albausy (25 Jan 2025)** held that an authority issuing a European Certificate of Succession does not act as a "court" for Art. 267 TFEU references ([conflictoflaws.net Albausy comment](https://conflictoflaws.net/2025/cjeu-in-albausy-on-inadmissibility-of-questions-for-a-preliminary-ruling-under-succession-regulation/)). The ECtHR went the other direction on the underlying right: **Jarre v France** and **Colombier v France (15 Feb 2024)** held that forced-heirship beneficiaries hold neither an "existing" property right nor a "legitimate expectation" under Art. 1 Protocol 1 ECHR — confirming that whether *réserve héréditaire* / *zachowek* / *povinný díl* survives a foreign-law election is **forum-dependent ordre public**, not a Convention right ([conflictoflaws.net 2026 — protection of forced heirs](https://conflictoflaws.net/2026/protection-of-forced-heirs-and-international-public-policy/)).

**AZ (Azerbaijan)** — Civil Code (Mülki Məcəllə) provisions on məcburi pay; *the specific text of Art. 1193 is not surfaced in publicly available English-language primary sources* — verify at the official ILO/NATLEX deposit copy at [natlex.ilo.org AZ Civil Code](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/114484/AZE-114484.pdf). **BA (Bosnia & Herzegovina)** — Two-tier statute: Federation BiH Zakon o nasljeđivanju ("Sl. novine FBiH" 80/14) and Republika Srpska Zakon o nasljeđivanju ("Sl. glasnik RS" 1/09 and later) ([RS Zakon — Paragraf BA](https://www.paragraf.ba/propisi/republika-srpska/zakon-o-nasljedjivanju.html); [FBiH Zakon — Paragraf BA](https://www.paragraf.ba/propisi/fbih/zakon-o-nasledjivanju-u-federaciji-bih.html)). Hidden trap: cross-entity property — choice of FBiH vs RS statute turns on the *deceased's last residence*, not on lex rei sitae of the immovable. **Confidence: MEDIUM.**

**BG (Bulgaria)** — Закон за наследството Art. 28-29: запазена част for **descendants** = **1/2** if the testator leaves one child or descendants of one child; **2/3** for two or more children; for the **spouse** = **1/2** if inheriting alone, **1/3** if inheriting alongside parents, equal-to-each-child if inheriting alongside descendants ([justice.government.bg Закон за наследството](https://justice.government.bg/home/normdoc/2121542657)). Brussels IV in force since 17 Aug 2015; **C-651/21 M.Ya.M.** is the leading BG case and concerns Art. 13 cross-border waiver registration, not the substantive fraction. Hidden trap: запазена част calculation re-aggregates lifetime gifts (donatio inofficiosa) — gifts can be clawed back to satisfy the share. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**CY (Cyprus)** — Wills and Succession Law Cap. 195: disposable portion = **1/4** if the deceased leaves spouse + children/descendants; **1/2** if deceased leaves spouse + parents; the **whole** estate if no spouse/children/parents. Forced (statutory) portion is therefore 75% / 50% / 0% respectively ([cylaw.org Cap. 195 PDF](http://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/arith/CAP195.pdf)). Brussels IV applies; **for Cyprus-situs immovables Cap. 195 forced-heirship rules apply regardless of testator's domicile** post-Section 42 repeal (Law 96(I)/2015, deaths on/after 3 Jul 2015). Hidden trap: pre-2015 wills relying on s.42 freedom for Commonwealth-domiciled testators are now **vulnerable to challenge** by natural children. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**CZ (Czech Republic)** — Občanský zákoník 89/2012 Sb. § 1642-1645: nepominutelný dědic = descendants only; povinný díl = **1/4 of the intestate share** for adult descendants and **3/4 of the intestate share** for minor descendants ([§ 1643 — kurzy.cz](https://www.kurzy.cz/zakony/89-2012-obcansky-zakonik/cast-3-hlava-3-dil-5-oddil-1/); [Justice ministry NOZ explanation](http://obcanskyzakonik.justice.cz/index.php/dedicke-pravo/konkretni-zmeny/pravo-na-povinny-dil-a-nepominutelny-dedic)). Brussels IV in force. Hidden trap: NOZ 2014 *halved* the pre-2014 ObčZ 1964 entitlement (which was 1/2 for adults and the full intestate share for minors) — wills written before 1 Jan 2014 should be re-reviewed. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**EE (Estonia)** — Pärimisseadus § 104: sundosa = **1/2** of the value of the intestate share that the testator's descendants, parents or surviving spouse would have received, *gated on the deceased owing them a Family Law Act maintenance obligation at the time of death* ([riigiteataja.ee Law of Succession Act, English consolidation](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/en/eli/506112013007/consolide)). Brussels IV in force. Hidden trap: the *maintenance-obligation* gate makes EE one of the narrowest forced-heirship regimes in CEE — financially independent adult children can be cleanly disinherited. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**GE (Georgia)** — Civil Code of Georgia (1997, Law No. 786) provisions on *обязательная доля* in the Russian-template 1/2-of-intestate-share family. *Specific Art. 1372 text not surfaced in primary English-language sources during this pass — verify at the official Legislative Herald [matsne.gov.ge Civil Code](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/31702) before quoting the article number*. Not in Brussels IV. **Confidence: LOW.**

**HR (Croatia)** — Zakon o nasljeđivanju ("NN" 48/03, 163/03, 35/05, 127/13, 33/15, 14/19) Art. 69-72: nužni dio = **1/2** of the intestate share for descendants, adoptees + their descendants, and the surviving spouse; **1/3** for parents/adopters and other ancestors, and only if they are permanently unable to work and lack means ([narodne-novine.nn.hr 48/03 ZN](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2003_03_48_604.html); [zakon.hr ZN](https://www.zakon.hr/z/87/zakon-o-nasljedivanju)). Brussels IV in force. Hidden trap: 2003 reform replaced the 1955 Federal SFRY law and *narrowed* parents' entitlement — pre-2003 estates may have different fraction expectations than post-2003 ones. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**HU (Hungary)** — Polgári törvénykönyv Act V of 2013, Book Seven, **§ 7:75-7:82**: kötelesrész = **1/3 of the intestate share** for descendants, surviving spouse, and parents who would be statutory heirs ([Act V of 2013 — tdziegler PDF](https://tdziegler.files.wordpress.com/2014/06/civil_code.pdf); [net.jogtar.hu PTK](https://net.jogtar.hu/jogszabaly?docid=a1300005.tv)). Brussels IV in force. Hidden trap: 2014 PTK *cut* the kötelesrész from 1/2 (under the 1959 Code) to 1/3 — wills written under the 1959 Code may now over-distribute relative to current entitlements. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**LT (Lithuania)** — Civilinis kodeksas Art. 5.20: privalomoji dalis = **1/2 of the intestate share** for the testator's children, spouse, and parents *who at the time of the testator's death were dependent on the testator and unable to work* ([wipo.int LT Civil Code](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/8191)). Brussels IV in force; LT has no inheritance tax. Hidden trap: *dependence + incapacity-to-work* dual gate at the moment of death — financially independent adult children with capacity get **nothing** by way of forced share. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**LV (Latvia)** — Civillikums Sub-Chapter 3 (Sections 422-426) — neatraidamie mantinieki + neatņemamā daļa: the forced share = **half of the value of the intestate share** that the forced heir (descendants, surviving spouse, and parents with no other support) would have received ([civillikums.lv Inheritance Law (English)](https://civillikums.lv/en/civil-law/inheritance-law)). Latvia operates a flat 1/2 of the intestate share, NOT a Bulgarian-style sliding scale. Brussels IV in force. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**MD (Moldova)** — Codul civil, Cartea a IV-a (post-2018 recodification via Law 133/2018, in force 1 Mar 2019): cota succesorală rezervată = **at least 1/2 of the share** that a Class I successor (children, parents, surviving spouse) **who is unable to work** would have received in legal succession — **article number not confirmable from a primary source this pass; `verify the article number and text at [legis.md](https://www.legis.md/)` (State Register of Legal Acts, Ministry of Justice), opened in a browser, and check for amendments after Law 133/2018**. Not in Brussels IV (Association Agreement state, EU candidate status June 2022). Hidden trap: like LT, the *incapacity-to-work* gate restricts the entitled circle far more narrowly than the post-Soviet template suggests at first glance. **Confidence: LOW** (fraction and incapacity gate consistent across sources; article number and post-2018 amendment history unverified).

**ME (Montenegro)** — Zakon o nasljeđivanju ("Sl. list CG" 74/08, latest 75/17): nužni dio = **1/2 of the intestate share** for descendants, adoptees + descendants, and spouse; **1/3** for other forced heirs (parents, siblings, grandparents) only if permanently incapable of work and without means ([paragraf.me Zakon o nasljeđivanju](https://www.paragraf.me/propisi-crnegore/zakon-o-nasljedjivanju.html)). Not in Brussels IV (EU candidate; accession on accelerated track for 2028). Hidden trap: ME-situs immovables are subject to ME forced-heirship regardless of foreign-buyer's habitual residence — Brussels IV does not yet bind ME courts. **Confidence: MEDIUM.**

**MK (North Macedonia)** — Закон за наследување ("Сл. весник на РМ" 47/96, with later amendments): нужен дел for descendants, adoptees + descendants, and spouse follows the SFRY-1955 1/2 template; ascendants and siblings 1/3 conditional on incapacity. *Primary-text article numbering not independently confirmed in this pass — verify at official "Службен весник" before citing specific articles.* Not in Brussels IV (EU candidate since 2005, negotiations stalled per [Nov 2025 EC Report](https://europeanwesternbalkans.com/2025/11/10/north-macedonia-ec-report-2025-stagnation-and-frustration/)). **Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM.**

**PL (Poland)** — Kodeks cywilny Art. 991 § 1: zachowek = **1/2 of the intestate share** ordinarily; **2/3 of the intestate share** if the entitled person is permanently unable to work or is a minor descendant; circle = descendants, spouse, parents who would be intestate heirs ([sip.lex.pl Art. 991 KC](https://sip.lex.pl/akty-prawne/dzu-dziennik-ustaw/kodeks-cywilny-16785996/art-991); [Mondaq — succession law in Poland](https://www.mondaq.com/wealth-asset-management/1618858/%E2%80%8Bsuccession-law-in-poland-the-rules-on-forced-heirship-zachowek)). Brussels IV in force; **C-21/22 OP v Notariusz Gawlica (12 Oct 2023)** is a PL-referred case and confirms third-country nationals resident in PL may elect their national law under Art. 22. **RO (Romania)** — Codul civil (Law 287/2009, in force 1 Oct 2011) Art. 1086-1088: rezerva succesorală = **1/2 of the share** that descendants, surviving spouse, and privileged ascendants (parents) would have received in intestate succession ([CNUE Romania](https://www.notariesofeurope.eu/en/faq3/successions-in-europe/romania/); [IBA 2025 RO Estate Planning Guide](https://www.ibanet.org/document?id=PCT-estate-guides-romania-2025)). Hidden trap: clawback (*acțiune în reducțiunea liberalităților excesive*) operates only *within the limit of the reserved portion of the applicant* and only to the applicant's benefit, so each forced heir must individually sue. Most recent Civil Code amendment via **Law 231/2025** (Of. Gazette 1164/16 Dec 2025) — verify whether it touches succession before relying on pre-2025 text. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**RS (Serbia)** — Zakon o nasleđivanju ("Sl. glasnik RS" 46/95, 101/03, 6/15) Art. 39 + Art. 40: nužni naslednici = descendants, adoptees + descendants, spouse, parents, adoptive parent, siblings, grandparents and other ancestors; nužni deo = **1/2 of the intestate share** for descendants/adoptees + descendants and spouse, **1/3 of the intestate share** for the other categories ([pravno-informacioni-sistem.rs ZON 46/95](https://pravno-informacioni-sistem.rs/SlGlasnikPortal/eli/rep/sgrs/skupstina/zakon/1995/46/1/reg)). Not in Brussels IV (EU candidate; negotiations open). Hidden trap: the broad nužni-naslednici circle (siblings, grandparents) goes well beyond the descendants-only circles used in CZ/EE/LT — RS forced heirship reaches further down the family tree than most of the region. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**SI (Slovenia)** — Zakon o dedovanju (1976, with amendments — most recent ZD-C "Ur. l. RS" 31/13) Art. 25-30: nujni delež = **1/2 of the intestate share** for descendants, adoptees + descendants and spouse; **1/3** for parents, grandparents, siblings only if permanently incapable of work and without means ([pisrs.si Zakon o dedovanju](http://www.pisrs.si/Pis.web/pregledPredpisa?id=ZAKO317)). Brussels IV in force. Hidden trap: SI follows the SFRY 1955 template virtually unchanged — practitioners familiar with HR/RS/BA can navigate SI rules identically. **Confidence: HIGH.**

**SK (Slovakia)** — Občiansky zákonník 40/1964 Zb. § 479: neopomenuteľný dedič = descendants only; minor descendants must receive **at least the full intestate share**; adult descendants must receive **at least 1/2 of the intestate share** ([e-justice.europa.eu SK succession portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/topics/family-matters-inheritance/inheritance/succession/sk_en); [zakonypreludi.sk OZ 40/1964](https://www.zakonypreludi.sk/zz/1964-40)). This is a 1964-vintage Communist-era statute still in force — SK has yet to enact a NOZ-style modern Civil Code. Brussels IV in force. **Pending reform: Sept 2025 government draft NOZ** ([Návrh OZ — justice.gov.sk PDF](https://www.justice.gov.sk/dokumenty/2025/09/Navrh-Obcianskeho-zakonnika.pdf)) would *cut* minor children's mandatory share to 2/10 (20%) of the intestate share and add an inheritance-contract mechanism enabling care-for-flat exchanges; in interministerial consultation through 1 Dec 2025; targeted effective date **1 Jul 2027** if enacted. **Confidence: HIGH** (current law); **MEDIUM** (reform timing).

## MENA + GCC + Israel + Türkiye

### Regional pattern

This region is the most heterogeneous on the planet for inheritance law applied to a foreign-owned residence — three distinct legal traditions overlap with bilateral lex-situs and confessional carve-outs. The default for the eight Muslim-majority jurisdictions in the set (SA, QA, KW, BH, OM, JO, MA, EG) is **classical Sharia inheritance** as codified through national Personal Status Codes: fixed Quranic shares from Quran 4:11/12 (son receives 2× daughter's share; surviving wife 1/8 of the estate where there are descendants and 1/4 where there are none; surviving husband 1/4 with descendants and 1/2 without; parents typically 1/6 each where descendants exist), with testamentary freedom capped at **1/3 of the estate (wasiyya)** and that 1/3 generally not assignable to a forced heir without the others' consent (Saudi MoJ — `data not publicly available — verify at https://www.moj.gov.sa/` (the previously cited MoJ PDF is the repealed 2000 real-estate-ownership law and carries no inheritance provisions), [Egypt Civil Code — UMN HR Library (PDF)](https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/research/Egypt/Civil%20Law.pdf)).

Three jurisdictions break that default. **Türkiye** has applied a Swiss-derived secular Civil Code since 1926; forced heirship (*saklı pay*) is now codified in **Turkish Civil Code (TMK) Article 506** at 1/2 of the statutory share for descendants, 1/4 of the estate for the spouse where descendants exist (1/2 where none), and 1/4 of the statutory share for parents — and lex situs (Code 5718, Art. 20) overrides any foreign-nationality choice on Turkish immovables ([CoE — Code 5718 PDF](https://rm.coe.int/law-on-international-private-and-procedure-law/1680a3bcae)). **Israel**'s Succession Law 5725-1965 is a unified civil regime (no Quranic shares); intestate spouse takes 1/2 with descendants and 2/3 with no descendants but other heirs, and Israeli law has no continental-style forced share — only a discretionary maintenance claim by spouse, minor/disabled child or dependent parent under Chapter 6 ([Succession Law 1965 — PDF](https://muslimfamilylawindex.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Succession-Law-1965.pdf)). **Tunisia**'s 1956 Personal Status Code retains Sharia shares but bans polygamy and is the most reformist Muslim-majority code in the region ([Equality Now — PSC 1956](https://equalitynow.org/resource/uncategorised/tunisia_personal_status_code_of_1956/)).

The escape hatches for non-Muslim foreign buyers vary. The **UAE** Federal Decree-Law 41/2022 (in force 1 Feb 2023) entitles non-Muslim foreigners to opt out of Sharia entirely on UAE assets and apply law of nationality, with the **DIFC Wills Service Centre** (Dubai/RAK) and the **Abu Dhabi Judicial Department (ADJD) Wills Registry** (covering all seven emirates) as the registration channels ([UAE Legislation — Decree-Law 41/2022](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1586), [DIFC Courts Wills](https://www.difccourts.ae/ws), [ADJD Wills FAQ](https://www.adjd.gov.ae/EN/Pages/NonMFAQs.aspx)) ❌ DEPRECATED — dead on 2026-09-18; verify with the authority named in this row. **Qatar** Family Law (22/2006) Art. 3 carries the principle that family-law matters of non-Muslims are subject to "their own provisions," and inheritance follows the deceased's national law for non-Muslim foreigners ([Al Meezan — Law 22/2006](https://www.almeezan.qa/LawPage.aspx?id=2558&language=en)). **Bahrain** routes non-Muslim succession to the High Civil Court applying the deceased's national law ([UK Gov — Family law in Bahrain](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/family-law-in-bahrain/family-law-in-bahrain)). **Egypt** Civil Code Art. 17 lets foreigners apply national law to their inheritance, but Art. 18 carves out Egyptian immovables to Egyptian (Sharia-derived) law as lex situs ([Egypt Civil Code — UMN PDF](https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/research/Egypt/Civil%20Law.pdf)).

**Lebanon** is the regional outlier and the lowest-confidence jurisdiction in the set: 18 officially recognised confessions, each with its own personal-status court and law for marriage/divorce. Inheritance, however, splits two-track: **Mohammedan** communities (Sunni, Shia, Alawi) follow Sharia inheritance via their religious courts; **all non-Mohammedan confessions** (12 Christian denominations + Jewish + Druze) are governed by the **Civil Inheritance Law of 23 June 1959**, applied by ordinary civil courts (the Druze are deemed non-Mohammedan for inheritance under the 1959 Act despite being Muslim-derived) ([Tohme Law — Lebanon Succession](https://tohmelaw.com/publications/Lebanon%20Succession%20Laws), [IBA — Lebanon estate planning](https://www.ibanet.org/document?id=Lebanon-international-estate-planning)). The 1959 Act includes its own forced-heirship and reserved-portion rules for non-Mohammedans.

For a foreign property buyer, the practical hierarchy is: (1) is the buyer Muslim? if yes, every Muslim-majority jurisdiction in this list applies Sharia by default and only AE provides a meaningful opt-out (and only for non-Muslims, so this column closes); (2) if non-Muslim, what does the bilateral nationality-law channel allow on the immovable specifically? Egypt and Türkiye both narrow it via lex situs on real estate; UAE/Qatar/Bahrain/Oman/Lebanon/Morocco generally allow nationality-law application; (3) is there a registered will in the right register? The default in every jurisdiction here, with no will, is the local regime — meaning for a non-Muslim foreigner without a registered nationality-law will, Sharia (or 1959 Act, or TMK 506) applies by operation of law.

### Country one-liners

**AE (United Arab Emirates)** — Federal Decree-Law 41/2022 (in force 1 Feb 2023) lets non-Muslim foreigners opt out of Sharia entirely; intestate default under the new Civil Personal Status regime gives 1/2 to surviving spouse and 1/2 split equally between children regardless of sex ([UAE Legislation](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1586), [LoC](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2023-03-13/united-arab-emirates-personal-status-law-for-non-muslims-enters-into-force/)); registration channels are the **DIFC Wills Service Centre** (assets in Dubai + RAK) and the **ADJD Wills Registry** (assets across all 7 emirates; **registration-volume figures: data not publicly available — verify at the [Abu Dhabi Judicial Department](https://www.adjd.gov.ae/) / Non-Muslim Wills Registration Office**) ([DIFC Courts Wills](https://www.difccourts.ae/ws), [UAE Legislation — Decree-Law 41/2022](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1586)); **trap**: opt-out is not automatic — without a registered will at DIFC or ADJD (or an explicit nationality-law election in a will valid under Decree-Law 41/2022), Sharia remains the default for Muslims and the new civil regime for non-Muslims, and Muslim foreigners cannot use the opt-out at all; **Confidence: HIGH**.

**SA (Saudi Arabia)** — classical Sharia inheritance applied directly by general (Sharia) courts under the Hanbali school; testamentary freedom capped at 1/3 (*wasiyya*); fixed Quranic shares (son 2× daughter; wife 1/8 with kids, 1/4 without; husband 1/4 with kids, 1/2 without; parents 1/6 each with kids); foreign-buyer regime replaced by the Law on Non-Saudis Ownership of Real Estate, Royal Decree M/14 issued 14 Jul 2025 (Cabinet approval 8 Jul 2025; gazetted in *Umm Al-Qura* 25 Jul 2025; in force Jan 2026), which repeals the 2000 law (RD M/15 of 17/4/1421H — [repealed text, Saudi MoJ](https://www.moj.gov.sa/Documents/Regulations/pdf/En/37.pdf)) and moves to a designated-zone model; **Mecca and Medina are no longer closed to all non-Saudis — under art. 2(4) a non-Saudi *Muslim* individual may take title there, a non-Muslim may not, and foreign companies are barred except narrow operational / CMA-licensed cases; whether M/14 preserves the old art. 5(1) inheritance / waqf route into the two cities is not established — verify before advising a non-Saudi heir** (2026 data, sources [Curtis](https://www.curtis.com/our-firm/news/saudi-arabias-new-law-on-foreign-ownership-of-real-estate-a-major-development-in-the-saudi-real-estate-sector), [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/real-estate-ownership-by-non-saudis-law-now-published.html)) — *law-firm alerts only; the primary Umm Al-Qura text of M/14 was not located, verify at [Umm Al-Qura](https://uqn.gov.sa/) before relying*; **trap**: no foreign-nationality opt-out exists for any party on Saudi-situs real estate; lifetime gift (*hiba*) and 1/3 *wasiyya* are the only planning vehicles; non-Muslims cannot inherit from Muslims and vice versa; **Confidence: MEDIUM** (downgraded 2026-09-18 — the primary Umm Al-Qura text of RD M/14 is not yet cited).

**QA (Qatar)** — Family Law 22/2006 applies Hanbali Sharia to Muslims; for non-Muslim foreigners, family-law matters (including inheritance) follow the deceased's national law via the principle that "non-Muslim parties are subject to their own provisions" ([Al Meezan — Law 22/2006](https://www.almeezan.qa/LawPage.aspx?id=2558&language=en), [UK Gov — Family law in Qatar](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/family-law-in-qatar/family-law-in-qatar)); estate may be temporarily frozen until heirs present a foreign heir's certificate; **trap**: Qatari Family Court still adjudicates the procedural steps and can decline to recognise foreign-law clauses that conflict with Qatari public policy; **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**KW (Kuwait)** — Personal Status Law 51/1984 applies Sharia, with the Maliki school as the default for Sunnis and separate Jafari Sharia courts for Shia (significant inheritance divergence — under Shia law the wife may inherit the entirety of the estate where there is no male agnatic heir, vs Sunni cap at 1/8 for the wife); non-Muslims fall under their own confessional law. **Article-level detail (the 347-article count and the Art. 346 / Art. 346(b) numbering) rested on a paywalled commercial database and an NGO overview table, neither citable as primary — dropped; `statute text not publicly available — verify Law 51/1984 with the Kuwait Ministry of Justice or the official gazette Kuwait Al-Youm (الكويت اليوم)`** ([Musawah — Kuwait](https://www.musawah.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Kuwait-Overview-Table-2022.pdf) *(NGO secondary — school-of-law framing only)*); **trap**: a Muslim and a non-Muslim cannot inherit from each other under Sharia, so non-Muslim spouses of Kuwaiti Muslims often use lifetime "sale" to the spouse as a workaround ([HRW — Kuwait](https://www.hrw.org/reports/2000/kuwait/kuwait-05.htm)); **Confidence: LOW** (no primary statute text; school-of-law framing is NGO/secondary-sourced).

**BH (Bahrain)** — unified Family Law 19/2017 codified both Sunni and Shia (Jafari) jurisprudence for the first time; the school applied at the marriage contract determines inheritance jurisprudence in mixed cases; **non-Muslim succession is heard by the High Civil Court applying the deceased's national law**, and non-Muslims may notarise a will with a Bahraini notary covering Bahraini and foreign assets ([UK Gov — Family law in Bahrain](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/family-law-in-bahrain/family-law-in-bahrain), [State Dept — Bahrain 2022 IRF](https://www.state.gov/reports/2022-report-on-international-religious-freedom/bahrain)); **trap**: Sunni–Shia divergence in fixed shares means an interfaith Muslim couple's estate plan depends on the marriage-contract school; **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**OM (Oman)** — Sultani Decree 32/97 Personal Status Law applies Sharia to Muslims; non-Muslims follow the law of their community, and for foreigners the law of the country of origin generally governs movables, but **Omani law applies to Omani-situs immovables** ([Oman Supreme Judiciary Council — Personal Status Law, SD 32/97 (English PDF), art. 282](https://www.sjc.gov.om/InnerPage.aspx?ID=d0321e66-105a-4335-9bcf-a84d7a7c139d&culture=en)); foreign property ownership is restricted to designated Integrated Tourism Complexes (ITCs) under Sultani Decree 12/2006 + amendments, and inheritance of ITC freeholds by foreign heirs is permitted ([Al Tamimi — Oman foreign ownership](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/foreign-ownership-of-real-estate-in-oman/)); **trap**: SD 32/97 expressly bars cross-religion inheritance; **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**IL (Israel)** — Succession Law 5725-1965 is a unified civil regime applied by the Family Court (Inheritance Registrar for uncontested estates); intestate spouse takes 1/2 with descendants and 2/3 with no descendants but other relatives; **no continental-style forced share** — testamentary freedom is broad, with only a discretionary maintenance claim under Chapter 6 for spouse, minor/disabled child, or dependent parent ([Succession Law 1965 PDF](https://muslimfamilylawindex.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Succession-Law-1965.pdf)); **trap**: religious courts (Rabbinical, Sharia, Druze, Christian) retain concurrent jurisdiction over estates of their own community by consent of all parties — forum-shopping risk for spouse share; for foreign nationals Art. 137 directs application of the deceased's last domicile law, subject to Israeli public-policy override; **Confidence: HIGH**.

**JO (Jordan)** — Personal Status Law 36/2010 applies Hanafi Sharia to Muslims via Sharia courts; **inheritance for Christians is administered by the church councils of the relevant denomination but governed substantively by Civil Code Art. 1086 which still routes to Sharia principles** unless the Christian community has obtained a derogation (Christian leaders approved a draft equal-inheritance bill May 2023, not yet enacted as of May 2026) ([Jordan Times — 2023 Christian leaders bill](https://jordantimes.com/news/local/christian-leaders-unanimously-approve-bill-granting-gender-equality-inheritance), [Coptic Solidarity — Jordan 2023](https://www.copticsolidarity.org/2023/05/22/jordans-christians-approve-new-law-with-equal-inheritance-rights/)); **trap**: even Christians in Jordan default to Sharia-derived shares unless they actively elect their confessional code, and Druze/Bahá'í are governed by separate codes; **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**LB (Lebanon)** — two-track: **Mohammedan communities** (Sunni, Shia, Alawi) follow Sharia inheritance through religious courts; **all non-Mohammedan confessions** (12 Christian denominations + Jewish + Druze) are governed by the **Civil Inheritance Law of 23 June 1959** (Official Gazette 31/59) applied by ordinary civil courts, which itself contains forced-heirship/reserved-portion rules ([Tohme Law — Lebanon](https://tohmelaw.com/publications/Lebanon%20Succession%20Laws), [IBA — Lebanon estate planning](https://www.ibanet.org/document?id=Lebanon-international-estate-planning)); confessional jurisdiction is determined by **religion of the deceased at death**, so a religious conversion mid-life can flip the entire regime; the Druze sect's own personal status law (24 Feb 1948) governs Druze marriage/divorce/custody, but Druze inheritance falls under the 1959 Act; **Confidence: LOW** — case-specific by sect and conversion history.

**MA (Morocco)** — Moudawana 2004 (Family Code) applies Maliki Sharia inheritance to Muslims (son 2× daughter; *taasib* preference); **Moudawana applies to all Moroccan nationals regardless of second nationality, but does not apply to two foreigners both resident in Morocco — they fall under their national law(s)** ([Moudawana 2004 EN PDF](https://learningpartnership.org/sites/default/files/resources/pdfs/Morocco%20Family%20Code%20(Moudawan)%202004%20English.pdf), [Avocat Marocain — Moudawana FAQ](https://avocatmarocain.com/en/faq-droit-de-la-famille-au-maroc/)); for non-Muslim foreigners owning Moroccan property, nationality-law clauses in Moroccan-notarised wills are generally honoured; the Dec 2024 Moudawana revision adds a route to transfer property to daughters during the parent's lifetime to neutralise *taasib* but does not change post-mortem shares ([Morocco World News — Dec 2024 reforms](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2024/12/166811/approved-reforms-in-moroccos-family-code-whats-changing/)); **trap**: a Moroccan-Muslim co-owner spouse pulls the entire property back into Sharia distribution even if the foreign co-owner intended otherwise; **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**TN (Tunisia)** — Personal Status Code 1956 (Bourguiba) retains Sharia shares (son 2× daughter; spouse 1/8 / 1/4 / 1/2 per Quranic schedule depending on heirs) but applies them through ordinary civil courts and is procedurally the most reformist in the Arab world; the 2018 Essebsi equal-inheritance bill (with opt-out by notarised will) was approved by cabinet 23 Nov 2018 but **stalled in parliament after Essebsi's death (2019) and President Saied's public rejection (Aug 2020), and is not enacted as of May 2026** ([HRW — Tunisia 2018 bill](https://www.hrw.org/news/2018/12/04/tunisia-parliament-should-back-gender-equality-inheritance), [LoC — Tunisia 2018](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2018-12-04/tunisia-cabinet-approves-bill-requiring-equal-inheritance-shares-for-men-and-women/), [Al Jazeera — Aug 2023 status](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2023/8/22/will-inheritance-equality-ever-be-up-for-debate-in-tunisia)); foreign-nationality choice via Tunisian PIL for movables, lex situs for Tunisian immovables; **trap**: foreign-buyer access to Tunisian immovables outside designated tourism zones requires Governorate authorisation, which carries inheritance implications for non-resident heirs; **Confidence: MEDIUM**.

**TR (Türkiye)** — secular Civil Code (TMK) since 1926; forced heirship (*saklı pay*) under **TMK Art. 506**: descendants 1/2 of their statutory share, surviving spouse 1/4 of the estate where descendants exist (1/2 where none but other heirs), parents 1/4 of their statutory share; remainder freely disposable by will, but heirs may file *tenkis davası* (reduction action) to claw back any over-disposition ([Code 5718 PDF](https://rm.coe.int/law-on-international-private-and-procedure-law/1680a3bcae)); for foreigners, **Code 5718 (MÖHUK) Art. 20 applies the deceased's national law to inheritance generally but Turkish law to immovables in Türkiye** — i.e. Turkish forced-heirship rules apply to Turkish-situs property regardless of foreign-nationality clauses; for dual nationals where one nationality is Turkish, Turkish law prevails; **trap**: the Art. 20 lex-situs override on Turkish immovables defeats most foreign-will planning for property in Türkiye; foreign heirs of inherited Turkish real estate not liquidated within 1 year may be force-sold under foreign-ownership ceiling rules; **Confidence: HIGH**.

**EG (Egypt)** — Personal Status Law 25/1929 (and Law 25/1920) applies Sharia inheritance to Muslims; Christians and Jews follow their own personal-status law for marriage/divorce but **inheritance for all Egyptians is administered through the Sharia-rooted regime under Civil Code Art. 875** unless explicitly elected otherwise; for foreigners, **Civil Code Art. 17 applies the deceased's national law to inheritance, wills and post-mortem dispositions — but Egyptian law (i.e. Sharia-derived) governs Egyptian-situs immovables under Art. 18 lex situs** ([Egypt Civil Code PDF](https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/research/Egypt/Civil%20Law.pdf), [bylawme — Egypt inheritance](https://www.bylawme.com/post/inheritance-law-egypt-wills-religion-gender-foreigners), [alzayat — Egyptian inheritance 2025 guide](https://alzayatfirm.com/egyptian-inheritance-law/)); foreigners cannot acquire agricultural or desert land; **trap**: nationality-law election for foreigners works on Egyptian movables (bank accounts, shares) but **fails on Egyptian real estate** — Egyptian Sharia-derived shares apply to the property regardless of testator nationality; **Confidence: HIGH**.

## APAC + Central Asia

### Regional pattern

APAC succession law splits into four families that determine forced-heirship exposure for foreign property buyers. **Civil-law forced-share** group — China (Civil Code 2021), Japan (Minpō iryūbun), Korea (Civil Act yuryubun), Taiwan (Civil Code Art. 1223), Macau (Portuguese-derived legítima), Cambodia, Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan (post-Soviet codes) — all impose a fixed-fraction reserved share that overrides the will. **Common-law testamentary-freedom** group — Singapore, Hong Kong, Malaysia (non-Muslims), India (Christians/Parsis under ISA 1925), Sri Lanka (Roman-Dutch base), Thailand — leaves the will largely free, with dependants getting only a discretionary maintenance claim through statutes modelled on the UK Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975. **Sharia/personal-law-pluralism** group — Indonesia, Malaysia (Muslim citizens), India (Muslims), Maldives, Sri Lanka (Muslims), and the Muslim-Uzbek customary overlay — applies Faraid 2:1 male:female fixed shares with a 1/3 bequest cap. **Hybrid pluralism** — Philippines (legítima civil-law overlay on a US-influenced common-law procedure), Sri Lanka (Roman-Dutch + Kandyan + Thesavalamai + Muslim), India (Hindu/Muslim/Christian/Parsi parallel codes).

The **CN Civil Code (1 Jan 2021)** is the regional watershed — it consolidated the 1985 Inheritance Law into Book VI of the Civil Code, retained the discretionary "necessary share" for incapacitated dependants under Art. 1141 (no fixed %), and expanded testamentary forms. The **JP 2018 reform (effective 1 Jul 2019)** converted iryūbun from an in-kind property claim to a monetary claim under Art. 1046 — a structural shift that simplifies foreign-buyer succession because heirs no longer regain partial ownership of the actual property. The **KR Constitutional Court ruling of 25 Apr 2024** struck down Article 1112(4) (sibling yuryubun) as immediately void and gave the National Assembly a deadline of 31 Dec 2025 to revise paragraphs (1)-(3) — a major reform window still active.

**Brussels IV is irrelevant in APAC** — no APAC jurisdiction is a signatory to EU Regulation 650/2012, so foreign buyers cannot use a will-based choice-of-nationality-law to escape local forced shares. Each country applies its own private-international-law conflict rules: most civil-law APAC jurisdictions apply *lex situs* (location of property) for immovables, which means the country playbook applies regardless of buyer nationality. Notable exceptions — Philippines applies the *nationality* of the decedent for movables/successional order but cannot avoid PH legítima for PH-located real property; Taiwan permits a foreign testator to elect their national law for movables. **Sharia jurisdictions** (ID Muslim, MY Muslim citizens, MV, IN Muslim) determine the applicable regime by **religion of the deceased at death**, not domicile or nationality — a conversion-at-deathbed risk for mixed-religion families.

The biggest SE-Asia traps are **Vietnam Art. 644** (minor + disabled-adult descendants get 2/3 of statutory share even with a valid will to the contrary), **Indonesia's religion-of-deceased court routing** (Pengadilan Negeri civil court vs Pengadilan Agama religious court), and **Malaysia's Faraid/Distribution Act split** triggered by religion at death. The biggest Central-Asia trap is data opacity — KZ, UZ Civil Code Russian-language sources are well-documented but English-language secondary commentary is sparse, requiring local-counsel verification.

### Country one-liners

**CN (China)** — Civil Code Book VI (effective 1 Jan 2021) Art. 1141 reserves a court-determined "necessary share" for successors lacking work capacity AND independent income (no fixed %); Art. 1142 governs will revocation; testator otherwise has full freedom; Brussels IV n/a; **trap**: pre-2021 wills under the 1985 Inheritance Law need re-verification — 1985 Law's discretionary regime closely tracks Art. 1141, but spouse status as statutory heir was clarified in 2021; foreign buyers of PRC real estate face *lex situs* PRC law for immovables; primary heirs Art. 1127 = spouse + children + parents; **Confidence: HIGH** (article texts published at China Justice Observer, sourced to NPCSC).

**HK (Hong Kong)** — **No forced heirship and no community property** under HK law; testamentary freedom is the rule; Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Ordinance Cap. 481 allows a *dependant* maintenance claim (spouses get a "divorce standard" share, others get "reasonable maintenance" only); applies only to a deceased domiciled in HK or ordinarily resident in HK during 3 years before death; Brussels IV n/a; **trap**: foreign buyers domiciled abroad escape Cap. 481 entirely — the "ordinarily resident 3 years" gateway is jurisdictional; **Confidence: HIGH**.

**ID (Indonesia)** — pluralist regime: non-Muslim Indonesians under **Burgerlijk Wetboek (KUH Perdata) 1838** with *legitime portie* (Dutch-derived absolute share for descendants/ascendants) under Pengadilan Negeri civil court; Muslim Indonesians under **Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) 1991 + Faraid** with bequest cap of 1/3 of estate and only by consent of all heirs, under Pengadilan Agama religious court; **trap**: religion of the deceased at death determines court routing AND legal regime — a deathbed conversion materially changes the inheritance distribution; foreigners cannot own freehold (only Hak Pakai use-right), so estate-planning is layered over a non-freehold base; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: MEDIUM** (regime well-documented in Indonesian academic sources, but case-routing nuances by region need local counsel).

**IN (India)** — **No single succession law**: Hindu Succession Act 1956 (amended by HSA 2005, effective 9 Sep 2005, retroactive — daughters of Mitakshara-school coparceners get equal coparcenary rights by birth, same as sons) applies to Hindus/Sikhs/Jains/Buddhists; Muslim Personal Law (Sharia) applies to Muslims with bequest cap of 1/3 of net estate (after debts/funeral) without heir consent; Indian Succession Act 1925 applies to Christians (Part V Ch. II) and Parsis (Part V Ch. III) and gives broad testamentary freedom subject only to dependant-maintenance; **trap**: Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) coparcenary survives HSA 1956 — daughters' 2005 equal-share confirmed retrospective by SC ruling (Vineeta Sharma v Rakesh Sharma 2020); a foreign-buyer married to an Indian Hindu has property-rights interactions with HUF coparcenary that need a Hindu-law specialist; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** for Hindu/Muslim/Christian regimes (statutes and SC jurisprudence published at India Code).

**JP (Japan)** — Civil Code Art. 1042 (renumbered in 2018; pre-reform Art. 1028) — **iryūbun (遺留分) = 1/2 of intestate share** for spouse + descendants + ascendants when spouse/descendants are heirs; **1/3** when only ascendants are heirs; siblings have NO iryūbun; **2018 reform (effective 1 Jul 2019)** converted iryūbun from in-kind property claim to monetary claim under Art. 1046 (Iryūbun Shingai-gaku Seikyū-ken) — heirs no longer take partial ownership of specific assets but instead claim cash from beneficiaries; **trap**: 2018 reform applies to deaths after 1 Jul 2019 — pre-July-2019 estates retain in-kind claim; foreign-buyer can elect home-country law for movables under Hōrei Art. 36 but lex situs governs immovables; statute-of-limitations: 1 yr from knowledge of infringement, max 10 yr from death; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (full Civil Code at japaneselawtranslation.go.jp).

**KH (Cambodia)** — Civil Code 2007 Book VIII (Succession) — reserved-portion regime exists but exact article-text in English public sources is sparse; secondary sources describe a forced-share regime where descendants/spouse get a **reserved share** (some sources cite 1/2 of statutory share for descendants and 1/3 for ascendants/spouse — verify with local counsel against the FAOLEX Khmer text); foreigners cannot own land in Cambodia (constitutional ban) but can own condo units above ground floor; **trap**: post-Khmer-Rouge property restitution overlay — pre-1979 ownership claims interact with succession; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: LOW** (English-language Article-1233 verification not publicly available — verify at FAOLEX Khmer Civil Code text [`faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/cam204996.pdf`](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/cam204996.pdf)).

**KR (South Korea)** — Civil Act Art. 1112 (yuryubun, 유류분) **= 1/2 of intestate share** for spouse + descendants; **1/3 of intestate share** for parents; **siblings: 1/3 of intestate share PRE-25-Apr-2024 — STRUCK DOWN as of 25 Apr 2024 by Constitutional Court** (Decision 2020Hun-Ga4 et al., unanimous), Article 1112(4) immediately void; paragraphs (1)-(3) declared incompatible-with-Constitution with deadline 31 Dec 2025 for National Assembly revision; National Assembly received an amendment bill on 18 Dec 2025 (bill 2215371) — **monitor this reform window closely**; **trap**: existing yuryubun structures drafted before Apr 2024 may be partially invalid by 1 Jan 2026 if Assembly fails to legislate; foreign-buyers of Korean real estate face KR *lex situs*; statute of limitations: 1 yr from knowledge / 10 yr from death; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (KR Constitutional Court ruling published; statute at elaw.klri.re.kr).

**KZ (Kazakhstan)** — Civil Code Special Part (Section VI Inheritance) Art. 1069 **обязательная доля (obligatory share) = 1/2 of intestate share** reserved for minor children + adult-incapacitated children + spouse + parents who are dependent on testator; otherwise testamentary freedom; pre-nuptial waiver of spousal share is permitted; "unworthy heir" disqualification under Art. 1045 (intent on testator's life, deprivation of parental rights, non-payment of child support); **trap**: foreign-buyers of KZ residential property face KZ *lex situs*; foreign-currency mortgage and tenge inheritance-tax exposure are layered atop the succession; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: MEDIUM** (Article-1069 referenced in academic sources, full text at adilet.zan.kz Russian-language).

**LK (Sri Lanka)** — pluralist hybrid: General Roman-Dutch law (default for non-Kandyan Sinhalese) gives testamentary freedom + dependant claims; Kandyan Law Ordinance for Sinhalese Buddhist Kandyans (intestate succession only — Kandyans who marry under General Marriage Ordinance shift to Roman-Dutch); Thesavalamai personal law for Tamils of Jaffna under Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance (Jaffna) Ordinance — applies to property; Muslim Marriage and Divorce Act 1951 + Faraid for Muslims; **trap**: applicable regime depends on community at death + which marriage ordinance was used + property location (Thesavalamai is geographically anchored to Jaffna); foreigners face general Roman-Dutch default for succession of LK property they acquired through 99-yr lease (freehold barred); Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: MEDIUM** (regime well-documented in Globalex, but parcel-level Kandyan/Thesavalamai routing needs local counsel).

**MO (Macau)** — Civil Code 1999 (Portuguese-derived, modelled on Portuguese Civil Code 1966 + BGB) — **forced-heirship regime** with **legítima** for spouse + descendants + ascendants; surviving spouse gets ~1/3 of estate when descendants exist, ~1/2 when no descendants alongside ascendants/siblings (the Macau equivalent of PT Art. 2156-2161 — exact MO article numbers not publicly verifiable in English; verify at the Macau Printing Bureau Portuguese text [`bo.io.gov.mo`](https://bo.dsaj.gov.mo/)); intestate order: spouse+descendants → spouse+ascendants → common-law spouse → siblings → 4th-degree collaterals → MO SAR; **trap**: Portuguese-derived legítima is non-waivable inter vivos; Brussels IV n/a (MO is not in EU); **Confidence: MEDIUM** (regime confirmed by Manuela António / Lawgratis but specific article numbers and exact fractions need verification against Portuguese-language source).

**MV (Maldives)** — **Sharia inheritance is constitutionally mandatory under Constitution 2008 Art. 142** (which retained the 1968 Constitution's structural rule that judges apply Sharia where statute is silent); Faraid fixed shares apply to Muslim Maldivians (all citizens, since Maldives is a Muslim-only-citizenship state under Art. 9(d)); bequest cap of 1/3 to non-heir; non-Muslims (foreign residents only) have testamentary freedom for personal property; **trap**: foreigners cannot own freehold (post-2015 amendment briefly allowed it for >USD 1bn investments but was repealed in 2019; current regime allows only 99-year SEZ leasehold); estate-planning for foreign-held MV leasehold is limited to the lease-residue value; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: MEDIUM** (Sharia primacy is constitutional; Faraid mechanics standard).

**MY (Malaysia)** — religion-of-deceased determines regime: **Muslim Malaysian citizens** → Faraid (Sharia, fixed 2:1 male:female shares, bequest cap 1/3 without heir consent) under State Syariah Court; **non-Muslims** → Distribution Act 1958 (revised 1983, amended 1997) for intestate succession + Wills Act 1959 + testamentary freedom subject to dependant claims; no formal forced-heirship for non-Muslims at common law (intestate-only Distribution Act); **trap**: deathbed conversion to Islam by a non-Muslim parent retroactively switches regime to Faraid for the estate (with civil-courts/Syariah-courts jurisdictional fights documented); Penang as a former Straits Settlement follows Distribution Act 1958 like rest of MY for non-Muslims (no separate Penang regime); foreign-buyer threshold typically RM1m+ depending on state — succession of MM2H-held property follows MY regime; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (Distribution Act 1958 published at commonlii.org).

**PH (Philippines)** — Civil Code Art. 886-906 — **legítima = 1/2 of estate to compulsory heirs** (legitimate descendants + surviving spouse + legitimate parents in default of descendants); free portion = 1/2 (testator's disposable share); spouse legítima alongside one legitimate child = same as that child's share (Art. 892); illegitimate children get 1/2 of legitimate child's share post-2018 jurisprudence reform; Art. 906 entitles compulsory heir to demand full legítima if shorted; Art. 907 reduces inofficious testamentary dispositions; **trap**: PH applies *nationality* law for succession order but **legítima for PH-located real property cannot be excluded by foreign-law election** — a US testator with a PH-condo cannot avoid PH legítima via US choice-of-law; foreigners cannot own land (constitutional ban Art. XII §7) except via hereditary succession (Art. XII §7 exception); Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (Civil Code at chanrobles.com, jurisprudence at e-library.judiciary.gov.ph).

**PK (Pakistan)** — **mixed system: English common-law legacy overlaid with Islamic law** ([Supreme Court of Pakistan](https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk/)) — so succession of a Muslim's estate runs on **Islamic personal law (Sharia)** while the common-law machinery handles procedure; final court for civil/property matters = **Supreme Court of Pakistan** (constitutional jurisdiction moved to the new Federal Constitutional Court by the 27th Amendment, signed 13 Nov 2025 — verify Art. 175E at [na.gov.pk](https://na.gov.pk/); **no external / Privy Council appeal**); **the specific Sharia reserved-share (faraid) fractions + the governing personal-law statute were NOT enumerated in the Pakistan playbook this round — `data not independently confirmed — verify the applicable Muslim personal-law / succession rules + any non-Muslim minority succession regime with Pakistani counsel before relying`** (do NOT assume a generic faraid split without per-case advice; non-Muslim Pakistanis are governed by their own community succession rules); **land records are PROVINCIAL** (each province's Board of Revenue; title = the mutation/*intiqal* entry in the Record of Rights + a registered deed under the Registration Act 1908 — **presumptive, NOT a Torrens guaranteed title**), so an estate's land devolution is administered province-by-province with **no national cadastre**; the practical foreign-buyer market is overwhelmingly the **diaspora** (NRPs + POC holders via SBP Roshan Apna Ghar) — non-origin foreigners need Ministry-of-Interior approval to acquire ([Foreigners Act 1946](https://fia.gov.pk/files/act/4.pdf)); **Brussels IV n/a**; **hidden trap** — agricultural/rural land is governed by provincial land-revenue law and is subject to foreign-acquisition restrictions, and cantonment/border/diplomatic-enclave land needs security clearance — what a foreign-linked heir can lawfully take may be narrower than the deed suggests. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (mixed common-law + Islamic-law frame + final court + provincial-record structure primary-anchored; the exact faraid quantum + personal-law statute are the deliberately-hedged unresolved items — NOT fabricated).

**SG (Singapore)** — **No forced heirship**; full testamentary freedom under Wills Act 1838 (rev. 1996); Inheritance (Family Provision) Act 1966 (UK 1938-derived) allows dependant to apply for "reasonable provision for maintenance" — single standard (court has no power to award beyond maintenance); **Muslim Singaporeans** under Administration of Muslim Law Act (AMLA) 1966 → Faraid via Syariah Court for succession; Family Justice (Probate and Other Matters) Rules 2024 (S 723/2024, in force 16 Sep 2024) updated procedure (NOT substantive forced-heirship); **trap**: a foreign-buyer-owner of SG real estate domiciled abroad escapes IFPA 1966 (deceased must be domiciled in SG); Muslim foreign-buyers of SG property face Syariah-court routing if they are SG-domiciled at death; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (statutes at sso.agc.gov.sg).

**TH (Thailand)** — Civil and Commercial Code Book VI (Succession), Section 1620-1755 (in force since 1935, no major succession reform since) — **no forced-heirship**; full testamentary freedom; statutory-heir hierarchy in 6 classes under Section 1629 (descendants → parents → full siblings → half siblings → grandparents → uncles/aunts) applies only intestate; spouse inherits alongside applicable class under Section 1635; **trap**: a Thai testator can validly disinherit children — TH is regionally unique in this; foreigners cannot own land (Land Code §86) but can own condo (max 49% foreign ownership in any building); succession of foreign-held TH condo follows TH lex situs but condo can pass to non-Thai heirs only if heirs themselves qualify as condo-eligible foreigners (otherwise 1-yr forced sale); Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (CCC at samuiforsale.com / juslaws.com, no recent reform).

**TW (Taiwan)** — Civil Code Art. 1223 — **special portion (特留分, tèliúfēn) = 1/2 of intestate share** for spouse + lineal descendants + parents; **1/3 of intestate share** for siblings + grandparents; Art. 1187 governs testamentary disposal subject to special portion; Art. 1225 allows shorted heir to deduct from legacies; **trap**: a foreign testator can elect to apply the law of their nationality for movables under TW Act Governing Choice of Law in Civil Matters Art. 60 but TW *lex situs* governs immovables — so TW-located real estate is fully subject to TW special portion regardless of testator nationality; Brussels IV n/a; statute of limitations: 2 yr from knowledge / 10 yr from death; **Confidence: HIGH** (Civil Code at law.moj.gov.tw English version).

**UZ (Uzbekistan)** — Civil Code Special Part Art. 1145 (post-Soviet code, structurally similar to KZ Art. 1069) — **обязательная доля (obligatory share) = 1/2 of intestate share** for minors, adult-incapacitated descendants, dependent ascendants, dependent spouse; testamentary freedom otherwise; **trap**: customary Muslim-majority succession practices may overlay statutory civil-law rules in informal allocations (not legally binding but commonly observed); foreign-buyers of UZ residential property face UZ lex situs; Russian-language Civil Code at lex.uz is the authoritative text — English secondary sources at warnathgroup.com / cis-legislation.com confirm framework but exact Art. 1145 wording not in English-language public domain; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: LOW** (Article-1145 verification limited; verify at [`lex.uz`](https://lex.uz/) Russian text).

**VU (Vanuatu)** — **mixed legal system: English common law + French civil-law influences + customary land law** ([Court of Appeal of Vanuatu](https://courts.gov.vu/about-us/court-of-appeal)), final court = **Court of Appeal of Vanuatu** (no UK Privy Council appeal — republic since 1980); the dominant structural fact for succession is **tenure, not forced shares — Vanuatu abolished freehold at 1980 independence and reserves perpetual custom ownership to indigenous citizens (Constitution Art. 75); a foreigner can hold ONLY a registered lease (≤75-yr ceiling) under the Land Leases Act [Cap 163]** ([Constitution Ch. 12, gov.vu](https://www.gov.vu/images/legislation/constitution-en.pdf); [Land Leases Act, FAOLEX](https://www.fao.org/faolex/results/details/en/c/LEX-FAOC088905/)) — so on death it is the **lease residue** (years remaining + ground rent) that passes, not freehold land; **NO inheritance / wealth tax** (Vanuatu is a zero-direct-tax jurisdiction — there is no Income Tax Act; the ABSENCE of the tax on the [DCIR taxes index](https://vanuatucustoms.gov.vu/taxes-and-licensing/taxes/value-added-tax-vat/introduction.html) is the evidence, corroborated by [VFIPA "Low tax jurisdiction"](https://investvanuatu.vu/untapped-potential/low-tax-jurisdiction/)); **Brussels IV n/a**; **the substantive forced-heirship / reserved-share position of the mixed (common-law + French-civil) regime was NOT resolved in the Vanuatu playbook — whether a French-derived réserve survives over leasehold interests or whether common-law testamentary freedom governs is `data not publicly confirmed — verify with a Vanuatu conveyancing lawyer + the relevant succession statute at [PacLII vu/legis](http://www.paclii.org/) before relying`**; **hidden trap** — custom owners hold the perpetual interest under the lease, so lessor consent + custom-land dispute exposure sit beneath any succession plan. **Confidence: LOW** (legal-family + tenure + no-inheritance-tax + final-court frame is primary/regulated-confirmed; the exact reserved-share quantum of the mixed regime is the deliberately-hedged unresolved item — NOT fabricated).

**VN (Vietnam)** — Civil Code 2015 Art. 644 (effective 1 Jan 2017) — **forced share = 2/3 of statutory share** for: minor children, adult children incapable of working, parents, spouse — even if explicitly disinherited or under-allocated by will; Art. 651 lists statutory heirs in 3 classes (parents+spouse+children → grandparents+siblings+grandchildren → great-grandparents+great-grandchildren+aunts/uncles); **trap**: Art. 644 protects minors AND disabled-adult descendants — a foreign-buyer with adult disabled children cannot disinherit them via will; foreigners can own a house attached to land use rights for max 50 yr (extendable once to 100 yr) under Housing Law 2023 Art. 17 + ownership cap (30% of any condo / 250 detached houses per ward); inherited LUR/house transfers to foreign heir but if heir cannot legally own, heir is entitled to value (cash) only; Brussels IV n/a; **Confidence: HIGH** (Civil Code 2015 published, well-documented in academic / law-firm sources).

## LATAM + Caribbean

### Regional pattern

Latin America and the Caribbean fragment into four succession families. **Spanish-derived civil-law jurisdictions** (AR, CL, CO, PE, UY, EC, PY, plus PA/CR with weakened forms) inherit the Iberian *legítima* model: a fixed reserved share (between 1/2 and 4/5) automatically vests in descendants, then ascendants, then spouse, regardless of the will. **Portuguese-derived BR** runs a parallel 1/2 *legítima* under the 2002 Código Civil (Lei 10.406), with the spouse promoted to *herdeiro necessário* status — and after STF tema 809 (2017) the *companheiro/a* in *união estável* is treated equivalently to a spouse for succession. **French-derived DO** retained the original 1804 Code Napoléon's *réserve héréditaire* almost verbatim (1/2 with one child, 2/3 with two, 3/4 with three or more) — the only LATAM jurisdiction with the historical FR sliding scale. **Common-law / Roman-Dutch overlay jurisdictions** (BB, JM, BS, BZ) follow the English testamentary-freedom model, qualified by Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Acts that grant courts discretion to vary a will or intestacy outcome where reasonable provision was not made for spouse, minor children, or other dependants.

The buyer-protection consequence is large: in legítima jurisdictions, **a foreign-law-choice clause in the will (Brussels-IV-style) cannot defeat the local reserved share for property situated in-country**, because the *lex rei sitae* applies to immovables and the legítima is treated as ordre public. Brussels IV (Reg. EU 650/2012) has no in-force application here — the EU regulation reaches only EU residents, and even an EU testator's professio iuris is overridden by host-state mandatory rules under recital 38 / art. 30 where the situs state classifies legítima as a special restriction on immovables. Practical implication: a foreign buyer of a Buenos Aires apartment cannot leave it 100% to a non-relative if Argentine descendants exist.

The two notable testamentary-freedom outliers are **MX** (Código Civil Federal art. 1281+ plus 32 state codes — federal model preserves only alimentary obligations, not a forced share, and most states follow) and **PA / CR / BB / JM / BS / BZ**. These five attract foreign asset-protection structuring precisely because the host-state ordre public does not impose a mandatory share. In MX, the *fideicomiso* (bank trust required for foreigners in the 50km coastal / 100km border *zona restringida* under the 1973 Foreign Investment Law and 1993 update) doubles as a succession vehicle: beneficiary designations transfer trust-held property without inventory, provided the trust deed names successor beneficiaries.

The 2015–2025 reform pattern is one of *liberalisation*: AR's 2015 Código Civil y Comercial (Ley 26.994) reduced the descendant legítima from 4/5 to 2/3 and the ascendant share from 2/3 to 1/2 (CCyC art. 2445); BR's 2002 Code recast the spouse as *necessário* (CC art. 1845); CO's Ley 1934 of 2018 freed the *cuarta de mejoras* into testamentary disposition; BR's Lei 14.382/2022 enabled extrajudicial inventário at notary offices (15 days vs. 10 years judicial). PY (4/5 to descendants under CC art. 2598) remains the most restrictive on the continent.

The Caribbean common-law tier (BB, JM, BS, BZ) presents a different risk: not a reserved share, but **bench discretion under the Inheritance Provision Acts**, which allows surviving spouses, minor children, ex-spouses, and financially dependent adult children to apply to court to vary even a fully testamentary disposition. Outcomes are case-law-driven and harder to underwrite at planning time than a fixed legítima fraction.

### Country one-liners

**AG (Antigua and Barbuda)** — English common-law twin-island Commonwealth realm (independent 1981); **testamentary freedom is the default; NO forced-heirship / no reserved share**; moderated only by an English-tradition family-provision / dependants'-relief jurisdiction allowing a spouse, minor child, or dependant to apply to the High Court for *reasonable provision* where the will/intestacy fails them (the specific Antigua statute name + section were not located in a primary source this round — *verify the Antiguan Inheritance/Family-Provision statute at [legalaffairs.gov.ag](https://legalaffairs.gov.ag/) before relying*; the dependant-relief concept is standard across the OECS common-law tier). Probate runs through the **Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court (ECSC) — resident High Court division in Antigua**; final appeal to the **UK Privy Council (JCPC)** (A&B has NOT adopted the CCJ). **Brussels IV n/a** (non-EU; lex situs governs Antiguan immovables). Hidden trap — **Barbuda land is historically held in COMMON (communal, no individual freehold)**, so the succession question on Barbuda is *tenure*, not forced shares; do not assume freehold there. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (common-law freedom + ECSC/JCPC frame HIGH; exact family-provision statute citation unverified).

**AR (Argentina)** — Civil and Commercial Code (Ley 26.994, in force 1 Aug 2015) art. 2444–2445: legítima of descendants 2/3, ascendants 1/2, spouse 1/2 (reduced from prior 4/5 / 2/3 / 1/2 of the 1869 Vélez code); spouse is legitimary, *cohabitante* (convivencia) is not unless designated; Brussels IV n/a; hidden trap — pre-1 Aug 2015 wills are governed by the old 4/5 regime and pre-existing real-estate donations carry the older 10-year reduction-action vulnerability. **Confidence: HIGH** (primary text on argentina.gob.ar).

**BB (Barbados)** — Succession Act Cap. 249 (modelled on Irish Succession Act 1965): no fixed forced-heirship fraction but s. 96–98 *legal right share* — surviving spouse legally entitled to 1/2 of the estate (or 1/3 where there is also a child), and any clause attempting to disinherit a minor child or a spouse not judicially separated for ≥3 years is void; court discretion on lump-sum maintenance for children. Hidden trap — the legal right share operates as a near-equivalent to legítima for spouse, despite Barbados being labelled a "common-law testamentary freedom" jurisdiction. **Confidence: HIGH** (statute on barbadoslawcourts.gov.bb).

**BM (Bermuda)** *(North Atlantic BOT — NOT Caribbean; filed here for pattern affinity)* — English common-law British Overseas Territory; **testamentary freedom; NO forced-heirship / no reserved share**; moderated by the **Succession Act 1974** (Part on provision for family + dependants), under which a **spouse, former spouse who has not remarried, child, or a grandchild who was being wholly/partly maintained by the deceased** may apply to the **Supreme Court of Bermuda** for *reasonable financial provision* — for a spouse the court need not find the provision necessary for *maintenance* (lower threshold); **strict ~6-month limitation** from the first grant of representation (extendable only with leave); **narrower than the UK 1975 Act** — Bermuda does NOT yet extend standing to 2-year cohabitants ([Bermuda Succession Act 1974, bermudalaws.bm](https://www.bermudalaws.bm/Laws/Consolidated%20Law/1974/Succession%20Act%201974); [Appleby — "Where there is a will, there is a claim"](https://www.applebyglobal.com/publications/where-there-is-a-will-there-is-a-claim/)). **NO inheritance/estate tax** (stamp duty applies to dutiable transfers). **Brussels IV n/a**. Hidden trap — cohabiting partners have NO statutory claim; private estate planning (trust/joint tenancy) is the only route. **Confidence: HIGH** (Succession Act 1974 primary-citable; Supreme Court of Bermuda forum verified).

**BR (Brazil)** — Código Civil (Lei 10.406/2002) art. 1845–1846: 50% *legítima* reserved for *herdeiros necessários* (descendants, ascendants, spouse — and post-STF tema 809 of 2017, *companheiro/a* in união estável on equivalent terms); 50% disposable. CC art. 549 voids any inter-vivos donation that exceeds the disposable portion at the moment of gifting (*doação inoficiosa* — STJ 2023 confirmed timing test); **reduction action follows STJ's 10-year ordinary prescription (CC art. 205)**, NOT 4 years. Lei 14.382/2022 enabled extrajudicial cartório-based inventário (≈15 days). Hidden trap — *companheiro* status as legitimary depends on proof of união estável, not on registration; disputes after death are routine. **Confidence: HIGH** (planalto.gov.br + STF/STJ jurisprudence).

**BS (Bahamas)** — Wills Act 2002 + Inheritance Act 2002 (Ch. 116): testamentary freedom is the default rule; Part III of the Inheritance Act gives surviving spouse, minor children (<18), and 18–23-year-old children in financial dependence at death a right to apply to court for *reasonable financial provision* where the will or intestacy fails them. Hidden trap — no fixed share, outcomes are judicial-discretion driven; offshore-trust structures common. **Confidence: HIGH** (laws.bahamas.gov.bs).

**BZ (Belize)** — Administration of Estates Act Cap. 197 + Wills Act: common-law testamentary freedom inherited from the English model; no formal reserved share; dependant claims available via inheritance-provision rules in the English tradition. Hidden trap — Belize is a common-law island in a civil-law neighbourhood; foreign owners often misapply nearby civil-law assumptions. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (statute confirmed; bench-discretion case law thinner than JM/BB).

**CL (Chile)** — Código Civil art. 1167 (asignaciones forzosas) and art. 1184: half of the estate is reserved as *legítima rigorosa* split among descendants, ascendants, and surviving spouse; a *cuarta de mejoras* (1/4) can be allocated by the testator within descendants/ascendants/spouse; the remaining *cuarta de libre disposición* (1/4) is unrestricted. Hidden trap — the *cuarta de mejoras* is often misread as fully discretionary; it can only go to descendants/ascendants/spouse, not to outsiders. **Confidence: HIGH** (BCN leychile.cl).

**CO (Colombia)** — Código Civil art. 1242 (as amended by Ley 1934 of 2018, in force 1 Jan 2019): half of the estate forms the *legítima rigurosa* divided among descendants per intestacy rules; **Ley 1934 abolished the obligation to allocate the *cuarta de mejoras* to a legitimary** (art. 1243 was derogated), so the testator now freely disposes of 50% to anyone. Hidden trap — wills made before 1 Jan 2019 may still be drafted on the pre-1934 mejoras assumption; the legítima half itself was not reduced. **Confidence: HIGH** (secretariasenado.gov.co + funcionpublica.gov.co).

**CR (Costa Rica)** — Código Civil art. 595: full testamentary freedom subject to an *alimentary credit* in favour of children, parents, and spouse who lack means of subsistence at the testator's death — claimant must prove (i) need, (ii) no alternative provision in the estate, (iii) not already named heir; the obligation lapses with the obligated person's death. Hidden trap — "freedom to disinherit" is widely advertised but the alimentary credit operates as a contingent claim that can swallow material estate value where minor children or a dependent spouse exist. **Confidence: HIGH** (poder-judicial.go.cr).

**DO (Dominican Republic)** — Código Civil (1845, French-derived) art. 913–915: *réserve héréditaire* on the historical FR sliding scale — disposable quotient is 1/2 with one child, 1/3 with two, 1/4 with three or more; 1/2 if no descendants but ascendants in both lines, 3/4 if ascendants in one line only. This is the only LATAM regime that mirrors pre-2006 France verbatim. Hidden trap — the DO scale is more restrictive than the modern Spanish-derived 1/2 model; foreign buyers from Spain or Argentina routinely under-estimate the children's share. **Confidence: HIGH** (codes published; tribunalconstitucional.gob.do TC/0221/14 reaffirmed).

**EC (Ecuador)** — Código Civil art. 1207 (codification 2005-010, presidencia.gob.ec consolidated text 2024): half of the divisible estate goes to *legítimas rigorosas* (legitimaries are children and parents per art. 1205, children excluding parents); 1/4 is the *cuarta de mejoras* for descendants chosen by the testator; 1/4 is freely disposable. Spouse share via separate *gananciales* / portion rules, not as primary legitimary. Hidden trap — USD-dollarised economy + Catholic-tradition Civil Code preserves a Chilean-style structure but with a narrower legitimary class than CL (children + parents only at art. 1205, not spouse as primary). **Confidence: HIGH** (presidencia.gob.ec PDF 2024).

**GD (Grenada)** — English common-law island state (Grenada + Carriacou + Petite Martinique); **testamentary freedom; NO forced-heirship / no reserved share**; moderated by an English-tradition dependants'-relief jurisdiction (spouse/minor-child/dependant application for reasonable provision — *specific Grenadian Inheritance/Family-Provision statute name + section not located this round; verify at [laws.gov.gd](https://www.laws.gov.gd/index.php/chapters/d-76-84a/133-chapter-79-deeds-and-land-registry-act)*). Title runs on a **deeds-registration (NOT Torrens)** system under the **Deeds and Land Registry Act, Cap. 79**; probate + deeds recorded at the **Deeds & Land Registry of the ECSC**; superior court = resident **High Court** division of the **ECSC**; final appeal to the **UK Privy Council (JCPC)** (Grenada rejected the CCJ at a 2018 referendum). **NO general inheritance/estate tax** (deed-of-gift PTT applies above XCD 150,000). **Brussels IV n/a**. Hidden trap — **"family land" (old undivided inherited holdings) is a recurring Caribbean title complication**: a deceased may hold only a fractional undivided interest, so a will purporting to devise "the parcel" can fail against co-heirs irrespective of testamentary freedom; the 2019 conveyancing reform cut the good-title search period 60 → 30 years. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (common-law freedom + deeds-system + ECSC/JCPC frame HIGH; exact family-provision statute citation unverified).

**JM (Jamaica)** — Wills Act + Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act (effective 20 May 1993): testamentary freedom is the default; the 1993 Act lets a spouse, ex-spouse not remarried, parent, or child (including adult children dependent at death) apply to court for *reasonable financial provision* where the will or intestacy outcome fails them. Hidden trap — bench discretion is wide and case-law-driven; planning-time certainty lower than legítima jurisdictions. **Confidence: HIGH** (laws.moj.gov.jm).

**KN (St. Kitts and Nevis)** — English common-law twin-island federation (Commonwealth realm, independent 1983); **testamentary freedom; NO forced-heirship / no reserved share**; moderated by an English-tradition dependants'-relief jurisdiction (spouse/minor-child/dependant application for reasonable provision — *specific Federation Inheritance/Family-Provision statute name + section not located this round; verify at [legal.gov.kn](https://www.legal.gov.kn/)*). Superior court = **ECSC** resident **High Court of Justice** division; final appeal to the **UK Privy Council (JCPC)** (St K&N has NOT adopted the CCJ). **NO income / capital-gains / inheritance/estate tax** (a core CBI pitch). **Brussels IV n/a** (lex situs governs Federation immovables). Hidden trap — **Nevis has its own island land administration + the Nevis International Exempt Trust Ordinance (NIETO)** with strong asset-protection/firewall features used precisely to repel foreign forced-heirship and creditor claims against trust-held property; St-Kitts-side vs Nevis-side rules can diverge. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (common-law freedom + ECSC/JCPC frame HIGH; exact family-provision statute citation unverified).

**KY (Cayman Islands)** — English common-law British Overseas Territory; **testamentary freedom is the foundational rule; NO forced-heirship** (Cayman expressly has no forced-heirship laws); a limited family-provision safety-net exists under the **Succession Act (2021 Revision)** allowing a *dependant* (spouse, child, financially dependent cohabitee/relative) to apply to court for *reasonable financial provision* where the deceased failed to make it — narrower than England's 1975 Act ([Carey Olsen — Wills and estates in the Cayman Islands](https://www.careyolsen.com/insights/briefings/wills-and-estates-cayman-islands); [Higgs & Johnson — Testamentary Freedom and Wills in the Cayman Islands](https://higgsjohnson.com/testamentary-freedom-and-wills-in-the-cayman-islands/)). Probate is the **Grand Court of the Cayman Islands** (NOT the ECSC — Cayman is outside the Eastern Caribbean system); appeals → Cayman Islands Court of Appeal → **Privy Council**. **NO inheritance/estate tax** (no direct taxation at all). **Brussels IV n/a**. Hidden trap — Cayman trust law (incl. STAR trusts + firewall provisions in the Trusts Act) is engineered to defeat foreign forced-heirship claims against Cayman-situs trust property; settling a trust *before* domicile/asset accumulation is the standard structuring move. **Confidence: HIGH** (no-forced-heirship + Succession Act (2021 Revision) dependant-provision + Grand Court forum all verified).

**LC (Saint Lucia)** — the **civil-law outlier** of the Caribbean common-law tier: Saint Lucia's **Civil Code (Cap. 242, Rev. Ordinances 1957)** is derived from the **Quebec Civil Code of Lower Canada (1866)**, with overlaid English common law + statute; conveyancing + notarial form retain civil-law characteristics (deed of sale executed before an attorney-at-law acting as notary). ⚠️ **BUT the expected French *réserve héréditaire* / forced-share is NOT confirmed**: the Lower-Canada parent model carried **unlimited testamentary freedom** (Quebec Act 1774), and a direct read of the Civil Code of Saint Lucia surfaced **no legitime / compulsory-heirs / reserved-portion section** — the Code governs intestate distribution (Arts 567A–579: e.g. surviving spouse 1/3 + issue 2/3 on intestacy) and the *form* of wills, but **does not appear to impose a forced share on a testator with children**. **Any specific reserved fraction is `data not publicly confirmed — verify the legitime/reserved-portion position with a Saint Lucia attorney + the Civil Code Cap. 242 before relying`** ([Civil Code of Saint Lucia — AG Chambers](http://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/civil-code-of-saint-lucia); the lc playbook itself notes "the Civil Code's forced-heirship history may be relevant; verify"). Final appellate court = the **Caribbean Court of Justice (CCJ) since 20 July 2023** (Privy Council appeals abolished that date — do NOT cite the JCPC for LC); first-instance/intermediate via the **ECSC** resident High Court division. **NO inheritance/estate tax**; **Brussels IV n/a**. Hidden trap — civil-law *form* (attorney-as-notary, Civil-Code conveyancing) co-exists with **common-law-style testamentary freedom in substance**; foreign buyers from France/Quebec who assume a French-style réserve, AND those who assume pure English freedom, can both be wrong on edge cases — get LC-counsel-of-record on the will. **Confidence: MEDIUM-LOW** (civil-law Civil-Code + CCJ-since-2023 frame HIGH; the *presence/absence + quantum of any legitime* is the unresolved item — deliberately hedged, NOT fabricated).

**MX (Mexico)** — Código Civil Federal art. 1281+ (federal scheme governs Federal District / CDMX historically and acts as template for state codes); 32 state Civil Codes set their own rules and most preserve testamentary freedom limited only to alimentary obligations. Restricted-zone property (≤50km coast, ≤100km border) for foreigners is held via *fideicomiso* with a Mexican bank as trustee under the 1973 Foreign Investment Law (BBVA / HSBC / Banamex / Santander typically); fideicomiso permits successor-beneficiary designation, transferring property at death without inventario. Hidden trap — the testator's domicile-state matters: a Yucatán or CDMX domicile gives near-total freedom; some states have stricter alimentary regimes. The 50-year fideicomiso term renews indefinitely. **Confidence: HIGH** for federal; **MEDIUM** for state-by-state without per-state pull.

**PA (Panama)** — Código Civil (Chapter XI on testamentary freedom) governs (per Justia and Órgano Judicial primary text): testamentary freedom subject only to alimentary obligations to legally entitled persons; no formal legítima. Combined with the Friendly Nations / Qualified Investor visa pathways and USD-dollarisation, PA is one of the continent's most popular asset-protection jurisdictions. Hidden trap — Executive Decree 193 of October 2024 confirmed the USD 300,000 minimum for the Qualified Investor real-estate route and a 5-year holding period; sale below threshold during that window can void residency status. **Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** (Justia + paralelaw.com tax-residency confirms 2024 changes; no inheritance-law amendment in 2024).

**PE (Peru)** — Código Civil (Decreto Legislativo 295 of 24 Jul 1984) art. 723: *legítima* is the share of the estate that the testator cannot freely dispose of when there are forced heirs. Art. 724 names *herederos forzosos*: children and other descendants, parents and other ascendants, and the spouse or surviving partner of a *unión de hecho*. Standard Peruvian doctrine: legítima = 2/3 where descendants exist; 1/2 where only ascendants and/or spouse. Hidden trap — *unión de hecho* (cohabitation) was elevated to forced-heir status under post-1984 reforms, but only where formally registered or judicially declared. **Confidence: HIGH** for code text; **MEDIUM-HIGH** for the 2/3 vs. 1/2 fractions (verify at https://diariooficial.elperuano.pe).

**PY (Paraguay)** — Código Civil (Ley 1.183/85) art. 2598: legítima of descendants = **4/5** of the estate (the most restrictive in the continent), ascendants = 2/3, spouse = 1/2 where neither descendants nor ascendants exist. Disposable portion to descendants is therefore only 1/5. Hidden trap — proposed reform Bill (silpy.congreso.gov.py expediente 2994) would relax the 4/5 share but as of May 2026 not enacted; foreign buyers must plan around the harshest legítima on the continent. **Confidence: HIGH** (bacn.gov.py).

**TC (Turks and Caicos Islands)** — English common-law British Overseas Territory; **testamentary freedom; NO forced-heirship / no reserved share**; moderated by the **Inheritance (Family Provision) Ordinance, CAP 11.08** (originally 1958, amended 1978) under which a *dependant* (spouse, infant son, unmarried daughter, or disabled child — categories are dated/gendered) may apply for continuing provision from the estate; intestacy is governed separately by the **Intestacy Ordinance, CAP 11.10** (England-and-Wales-style distribution); narrower than England's 1975 Act ([Lexology — Snapshot: succession law in Turks & Caicos Islands](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=bc75524d-93f5-403d-977b-202933550e8f); [TCI AG Chambers — Inheritance (Family Provisions) Ordinance 11.08](https://gov.tc/agc/2021-revised-laws)). Probate is the **Supreme Court of the Turks and Caicos Islands** (TCI's own superior court — **NOT** part of the ECSC). **NO inheritance/estate tax** (no recurring/inheritance tax at all). **Brussels IV n/a**. Hidden trap — the CAP 11.08 dependant categories are **archaic + facially discriminatory** (treat sons vs daughters, married vs unmarried daughters differently) and have not been modernised to the 1975-Act standard; rely on a clear will + structuring, not the Ordinance. **Confidence: HIGH** (CAP 11.08 + CAP 11.10 + Supreme Court of TCI forum verified).

**TT (Trinidad and Tobago)** — **English-derived common law** (twin-island republic, independent 1962); **testamentary freedom is the default; NO fixed forced-heirship / reserved share** in the continental-legítima sense — the regime sits in the Caribbean common-law tier alongside BB/JM/BS/BZ, **moderated by an English-tradition family-provision / dependants'-relief jurisdiction (spouse / minor child / dependant application for reasonable provision where the will or intestacy fails them — *the specific Trinidad & Tobago Wills / family-provision statute name + section were NOT located in a primary source this round; verify at [laws.gov.tt](https://laws.gov.tt/) before relying*; the dependant-relief concept is standard across the anglophone-Caribbean common-law tier)**; **final / apex court = the UK Judicial Committee of the Privy Council (JCPC), London — NOT the CCJ** (T&T retains the JCPC for final appeals and has acceded only to the CCJ's Original Jurisdiction) ([Globalex/NYU](https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/Trinidad_Tobago1.html)); **NO separate notary profession** — conveyancing is handled by an **attorney-at-law** (deeds / Certificates of Title); **NO inheritance / estate / succession duty** (T&T abolished estate/succession duties decades ago; no current inheritance tax documented — [VERIFY current position at IRD/BIR](https://ird.gov.tt/)); **Brussels IV n/a** (lex situs governs T&T immovables); **hidden trap** — T&T runs a **DUAL land-registration system**: ~75–80% of land is **Old-Law deeds** proven by a Chain of Title with **no state guarantee** (vs the minority **RPA/Torrens** Certificate of Title), so probate of an estate's land turns on an attorney's title search, not a single authoritative register — a will devising "the parcel" can fail where the deceased held only a defective or fractional chain. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (common-law freedom + JCPC forum + no-estate-tax + dual-title frame primary-confirmed; the exact family-provision statute citation is the deliberately-hedged unresolved item — NOT fabricated).

**UY (Uruguay)** — Código Civil (Ley 16.603/1994 codification) art. 884–887: legítima sliding by number of children — 1 child → 1/2 of estate; 2 children → 2/3; 3+ children → 3/4 (impo.com.uy text); ascendants take 1/2 absent descendants; spousal portion (*porción conyugal*) under art. 881 is 1/4 of the estate in all orders, except where descendants exist the surviving spouse is counted among children for the art. 887 division and receives the rigorous legítima of one child. Hidden trap — the UY sliding scale is closer to the historical FR/DO model than to the flat-1/2 Brazilian model; foreign buyers from BR routinely under-budget children's share. **Confidence: HIGH** (impo.com.uy primary text per article).

**VG (British Virgin Islands)** — English common-law British Overseas Territory; **the strongest testamentary-freedom outlier of the 8: full freedom of disposition, NO forced-heirship, AND NO equivalent of England's Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975** — i.e. no statutory family-provision claw-back exists in the BVI at all ([Ogier — Making a will in the BVI](https://www.ogier.com/news-and-insights/insights/making-a-will-in-the-british-virgin-islands/); [Harneys — Succession Planning BVI & Cayman](https://www.harneys.com/insights/succession-planning-british-virgin-islands-and-cayman-islands/)). For a BVI-*domiciled* deceased there are **no restrictions** on who inherits; for a non-BVI-domiciled deceased, **foreign forced-heirship of the domicile may bite on personalty** — BUT the **BVI Trustee Act firewall provisions** expressly disapply any foreign succession/forced-heirship rule against personal property settled into a BVI trust (the standard structuring shield, esp. for BVI-company-held real estate). Probate is the **High Court of Justice (Virgin Islands), a division of the ECSC**; final appeal to the **Privy Council**. **NO inheritance/estate/gift tax**. **Brussels IV n/a**. Hidden trap — BVI real estate is frequently held *through a BVI company*; on death it is the **shares** (movable, governed by domicile law subject to the trust firewall), not the land directly, that pass — get the holding-structure mapped before assuming the land follows the will. **Confidence: HIGH** (no-forced-heirship + no-1975-Act-equivalent + Trustee-Act firewall + ECSC/Privy-Council forum verified).

## Africa + Anglo-non-EU

### Regional pattern

The 14 countries split into three doctrinal families with sharply different blocking power against a foreign buyer's will. **Anglo-non-EU (AU/CA/NZ/US-49-states)** all run on inherited English testamentary freedom plus a court-discretion safety net — a will allocates as the testator wishes, but a spouse, dependent child, or eligible cohabitant can sue the estate for "adequate provision" (AU state Family Provision Acts), "moral duty" (NZ Family Protection Act 1955), "dependant's relief" (Canada provincial; BC WESA s. 60 also lets adult independent children claim), or an "elective share" (≈1/3, US 49 states). Foreign buyers should treat these as **deferred forced-heirship-by-litigation** — the will is honoured if undisputed, re-written by court if not.

The **two civil-law carve-outs inside the Anglo bloc are load-bearing**: **Quebec** (CCQ 1991, in force 1994) **abolished classical French légitime in its 1994 reform** — Quebec is now closer to common-law dependants relief than to French réserve héréditaire; the modern QC tool is the Art. 684 *survie de l'obligation alimentaire* (food-support obligation, max 12 months of support, capped at 1/2 of intestate share) — NOT classical légitime. **Louisiana** (LSA-CC Art. 1493) is the only US state retaining classical forced-heirship: descendants ≤23 years old at decedent's death, OR descendants of any age permanently incapable due to mental incapacity / physical infirmity / inherited incurable disease — forced portion = 1/4 with one forced heir, 1/2 with two-or-more. This survived the 1995-1996 reform attempt (Const. amendment narrowed but did not abolish it).

**Africa** splits four ways. **South Africa** is Roman-Dutch — testamentary freedom is constitutional bedrock, but the **Maintenance of Surviving Spouses Act 27 of 1990** plus common-law aquilian support for minor children plus customary-law overlay (Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 1998 + post-*Bhe v Magistrate Khayelitsha* jurisprudence) carve real exceptions. **Mauritius** and **Seychelles** are French-Roman-Dutch hybrids: MU still runs Code Civil 1808 réserve héréditaire (Arts 913-930) — 1/2 reserved for one child, 2/3 for two, 3/4 for three-or-more — with the Trusts Act 2001 s. 14 as the well-known escape valve (Mauritian-law trust deed displaces foreign forced-heirship); SC **abolished forced-heirship** in its 2020 Civil Code (Act 1 of 2021, in force 1 Jul 2021) — SC is now closer to AU/NZ than to MU. **Cabo Verde** is Portuguese-civil-law — Código Civil 1997 (Decree-Law 31-A/99) preserves *legítima*: 1/2 reserved with one descendant (and Art. 242 lets reserved heirs unwind donations made to harm them).

**Common-law Africa (KE/GH/NG)** layers statutory testamentary freedom over a customary-law substrate that often **silently overrides the will for ancestral land**. Kenya (Law of Succession Act Cap. 160, s. 26) and Ghana (Wills Act 1971 + Intestate Succession Act 1985 / PNDCL 111) and Nigeria (state Wills Acts + Marriage Act, plus Sharia in northern Muslim-majority states) all have family-provision statutes — but the **customary tribal-land overlay can disinherit a foreign-married widow even with a valid will** (Akan matrilineal succession in GH passes property to sister's son not own children; NG customary-law widow exclusion is documented despite *Ukeje v Ukeje* 2014 SCN reforms). **Rwanda** under Law N° 27/2016 of 8 May 2016 explicitly equalised inheritance for widows + daughters (formalising the 1999 reform plus the 1956 Rwubusisi-case principle).

**No Brussels IV / EU Regulation 650/2012 reach** for any of the 12 — each applies its own private international law (PIL): MU/SC/CV typically use **lex situs for immovables + lex domicilii for movables**; AU/CA/NZ/US typically use **lex situs for immovable + lex domicilii for movable** with renvoi quirks; ZA likewise. Foreign buyers in MU/CV/Quebec/Louisiana cannot rely on choice-of-law-by-will the way EU Reg 650/2012 permits — **the locally-located house is governed by local succession law, full stop**.

## 7 universal mitigations

1. **Make a will FIRST, then buy the property.** The "I'll sort the inheritance later" plan defaults to local intestate-succession rules — which apply local forced-shares. Drafting a Brussels-IV-compliant will (or local equivalent) BEFORE acquiring foreign property is cheaper + clearer than retroactive estate planning. Cost: EUR 200-2,000 at a notary in EU; USD 500-5,000 with a cross-border tax/estate lawyer outside EU.
2. **Cite Brussels IV professio juris explicitly in any new will if EU-22 property is involved.** Use a clause like "I elect the law of <my nationality> to govern the entirety of my succession under Article 22 of EU Regulation 650/2012." Verify: (a) the country where you're tax-resident OR where the property sits applies Brussels IV (DK + IE opt-out, UK Brexit-out); (b) FR-located property triggers prélèvement compensatoire even with valid choice (deaths after 1 Nov 2021); (c) ES regional forals can complicate even within Brussels IV.
3. **For Sharia-jurisdiction property: register with DIFC Wills Service Centre (AE) or local equivalent.** AE post-Federal Decree-Law 41/2022 (1 Feb 2023): non-Muslim foreigners can opt out of Sharia and apply nationality law via DIFC or Abu Dhabi Civil Court will registration. QA: Article 12 Family Law same. SA / KW / OM: NO opt-out — Sharia faraid applies; foreigners largely barred from real estate ownership outside specific zones, so the issue rarely arises. TR: civil-law nationality choice via Code 5718 Art. 20.
4. **Treat regional forals like jurisdictional minefields.** ES: Cataluña / Aragón / País Vasco / Navarra / Galicia / Baleares each have own legítima rules — residence at death controls. MX: 32 state Civil Codes; testator's domicile-state controls. US: Louisiana retains forced-heirship for under-24/disabled descendants; 11 community-property states (CA/TX/AZ/NM/NV/WA/ID/LA/WI/AK opt-in/PR) split marital property 50/50. CA: QC has légitime even for foreign QC-property owners. Verify the SUBNATIONAL forum, not just the country.
5. **Lifetime gifts do not necessarily defeat forced-heirship.** Most civil-law jurisdictions claw back gifts: DE 10 years (BGB § 2325 with sliding scale); FR unlimited for direct descendants; BR 4 years (Art. 549); BG 5 years; IT 10-year limitation. Trusts + foundation structures need country-specific advice — Brussels IV nationality-law choice is the simplest route for EU-22 property.
6. **Notarised authentic wills (testament authentique / öffentliches Testament / atto pubblico) survive cross-border challenge better than holographic wills.** Notary-attested wills have higher evidentiary weight + presumption of validity than handwritten holographic wills (testament olographe / eigenhändiges Testament / olografo). For cross-border situations involving Brussels IV nationality choice, notarised will registered with national wills registry (FR Fichier Central des Dispositions de Dernières Volontés / DE Zentrales Testamentsregister / IT Registro Generale dei Testamenti / ES Registro de Últimas Voluntades) is the gold standard. Cost: EUR 200-500 at notary + EUR 5-50 registration.
