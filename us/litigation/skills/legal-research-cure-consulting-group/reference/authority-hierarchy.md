# Authority hierarchy — what binds whom

Load when: deciding whether an authority is binding or persuasive, or which of two conflicting
rulings a court must follow. Verified 2026-09-28 where marked; the rest is settled structure.

## Order of authority (any jurisdiction)

1. Constitution (U.S., then state; a state constitution can grant more protection than the
   federal floor. NY often does, e.g. right to counsel and search-and-seizure).
2. Statutes (and treaties). A later statute or a more specific one controls on conflict.
3. Regulations (valid if within the statutory grant; after *Loper Bright* (U.S. 2024) federal
   courts no longer defer to an agency's reading of an ambiguous statute. NY courts apply their
   own deference doctrine to state agencies).
4. Binding case law, from a higher court in the same system.
5. Persuasive authority: other courts, Restatements, treatises, law reviews, uniform-act comments.

A holding binds; dicta don't. A memorandum decision or summary affirmance binds only on what
it necessarily decided.

## New York state courts

| Court | Role | Binds |
|---|---|---|
| **Court of Appeals** (7 judges) | Highest court; mostly questions of law | Every NY court |
| **Appellate Division** (1st–4th Departments) | Intermediate appellate; reviews law **and facts** | See the stare decisis rule below |
| **Appellate Term** (1st and 2nd Depts only) | Appeals from NYC Civil/Criminal Courts, District and some City/Justice Courts | Courts below it in its district |
| **Supreme Court** | **Trial** court of general jurisdiction (not the highest court) | Nobody |
| County, Surrogate's, Family, Court of Claims, NYC Civil/Criminal, District, City, Town & Village | Limited-jurisdiction trial courts | Nobody |

Departments: 1st (Manhattan, Bronx), 2nd (Brooklyn, Queens, Staten Island, Long Island, the
lower Hudson Valley counties), 3rd (Albany/Capital region, central-eastern NY), 4th (western and
central-western NY, including Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse). Confirm a county's department
before relying on it.

**Appellate Division stare decisis** — *Mountain View Coach Lines v. Storms*, 102 A.D.2d 663
(2d Dep't 1984) (verified 2026-09-28): the Appellate Division is one statewide court. A trial
court must follow its own department; where its department hasn't ruled, it must follow
another department's ruling until its own department or the Court of Appeals says otherwise. One
department isn't bound by another, so **department splits exist**. Name the department whenever
you cite the Appellate Division.

Unreported decisions (NY Slip Op "(U)") have less precedential weight; cite them as such.

## Federal courts sitting in New York

- Districts: S.D.N.Y., E.D.N.Y., N.D.N.Y., W.D.N.Y. Circuit: **Second Circuit** (NY, CT, VT).
- On **federal** questions: Supreme Court → Second Circuit → district courts. District court
  opinions bind no one, including the same judge in a later case.
- On **NY law** (diversity, supplemental jurisdiction): federal courts apply NY law as the Court
  of Appeals has declared it; absent a ruling, they predict how it would rule, giving weight to
  Appellate Division decisions. The Second Circuit may **certify** an unsettled, determinative
  question to the Court of Appeals (22 NYCRR 500.27).
- A federal court's reading of NY law does **not** bind NY state courts.

## Persuasive sources, ranked

1. Court of Appeals dicta; Appellate Division rulings from another department (for an appellate court).
2. Other states' high courts on a uniform act (UCC, UPC-derived provisions), with the official comments.
3. Restatements (courts adopt sections piecemeal; say whether NY has adopted the section).
4. Treatises: Siegel & Connors, *New York Practice*; McKinney's Practice Commentaries; Prince,
   *Richardson on Evidence*; Warren's Heaton on Surrogate's Court Practice. Useful finding aids;
   cite the primary authority they lead to.
5. Bar outlines and model output: **never** authority. Finding aids only.

## Currency checks before relying on anything

- Statute: current text on nysenate.gov plus the effective date of the amendment that applies to
  your facts (claims often vest under the version in force when they accrued).
- Case: subsequent history (affirmed, modified, reversed), later negative treatment, and
  legislative override. Without a citator, search later decisions citing the case and say so.
- Regulation: current NYCRR / eCFR version and any pending rulemaking.
