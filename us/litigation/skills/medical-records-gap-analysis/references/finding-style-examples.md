# Finding Style Examples

Use these examples when drafting or revising the attorney-facing report. Preserve the source-supported facts and citations; do not copy details from an example into another matter.

## Planned physical therapy

**Prefer:**

> Physical therapy was recommended twice weekly for six weeks, or 12 planned visits. The production contains the evaluation and three visit notes through [date], but no complete attendance history, later progress report, or discharge record. The available records do not show whether the remaining visits occurred or whether treatment ended early. [citations]

**Avoid:** “The physical-therapy episode remains unreconciled.”

State the planned visit count, produced visit count, produced date range, and missing completion evidence. Do not imply that every planned visit is missing when some are documented.

## Interval with evidence of unproduced care

**Prefer:**

> The last produced physical-therapy note is dated [date]. The next produced encounter is a sports-medicine telemedicine visit on [date], [n] days later. A later neurology history says the patient was receiving aquatic therapy during this period, but those therapy records were not produced. The report therefore cannot determine whether this was a break in treatment or a missing-record period. [citations]

**Avoid:** A table row labeled “significant apparent-treatment gap” with “bracketing documentation.”

## Referral with no completed visit record

**Prefer:**

> Dr. [name] referred the patient to neurology on [date]. No neurology consultation appears in the reviewed records. The available materials do not show whether the appointment occurred or whether the consultation record is missing. [citations]

## Completed consultation with missing follow-up

**Prefer:**

> The neurologist evaluated the patient on [date] and recommended follow-up in two to three months. No later neurology visit was found. This is missing follow-up after a completed consultation, not an uncompleted neurology referral. [citations]

## Documented no-show

**Prefer:**

> The patient did not attend the sports-medicine appointment scheduled for [date]. The next documented encounter was [provider and visit type] on [date]. [citations]

Do not automatically add that care may have occurred elsewhere. Add that qualification only when another source identifies outside or ongoing care.

## Billing that identifies a missing clinical record

**Prefer:**

> A bill lists treatment by [provider] on [date], but no corresponding clinical note was produced. This may be a missing-record issue rather than a break in treatment. [citations]

**Omit or move to source accounting:** Generic statements that billing is incomplete when no billed service date, unknown provider, or clinical mismatch changes the gap analysis.

## Specialty-specific terminology

**Prefer:**

> The chiropractic records use spinal subluxation or dislocation diagnosis codes. The hospital CT reported no acute fracture or malalignment. These terms may reflect different specialty or coding conventions. The gap-analysis issue is whether the chiropractic imaging or examination supporting the codes was referenced but not produced. [citations]

**Avoid:** Calling the coding inconsistent with hospital imaging without first establishing that the terms are clinically equivalent.

## Missing pre-accident records

**Prefer:**

> Records from [provider] for February 2016 through February 2021 were not produced. An older workers' compensation record documents prior neck and low-back injuries, so the intervening records may be relevant to the claimed conditions. [citations]

**Avoid:** “The five-year baseline window is incomplete for the claimed neurologic and musculoskeletal systems.”

## Patient-identity issue

**Prefer:**

> One page contains a different patient name or birth date. It should not be attributed to this patient without verification. Redaction, OCR, or document conversion may explain the discrepancy. [citation]

**Avoid:** Inferring that a provider copied another patient's chart unless a reliable source establishes that fact.

## Plain-language limitation

**Prefer:**

> Because the physical-therapy and neurology records are incomplete, the report cannot determine whether treatment stopped during this period.

**Avoid:** Methodological jargon or a multi-clause disclaimer that requires the reader to infer the practical limitation.
