# Customer Notification — January 31 Deadline

Form 8300 has a second-stage obligation that catches many filers by surprise: a written (or, with consent, electronic) statement to each person named on the form, due by **January 31 of the year following the calendar year in which the cash was received**. Failure carries an independent penalty under IRC §6722.

## Statutory and regulatory basis

- **IRC §6050I(e)** — "Every person required to make a return under subsection (a) shall furnish to each person whose name is required to be set forth in such return a written statement showing— (1) the name, address, and phone number of the information contact of the person required to make such return, and (2) the aggregate amount of cash described in subsection (a) received by the person required to make such return." The statement is due "on or before January 31 of the year following the calendar year for which the return under subsection (a) was required to be made."
- **26 CFR §1.6050I-1(f)** — a single, annual, written statement to each identified person; no required format; must show the filer's name and address, the aggregate reportable cash received during the calendar year in all cash transactions relating to that person, and a legend that the information is being reported to the IRS; due January 31 of the year following the calendar year in which the cash is received; mailing to the person's last known address counts as furnishing.
- **IRC §6722** — penalty for failure to furnish correct payee statements; mirrors the §6721 information-return penalty structure.
- **IRS Publication 1544** (Rev. September 2014), "Required statement to buyer," and the Form 8300 instructions, "Statement to be provided" — list the required content. Neither provides model wording.

## Who gets notified

Every person named on a required Form 8300 must receive a statement: each individual in Part I (including the extra Part I entries on page 2) AND each person in Part II on whose behalf the transaction was conducted (Form 8300 instructions, "Statement to be provided"; 26 CFR §1.6050I-1(f)(1), "each person whose name is set forth in a return").

Do NOT send a statement for a Form 8300 filed voluntarily to report a suspicious transaction, and never indicate in any statement that the filer flagged a transaction as suspicious.

## When the notification is due

**By January 31 of the year following the calendar year in which the cash was received.**

- Cash received March 2026, 8300 filed March 2026 → statement due by January 31, 2027
- Cash received December 28, 2026, 8300 filed January 12, 2027 → statement still due by January 31, 2027 (the year after the cash was received, not the year after filing)

If January 31 falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, IRC §7503 makes the next business day timely. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so Monday, February 1, 2027 is timely; send earlier to be safe.

## What the notification must contain

Per IRC §6050I(e), 26 CFR §1.6050I-1(f)(2), the Form 8300 instructions, and Pub 1544, the statement must contain:

1. **The name and address of the business** that filed Form 8300
2. **The name and telephone number of the business's contact person** (the "information contact")
3. **The aggregate amount of reportable cash received** during the calendar year from or for the person receiving the statement
4. **A statement that the information was furnished to (is being reported to) the IRS**

Some practitioners add the date(s) the 8300(s) were filed — not required, but it helps the customer reconcile their own records.

## Sample statement language (not IRS-issued)

The IRS does not publish model wording; the statement "need not follow any particular format" (26 CFR §1.6050I-1(f)(2)). This sample covers the four required elements:

> _On [date], we filed IRS Form 8300, Report of Cash Payments Over $10,000 Received in a Trade or Business. The form was filed because we received from you more than $10,000 in cash in [year]. The aggregate amount of reportable cash we received from you in [year] was $[amount]. This information was reported to the Internal Revenue Service._
>
> _[Business name]_
> _[Business address]_
> _Contact: [contact person name], [telephone number]_

The statement can be longer or include additional context, but it must contain the four required elements.

## How to deliver the notification

- **Mailed paper letter to the customer's address of record on the 8300** is the audit-proof default. Use first-class mail; certified mail is overkill but harmless.
- **Electronic delivery is allowed** only if the recipient agrees to receive it in that format (Pub 1544). Document the consent (signed form, recorded acknowledgment).
- **Hand delivery** is permitted but creates documentation difficulty — capture a signed receipt if used.

Retain proof of delivery for 5 years:

- For mailed letters: a copy of the letter, the date mailed, and (if certified) the certified-mail tracking number
- For email: a copy of the email, sent timestamp, and the recorded consent

## What the notification must NOT contain

- Do **not** include the customer's SSN, ID number, or other PII beyond their name and address
- Do **not** say or imply that the transaction was reported as suspicious (Form 8300 instructions)
- Do **not** send a copy of the Form 8300 itself; the instructions call it not advisable because the form carries the filer's EIN or SSN
- Do **not** include the BSA tracking ID's full string in plaintext on a piece of paper that could be intercepted (last 4 digits is fine if any)
- Do **not** speculate about the IRS's future actions or imply audit risk to the customer

## Penalty for failure to notify

Under IRC §6722, the failure-to-furnish-payee-statement penalty matches the §6721 failure-to-file structure. For statements required to be furnished in 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2024-40, §2.59; verify the current year):

- **$340 per statement** under the general rule (furnished after August 1 or not at all)
- $60 if corrected within 30 days after January 31; $130 if corrected after 30 days but by August 1
- **Intentional disregard:** the greater of $680 per statement or 10% of the aggregate amount required to be reported, with no annual cap

For statements required to be furnished in 2027 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, §4.58): $340 / $60 / $130; intentional disregard greater of $690 or 10%.

Critically, **this penalty is independent of and in addition to any 8300-filing penalty.** A business that filed the 8300 timely but missed the January 31 customer notification still owes the §6722 penalty.

## Common failure modes

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Treating the notification as optional | $340 per statement (2026) | Schedule on the calendar at time of 8300 filing |
| Statement that omits some of the year's reported cash when multiple 8300s were filed for the same person | Incorrect statement | The regulation calls for a single annual statement showing the aggregate for the calendar year (26 CFR §1.6050I-1(f)) |
| Sending the statement before year-end, then receiving more reportable cash from the same person | Statement no longer shows the year's aggregate | Send after December 31 so one statement covers the whole calendar year |
| Sending after January 31 | Late-filing penalty tier | File the late notification anyway — late is cheaper than not at all |
| Including the customer's SSN in the notification | Privacy and security exposure | Remove; the customer already knows their SSN |

## When the customer is the same across multiple 8300s in one year

If the business filed three 8300s in 2026 for the same customer (different transactions), send one consolidated statement by January 31, 2027; the regulation requires "a single, annual, written statement" (26 CFR §1.6050I-1(f)(1)). The statement shows the aggregate of all 2026 reportable cash relating to that customer.

## Practical workflow

1. **At time of 8300 filing:** add a calendar reminder for January 15 of next year — review all 8300 filings from the prior year and identify unique customers.
2. **By January 31 of the next year:** mail one statement per person named on any of the year's 8300s (Part I and Part II), covering all reportable cash relating to that person in the prior year.
3. **Retain proof of mailing or email delivery for 5 years** alongside the 8300 records.

Most §6722 failures are not malicious; they are forgotten. A calendar reminder set at the time of filing prevents most of them.
