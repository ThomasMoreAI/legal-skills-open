# Substantiation Requirements by Deduction

§6001 requires records sufficient to establish every item. But some deductions
carry **strict statutory substantiation** — for those, the Cohan rule (courts
estimating a reasonable amount) is **unavailable**, and missing records mean a
total disallowance no matter how obviously real the expense was.

## §274(d) strict-substantiation categories — no estimates allowed

For **travel, gifts, and listed property** (and, historically, entertainment),
you must prove **all four** elements by adequate records or sufficient evidence
corroborating your own statement:

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Amount** | The dollar amount of each separate expenditure |
| **Time** | Date of the expense or use |
| **Place** | Destination, or location of use |
| **Business purpose** | The business reason, and for gifts/meals the **business relationship** of the people involved |

"Adequate records" means an account book, log, diary, or expense report made **at
or near the time** of the expense, plus documentary evidence (receipts) for
lodging and for any expense ≥ $75. A log reconstructed at audit is worth
substantially less and is routinely rejected.

### Listed property (§280F(d)(4))
Passenger autos and other property prone to personal use. Requires a **use log
allocating business vs personal** for every period of use. Without it, business
use is treated as **zero** — no depreciation, no §179, no mileage.

## By deduction

| Deduction | Required file contents |
|---|---|
| **Vehicle / mileage** | Contemporaneous mileage log: date, starting/ending odometer or miles, destination, business purpose. Total annual miles and commuting miles. If actual method: all receipts plus the business-use %. A calendar cross-reference is strong corroboration. |
| **Home office** | Floor plan or measurement of the exclusive-use area and total home area; photographs; the underlying home expenses (mortgage/rent, utilities, insurance, repairs); proof the space is the principal place of business. Exclusive use is the element the IRS attacks — a desk in a bedroom fails. |
| **Meals** | Receipt, date, place, amount, **names and business relationship of attendees**, and the business topic discussed. 50% limit. Keep the receipt itemized, not just the card slip. |
| **Travel** | Itinerary, lodging receipts (any amount), transportation receipts, business purpose, and a day-by-day allocation if the trip mixed personal days. Conference agendas and meeting confirmations are strong evidence. |
| **Charitable — cash <$250** | Bank record or written communication from the charity. Cancelled check alone is enough. |
| **Charitable — ≥$250** | **Contemporaneous written acknowledgment** from the charity stating the amount and whether goods or services were received, obtained **before the earlier of filing or the due date**. Getting it after an audit starts is too late — this is a strict rule with no reasonable-cause escape. |
| **Charitable — non-cash >$500** | Form 8283 Section A, plus acquisition date, cost basis, and how value was determined. |
| **Charitable — non-cash >$5,000** | **Qualified appraisal** by a qualified appraiser, Form 8283 Section B signed by the appraiser and the donee. |
| **R&D credit (§41)** | Project-by-project documentation: what technical uncertainty existed, what alternatives were evaluated, the process of experimentation, and **time-tracking allocating wages to qualified services**. Contractor agreements showing who bore the economic risk and who retained rights (only then is it a 65% contract QRE). This is the most document-intensive credit in the Code. |
| **§179 / depreciation** | Purchase invoice, placed-in-service date, business-use percentage, and the asset's class life. Placed in service ≠ purchased — the asset must be ready and available for use. |
| **Startup costs (§195)** | Dated invoices showing the expense preceded the business start, plus evidence of the date the business **began** (first sale, first customer, doors open). The start date determines what is §195 vs §162. |
| **Augusta rule (§280A(g))** | Written lease between owner and entity, **market-rate comparables** (3+ written quotes from comparable venues), corporate minutes for each meeting held, agenda and attendee list, and a 1099 analysis. Days must be ≤14. This is a heavily scrutinized position — document as if audited. |
| **Reasonable compensation (S corp)** | Independent comp study or benchmark data, job description, hours worked, and board resolution setting salary. The `reasonable-compensation` binding can generate the analysis and the memo, but the comparable data behind it is what defends the number. |
| **Accountable plan** | Written plan document, employee expense reports with receipts submitted within a reasonable time (60 days safe harbor), and evidence excess advances were returned (120 days safe harbor). |
| **Hobby-loss defense (§183)** | Business plan, separate bank account and books, time logs, evidence of changes made to improve profitability, expertise/advisors consulted, and the history of income and losses. See the nine factors in Reg. §1.183-2(b). |
| **Basis (any pass-through loss)** | Contribution records, loan documents (for debt basis), and a **running basis schedule by year**. Basis is the most commonly undocumented item in a small-entity audit, and the burden is on the taxpayer. |

## Retention

| Record | Keep for |
|---|---|
| General return support | 3 years from filing (§6501(a)) |
| Substantial omission (>25% of gross income) | 6 years (§6501(e)) |
| No return / fraudulent return | Indefinitely (§6501(c)) |
| **Basis records** (property, stock, capital accounts) | **Until 3 years after the year the property is disposed of** — often decades |
| **QSBS qualification evidence** | Until 3 years after the exclusion is claimed — potentially 10+ years after issuance |
| Employment tax records | 4 years after the tax is due or paid |
| Depreciation schedules | Life of the asset + 3 years |

**QSBS in particular**: the gross-assets test, the active-business test, and the
original-issuance requirement all have to be provable at *exit*, which may be a
decade after the facts. Capture the evidence at issuance — cap table, balance
sheet showing gross assets at and immediately after issuance, and a business
description supporting the qualified-trade-or-business test — and store it in the
documentation vault, not in someone's memory.
