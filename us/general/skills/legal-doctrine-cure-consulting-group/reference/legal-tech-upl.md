## NY statutes — the UPL perimeter (Judiciary Law art. 15)
- **§478:** a natural person may not practice or appear as an attorney for another in a NY court of record, or hold out as one, unless admitted (exceptions: supervised law students/graduates, pro hac vice, legal consultants).
- **§484:** no natural person may **ask or receive compensation** for appearing as attorney, **or for preparing** deeds, mortgages, leases or other real-estate instruments, **wills, codicils, or any other instrument affecting the disposition of property after death, or decedents' estates, or pleadings of any kind** in a court of record, unless admitted. This is the provision a self-help probate or estate product hits first.
- **§495 (corporations and voluntary associations):** (1) may not practice or appear as attorney, hold out as entitled to practice, furnish attorneys or counsel, render legal services, or advertise as a law office; (2) may not **solicit claims** to represent anyone in pursuing a civil remedy (fine up to \$5,000; individuals involved commit a misdemeanor); (3) may not **receive compensation for preparing** real-estate instruments, **wills, codicils, or estate instruments**; (4) (1)–(2) don't apply to businesses authorized by another statute; (5) title companies, corporations employing lawyers for their own affairs, and **clerical services to lawyers** who keep full professional and direct responsibility; (6) BCL art. 15/15-A professional corporations; (7) prepaid legal plans, nonprofits providing legal services incidental to their primary mission, and legal-aid organizations.
- **§479 / §482:** it is unlawful for **any person** to solicit or procure legal business for an attorney through solicitation (§479), and for an attorney to employ anyone to solicit (§482). Paid lead generation that crosses into solicitation hits these statutes directly, not just the ethics rules.
- **Penalties/enforcement:** §485 makes violations of §§478–484 a **misdemeanor**; **§485-a** makes it a **class E felony** when someone falsely holds out as a NY-licensed lawyer and causes more than \$1,000 in damage or material harm to a legal right. §476-a: the Attorney General sues (civil or criminal) on its own information or on complaint by a person or bar association; a bar association may sue only with Supreme Court leave after asking the AG at least 20 days earlier.

## What software may and may not do (NY)
- **Rule:** NY defines the practice of law by case law. Its core is applying legal principles to a particular person's problem, advising, or preparing documents that require legal judgment for a specific client (*Matter of Rowe*, 80 N.Y.2d 336 (1992): an attorney's published article on patients' rights was not practice, because practice involves legal advice directed to particular clients; *El Gemayel v. Seaman* (N.Y. 1988): calls from a foreign-licensed lawyer about a foreign proceeding were not NY practice "without more").
- **The publication/scrivener line:** ***NYCLA v. Dacey***, 21 N.Y.2d 694 (1967): the Court of Appeals reversed an injunction against *How to Avoid Probate!* (text plus fill-in forms), adopting the Appellate Division dissent. Selling general forms and information to the public at large is not practice because it creates **no personal relationship of trust and confidence**. A pure scrivener who types exactly what the customer dictates is the other safe pole. The risk sits between them: interviewing the user, **selecting** forms for the user, **filling** them based on the user's facts, or reviewing and correcting the user's answers.
- ***Upsolve, Inc. v. James*** (2d Cir. Sept. 9, 2025): nonlawyer "Justice Advocates" giving individualized advice on NY's check-box debt-collection answer form admittedly violated the UPL statutes. As applied, the UPL statutes regulate speech but are **content-neutral**, so **intermediate scrutiny** applies. The court vacated the 2022 preliminary injunction (Crotty, J.). **Status (verified 2026-09-28):** on remand, S.D.N.Y. (Kaplan, J.) dismissed with prejudice on **Mar. 5, 2026**; SCOTUS **denied cert Mar. 30, 2026** (No. 25-948). No First Amendment escape hatch for individualized nonlawyer advice in NY. Whether Upsolve appealed the dismissal is unconfirmed (confirm before use).
- **LegalZoom:** it settled or beat UPL suits elsewhere (Cal., Mo., Wash. settlements — confirm before use; N.C. consent judgment Oct. 22, 2015, later largely codified, with registration and consumer-protection conditions); no NY court ruling against it was found (confirm any NY-specific history before citing).
- **Applies to a product when:** a wizard branches on the user's facts and picks the form or the legal path; the product tells the user what to file or what to claim; a human "specialist" reviews answers; the product fills Surrogate's Court or estate documents for a fee (§§484, 495(3)); a chatbot answers "what should I do" questions.
- **Safer patterns:** general legal information, not advice about the user's situation; user-directed completion of official court forms (NY courts' DIY forms); transparent logic and no human legal review; conspicuous not-a-law-firm / no-attorney-client-relationship disclosures (helpful but **not a safe harbor in NY**, unlike Texas); attorney-review tiers delivered by an independent law firm (see below).
- **Traps:** disclaimers do not cure individualized advice; charging for estate or real-estate instruments is the specific statutory target; a nonprofit wrapper helps under §495(7) only if legal services are incidental to its primary mission and the rest of the statute is met; AI output tailored to the user's facts looks like advice, not publication.

## Lawyer-involvement models (NY RPC, 22 NYCRR Part 1200)
- **Attorney-of-record / law-firm delivery:** the legal service is sold and delivered by a NY-admitted firm that controls legal judgment; the platform is a vendor. **Rule 5.4(a):** no sharing legal fees with a nonlawyer; **5.4(d):** no practicing through an entity a nonlawyer owns any interest in or directs (NY has no ABS). The platform's fee must be for the service and not tied to the legal fee: flat SaaS or per-use.
- **Limited-scope representation — Rule 1.2(c):** allowed if the limitation is reasonable under the circumstances, the client gives informed consent, and, where necessary, notice goes to the tribunal and/or opposing counsel. Fits a "document review by a lawyer" tier. The lawyer owns competence and conflicts for that slice.
- **Rule 5.8:** a lawyer may contract with a nonlegal professional firm to offer legal plus nonlegal services to the public on a systematic, continuing basis **only if** the profession is on the Appellate Divisions' list (maintained under 22 NYCRR §1205.3, set out in §1205.5: architecture, certified public accountancy, professional engineering, land surveying, certified social work) and the nonlawyer gets **no ownership, investment, managerial, or supervisory** role in the law practice (plus further conditions in 5.8(a)). Software/marketing companies are not a listed profession.
- **Rule 7.2(a):** a lawyer may not give anything of value to anyone to **recommend** or obtain employment, or as a reward for a recommendation, except (1) Rule 5.8 reciprocal referrals with no money and no fee sharing, and (2) usual and reasonable fees to a **qualified legal assistance organization** and lawyer-to-lawyer referral fees under Rule 1.5(g). **7.2(b)** lists the qualified organizations: legal aid/public defender offices, military legal assistance, **bar-association-operated referral services**, and certain bona fide member/beneficiary organizations. A for-profit "referral service" is **not** one of them.
- **Rule 7.3 (solicitation):** in-person and telephone solicitation is barred except for close friends, relatives, and former or existing clients (7.3(a)(1)); a copy of a written solicitation is filed with the attorney disciplinary committee of the judicial district when disseminated (7.3(c)(1)); no solicitation about a specific personal-injury or wrongful-death incident before the 30th day after it (7.3(e)). Rule 7.1 governs "Attorney Advertising" labeling and retention.

## Lead-generation and paid referrals to law firms (NY)
- **Per-lead flat fee: permitted with conditions.** NYSBA Op. **1131** (2017, overruling Op. 902): a lawyer may pay a for-profit service for leads if the selection uses **transparent, mechanical criteria**, not analysis of the person's legal problem, the service does not recommend any lawyer explicitly or implicitly, and its communications comply with Rule 7.1. Op. **1294** (Mar. 11, 2026) restates the conditions: neutral, disclosed selection criteria; the platform states it doesn't endorse any lawyer; and **payment does not vary with whether the lead retains the lawyer or with the fee amount**. It also finds those contacts are neither solicitations nor advertisements.
- **Percentage or commission: prohibited.** Op. **1271** (Aug. 22, 2024): a platform keeping a share (7.5%) of the legal fee is fee sharing under Rule 5.4(a); vetting lawyers' skills and marketing them as "experts" is a **recommendation** under 7.2(a). Op. **1267** (June 18, 2024): a matching site that "carefully vets" "top-flight attorneys" recommends lawyers, so lawyers' listing fees violate 7.2(a) whatever the fee structure. Op. **1279** (Feb. 18, 2025): paying referrers indirectly (a per-referral "administrative" job) is still a thing of value under 7.2(a).
- **NYC Bar Formal Op. 2017-1:** bar-operated referral services may share fees with each other as long as the lawyer's total cost doesn't rise. This confirms the bar-service channel; it is not authority for for-profit services.
- **Applies to a product when:** a public-interest or data site sells "connect with a lawyer" leads; pricing is per signed client, a revenue share, or tiered by case value; the site ranks, rates, or "matches" firms by quality.
- **Design that fits the opinions:** flat per-lead (or flat subscription) fee not contingent on retention or fee size; mechanical, disclosed routing such as geography, practice area, or rotation; express "we do not recommend or endorse any lawyer"; no quality claims; nonlawyers never analyze the user's legal problem; the firm's follow-up complies with 7.1/7.3.
- **Traps:** a "success fee" or "per retained client" price is a 7.2(a) problem and, if percentage-based, a 5.4(a) problem; badges like "top-rated" or "best match" turn lead-gen into a recommendation; §479/§495(2) apply to the **platform itself** — the lawyer-side ethics rules are not the only exposure.

## Other states for a multi-state launch
- **Texas:** Gov't Code §81.101(c) (1999 amendment): the "practice of law" **does not include** designing, publishing, distributing, displaying, or selling (including online) written materials, books, forms, **computer software**, or similar products **if the products clearly and conspicuously state they are not a substitute for the advice of an attorney**. Enacted after *Unauthorized Practice of Law Comm. v. Parsons Technology*, 1999 WL 47235 (N.D. Tex. 1999) (Quicken Family Lawyer enjoined); vacated after the amendment, 179 F.3d 956 (5th Cir. 1999). This is a statutory **software safe harbor** that turns on the disclaimer. It doesn't cover human advice, and §81.101(c) doesn't override Gov't Code ch. 83 (barratry/solicitation) (confirm).
- **California:** B&P **§6125**: no one may practice law unless an active State Bar licensee (§6126(a): a misdemeanor, up to 1 year in county jail and/or a \$1,000 fine). ***People v. Landlords Professional Services***, 215 Cal. App. 3d 1599 (1989): an eviction service that interviewed clients, **selected and prepared forms** from the interview, and counseled on procedure was practicing law. **Legal Document Assistants (§6400 et seq.):** registered LDAs may prepare documents for self-represented people **at their specific direction** but may not advise on rights, remedies, **selection of forms**, or strategy. **§6155:** no nongovernmental entity may operate, even in part, to refer potential clients to attorneys unless **certified by the State Bar** and compliant with its minimum standards, and no attorney may accept such referrals; enforcement in §6155 runs through certification denial, suspension, or revocation (confirm any separate penalty before use). A lawyer-matching feature in California needs certification or a lead model that doesn't count as "referring."
- **Florida:** ***Florida Bar v. Brumbaugh*** (Fla. 1978): nonlawyers may sell forms and type information the customer provides **in writing**, but may not advise on or correct forms. ***Florida Bar v. TIKD Services***, 326 So. 3d 1073 (Fla. Oct. 14, 2021) (4–3; Canady, C.J., concurring in result): a platform that took traffic tickets for a fee and routed them to contracted lawyers engaged in UPL and was permanently enjoined. The court rejected the referee's "administrative services only" view. The Florida Bar's UPL rules (Rules Regulating the Fla. Bar ch. 10; Rules 10-2.1(a) and 10-2.2 let a nonlawyer help complete **Florida Supreme Court-approved forms**, limited to oral communication needed to elicit facts and explain filing, with no changes to the form and no advice) are enforced by Bar petition to the Supreme Court.
- **Traps:** a Texas-style disclaimer strategy does **not** export to NY, CA, or FL; CA §6155 reaches the platform directly; FL's TIKD makes "we just connect you to a lawyer, bundled with price" risky even with licensed lawyers doing the work.

## AI legal tools
- **22 NYCRR Part 161 (NY courts):** adopted by **AO/75/26 (Mar. 25, 2026), effective June 1, 2026**, statewide in civil and criminal matters. AI may be used to prepare court papers with **no mandatory disclosure**. Anyone (attorney **or party**, so pro se users too) who uses AI on a filed or served paper must review it and independently ensure it contains no fabricated or fictitious cases, statutes, or other material; **signing certifies** that review. It ties to 22 NYCRR §130-1.1 (frivolous conduct) and RPC 3.3. Individual judges may add part rules. Pull the text from nycourts.gov/rules/chiefadmin/161.shtml before quoting (internal section numbers: confirm before use).
- **ABA Formal Op. 512 (July 29, 2024):** lawyers using generative AI must meet competence (1.1) by understanding capabilities and limits and reviewing output; confidentiality (1.6), which requires **informed client consent before inputting client information into self-learning tools**; communication (1.4) about use when relevant; candor (3.1, 3.3); supervision (5.1, 5.3); and reasonable fees (1.5), meaning no billing hours saved by AI, and learning time is generally not billable. It is ABA guidance, not binding in NY — pair with NYSBA's AI task-force materials (confirm current NYSBA guidance).
- **Applies to a product when:** the product drafts pleadings or estate papers users will file (Part 161 certification falls on the user, so build a citation-verification step and warn the user); a lawyer-customer inputs client data (Op. 512 confidentiality means training opt-outs and DPAs); an AI assistant answers user-specific legal questions (UPL analysis above — automation doesn't avoid §§478/484/495 in NY).
- **Traps:** "the AI, not a person, gave the advice" is no NY defense after *Upsolve*; hallucinated citations in a pro se filing expose the user to sanctions, and the product to consumer-protection claims.

## Where this stops
- Whether a specific interview-driven form flow crosses from publication (*Dacey*) into individualized practice in NY — fact-specific; counsel should review the actual decision tree and copy.
- Structuring any law-firm partnership, attorney-review tier, or lead program (fee design, recommendation language, 5.4/7.2/5.8 compliance) — needs NY ethics counsel and possibly a NYSBA opinion request.
- State-by-state launch clearance (CA §6155 certification, TX disclaimer sufficiency, FL post-TIKD risk).
- Estate/probate document products for a fee under §§484 and 495(3).
