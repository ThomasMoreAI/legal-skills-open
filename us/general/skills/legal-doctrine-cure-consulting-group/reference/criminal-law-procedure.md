# Criminal Law and Procedure — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: homicide, mens rea, parties, inchoate crimes, defenses, property crimes, 4th/5th/6th/8th Amendment procedure, NY Penal Law and CPL issues.
Scope: MBE rule first (common law unless stem says MPC; federal constitutional procedure); NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified by web search: 2025 CPL Art. 245 amendments exist (details confirm-before-use). Other doctrine from model knowledge; numbers marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Mens rea & actus reus
- **Actus reus:** voluntary act or omission with legal duty (statute, contract, relationship — parent/spouse, voluntary assumption with seclusion, creation of peril). Possession = knowing control long enough to terminate.
- **CL:** specific intent (assault w/ intent, larceny, burglary, robbery, forgery, false pretenses, embezzlement, first-degree premeditated murder, attempt, conspiracy, solicitation); malice (murder, arson); general intent; strict liability (public welfare, statutory rape).
- **MPC §2.02:** purposely, knowingly, recklessly (conscious disregard of substantial and unjustifiable risk), negligently. Statute silent → recklessness minimum. Culpability term in statute applies to all material elements unless contrary purpose.
- **Mistake:** of fact negates specific intent even if unreasonable; must be reasonable for general intent/malice; irrelevant for strict liability. Mistake of law no defense (exceptions: reliance on statute later invalidated, official interpretation, not published; negates specific intent element like "property of another").
- **NY:** PL 15.05 — intentionally (conscious objective), knowingly (aware), recklessly (aware of and consciously disregards substantial and unjustifiable risk; **voluntary intoxication-induced unawareness is still recklessness**), criminal negligence (fails to perceive). PL 15.15: no culpability specified → strict liability only if clear legislative intent. PL 15.20 mistake of fact/law; PL 15.25 intoxication may be offered whenever relevant to negate an element (not just specific intent).

## Homicide
- **CL murder:** malice aforethought — intent to kill; intent to inflict great bodily harm; depraved heart (extreme reckless indifference); felony murder (BARRK: burglary, arson, rape, robbery, kidnapping; + mayhem, sodomy). Statutory degrees: first-degree = premeditated/deliberate (can be brief) or enumerated felony.
- **Felony murder limits:** inherently dangerous felony; independent of killing (merger — no felony murder from assault); during commission incl. flight until place of temporary safety; **agency theory (majority)** — no liability for killing by non-felon (police/victim); proximate-cause theory (minority) — liable. Co-felon killed by victim: no liability under agency; many jurisdictions none either way.
- **Voluntary manslaughter:** heat of passion — adequate provocation (serious battery, spouse adultery discovered, mutual combat; not words alone), actually provoked, no reasonable cooling time, didn't cool. Imperfect self-defense (some states). **MPC §210.3:** extreme mental or emotional disturbance with reasonable explanation, judged from actor's situation as he believes it — no cooling-off rule, words can suffice.
- **Involuntary manslaughter:** criminal negligence/recklessness (MPC: reckless = manslaughter; negligent = negligent homicide); misdemeanor-manslaughter.
- **Causation:** actual + proximate; eggshell victim; intervening medical negligence foreseeable; year-and-a-day rule abolished in most states.
- **NY:** PL 125.25 **murder 2**: (1) intent to cause death — affirmative defense of **extreme emotional disturbance** (PL 125.25(1)(a)) → manslaughter 1 (PL 125.20(2)); defendant proves by preponderance; subjective + reasonable explanation from defendant's viewpoint; no cooling-off requirement — *People v. Casassa* (N.Y. 1980); *Patterson v. New York* (U.S. 1977) upheld burden allocation. (2) **depraved indifference** + recklessly creates grave risk + causes death — depraved indifference is a **culpable mental state** (utter disregard for human life), not objective circumstances — *People v. Feingold* (N.Y. 2006) overruling *People v. Register* (N.Y. 1983); one-on-one shooting/stabbing generally intentional, not depraved (*People v. Payne* (N.Y. 2004); *People v. Suarez* (N.Y. 2005)). (3) **felony murder** — predicates: robbery, burglary, kidnapping, arson, rape 1, criminal sexual act 1, sexual abuse 1, aggravated sexual abuse, escape 1/2; death of a **non-participant**; proximate-cause approach — *People v. Hernandez* (N.Y. 1993) (police officer killed by fellow officer's fire during robbery → felony murder). **Affirmative defense PL 125.25(3)** (non-sole participant must prove ALL): (a) did not commit, solicit, request, command, importune, cause, or aid the homicidal act; (b) not armed with deadly weapon/dangerous instrument; (c) no reasonable ground to believe any other participant was armed; (d) no reasonable ground to believe any other participant intended conduct likely to result in death or serious physical injury.
- **NY murder 1** (PL 125.27): intent to kill + aggravator (victim police/peace officer/corrections, witness, contract killing, torture, serial, killing during enumerated felony, terrorism) + defendant **more than 18** at the time of the crime (PL 125.27(1)(b)). Manslaughter 2 (PL 125.15) recklessly causes death; criminally negligent homicide (PL 125.10). Aided suicide = manslaughter 2.

## Parties
- **Accomplice:** aids/encourages with intent that crime be committed (MPC: purpose to promote/facilitate); mere presence/knowledge insufficient (majority; knowledge alone insufficient for sale of ordinary goods). Liable for crime and natural and probable consequences (majority CL; MPC rejects). Withdrawal before crime: repudiate encouragement, neutralize assistance (e.g., notify police). Accessory after the fact separate, lesser crime.
- **NY:** PL 20.00 — accomplice must act "with the mental culpability required for the commission" of the offense and solicit, request, command, importune, or intentionally aid. **No natural-and-probable-consequences liability**. PL 20.05: no defense that other person not prosecuted/convicted/lacks capacity. PL 40.10(1) renunciation (affirmative defense: withdrew and made substantial effort to prevent commission). **Accomplice-testimony corroboration — CPL 60.22:** no conviction on uncorroborated testimony of an accomplice (one who may reasonably be considered to have participated in the offense charged or an offense based on same facts); corroboration must tend to connect defendant (MBE/federal: no such rule).

## Inchoate crimes
- **Solicitation:** merges into conspiracy/target; no renunciation at CL (MPC: renunciation if prevents crime).
- **Conspiracy (CL):** agreement of two+ with intent to agree and to achieve objective; majority requires **overt act** (federal §371 yes; CL no). **Bilateral (CL)** — both genuinely agree; **unilateral (MPC)** — undercover agent partner OK. Wharton's rule; legislative-exclusion rule (protected class). Does NOT merge (can convict of both). Withdrawal: no defense to conspiracy itself (CL) but cuts off later crimes if communicated to all; MPC: renunciation requires thwarting. **Pinkerton v. United States** (U.S. 1946): each conspirator liable for co-conspirators' crimes in furtherance and foreseeable (federal/majority; MPC rejects). Impossibility: factual no defense; legal impossibility defense (CL).
- **Attempt:** specific intent to commit target (no attempted depraved-heart murder, no attempted felony murder, no attempted involuntary manslaughter) + act beyond preparation — CL proximity/dangerous proximity; **MPC substantial step strongly corroborative** (lying in wait, reconnoitering, possessing materials). Merges into completed crime. Abandonment: CL none; MPC voluntary and complete. Factual impossibility no defense; true legal impossibility is.
- **NY:** attempt — PL 110.00 "tends to effect" commission; courts require "dangerously near" completion (*People v. Rizzo* (N.Y. 1927); *People v. Naradzay* (N.Y. 2008)). PL 110.10: factual or legal impossibility is no defense if crime could have been committed had circumstances been as believed. Conspiracy — PL 105.20 **overt act** required (by any conspirator), PL 105.30 **unilateral**: no defense that co-conspirator lacks capacity, is not prosecuted, or is an undercover officer feigning agreement (*People v. Schwimmer* (N.Y. 1978)). **NY rejects Pinkerton** — conspiracy alone does not make one liable for co-conspirators' substantive crimes; need accomplice liability under PL 20.00 (*People v. McGee* (N.Y. 1979)). Renunciation PL 40.10 affirmative defense for inchoate crimes.

## Defenses
- **Insanity:** M'Naghten (didn't know nature/quality or wrongfulness — federal 18 U.S.C. §17 variant, clear and convincing burden on defendant); irresistible impulse; Durham (product) — NH only; **MPC/ALI**: lacks substantial capacity to appreciate criminality or conform conduct. Competency to stand trial separate (*Dusky* (U.S. 1960)).
- **NY:** PL 40.15 — affirmative defense (preponderance): lacked **substantial capacity to know or appreciate** nature and consequences or that conduct was wrong (cognitive prong only; no volitional prong).
- **Intoxication:** voluntary — defense to specific intent only (CL); involuntary — treated as mental disease. **NY:** PL 15.25 — may negate any element; jury charge when reasonable view supports.
- **Self-defense:** non-deadly force when reasonably necessary against imminent unlawful force; deadly force when reasonably believe imminent death/serious bodily harm. Retreat: majority none; minority requires retreat if completely safe, except home (castle), and not for victims of certain crimes. Initial aggressor regains right by withdrawal and communication, or if victim escalates to deadly force. Imperfect self-defense → manslaughter (some states).
- **NY:** PL 35.15 justification (prosecution disproves beyond a reasonable doubt once raised). Deadly force: reasonable belief (subjective + objective, *People v. Goetz* (N.Y. 1986)) of imminent deadly force — but **duty to retreat** if actor knows he can retreat with **complete safety**, **except** (i) in his **dwelling** and not the initial aggressor, (ii) police/peace officers or those assisting. Deadly force also allowed (no retreat) to prevent kidnapping, forcible rape, forcible criminal sexual act (wording may track the 2024 sex-offense amendments; confirm before use), or **robbery**; and PL 35.20 to terminate a **burglary** of a dwelling or in premises reasonably believed being burglarized (dwelling). Non-deadly force: no retreat duty.
- **Duress:** threat of death/serious bodily harm to self/others; not a defense to intentional murder (CL). MPC: threat a person of reasonable firmness couldn't resist; applies to murder. **NY:** PL 40.00 affirmative defense (person of reasonable firmness) — unavailable if intentionally or recklessly placed self in situation.
- **Necessity:** choice of lesser evil; not for homicide (*Regina v. Dudley & Stephens* (1884)); not where created situation. **NY:** PL 35.05(2) emergency justification.
- **Entrapment:** majority/federal **subjective** — government induced + defendant not predisposed (*Sherman* (U.S. 1958); *Jacobson v. United States* (U.S. 1992)); MPC objective. **NY:** PL 40.05 affirmative defense — induced by public servant using methods creating substantial risk offense committed by person not otherwise disposed; mere opportunity not entrapment.
- **Infancy:** CL <7 none, 7–14 rebuttable presumption, 14+ capable. **NY Raise the Age (2017; effective 2018–2019):** criminal responsibility age 18 for most crimes; 16–17 year-olds charged with felonies are "adolescent offenders" in the Youth Part of superior court (many removed to Family Court); juvenile offenders 13–15 for enumerated serious crimes (PL 30.00) (confirm before use of transfer details).

## Property & other crimes
- **Larceny:** trespassory taking and carrying away (asportation) of personal property of another with intent to permanently deprive; intent at taking (continuing trespass for wrongful taking). **Embezzlement:** fraudulent conversion by one in lawful possession. **False pretenses:** title obtained by knowing false statement of past/present fact. **Larceny by trick:** only custody/possession obtained by deceit. **Robbery:** larceny + force/fear from person/presence. **Extortion:** threat of future harm (CL; no presence needed). **Burglary (CL):** breaking and entering dwelling of another at nighttime with intent to commit felony therein — modern statutes drop most. **Arson (CL):** malicious burning (charring) of dwelling of another. **Receiving stolen property:** knowing; goods must actually be stolen (police-recovered goods → attempt).
- **NY:** larceny consolidated (PL 155.05 includes embezzlement, false pretenses, trick, extortion, acquiring lost property). Degrees by value: grand larceny 4th > \$1,000; 3rd > \$3,000; 2nd > \$50,000; 1st > \$1,000,000 (confirm before use). Burglary (PL 140.20–.30): knowingly enters or remains unlawfully in a **building** with intent to commit **any crime** therein; no nighttime or dwelling element for 3rd degree. Robbery = forcible stealing (PL 160.00); degrees by weapon, injury, aided by another present. Assault 2 (PL 120.05) etc. Rape 3rd now includes lack of consent; statutory definitions amended 2024 to broaden "sexual intercourse" (confirm before use).

## Fourth Amendment
- **Search:** government action + reasonable expectation of privacy (*Katz* (U.S. 1967)) or physical trespass on persons, houses, papers, effects (*United States v. Jones* (U.S. 2012) GPS). No REP: open fields, trash at curb, things in plain view, bank records, pen register (third-party doctrine) — but **historical cell-site location data requires a warrant** (*Carpenter v. United States* (U.S. 2018)); cell phone search incident to arrest needs warrant (*Riley v. California* (U.S. 2014)); curtilage — *Collins v. Virginia* (U.S. 2018) (no automobile exception in curtilage); dog sniff at doorstep is a search (*Florida v. Jardines* (U.S. 2013)); thermal imaging home (*Kyllo* (U.S. 2001)).
- **Standing:** own REP in place searched — owner, resident, overnight guest (*Minnesota v. Olson* (U.S. 1990)); not short commercial visitor (*Minnesota v. Carter* (U.S. 1998)); passengers can't challenge car search but can challenge the stop (*Brendlin* (U.S. 2007)); unauthorized rental driver may have standing (*Byrd* (U.S. 2018)).
- **Warrant:** probable cause (totality — *Illinois v. Gates* (U.S. 1983), replacing Aguilar-Spinelli), particularity, neutral magistrate. Knock-and-announce violations don't trigger exclusion (*Hudson v. Michigan* (U.S. 2006)). *Franks* hearing for deliberate/reckless falsehoods.
- **Exceptions:** search incident to lawful arrest (person + wingspan; vehicle only if arrestee unsecured within reach or reasonable to believe evidence of the crime of arrest in vehicle — *Arizona v. Gant* (U.S. 2009)); automobile (PC → whole car and containers, *Acevedo*; *Ross*); plain view (lawful vantage, immediately apparent); consent (voluntary, no knowledge of right needed; third-party apparent authority; present objecting co-occupant controls — *Georgia v. Randolph* (U.S. 2006)); stop and frisk (*Terry* (U.S. 1968): reasonable suspicion; frisk for weapons; plain feel); hot pursuit; exigency (evanescent evidence — DUI blood draw needs case-by-case, *Missouri v. McNeely* (U.S. 2013); breath test OK incident to arrest, *Birchfield* (U.S. 2016); emergency aid; no "community caretaking" home entry, *Caniglia v. Strom* (U.S. 2021)); inventory; administrative/special needs; border.
- **Seizures:** arrest in public needs PC, no warrant (*Watson*); home arrest needs arrest warrant (*Payton* (U.S. 1980)); third-party home needs search warrant (*Steagald*). Traffic stop pretext OK (*Whren* (U.S. 1996)); reasonable mistake of law OK (*Heien* (U.S. 2014)); extending stop for dog sniff needs RS (*Rodriguez* (U.S. 2015)). Flight in high-crime area → RS (*Wardlow* (U.S. 2000)).
- **Exclusionary rule:** fruit of poisonous tree; exceptions — independent source, inevitable discovery, attenuation (*Utah v. Strieff* (U.S. 2016): outstanding warrant), **good-faith reliance** on defective warrant (*Leon* (U.S. 1984)), later-invalidated statute (*Krull*), binding appellate precedent (*Davis* (U.S. 2011)), clerical database error (*Herring* (U.S. 2009)). Not at grand jury, civil, parole hearings; impeachment use allowed. Good faith fails: affidavit so lacking in PC, facially deficient warrant, misled magistrate, abandoned magistrate.
- **NY:** **rejects the good-faith exception** under NY Const art I §12 — *People v. Bigelow* (N.Y. 1985). Retains **Aguilar-Spinelli** (veracity/reliability + basis of knowledge) for hearsay informant tips supporting warrants/PC — *People v. Griminger* (N.Y. 1988); *People v. Johnson* (N.Y. 1985). **De Bour four levels** (*People v. De Bour* (N.Y. 1976); *People v. Hollman* (N.Y. 1992)): (1) request for information — objective credible reason, not necessarily indicative of criminality; (2) common-law right of inquiry — founded suspicion that criminality is afoot (more pointed questions, no detention); (3) forcible stop and frisk — reasonable suspicion of crime (CPL 140.50), frisk if reasonably suspects danger; (4) arrest — probable cause. Flight alone does not justify pursuit; pursuit requires reasonable suspicion (*People v. Holmes* (N.Y. 1996)). Automobile search incident to arrest broader state analysis; *People v. Torres* (N.Y. 1989) rejects *Michigan v. Long* car-frisk. Standing: NY automatic standing for possession charges based on statutory presumption (*People v. Millan* (N.Y. 1987)). *People v. Weaver* (N.Y. 2009): GPS tracking requires warrant (pre-Jones).

## Fifth Amendment
- **Miranda** (U.S. 1966): custodial interrogation by known government agent. Custody = reasonable person wouldn't feel free + inherently coercive (routine traffic stop not custody, *Berkemer*); interrogation = words/actions police should know reasonably likely to elicit incriminating response (*Rhode Island v. Innis* (U.S. 1980)). Public safety exception (*Quarles*). Impeachment use of un-Mirandized voluntary statements allowed (*Harris*). Physical fruits admissible (*Patane* (U.S. 2004)). Deliberate two-step warnings → suppression (*Missouri v. Seibert* (U.S. 2004)); innocent failure then warned confession OK (*Elstad*). No §1983 for Miranda violation (*Vega v. Tekoh* (U.S. 2022)).
- **Invocation:** right to silence must be unambiguous (*Berghuis v. Thompkins* (U.S. 2010)); police must scrupulously honor, may re-approach later on another crime (*Michigan v. Mosley*). **Counsel:** unambiguous request (*Davis* (U.S. 1994)) → all interrogation stops, on any crime (*Edwards v. Arizona* (U.S. 1981); *Arizona v. Roberson*) until counsel present or defendant initiates; ends after **14-day break in custody** (*Maryland v. Shatzer* (U.S. 2010)).
- **Voluntariness (Due Process):** totality; requires police coercion (*Colorado v. Connelly* (U.S. 1986)). Involuntary confession inadmissible for any purpose; harmless error applies (*Fulminante*).
- **Self-incrimination:** testimonial + compelled + incriminating; not physical evidence (blood, lineup, handwriting); corporations have none; act of production may be testimonial. Prosecutor can't comment on defendant's silence at trial (*Griffin*); post-Miranda silence not usable to impeach (*Doyle*); pre-arrest silence if not invoked may be used (*Salinas* (U.S. 2013)). Immunity: use and derivative use suffices (*Kastigar*).
- **Double jeopardy:** attaches when jury sworn / first witness in bench trial. Same offense = *Blockburger* same-elements test; lesser included offenses same; separate sovereigns may both prosecute (*Gamble* (U.S. 2019)). Exceptions: hung jury, manifest necessity mistrial, defendant-requested mistrial (unless prosecutor goaded), successful appeal (unless evidentiary insufficiency). Collateral estoppel (*Ashe v. Swenson*). **NY:** CPL 40.20 bars separate prosecution for offenses based on same act/criminal transaction subject to exceptions — broader than Blockburger; CPL 40.20(2) limits successive state prosecution after federal (confirm scope before use).

## Sixth Amendment
- **Counsel:** attaches at formal charge (indictment, arraignment — *Rothgery* (U.S. 2008)); offense-specific (*McNeil*; *Texas v. Cobb* (U.S. 2001) — Blockburger for "same offense"). Deliberate elicitation by informants violates (*Massiah* (U.S. 1964)); passive listening OK (*Kuhlmann*). Critical stages: post-charge lineups, preliminary hearings, plea bargaining (*Lafler*/*Frye* (U.S. 2012)). Waiver can occur with Miranda warnings even after appointment (*Montejo v. Louisiana* (U.S. 2009)). Right to counsel where actual imprisonment imposed (*Scott v. Illinois*). Ineffective assistance: *Strickland* (U.S. 1984) deficient + prejudice; deportation advice (*Padilla* (U.S. 2010)). Violation of counsel of choice = structural (*Gonzalez-Lopez*). Self-representation (*Faretta*).
- **NY indelible right to counsel (NY Const art I §6):** far broader. Attaches (1) on **commencement of formal proceedings** (accusatory instrument filed, arrest warrant issued — *People v. Samuels* (N.Y. 1980); *People v. Settles* (N.Y. 1978)); cannot be waived outside presence of counsel. (2) When a person in custody is **actually represented** on the matter under interrogation or when counsel **has entered** the matter — *People v. Arthur* (N.Y. 1968); *People v. Hobson* (N.Y. 1976) (no waiver without counsel once attorney enters). (3) Once in custody on a charge on which he is **represented**, police cannot question him even on **unrelated** matters — *People v. Rogers* (N.Y. 1979). But *People v. Bing* (N.Y. 1990) (overruling *People v. Bartolomeo* (N.Y. 1981)): representation on a prior **unrelated pending** charge does not bar questioning on a new matter when defendant is not in custody on the represented charge. Request for counsel in custody → no waiver without counsel (*People v. Cunningham* (N.Y. 1980)). Violation → suppression regardless of voluntariness.
- **Confrontation:** testimonial hearsay inadmissible unless declarant unavailable + prior opportunity to cross (*Crawford* (U.S. 2004)); testimonial = primary purpose to establish past facts for prosecution (*Davis v. Washington*; *Michigan v. Bryant* — ongoing emergency nontestimonial); forensic reports testimonial (*Melendez-Diaz*, *Bullcoming*; *Smith v. Arizona* (U.S. 2024) — basis testimony by substitute expert conveys testimonial statements for their truth). Forfeiture by wrongdoing requires intent to prevent testimony (*Giles*). *Bruton*: non-testifying codefendant confession naming defendant; redaction with neutral references OK (*Samia v. United States* (U.S. 2023)). *Hemphill v. New York* (U.S. 2022) — opening the door doesn't waive confrontation.
- **Speedy trial:** *Barker v. Wingo* (U.S. 1972) balancing — length, reason, assertion, prejudice; remedy dismissal with prejudice. **NY:** CPL **30.30** statutory readiness: People must be ready within **6 months** of commencement for **felony**; **90 days** for misdemeanor punishable >3 months; **60 days** for misdemeanor ≤3 months; **30 days** for violation. Excludable periods (CPL 30.30(4)): defense motions, adjournments consented by defense, defendant's absence, exceptional circumstances. **2019 reform:** People cannot declare ready without a proper **certificate of compliance** (CPL 245.50; 30.30(5)); *People v. Bay* (N.Y. 2023) — COC valid only if People exercised due diligence and made reasonable inquiries before filing. 2025 budget amendments narrowed COC-based dismissals (prejudice/due-diligence standard, timing of challenges) (confirm before use). Constitutional speedy trial separately under *People v. Taranovich* (N.Y. 1975) factors.
- **Jury:** right for offenses with authorized term >6 months (*Baldwin*); **unanimity required** in state felony cases (*Ramos v. Louisiana* (U.S. 2020)); 6-person minimum; 6-person must be unanimous. *Batson* (U.S. 1986) — race; *J.E.B.* — sex. *Apprendi* (U.S. 2000) — facts increasing maximum (other than prior conviction) to jury beyond reasonable doubt; mandatory minimums too (*Alleyne* (U.S. 2013)); *Erlinger v. United States* (U.S. 2024) — ACCA "different occasions" to jury. **NY:** 12 jurors for felonies, 6 for misdemeanors in local criminal court (NYC misdemeanors punishable ≤6 months: no jury, CPL 340.40 (confirm before use)). Jury waiver: in writing, signed in open court, approved by the court; barred only for crimes punishable by death (NY Const art I §2).
- **NY Grand jury:** NY Const art I §6 — no person held for a **felony** (capital/infamous crime) without grand jury indictment; waiver by **superior court information** permitted (CPL 195.10) except for class A felonies punishable by life imprisonment, and defendant must not already be indicted. Defendant has right to testify before grand jury on notice (CPL 190.50). 12 votes of 16–23 grand jurors to indict. Federal: 5th Am indictment right not incorporated against states (*Hurtado* (U.S. 1884)).
- **NY CPL 710.30 notice:** People must serve notice within **15 days after arraignment** of intent to offer defendant's statements to a public servant or prior identification testimony (police-arranged ID) — else preclusion absent good cause for late notice. Confirmatory-ID exception (*People v. Rodriguez* (N.Y. 1992)).
- **NY discovery CPL Art. 245 (2019 reform, eff. 2020):** automatic disclosure of all items relating to subject matter (CPL 245.20) within set periods after arraignment (e.g., 20/35 days depending on custody; confirm before use), COC, continuing duty, sanctions (CPL 245.80). Pre-plea disclosure requirement.

## Eighth Amendment & sentencing
- Cruel and unusual: gross disproportionality (*Solem*; *Harmelin*); no death for non-homicide rape (*Kennedy v. Louisiana*), intellectually disabled (*Atkins*; *Hall*), under 18 at offense (*Roper*); no LWOP for juvenile non-homicide (*Graham*); no mandatory juvenile LWOP (*Miller*), but no permanent-incorrigibility finding required (*Jones v. Mississippi* (U.S. 2021)). Excessive fines incorporated (*Timbs v. Indiana* (U.S. 2019)). Punishing public camping by homeless not cruel and unusual (*City of Grants Pass v. Johnson* (U.S. 2024)). **NY:** no death penalty (*People v. LaValle* (N.Y. 2004) struck the statute).

## Identification
- Due process: unnecessarily suggestive police-arranged procedure + substantial likelihood of misidentification (reliability — *Manson v. Brathwaite* (U.S. 1977), *Neil v. Biggers* factors); no screening where suggestiveness not police-arranged (*Perry v. New Hampshire* (U.S. 2012)). Post-charge lineup counsel right (*Wade*); photo arrays no counsel (*Ash*). Independent source allows in-court ID.
- **NY:** **per se exclusion** of unduly suggestive pretrial ID — no *Manson* reliability balancing (*People v. Adams* (N.Y. 1981)); in-court ID allowed only on independent source by clear and convincing evidence. Expert testimony on eyewitness reliability should be admitted in proper cases (*People v. LeGrand* (N.Y. 2007)); cross-racial ID charge on request (*People v. Boone* (N.Y. 2017)). Wade hearing.

## Traps
- MBE felony murder: default agency theory → no liability for police/victim killing. NY: proximate cause (*Hernandez*) but decedent must be a non-participant.
- NY conspirator ≠ liable for co-conspirator's crimes (no Pinkerton); MBE/federal → Pinkerton applies.
- NY good-faith: suppress anyway. MBE: *Leon* saves the evidence.
- NY represented suspect in custody: statements suppressed even with Miranda waiver; MBE uses *Montejo*/*Edwards*.
- NY attempt: impossibility (factual or legal) no defense per PL 110.10.
- Depraved-indifference murder: one-on-one intentional shooting → intentional murder, not depraved (*Payne*/*Feingold*).
- EED and felony-murder defense are affirmative defenses — defendant bears preponderance burden in NY; justification is not (People disprove).
