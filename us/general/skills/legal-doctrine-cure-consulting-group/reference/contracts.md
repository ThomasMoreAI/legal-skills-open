# Contracts & UCC Article 2 — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: formation, consideration, statute of frauds, parol evidence, interpretation, conditions, breach, excuse, remedies, third parties, assignment, or any sale-of-goods (UCC Art. 2) issue.
Scope: MBE/MEE rule first (Restatement (Second) + uniform UCC); NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified: GOL 5-701(a)(1) and (a)(10) text checked on nysenate.gov (2026-09); other doctrine from model knowledge; numbers marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Threshold: which law governs
- **Rule:** UCC Art. 2 governs transactions in goods (movable at identification). Common law governs services, real estate, employment, IP licenses.
- **Mixed contracts:** majority = predominant-purpose test (whole contract under one body of law); minority = gravamen test (apply UCC only to the goods portion if the dispute concerns goods).
- **Merchant rules** (2-104): merchant = deals in goods of the kind or holds itself out as having knowledge/skill. Merchant-only rules: 2-201(2) merchant confirmation, 2-205 firm offer, 2-207(2) additional terms, 2-314 merchantability, 2-509(3) risk of loss, 2-103 good faith (NY: see below), 2-603 rejected-goods duties.
- **NY:** NY's UCC is the 1962-era Art. 2 text with later amendments; NY did **not** adopt the 2003 Art. 2 amendments (withdrawn nationally in 2011). NY adopted revised Art. 1 (1-201 et seq.) in 2014 (confirm before use). NY 2-201 threshold remains \$500.

## Formation
- **Offer:** manifestation of willingness inviting acceptance; ads are generally invitations unless definite and limit who may accept (e.g., "first 3 customers").
- **Termination of offer:** rejection, counteroffer, lapse (stated or reasonable time), revocation (direct or indirect via reliable info the offeror took inconsistent action), death/incapacity of either party (majority, even without notice).
- **Irrevocable offers:** option supported by consideration; R2d §87(2) reliance by offeree the offeror should reasonably expect (esp. subcontractor bids — *Drennan v. Star Paving* (Cal. 1958)); unilateral contract — beginning performance creates option (R2d §45); UCC 2-205 merchant firm offer.
- **NY:** GOL 5-1109 — a written offer signed by the offeror that states it is irrevocable is irrevocable for the stated time (or reasonable time) **without consideration**; applies beyond goods and to non-merchants. NY is historically less receptive to *Drennan*-style bid reliance (cf. *James Baird Co. v. Gimbel Bros.* (2d Cir. 1933)); confirm current App. Div. treatment before use.
- **Mailbox rule:** acceptance effective on dispatch; rejection effective on receipt; if offeree sends rejection then acceptance, whichever arrives first controls; acceptance under an option is effective on receipt.
- **Mirror image (common law):** acceptance must match; varying terms = counteroffer; last-shot rule.
- **UCC 2-206:** offer to buy for prompt shipment invites acceptance by promise or shipment; shipment of nonconforming goods is both acceptance and breach unless seller seasonably notifies it is an accommodation.
- **UCC 2-204:** contract may be made in any manner showing agreement, even if moment of formation undetermined and terms left open, if intent + reasonably certain basis for remedy. Gap fillers: price 2-305, delivery place 2-308 (seller's place of business), time 2-309 (reasonable), payment 2-310 (at receipt).
- **Output/requirements (2-306):** quantities in good faith; no quantity unreasonably disproportionate to stated estimate or prior output/requirements. Exclusive dealing implies best efforts. Illusory-promise objection fails.

### Battle of the forms — UCC 2-207
1. Is there a contract by the writings? A definite and seasonable expression of acceptance operates as acceptance even with additional/different terms **unless** acceptance is expressly made conditional on assent to the new terms (then it is a counteroffer).
2. Additional terms are proposals. **Between merchants** they become part of the contract unless (a) offer expressly limits acceptance to its terms, (b) they materially alter (e.g., arbitration — **NY:** *Marlene Indus. v. Carnac Textiles* (N.Y. 1978): arbitration clause is a material alteration), or (c) offeror objects within reasonable time.
3. **Different terms:** majority = knockout rule (conflicting terms cancel, UCC gap-fillers apply); minority = offeror's term controls; minority = treat as additional.
4. **2-207(3):** if writings do not form a contract but conduct recognizes one, terms = those on which writings agree + UCC gap-fillers.
- **Traps:** "expressly conditional" requires clear language, not boilerplate "our terms govern"; 2-207 does not apply the mirror-image rule; rolling-contract / terms-in-the-box cases (*Hill v. Gateway 2000* (7th Cir. 1997)) are a split.

## Consideration and substitutes
- **Rule:** bargained-for exchange of legal detriment. Past consideration, moral obligation, illusory promises, pre-existing duty = no consideration.
- **Pre-existing duty:** common law modification needs new consideration (exception R2d §89: fair and equitable modification in view of unanticipated circumstances). **UCC 2-209(1):** modification needs no consideration, only good faith.
- **Promissory estoppel (R2d §90):** promise + reasonably expected and actual reliance + injustice avoidable only by enforcement; remedy may be limited to reliance. Charitable subscriptions enforceable without proof of reliance (§90(2)).
- **Material benefit rule (R2d §86):** minority; promise made in recognition of benefit previously received.
- **NY:** GOL 5-1103 — a written, signed agreement to change or discharge a contract obligation is not invalid for lack of consideration. GOL 5-1105 — a promise in a signed writing is not unenforceable for past consideration if the consideration is expressed in the writing and proved to have been given. GOL 5-1107 — written assignment not invalid for lack of consideration. **NY promissory estoppel is narrow:** requires a clear and unambiguous promise, reasonable and foreseeable reliance, and injury; to defeat the statute of frauds NY requires reliance causing **unconscionable injury** beyond ordinary reliance losses (*Merex A.G. v. Fairchild Weston Systems* (2d Cir. 1994), applying NY law).
- **Traps:** a written release/modification in NY needs no consideration — MBE answer differs. A promise to pay a debt barred by limitations is enforceable if in writing (GOL 17-101 acknowledgment).

## Defenses to formation
- **Mistake:** mutual — basic assumption, material effect, adversely affected party did not bear the risk → voidable. Unilateral — also require unconscionability or other party knew/should have known.
- **Misrepresentation:** fraudulent or material + justifiable reliance → voidable. Nondisclosure actionable only with duty (fiduciary, half-truth, concealment, or correcting basic assumption).
- **Duress/undue influence**; **incapacity** — minors may disaffirm (liable for necessaries in quasi-contract); ratification on reaching majority. **NY:** age of majority 18.
- **Unconscionability** (UCC 2-302; R2d §208): measured at formation; court may refuse enforcement, sever, or limit. **NY:** generally requires both procedural and substantive unconscionability, though extreme substantive alone may suffice (*Gillman v. Chase Manhattan Bank* (N.Y. 1988)).
- **Illegality/public policy.**

## Statute of frauds
- **Categories (MYLEGS):** marriage consideration, contracts not performable within one year, land interests, executor promise to pay estate debts personally, goods ≥\$500, suretyship (not main-purpose/leading-object).
- **One-year rule:** measured from making; if performance *possibly* within a year, outside the statute (lifetime contracts are outside at common law — **not in NY**, see below). Full performance by one side takes it out (majority).
- **Land:** part performance (majority: two of payment/possession/improvements; must be unequivocally referable).
- **NY:** GOL 5-701(a)(1) one-year rule; NY applies it narrowly (only contracts that by their terms have "absolutely no possibility" of performance within a year — *D & N Boening v. Kirsch Beverages* (N.Y. 1984)). GOL 5-701(a)(10): a contract to pay compensation for services in negotiating a loan, a purchase/sale/lease of real estate, or of a business opportunity/business/goodwill/majority voting stock (a **finder's fee**) must be in a signed writing — exempt: licensed real estate brokers/salespersons, attorneys, auctioneers. GOL 5-703: real property interests and leases > 1 year; part performance exception must be "unequivocally referable" to the agreement. NY's part-performance exception is grounded in GOL 5-703(4) (real property); the Court of Appeals has **not** adopted a part-performance exception to 5-701 (*Messner Vetere Berger McNamee Schmetterer Euro RSCG Inc. v. Aegis Group* (N.Y. 1999), n.1 — a 5-703 lease case). GOL 5-701(b)(4): electronic communications may satisfy the writing requirement for qualified financial contracts (confirm scope before use). **NY departs from the common law on lifetime contracts:** 5-701(a)(1) expressly covers an agreement "the performance of which is not to be completed before the end of a lifetime" — so a lifetime employment or support promise must be in writing in NY (MBE: outside the statute because death could occur within a year). A contract that could end within a year only by breach is within the statute; one terminable at will by either party within a year is generally outside it. 5-701(a)(10) also reaches claims **implied in fact or in law** (quantum meruit) for finder's fees — the writing requirement cannot be evaded by suing in quasi-contract.
- **UCC 2-201:** writing signed by party to be charged, indicating a contract, stating quantity (enforceable only up to quantity stated). Exceptions: (2) merchant confirmation — merchant recipient with reason to know its contents who does not object in writing within 10 days is bound; (3)(a) specially manufactured goods not suitable for others, seller substantially began; (3)(b) admission in pleading/testimony; (3)(c) goods paid for and accepted (to that extent).
- **Traps:** 2-201(2) removes only the SOF defense — the party still must prove the contract. Suretyship main-purpose rule. Modification must satisfy SOF if the contract as modified is within it (2-209(3)).

## Parol evidence and interpretation
- **Rule:** integrated writing bars prior/contemporaneous extrinsic terms that contradict (partial integration) or add to (complete integration). Always admissible: formation defects (fraud, mistake, duress), conditions precedent to effectiveness, interpretation of ambiguity, later modifications, consideration recitals, reformation.
- **Split:** Williston four corners vs. Corbin/R2d (extrinsic evidence to decide whether integrated and whether ambiguous). UCC 2-202: course of performance, course of dealing, usage of trade always admissible to explain or supplement; consistent additional terms admissible unless intended as complete and exclusive; hierarchy express > course of performance > course of dealing > usage (1-303).
- **NY:** strict four-corners approach — extrinsic evidence may not be used to create an ambiguity in an unambiguous agreement (*W.W.W. Associates v. Giancontieri* (N.Y. 1990)). Merger clause bars claims of fraud based on representations the contract specifically disclaims (*Danann Realty Corp. v. Harris* (N.Y. 1959)); a general merger clause does not bar a fraud-in-the-inducement claim.
- **Interpretation maxims:** specific over general; handwritten > typed > printed; contra proferentem last resort. Latent ambiguity, each party attaches different meaning, neither knows → no contract (*Raffles v. Wichelhaus* (Eng. 1864)); if one knows, the other's meaning controls.

## Conditions and performance
- **Express conditions** require strict compliance (subject to excuse to avoid forfeiture, R2d §229). Satisfaction clauses: objective standard for commercial/operative fitness; subjective honest satisfaction for taste/judgment.
- **Constructive conditions:** order of performance — work before payment; simultaneous when possible.
- **Substantial performance:** common law — material breach suspends and, if uncured, discharges; minor breach → damages only. Factors R2d §241. **NY:** *Jacob & Youngs v. Kent* (N.Y. 1921) — nonwillful trivial deviation (wrong pipe brand): damages = diminution in value, not cost of completion, where cost is grossly disproportionate.
- **Divisible contracts:** recover for completed units. Willful breacher may recover in restitution (majority modern, R2d §374) less damages.
- **Anticipatory repudiation:** unequivocal; nonbreacher may suspend, sue immediately, urge performance; repudiator may retract until reliance or acceptance. **Adequate assurance** — UCC 2-609 (written demand; failure to respond within reasonable time ≤30 days = repudiation). **NY:** extends demand-for-assurance right to complex commercial contracts between sophisticated parties not covered by UCC (*Norcon Power Partners v. Niagara Mohawk Power Corp.* (N.Y. 1998)).
- **Waiver/estoppel:** waived conditions may be reinstated by notice unless reliance.
- **NY no-oral-modification:** GOL 15-301(1) — a written agreement containing a clause barring oral change cannot be changed by an **executory** oral agreement. Exceptions: oral modification fully performed, partial performance unequivocally referable to the oral modification, or equitable estoppel (*Rose v. Spa Realty Associates* (N.Y. 1977)). UCC 2-209(2) (signed NOM clause binds; merchant form clause must be separately signed by non-merchant) and 2-209(4) (ineffective modification may operate as waiver).
- **Good faith:** implied covenant in every contract; UCC 1-304. **NY:** implied covenant cannot create obligations inconsistent with express terms; claim duplicative of breach-of-contract is dismissed.

## Excuse
- **Impossibility/impracticability** (R2d §261; UCC 2-615): unforeseen event, nonoccurrence a basic assumption, party not at fault, did not assume risk. Death/incapacity of a personal-services performer; destruction of specific subject matter (*Taylor v. Caldwell* (Eng. 1863)); supervening illegality. Increased cost alone is not enough.
- **Frustration of purpose** (R2d §265): principal purpose substantially frustrated, both parties knew purpose (*Krell v. Henry* (Eng. 1903)).
- **UCC 2-613** casualty to identified goods without fault before risk passes → contract avoided (total) or buyer may accept with allowance (partial). **2-614** substituted performance for unavailable carrier/facility. **2-615(b)** allocate among customers; notice required.
- **NY:** impossibility is applied narrowly — only where destruction of subject matter or means of performance makes performance objectively impossible and the event could not have been foreseen and guarded against in the contract (*Kel Kim Corp. v. Central Markets* (N.Y. 1987)); force majeure clauses construed narrowly to the listed events (same case). *407 East 61st Garage v. Savoy Fifth Ave. Corp.* (N.Y. 1968) — economic hardship not excuse.

## Remedies
- **Expectation:** loss in value + incidental + consequential − cost avoided − loss avoided. Certainty required (new-business lost profits hard to prove). **NY:** lost profits for breach recoverable only if within the parties' contemplation at contracting and proven with reasonable certainty (*Kenford Co. v. County of Erie* (N.Y. 1986)).
- **Foreseeability (*Hadley v. Baxendale* (Eng. 1854)):** consequential damages only if arising naturally or within contemplation at formation.
- **Mitigation:** no recovery for avoidable loss; reasonable mitigation expenses recoverable. Employment: substantially similar job in same locality. Lost-volume seller exception.
- **Reliance** (when expectation uncertain; capped at expectation if breacher proves losing contract). **Restitution**: value of benefit conferred; available to breaching party (minority/modern) and in quasi-contract; losing-contract plaintiff may elect restitution not capped by price (majority).
- **Liquidated damages:** reasonable forecast in light of anticipated or actual harm + damages difficult to estimate; penalties unenforceable. **NY:** *Truck Rent-A-Center v. Puritan Farms 2nd* (N.Y. 1977) — enforceable if actual damages difficult to determine and sum not plainly disproportionate to probable loss. UCC 2-718: reasonable in light of anticipated or actual harm; defaulting buyer restitution of deposit exceeding the lesser of 20% of price or \$500 absent valid LD.
- **Specific performance:** inadequate legal remedy (land, unique goods — 2-716); not for personal services (but injunction against competing possible).
- **Punitive damages:** not for breach alone. **NY:** available only where conduct is egregious, directed at plaintiff, part of a pattern aimed at the public (*New York University v. Continental Ins. Co.* (N.Y. 1995); *Rocanova v. Equitable Life* (N.Y. 1994)).
- **Attorney's fees:** American rule unless contract/statute. **NY:** RPL 234 makes residential-lease landlord fee clauses reciprocal for tenants.

## Third parties
- **Intended beneficiary** (creditor/donee) may sue; incidental may not. Rights vest when beneficiary assents at promisor's request, sues, or materially relies; before vesting the parties may modify. Promisor has defenses against promisee.
- **NY:** *Lawrence v. Fox* (N.Y. 1859) (creditor beneficiary); *Seaver v. Ransom* (N.Y. 1918) (donee beneficiary). NY test: valid contract, intended to benefit third party, benefit sufficiently immediate to indicate assumption of duty to compensate (*Fourth Ocean Putnam Corp. v. Interstate Wrecking* (N.Y. 1985)). Members of the public generally incidental beneficiaries of government contracts.
- **Assignment:** rights freely assignable unless materially changes obligor's duty/risk, personal, or barred. Anti-assignment clause: majority — bars delegation of duties but only the right to assign (damages), not power, unless it says assignments are "void"; UCC 2-210 — "assignment of the contract" presumed to include delegation; right to damages for breach always assignable. **UCC 9-406(d):** a term restricting assignment of an account (right to payment for goods sold or services rendered) is ineffective; after notice of assignment, obligor discharges only by paying the assignee (9-406(a)). Gratuitous assignments revocable (unless writing delivered, token, or reliance). Priority among successive assignees: majority (American rule) first in time; exceptions for first-to-obtain payment/judgment/token if later assignee paid value without notice.
- **NY:** GOL 5-1107 (written assignment needs no consideration). NY enforces anti-assignment clauses that expressly render assignment void (*Allhusen v. Caristo Construction Corp.* (N.Y. 1952)).
- **Delegation:** permissible unless personal/barred; delegator remains liable; novation needed for release. Delegatee liable to obligee only if it assumed (promised) — then obligee is intended beneficiary.

## UCC Art. 2 performance and breach
- **Perfect tender (2-601):** buyer may reject if goods or tender fail in any respect. Limits: **cure 2-508** (before time for performance; after, if seller had reasonable grounds to believe tender acceptable, reasonable further time with notice); **installment 2-612** (reject installment only if substantial impairment that cannot be cured; entire contract breached only if impairs whole); acceptance; good faith.
- **Rejection:** within reasonable time, seasonable notice; merchant buyer must follow reasonable instructions / resell perishables (2-603). **Acceptance (2-606):** after reasonable opportunity to inspect, signifying acceptance, failing to reject, or act inconsistent with seller's ownership. **Revocation (2-608):** nonconformity substantially impairs value, and accepted on reasonable assumption of cure or difficulty of discovery; before substantial change. Buyer accepting goods must notify of breach within reasonable time or be barred (2-607(3)).
- **Risk of loss (no breach, 2-509):** by agreement; carrier — shipment contract (FOB seller's point) passes on due delivery to carrier; destination contract passes on tender at destination; goods held by bailee — on buyer's receipt of negotiable document / bailee acknowledgment; otherwise merchant seller → on buyer's **receipt**; non-merchant seller → on **tender**. Default is shipment contract. **2-510 breach:** nonconforming tender — risk stays on seller until cure or acceptance; buyer revoking — risk on seller to the extent buyer's insurance deficient; buyer repudiation re identified conforming goods — on buyer for commercially reasonable time to the extent seller's insurance deficient.
- **Entrustment 2-403(2):** entrusting goods to a merchant who deals in goods of the kind gives power to transfer all rights to a buyer in ordinary course. Voidable title (bad check, fraud) can pass good title to good-faith purchaser for value; thief has void title.

## Warranties
- **2-312 title** (disclaimable only by specific language or circumstances). **2-313 express:** affirmation, description, sample that is part of the basis of the bargain; puffery excluded; no reliance required per comment. **2-314 merchantability:** only merchant sellers of goods of that kind; fit for ordinary purposes. **2-315 fitness:** seller has reason to know particular purpose and buyer relies on seller's skill; any seller.
- **Disclaimers 2-316:** express warranties cannot be disclaimed inconsistently with them. Merchantability: must mention "merchantability," if written must be conspicuous. Fitness: writing and conspicuous. "As is"/"with all faults" excludes implied warranties. Examination before contract excludes defects examination should reveal.
- **Limitation of remedy 2-719:** limited remedy (repair/replace) valid unless it fails of essential purpose; consequential-damages exclusion for personal injury from consumer goods is prima facie unconscionable.
- **Privity 2-318:** Alt. A (family/household/guests), B (any natural person reasonably expected to use and injured in person), C (any person injured). **NY:** NY's 2-318 extends warranties to any natural person reasonably expected to use, consume, or be affected who is injured in person (Alt. B-type) (confirm before use).
- **Magnuson-Moss** (15 U.S.C. §2308): supplier giving written warranty on consumer products may not disclaim implied warranties (may limit duration to that of a limited written warranty).

## UCC remedies
- **Seller:** 2-703 menu; withhold delivery, stop in transit (2-705), resell **2-706** (contract price − resale price + incidental − expenses saved; good faith, commercially reasonable; notice of private sale); market damages **2-708(1)** (contract price − market price at time and place of tender); **2-708(2)** lost profit for lost-volume seller or where (1) inadequate; action for price **2-709** (accepted goods, conforming goods lost after risk passed, or identified goods not reasonably resalable); incidental 2-710 (sellers get no consequentials under Art. 2). 2-702: insolvent buyer — seller may reclaim goods on demand within 10 days after receipt (misrepresentation of solvency in writing within 3 months before delivery removes the 10-day limit).
- **Buyer:** cover **2-712** (cover price − contract price + incidental + consequential − expenses saved; reasonable, good faith, without unreasonable delay); market damages **2-713** (market price when buyer **learned of the breach** − contract price, at place of tender); accepted goods **2-714** (value as warranted − value as accepted, at time and place of acceptance); incidental/consequential **2-715** (consequentials limited to those buyer could not reasonably prevent by cover); specific performance/replevin **2-716**; deduct damages from price with notice 2-717.
- **2-725 limitations:** 4 years from accrual; accrues on tender of delivery regardless of knowledge, unless warranty explicitly extends to future performance (then on discovery). Parties may reduce to not less than 1 year, may not extend. **NY:** NY enacted 2-725 (4 years); contrast CPLR 213(2) **6 years** for common-law contracts. Contract claims accrue at breach, not discovery (*Ely-Cruikshank Co. v. Bank of Montreal* (N.Y. 1993)). CPLR 213(2) 6-year period can be shortened by written agreement if reasonable; cannot be extended at formation (GOL 17-103 allows extension agreements made after accrual, in a signed writing, confirm before use).
- **Traps:** 2-713 time = learning of breach (repudiation: majority reads as commercially reasonable time after learning); 2-708 time = tender. Seller who resells improperly is relegated to 2-708. Buyer need not cover (2-712(3)) but consequentials reduced if could have covered.
