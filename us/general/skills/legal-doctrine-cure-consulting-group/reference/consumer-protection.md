## NY GBL §349: deceptive acts and practices (now also unfair and abusive ones)
- **Rule:** GBL §349(a) prohibits deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any business, trade or commerce. Private-plaintiff elements under *Oswego Laborers' Local 214 Pension Fund v. Marine Midland Bank* (N.Y. 1995) are (1) consumer-oriented conduct, (2) that is materially misleading to a reasonable consumer, (3) that causes injury. No intent and no reliance are needed (*Koch v. Acker, Merrall & Condit Co.* (N.Y. 2012)).
- **FAIR Business Practices Act (S8416), Status (verified 2026-09-28):** signed 2025-12-19, in effect since 2026-02-17. It adds "unfair" acts (substantial injury, not reasonably avoidable, not outweighed by benefits: the FTC §5(n) test) and "abusive" acts (material interference with understanding the terms, or taking unreasonable advantage of a consumer's lack of understanding, inability to protect their interests, or reasonable reliance: the CFPB UDAAP test). **Only the AG** can enforce the unfair and abusive prongs. The private action (§349(h)) still covers deception only. Enacted as ch. 708 of 2025. The section text was last revised 2026-04-03, which suggests a chapter amendment.
- **Consumer-oriented requirement:** commentators disagree. Some say the Act removes it (Arnold & Porter). DLA Piper cites the Governor's approval memo, which says the chapter amendment keeps existing "consumer-oriented" case law. The Act does extend AG protection to small businesses and nonprofits (confirm before use against the chapter-amendment text).
- **Applies to a product when:** marketing copy, paywalls, onboarding, app-store listings or emails reach NY consumers. Standard form terms and mass-market UI flows are consumer-oriented almost by definition. Dark patterns in cancellation or consent are now pleadable by the AG as "abusive" without having to prove deception.
- **Numbers / deadlines:** §349(h) gives actual damages or \$50, whichever is greater. The court may treble up to \$1,000 for a willful or knowing violation, plus attorney's fees. Class actions for statutory minimums are limited by CPLR 901(b) in state court, but *Shady Grove* (U.S. 2010) lets them proceed in federal court. The limitations period is 3 years (CPLR 214(2); *Gaidon v. Guardian Life* (N.Y. 2001)). Before suing, the AG gives 10 days' notice by certified mail (§349(c)).
- **Defense:** §349(d), still in place after the FAIR Act: a complete defense that the act or practice is subject to and complies with the rules and regulations of, and statutes administered by, the FTC or another federal agency, as they interpret them.
- **Open questions for counsel:** How far "abusive" reaches choice architecture such as defaults, pre-checked boxes and confirmshaming. Whether a B2B SaaS (for example a cannabis-ops or POS platform) is now exposed to AG "unfair" claims brought on behalf of small-business customers.
- **Traps:** A disclaimer in the ToS does not cure a misleading net impression. Out-of-state sellers are still covered when the deception occurs in NY (*Goshen v. Mutual Life* (N.Y. 2002): the transaction must occur in NY).

## NY GBL §350 / §350-a: false advertising
- **Rule:** §350 prohibits false advertising in the conduct of business. §350-a defines "false advertising" as advertising that is misleading in a material respect, and counts the omission of material facts. Courts use the same reasonable-consumer standard as §349.
- **Numbers:** §350-e gives actual damages or \$500, whichever is greater. The court may treble up to \$10,000 for a willful or knowing violation, plus fees. Civil penalties under §350-d: up to \$5,000 per violation (up to \$15,000 or treble restitution for §349 violations during a declared abnormal market disruption).
- **Traps:** Some courts require reliance under §350 but not under §349 (confirm before use). Plaintiffs therefore plead both, and the \$500 floor makes §350 the more expensive claim. Unsubstantiated efficacy claims ("clinically proven", "improves focus") are both §350 exposure and FTC §5 exposure.

## NY AG: Executive Law §63(12)
- **Rule:** The AG may seek an injunction, restitution and damages (and, where applicable, cancellation of a certificate) for "repeated fraudulent or illegal acts" or "persistent fraud or illegality". "Fraud" is defined broadly and includes "unconscionable contractual provisions". A violation of any statute, including GBL §§349, 350 and 527-a or federal law, is "illegality" under the provision.
- **Traps:** The AG does not need to show intent or reliance, and repeated means more than one act. A §527-a violation carries a small per-violation penalty on its own, but becomes a §63(12) restitution action. Most AG subscription matters end in an Assurance of Discontinuance.

## NY automatic renewal law: GBL §§527, 527-a (amended by the FY2026 budget, Part W, signed 2025-05-09; in effect 2025-11-05)
- **Scope (§527):** an "automatic renewal" (a paid term that renews for a further term) or "continuous service" (runs until cancelled), sold to a consumer for personal, family or household purposes. "Clear and conspicuous" means larger type, or contrasting type, font or color, or set off by symbols.
- **Before the charge:** disclose clearly and conspicuously, before requesting consent: that the plan continues until cancelled, the cancellation policy and deadline, each recurring charge, the charge frequency, the length of the term, and, for a free trial or promotional price, how and when the price changes. Get **affirmative consent** to the renewal terms before the first charge, including for discounted or promotional terms.
- **After the charge:** send an **acknowledgment** the consumer can retain, containing the terms, charges, frequency, cancellation deadline and cancellation mechanism.
- **Cancellation (§527-a(1)(d), (d-1), (e)):** a simple mechanism, "as easy to use as" the one used to consent, offered through the same medium (§527-a(1)(d)) and through every medium the business accepts consent in. Where consent was given in person, also offer online or phone cancellation (§527-a(1)(d-1)). No obstruction or unreasonable conditions, such as hanging up or misstating how to cancel (§527-a(1)(e)). On a cancel request the business **may** present "a discounted offer, retention benefit or information regarding the effect of cancellation" (§527-a(1)(e)(ii)). The statute does not say "one", but keep it short; a maze of offers is exactly what regulators treat as obstruction.
- **Price increases (§527-a(1)(b-1)):** either obtain affirmative consent to the new price, or allow cancellation for at least 14 days after the first increased charge and refund it.
- **Material-change notice (§527-a(1)(g)):** at least 5 business days and no more than 30 days before the change, sent by the method the consumer selected.
- **Annual reminder (§527-a(1)(f)):** for a term of 1 year or longer, notify 15–45 days before the cancellation deadline.
- **Free-to-paid trials (§527-a(1)(h)):** notice 3–21 days before the cancellation deadline for the first paid period, with cancellation instructions. The trigger is a free gift or trial "for a period of more than a month".
- **Penalties (§527-a(3)):** not more than \$100 for a single violation and \$500 for multiple violations; if knowing, \$500 and \$1,000. Plus injunction and restitution. These amounts are small; the real exposure is §349, §63(12) and class claims.
- **Traps:** A 7-day trial on an annual plan still needs pre-charge disclosure and consent. The 3–21 day trial notice applies only past the trial-length threshold. "Same medium" cancellation for an in-app signup means in-app, not "email support".

## Federal: ROSCA and the FTC Negative Option Rule
- **ROSCA (15 U.S.C. §8401 et seq.; §8403):** for any online negative-option sale: (1) clearly and conspicuously disclose all material terms **before obtaining billing information**; (2) get **express informed consent** before charging; (3) provide **simple mechanisms** to stop recurring charges. A violation is treated as a violation of an FTC trade regulation rule, so it carries civil penalties, currently up to \$53,088 per violation (the 2025 inflation level; OMB M-26-11 cancelled the 2026 adjustment).
- **Click-to-Cancel Rule (16 CFR Part 425 as amended in 2024), Status (verified 2026-09-28):** vacated in full by the 8th Circuit on 2025-07-08 in a per curiam opinion (*Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC*, No. 24-3137), days before the July 14, 2025 compliance date, for skipping the preliminary regulatory analysis that Magnuson-Moss requires. On 2026-03-11 the FTC started over with an **ANPRM** (comments closed 2026-04-13), including whether to revive parts of click-to-cancel. No proposed rule has issued. The 1973 Prenotification Negative Option Rule remains in force for its narrow scope.
- **Enforcement continues under ROSCA and §5.** *FTC v. Amazon* (Prime) settled 2025-09-25 for \$2.5B: a \$1B civil penalty plus \$1.5B in redress. The theory was non-consensual enrollment through UI design and an obstructive cancellation flow. Other examples: Care.com (2024, \$8.5M).
- **Traps:** Clients hear "click-to-cancel was struck down" and assume the rule no longer applies. ROSCA's "simple mechanism" requirement plus the NY and CA statutes impose most of the same duties.

## Other states: California ARL (Bus. & Prof. Code §17600 et seq.)
- **Why it matters:** apps sell nationally, and California is the strictest broadly-applicable ARL, so building to it usually covers the rest.
- **AB 2863 (applies to contracts entered, amended or extended on or after 2025-07-01):** express affirmative consent to the renewal terms, **separate** from other terms. Retain proof of consent for 3 years or 1 year after termination, whichever is longer. Send an **annual reminder** for every ARL plan. Give notice of material changes. Online cancellation must be immediate ("click to cancel"), with limits on save offers (confirm before use: the save-offer conditions in §17602). Free-to-paid conversions are expressly covered.
- **Remedy:** unsolicited goods (§17603: product sent without consent is a gift), enforced through the UCL (§17200) and by public prosecutors. Plaintiffs' firms are active.
- **Others:** Colorado, Virginia, Minnesota and others have ARLs. Treat NY and CA as the design floor and have counsel run a 50-state check for any new pricing model.

## App-store subscriptions: who owes which disclosures
- **What Apple and Google provide:** billing, the payment sheet, the receipt, renewal processing, and cancellation through the OS subscription settings. Apple Guideline 3.1.2 requires the app itself to show the subscription title, length, price (and per-unit price where relevant), and working links to the privacy policy and Terms of Use. It also requires a period of at least 7 days and ongoing value.
- **What the developer still owns:** the paywall is the "offer". The developer controls the disclosure order, the prominence of renewal terms, trial-to-paid wording, and in-app marketing. NY and CA duties attach to the business making the offer, and the store's payment sheet does not relieve the developer of them.
- **Open questions for counsel:** (1) Whether the store's cancellation path satisfies "same medium, as easy as" (probably yes for in-app signups, but no case on point). (2) Who sends the NY 15–45 day annual reminder and the trial notice when the developer has no email address. (3) Whether the store's price-increase consent flow satisfies §527-a(1)(b-1) (confirm before use: current Apple and Google mechanics).
- **Web checkout / link-outs:** after *Epic v. Apple* (contempt affirmed, 9th Cir. 2025-12-11), US apps can link out to web checkout. The 9th Circuit **vacated the outright zero-commission ban** and remanded, so some commission on linked purchases may return. A web or Stripe funnel makes the developer the **sole** party responsible for every ARL, ROSCA and §527-a duty: consent capture, acknowledgment email, reminders, online cancellation. The Supreme Court **granted cert** on 2026-06-30 (*Apple Inc. v. Epic Games*, No. 25-1311: contempt for violating an injunction's "spirit", and scope under *Trump v. CASA*); merits briefing is under way. Re-check before advising.
- **Trap:** a subscriber who signs up through Apple and later manages the account on the web ends up with two cancellation paths. The web UI must not claim it can cancel a store-billed subscription it cannot reach.

## NY refund-policy posting: GBL §218-a
- **Rule:** a retailer must conspicuously post its refund policy: whether refunds are given, cash or credit, time limits, proof-of-purchase requirements, conditions for "sale" and "as is" items, and restocking fees. Online sellers post it near the item, or by a notice or hyperlink shown **before billing information is requested**.
- **Numbers:** if no policy is posted, the buyer is entitled to a cash refund or credit, at the buyer's option, within 30 days of purchase for unused goods with proof of purchase.
- **Applies to a product when:** physical merchandise (team gear, printed products). For purely digital subscriptions it is unclear whether §218-a applies ("merchandise"), so post the policy anyway (open question).

## Endorsements, influencers, and reviews
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255; revised version published 2023-07-26):** endorsements must reflect the endorser's honest, actual experience. Any material connection (payment, free product, family, employment) must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed in the endorsement itself, not in a bio. The definition of "endorser" now covers virtual (AI) influencers. Advertisers are liable for their influencers and need a monitoring program. The Guides are guidance, not a rule; violations are pursued under §5.
- **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465; effective 2024-10-21):** a **rule**, so civil penalties apply (up to \$53,088 per violation). §465.2: fake or false reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones and reviews from people who did not use the product. (§465.3 is reserved.) §465.4: buying reviews conditioned on sentiment. §465.5: undisclosed insider reviews. §465.6: company-controlled "independent" review sites. §465.7: review suppression through threats or false claims that a review is defamatory. §465.8: selling or buying fake social-media indicators. **Status (verified 2026-09-28):** the first enforcement step was 10 warning letters on 2025-12-22. The first complaints alleging Rule violations followed in May 2026 (e.g., Premium Home Service: employee-written reviews and discounts for five-star reviews).
- **NY synthetic performers (GBL §396-b, effective 2026-06-09):** a visual or audiovisual ad shown to a NY audience that includes an AI-generated human likeness that is not a real person needs a conspicuous disclosure. Penalties: \$1,000 for a first violation, \$5,000 for each later one. Exceptions include expressive works and translation-only uses.
- **Applies to a product when:** a youth-sports or NIL platform pays athletes or parents to post, a clinic or app uses patient testimonials, or an app gates its review prompt on satisfaction.
- **Traps:** Asking only happy users to review ("review gating") risks §465.7 and §5 exposure. A "results not typical" disclaimer does not cure an atypical testimonial; the ad must show generally expected results. Endorsements by minors raise both FTC issues and NIL/publicity-consent issues (see the NIL and privacy references).

## Guarantees, "risk-free," and money-back claims
- **Rule:** 16 CFR Part 239 (Guides for Advertising Warranties and Guarantees). §239.2 requires that an ad mentioning a guarantee disclose that the full terms are available before purchase. §239.3: "satisfaction guaranteed" or "money back" means a full refund at the buyer's option; any condition (time window, return shipping, restocking fee, "first-time customers only") must be disclosed in the ad. §239.5: an advertiser that does not honor a guarantee commits a §5 violation.
- **Traps:** "Risk-free trial" on an auto-renew plan that charges on day 8 invites both deception and ROSCA claims. "Cancel anytime" is false if cancellation takes effect only at the end of the term with no refund, unless that is disclosed. A money-back promise that routes to App Store refunds, which the developer cannot grant, is a misrepresentation.

## Calls and texts: TCPA (47 U.S.C. §227) and NY GBL §399-z
- **Federal rule:** marketing calls and texts sent with an autodialer or artificial/prerecorded voice need **prior express written consent**. Informational messages need prior express consent. Statutory damages are \$500 per violation, trebled to \$1,500 if willful or knowing (§227(b)(3)); the same amounts apply to Do-Not-Call violations under §227(c)(5), which require more than one call in 12 months. *Facebook v. Duguid* (U.S. 2021) narrowed the definition of "autodialer", but DNC and prerecorded-voice claims survive it. *McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson* (U.S. 2025): district courts are not bound by FCC interpretations.
- **One-to-one consent, Status (verified 2026-09-28):** **vacated** by *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* (11th Cir. 2025-01-24), the business day before its effective date. The FCC deleted the language and restored the prior rule. Lead-gen consent covering multiple sellers is again permissible federally, though state law and carrier rules may still restrict it.
- **Revocation of consent, Status (verified 2026-09-28):** revocation by any reasonable means, honored within 10 business days, is in effect. The "revoke all" provision (47 CFR §64.1200(a)(10)) was delayed repeatedly and then amended on 2026-09-09. Under the amendment, a marketing opt-out revokes consent to all marketing from that caller, while an informational opt-out applies only to that category. A caller may designate one **exclusive** opt-out method (STOP-type keywords, IVR, or a website/number) if clearly disclosed. It was adopted as a Report and Order with a further NPRM (revocation timing, universal opt-out). It takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication and replaces the twice-delayed Jan. 31, 2027 date (confirm the publication and effective date before use).
- **NY GBL §399-z (last amended 2024-11-29):** "telemarketing sales call" expressly **includes text messages**. Calls are allowed only 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time. Within 30 seconds the caller must disclose its name, the seller, the purpose, whether the call is recorded, and an option to join the seller's do-not-call list. A seller-specific DNC list must be honored. Numbers on the national DNC registry for more than 31 days are off-limits. Fines are up to \$20,000 per violation, assessed by the Secretary of State after a hearing (AG role: confirm before use). No express private action; plaintiffs use §349.
- **Other states:** Florida, Oklahoma and Maryland have "mini-TCPAs" with private actions and broader autodialer definitions. Geo-fence or add consent for those states.
- **Applies to a product when:** trial reminders or marketing go by SMS, recruiting apps text athletes or parents, clinics send appointment and marketing texts.
- **Traps:** Texting minors: consent must come from the subscriber of the phone number, usually a parent. Mixing a marketing message into an "informational" text turns it into telemarketing. Carrier (CTIA/10DLC) rules apply on top of the law.

## Health and physician advertising (pointer only)
- Health claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence under FTC §5; for health products the FTC expects randomized controlled trials. Use the FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (2022). Physician and clinic ads in NY also fall under Education Law professional-misconduct rules, and cannabis ads fall under OCM rules. Route to the health-privacy and cannabis references and to licensed regulatory counsel. For an ADHD or mood app, "treats" or "diagnoses" claims also trigger FDA device questions.

## Where this stops
- How far the FAIR Act's "abusive" prong reaches a specific UI flow, and whether consumer-oriented case law survives in AG actions, is a judgment call for counsel. No appellate court has ruled yet.
- Whether a store-mediated subscription's reminders and cancellation satisfy §527-a and the CA ARL needs counsel's risk opinion, not a lookup.
- Any substantiation file for efficacy, health, or earnings claims needs attorney review before launch.
- TCPA consent language for texts to minors or for lead-gen, and any response to an AG subpoena or demand letter, go to a licensed NY attorney immediately.
