# Torts — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: intentional torts, negligence, strict/products liability, defamation, privacy, nuisance, vicarious liability, economic torts, NY tort damages and limitations.
Scope: MBE/MEE rule first (Restatement/majority); NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified by web search: *Flanders v. Goodfellow* (N.Y. 2025) overruling *Bard*; Grieving Families Act vetoed 4th time Dec 2025 (EPTL 5-4.1 unchanged). Other doctrine from model knowledge; numbers marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Intentional torts
- **Intent:** purpose OR knowledge to substantial certainty. Transferred intent works among assault, battery, false imprisonment, trespass to land, trespass to chattels (not conversion, not IIED). Children and incompetents can form intent.
- **Battery:** intentional harmful or offensive contact with plaintiff's person (incl. things attached). No awareness needed at the time. **Assault:** intent + reasonable apprehension of *imminent* contact; words alone usually insufficient; conditional/future threats fail; plaintiff must be aware.
- **False imprisonment:** intentional confinement in bounded area, no reasonable known means of escape, plaintiff aware OR harmed. Moral pressure / future threats insufficient. Shopkeeper's privilege: reasonable belief, reasonable manner, reasonable time, on/near premises. **NY:** GBL 218 codifies shopkeeper defense (confirm before use).
- **IIED:** extreme and outrageous conduct, intent or recklessness, causing severe distress (no physical injury needed). Third parties: present + close relative + defendant knows presence (or present non-relative with bodily harm). Heightened for common carriers/innkeepers (mere insults). **NY:** Court of Appeals has never sustained an IIED claim on the merits (*Howell v. New York Post Co.* (N.Y. 1993)); disfavored where conduct falls within another tort (e.g., defamation).
- **Trespass to land:** intentional physical entry (intent to enter, not to trespass; mistake no defense). Nominal damages. **Chattels:** intermeddling → actual damages; **conversion:** serious interference → full value (forced sale). Good-faith purchaser of stolen goods can be liable for conversion.
- **Defenses:** consent (express/implied; exceeded scope; fraud as to essential matter voids; incapacity); self-defense (reasonable belief, proportional; retreat not required in tort majority); defense of others (reasonable belief majority); defense of property (no deadly force; no spring guns — *Katko v. Briney* (Iowa 1971)); recapture of chattels in hot pursuit; necessity (public = complete; private = must pay actual damages, *Vincent v. Lake Erie* (Minn. 1910); owner can't expel); arrest privilege; discipline.

## Negligence — duty
- **Rule:** duty of reasonable care to foreseeable plaintiffs. *Palsgraf v. Long Island R.R.* (N.Y. 1928): Cardozo zone-of-danger/foreseeable-plaintiff (majority, MBE); Andrews (dissent) — duty to world, limit by proximate cause.
- **Rescuers:** foreseeable; "danger invites rescue" — *Wagner v. International Ry.* (N.Y. 1921) (Cardozo). Rescuer's own negligence goes to comparative fault. Firefighter's rule bars professional rescuers (majority). **NY:** abolished by GOL 11-106 (1996) for suits against non-employer third parties; police/fire still barred against own employer/co-employees (confirm before use of scope).
- **No duty to rescue** absent: special relationship (carrier–passenger, innkeeper–guest, employer–employee, school–student, custodian–ward, business–invitee), defendant created peril, voluntary undertaking (can't leave worse off), statute. Duty to control third persons: parent (known dangerous propensity + ability), employer, custodian. *Tarasoff* (Cal. 1976) — therapist duty to warn identifiable victim.
- **NY duty limits:** Court of Appeals controls duty as policy to avoid limitless liability — *Strauss v. Belle Realty* (N.Y. 1985) (utility owes no duty to tenant of building it had no contract with in blackout); *532 Madison Ave. Gourmet Foods v. Finlandia Center* (N.Y. 2001) (no recovery for purely economic loss absent personal injury/property damage, even when foreseeable). Contractor to third parties: *Espinal v. Melville Snow Contractors* (N.Y. 2002) — duty only if (1) launched force or instrument of harm, (2) plaintiff detrimentally relied on continued performance, or (3) contractor entirely displaced owner's duty.
- **NY municipal special duty:** governmental function → no liability unless special duty: *Cuffy v. City of New York* (N.Y. 1987) — (1) assumption of affirmative duty by promises/actions, (2) municipality's knowledge inaction could lead to harm, (3) direct contact, (4) justifiable reliance. Even then, discretionary acts immune (*McLean v. City of New York* (N.Y. 2009); *Valdez v. City of New York* (N.Y. 2011)). Notice of claim GML 50-e within 90 days; sue within 1 year 90 days (GML 50-i). Prior written notice laws for sidewalk defects.
- **Standard of care:** objective reasonable person; physical disabilities considered, mental not; children — like child of age/experience/intelligence, but adult standard for adult activities (motor vehicles); very young children (<5 majority) incapable. Professionals — skill of member in good standing; medical: national standard (majority) for specialists. Informed consent: majority patient-centered (material risk) / minority physician standard. **NY:** PHL 2805-d — informed consent by reasonable-practitioner disclosure standard plus objective causation (reasonable person would have declined) (confirm before use).
- **Landowners (majority trichotomy):** trespassers — undiscovered: none; discovered/anticipated: warn/make safe known concealed artificial conditions posing death/serious harm; child trespassers — attractive nuisance (Rest. §339: knows children likely, condition likely to cause harm, children don't appreciate risk, burden small vs risk). Licensees: warn of known concealed dangers. Invitees: inspect + make safe. Minority (CA): unitary standard.
- **NY:** *Basso v. Miller* (N.Y. 1976) abolished trespasser/licensee/invitee — reasonable care in maintaining property in view of all circumstances; status bears only on foreseeability. Recreational-use immunity GOL 9-103 (willful/malicious exception, fee exception). Out-of-possession landlord liable only if contractually obligated to repair or retained control; statutory duties MDL 78 for multiple dwellings.
- **Negligence per se:** statute with criminal/civil standard; plaintiff within protected class; harm of the type statute aimed at. Effect: majority conclusively establishes duty+breach; excuse (compliance more dangerous, emergency, incapacity). **NY:** violation of a *statute* = negligence per se (*Martin v. Herzog* (N.Y. 1920)); violation of an *ordinance or regulation* = only some evidence of negligence (*Elliott v. City of New York* (N.Y. 2001)). VTL violations are per se negligence.

## Breach, causation, damages
- **Hand formula:** B < PL (*United States v. Carroll Towing* (2d Cir. 1947)). Custom is evidence, not conclusive (*The T.J. Hooper* (2d Cir. 1932)) — except medical custom sets standard.
- **Res ipsa loquitur:** event ordinarily doesn't occur absent negligence; attributable to defendant (exclusive control historically); plaintiff not responsible. Effect: permissive inference, survives directed verdict. **NY:** *Kambat v. St. Francis Hosp.* (N.Y. 1997) (sponge left in patient); inference only, rarely supports summary judgment for plaintiff (*Morejon v. Rais Constr.* (N.Y. 2006) — only when inference "inescapable").
- **Actual cause:** but-for; substantial factor for merged fires; *Summers v. Tice* (Cal. 1948) alternative liability — burden shifts to each negligent defendant; market share — *Hymowitz v. Eli Lilly* (N.Y. 1989) (NY adopts national market share for DES, several only, no exculpation by proving didn't cause plaintiff's injury). Loss of chance: minority recognizes (med mal).
- **Proximate cause:** foreseeability of the *type* of harm, not extent or manner. Eggshell plaintiff. Intervening forces: foreseeable (negligent rescuers, medical malpractice, subsequent disease, reaction forces) don't cut off; unforeseeable intentional crimes/acts of God with unforeseeable results do. *Petition of Kinsman Transit Co.* (2d Cir. 1964) (Kinsman I: liability where type of harm foreseeable though manner unusual); *Kinsman II* (2d Cir. 1968) (economic losses too remote). **NY:** *Derdiarian v. Felix Contracting* (N.Y. 1980) — intervening act a superseding cause only if extraordinary / far removed / independent; usually jury question.
- **Damages:** actual injury required (no nominal); property damage — cost of repair or diminution; mitigation; punitives need willful/wanton. Collateral source rule (majority): benefits from independent sources don't reduce award.
- **NY:** CPLR 4545 — court reduces economic damages (medical, lost earnings etc.) by collateral sources that replaced them with reasonable certainty (not life insurance; not sources with a statutory lien/subrogation right) (confirm scope before use). Itemized verdicts and structured judgments for future damages over threshold (CPLR Art. 50-A/50-B; threshold confirm before use).

## Emotional distress & special duties
- **NIED majority:** zone of danger (physical impact or threat + physical manifestation traditionally); bystander — *Dillon v. Legg* (Cal. 1968) / *Thing v. La Chusa* (Cal. 1989): close relative, present at scene, contemporaneously perceived injury.
- **NY:** rejects *Dillon*. Bystander recovery only under zone of danger: *Bovsun v. Sanperi* (N.Y. 1984) — plaintiff (1) was in zone of danger (threatened with bodily harm by defendant's negligence), (2) contemporaneously observed serious injury/death of (3) immediate family member. *Greene v. Esplanade Venture Partnership* (N.Y. 2021) — grandchild qualifies as immediate family. Direct NIED without impact allowed with guarantee of genuineness (mishandling corpse, false death notice, breach of special duty). No statutory expansion of zone of danger confirmed as of Sept 2026 (confirm before use).

## Comparative fault & multiple defendants
- **Contributory negligence:** complete bar (AL, MD, NC, VA, DC); last clear chance exception. **Pure comparative:** reduce by plaintiff's %. **Modified:** bar at ≥50% or >50%. MBE: assume pure comparative unless told otherwise.
- **Assumption of risk:** express (enforceable unless public interest/gross negligence/intentional) and implied (knew + voluntarily encountered); comparative jurisdictions merge implied secondary into fault.
- **NY:** CPLR 1411 — **pure comparative**; culpable conduct includes contributory negligence *and assumption of risk* (diminishes, does not bar). Primary assumption of risk survives only for sporting/recreational activities (as a no-duty rule): *Morgan v. State* (N.Y. 1997); *Trupia v. Lake George Cent. Sch. Dist.* (N.Y. 2010) (not horseplay); *Custodi v. Town of Amherst* (N.Y. 2012) (not rollerblading on residential street). Seatbelt nonuse: mitigation of damages only (*Spier v. Barker* (N.Y. 1974); VTL 1229-c(8)). Plaintiff's serious criminal conduct can bar recovery (*Barker v. Kallash* (N.Y. 1984)).
- **Joint and several (majority traditional)** for indivisible injury; many states several-only. Contribution by comparative fault; indemnity (vicarious, retailer vs manufacturer, contractual).
- **NY CPLR Art. 16:** CPLR 1601 — defendant ≤50% at fault is liable for **non-economic** damages only to its share; economic damages stay joint and several. CPLR 1602 exceptions (defendant fully J&S): e.g., motor vehicle use/operation, intentional acts, reckless disregard, Labor Law Art. 10 (240/241), release of hazardous substances, products liability where manufacturer not joinable, nondelegable duties/vicarious liability, acting in concert (confirm full list before use). Culpability of an employer immune under WCL §11 not counted unless "grave injury".
- **NY contribution:** CPLR 1401 (among persons liable for same injury), CPLR 1402 (by relative culpability). **GOL 15-108:** release of one tortfeasor reduces claim against others by the **greatest** of (i) amount stipulated, (ii) amount paid, or (iii) released tortfeasor's equitable share; settling tortfeasor is relieved of contribution liability and cannot itself seek contribution.
- **NY workers' comp:** WCL §11 — employer third-party contribution/indemnity barred unless "grave injury" (enumerated list: death, loss of limb, total deafness, permanent total disability from brain injury, etc.) or written indemnity contract.

## NY limitations periods
- CPLR 214(4)/(5): **3 years** property damage / personal injury (negligence, strict products).
- CPLR 214(6): nonmedical professional malpractice 3 years regardless of theory.
- CPLR 215(3): **1 year** — assault, battery, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution, libel, slander, false words causing special damage, Civil Rights Law 50/51 privacy violations. CPLR 215(8): if criminal action commenced against same defendant for same event, plaintiff gets at least 1 year from termination of criminal action. CPLR 213-c: civil claims for conduct constituting enumerated sex offenses — 20 years (confirm before use).
- CPLR 214-a: **medical/dental/podiatric malpractice 2½ years** from act/omission or **last treatment in a continuous course** for same condition. Foreign object: 1 year from discovery (or facts reasonably leading to it), if later. **Lavern's Law (2018):** failure to diagnose cancer or malignant tumor — runs from when plaintiff knew or reasonably should have known of the negligence, capped at **7 years** from the act/omission. Hospital negligence not constituting med mal = 3 years; distinguish by whether conduct involves medical judgment.
- CPLR 214-c: **toxic exposure** (latent effects of substances) — 3 years from discovery of injury or when it should have been discovered; plus: 1 year from discovery of *cause* if technical/scientific knowledge unavailable, provided within 5 years of discovering injury.
- EPTL 5-4.1: **wrongful death 2 years** from death; action by personal representative for distributees; recovery = pecuniary loss only (EPTL 5-4.3) — no grief/loss-of-society (Grieving Families Act vetoed Dec 2025; new bill pending 2026 — confirm before use). Survival EPTL 11-3.2: decedent's own claims incl. conscious pain and suffering; SOL governed by decedent's claim (CPLR 210 extension).
- Municipalities: 90-day notice of claim (GML 50-e), 1 year 90 days to sue (GML 50-i); wrongful death vs municipality 2 years.

## NY Labor Law (construction)
- **Labor Law 240(1) "Scaffold Law":** owners and general contractors (and agents with authority) **absolutely liable** for elevation-related (gravity) injuries from failure to provide proper safety devices (scaffolds, ladders, hoists, ropes). Worker's comparative negligence is **no defense**. Defenses: plaintiff's conduct was the **sole proximate cause** (*Blake v. Neighborhood Hous. Servs.* (N.Y. 2003)); recalcitrant worker (refused available adequate devices); not an enumerated activity (routine maintenance ≠ repair); no elevation differential (*Runner v. N.Y. Stock Exch.* (N.Y. 2009) — falling-object/gravity-force test). Exemption: owners of **one- and two-family dwellings** who contract for work but do not direct or control it.
- **240 vs 241(6):** 241(6) — nondelegable duty to comply with **specific** Industrial Code (12 NYCRR Part 23) provisions; general safety standards insufficient (*Ross v. Curtis-Palmer Hydro-Elec.* (N.Y. 1993)); comparative negligence **is** a defense. Same 1-2 family dwelling exemption.
- **Labor Law 200:** codifies common-law duty to provide safe workplace; requires owner/contractor **supervision/control** of the injury-producing work (means/methods) or notice of dangerous premises condition.

## NY motor vehicle
- **VTL 388:** owner vicariously liable for negligence of anyone using vehicle with owner's express or implied permission (presumption of permission; rebuttable by substantial evidence). Rental/leasing companies shielded by federal **Graves Amendment** 49 U.S.C. §30106.
- **No-fault (Insurance Law Art. 51):** first-party benefits for basic economic loss (up to \$50,000); suit for non-economic loss only on **"serious injury"** (Ins. Law 5102(d)) or economic loss beyond basic (5104). Serious injury categories: death; dismemberment; significant disfigurement; fracture; loss of fetus; permanent loss of use of a body organ/member/function/system; permanent consequential limitation; significant limitation of use; medically determined non-permanent injury preventing substantially all usual activities for **90 of the 180 days** after the accident. Threshold is decided on summary judgment with objective medical proof (*Toure v. Avis* (N.Y. 2002)).

## Strict liability
- **Abnormally dangerous activities** (Rest. 2d §520 factors / Rest. 3d): high risk even with reasonable care, not common usage — blasting, toxic storage, crop dusting. Liability limited to harms from the abnormal risk; plaintiff's knowing assumption or (in comparative states) fault reduces. **NY:** blasting strict liability — *Spano v. Perini Corp.* (N.Y. 1969).
- **Animals:** wild animals strict; domestic animals strict if owner knew/should have known of dangerous propensity ("one bite" rule); trespassing livestock strict. **NY:** *Flanders v. Goodfellow* (N.Y. Apr. 17, 2025) overruled *Bard v. Jahnke* (N.Y. 2006) — plaintiffs may now plead **ordinary negligence** against owners of domestic animals in addition to strict liability for known vicious propensities. Earlier carve-outs: *Hastings v. Sauve* (N.Y. 2013) (farm animals straying onto road); *Hewitt v. Palmer Veterinary Clinic* (N.Y. 2020) (veterinarian).

## Products liability
- **Defendants:** commercial suppliers in chain (manufacturer, distributor, retailer); not casual sellers; services generally not.
- **Defects:** (1) manufacturing — departs from intended design (strict); (2) design — majority/Rest. 3d risk-utility with **reasonable alternative design**; minority consumer-expectations (CA uses both, *Barker v. Lull*); (3) warning — foreseeable risks, not obvious; learned-intermediary for Rx drugs.
- **Theories:** strict liability (Rest. 2d §402A — defect unreasonably dangerous when left defendant's control, unchanged); negligence (privity abolished, *MacPherson v. Buick* (N.Y. 1916)); warranty (UCC 2-314 merchantability, 2-315 fitness, express 2-313; privity per 2-318 alternatives; notice of breach within reasonable time 2-607(3); 4-year SOL from tender, UCC 2-725; disclaimers possible).
- **Defenses:** comparative fault (misuse unforeseeable; substantial alteration); state of the art relevant to design/warning; assumption of risk; pure economic loss → contract (*East River S.S.* (U.S. 1986)). Rest. 3d: misuse is not a freestanding defense — foreseeable misuse must be designed/warned against and is apportioned as comparative fault; only unforeseeable misuse can negate defect or causation.
- **NY:** *Codling v. Paglia* (N.Y. 1973) (strict liability to bystanders); *Voss v. Black & Decker* (N.Y. 1983) (risk-utility design test; feasible alternative); *Denny v. Ford Motor Co.* (N.Y. 1995) — strict products design (risk-utility) and implied warranty (consumer expectation) are distinct; jury may find warranty breach without design defect; *Robinson v. Reed-Prentice* (N.Y. 1980) (substantial post-sale modification by third party defeats design claim); *Liriano v. Hobart Corp.* (N.Y. 1998) (failure-to-warn claim may survive substantial modification); post-sale duty to warn — *Cover v. Cohen* (N.Y. 1984). Successor liability limited: *Schumacher v. Richards Shear* (N.Y. 1983) (four exceptions). Strict products SOL 3 years from injury (CPLR 214), warranty 4 years from tender.

## Defamation
- **Elements (CL):** defamatory statement of fact of and concerning plaintiff; publication to third party; damage (libel presumed; slander requires special damages unless per se: crime of moral turpitude, loathsome disease, business/profession, unchastity of woman / serious sexual misconduct). Opinion protected only if not implying false facts (*Milkovich v. Lorain Journal* (U.S. 1990)). Republisher liable; §230 immunizes interactive computer services for third-party content.
- **Constitutional overlay:** public official/figure + any matter → actual malice (knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard; subjective, clear and convincing) — *New York Times v. Sullivan* (U.S. 1964); *Curtis Publishing v. Butts* (U.S. 1967) (public figures). Private figure + public concern → at least negligence as to falsity; presumed/punitive damages require actual malice — *Gertz v. Robert Welch* (U.S. 1974). Private figure + private concern → CL presumed damages OK (*Dun & Bradstreet v. Greenmoss* (U.S. 1985)). Plaintiff bears falsity burden on public concern (*Philadelphia Newspapers v. Hepps* (U.S. 1986)). Limited-purpose public figure: voluntarily injected into public controversy.
- **Privileges:** absolute (judicial proceedings, legislative, spouses, compelled broadcast); qualified (common interest, public interest, fair report) — lost by abuse (actual malice/excess publication).
- **NY:** private figure on matter of **arguably legitimate public concern** must prove publisher acted in a **grossly irresponsible manner** — *Chapadeau v. Utica Observer-Dispatch* (N.Y. 1975) (higher than Gertz negligence floor). Slander per se: crime, loathsome disease, trade/profession, **serious sexual misconduct** (*Liberman v. Gelstein* (N.Y. 1992)); imputations of homosexuality no longer defamatory per se (*Yonaty v. Mincolla* (3d Dep't 2012)). NY opinion test under state constitution broader — *Immuno AG v. Moor-Jankowski* (N.Y. 1991); *Steinhilber v. Alphonse* (N.Y. 1986). Fair report privilege: Civil Rights Law 74 (fair and true report of judicial/official proceeding — absolute). **Anti-SLAPP** (Civil Rights Law 70-a, 76-a, amended 2020): covers communications in public forum / on issue of public interest; actual malice required; mandatory costs and fees for prevailing defendant (confirm scope before use). SOL 1 year (CPLR 215(3)); single-publication rule incl. internet.

## Privacy
- **Majority four torts (Rest. §652):** appropriation of name/likeness for commercial advantage; intrusion upon seclusion (highly offensive to reasonable person; private matter); public disclosure of private facts (not newsworthy); false light (highly offensive; actual malice if public concern). Truth is a defense only to false light; personal rights — don't survive or extend to entities (except appropriation).
- **NY:** **no common-law right of privacy** — *Roberson v. Rochester Folding Box* (N.Y. 1902); *Howell v. New York Post* (N.Y. 1993). Only **Civil Rights Law 50 (misdemeanor) / 51 (civil)**: use of name, portrait, picture, or voice of **living** person for **advertising or trade purposes** without **written consent** → injunction + damages (exemplary if knowing). Newsworthiness exception broad (*Messenger v. Gruner + Jahr* (N.Y. 2000)); incidental use exception. Post-mortem right of publicity for deceased performers/personalities: Civil Rights Law 50-f (2021) (confirm before use). No NY false-light, intrusion, or private-facts tort. Unlawful dissemination of intimate images: Civil Rights Law 52-b.

## Nuisance
- **Private:** substantial and unreasonable interference with use/enjoyment of land (ordinary sensibilities); coming to the nuisance a factor, not bar. **Public:** unreasonable interference with right common to public; private plaintiff needs special injury different in kind. Remedies: damages, injunction (balance hardships — *Boomer v. Atlantic Cement* (N.Y. 1970): permanent damages in lieu of injunction). **NY:** private nuisance may rest on intentional, negligent, or abnormally dangerous conduct — *Copart Indus. v. Consolidated Edison* (N.Y. 1977).

## Vicarious liability
- **Respondeat superior:** employee within scope (frolic vs detour); intentional torts if in furtherance/natural incident. Independent contractors: no, unless nondelegable duty (inherently dangerous, public, premises open to public) or negligent hiring. Joint enterprise; bailor not liable (except by statute — NY VTL 388); parents not vicariously liable at CL (statutes cap for willful). **NY:** GOL 3-112 parental liability for willful destruction by minor 10+ (cap confirm before use). Dram shop: **GOL 11-101** — injured third party (not the intoxicated person) sues one who **unlawfully sold** alcohol (e.g., to visibly intoxicated person, ABC Law 65) — commercial sellers, not social hosts; **GOL 11-100** — anyone who knowingly unlawfully furnishes alcohol to a person **under 21** (incl. social hosts). Negligent entrustment separate.

## Economic torts
- **Misrepresentation:** intentional (scienter, intent to induce, justifiable reliance, pecuniary loss); negligent — business/professional context, to limited class of foreseeable users. **NY:** accountant liability to noncontractual parties requires near-privity — *Credit Alliance v. Arthur Andersen* (N.Y. 1985) (known purpose, known party relying, linking conduct); *Ultramares v. Touche* (N.Y. 1931).
- **Interference with contract:** valid contract, knowledge, intentional procurement of breach, damages. With prospective advantage: **NY** requires more culpable conduct — "wrongful means" (crime/independent tort) or sole purpose of harm — *Carvel Corp. v. Noonan* (N.Y. 2004).
- **Malicious prosecution:** initiation, termination in plaintiff's favor, no probable cause, malice. **NY:** favorable termination need only be not inconsistent with innocence — *Cantalino v. Danner* (N.Y. 2001) (civil); federal §1983 follows *Thompson v. Clark* (U.S. 2022). Abuse of process: improper use after issuance for collateral objective.

## Traps
- MBE comparative question without jurisdiction stated → pure comparative. NYLE → CPLR 1411 pure comparative, and implied assumption of risk only reduces.
- Economic damages always J&S in NY; CPLR 1601 limit is non-economic only, and 1602 motor-vehicle exception swallows most car cases.
- 1-year SOL traps: intentional torts and Civil Rights Law 51 (CPLR 215); IIED also 1 year.
- Labor Law 240(1): don't reduce for worker's negligence; 241(6): do.
- Dog bite in NY after 2025: negligence claim viable; pre-2025 materials saying "strict liability only" are stale.
