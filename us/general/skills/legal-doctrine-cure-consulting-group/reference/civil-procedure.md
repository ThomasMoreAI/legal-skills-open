# Civil Procedure — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: jurisdiction (SMJ/PJ), venue, Erie, pleading, joinder, class actions, discovery, motions, judgments, preclusion, appeals — federal (FRCP/Title 28) or NY (CPLR, GML notice of claim).
Scope: MBE/MEE federal rule first; NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified against nysenate.gov text: CPLR 202, 205(a), 214-a, 217(1), 308(2)/(4), 503(a)/(c), 3211(e), 3212(a), 7803; GML 50-e(1)/(5), 50-i(1); NYC Civil Court limit; Berk v. Choy. Other doctrine from model knowledge; items marked (confirm before use) were not source-checked.

## Subject-matter jurisdiction (federal)
- **Diversity §1332(a):** complete diversity (*Strawbridge v. Curtiss* (U.S. 1806)) + amount in controversy **> \$75,000** exclusive of interest and costs. Measured at filing; later moves don't destroy it.
- **Citizenship:** individual = domicile (presence + intent to remain indefinitely); corporation = every state of incorporation + principal place of business (nerve center, *Hertz Corp. v. Friend* (U.S. 2010)); LLC/partnership = citizenship of **every member**; decedent/infant/incompetent representative = that of the represented person (§1332(c)(2)); class action = named representatives only.
- **Aggregation:** one plaintiff may aggregate all claims vs one defendant; multiple plaintiffs may not aggregate unless common undivided interest. Legal-certainty test (*St. Paul Mercury* (U.S. 1938)). Counterclaim amounts don't count for original jurisdiction.
- **CAFA §1332(d):** > \$5,000,000 aggregate, minimal diversity, ≥100 class members; home-state/local-controversy exceptions; removable by any defendant without unanimity, no 1-year limit, forum-defendant rule inapplicable (§1453(b)).
- **Federal question §1331:** well-pleaded complaint (*Louisville & Nashville R.R. v. Mottley* (U.S. 1908)) — anticipated federal defense insufficient. State claim with embedded federal issue only if necessarily raised, actually disputed, substantial, and capable of resolution without disrupting balance (*Grable* (U.S. 2005); *Gunn v. Minton* (U.S. 2013)).
- **Supplemental §1367:** (a) same case or controversy = common nucleus of operative fact (*Gibbs* (U.S. 1966)). (b) In diversity-only cases, no supplemental jurisdiction over claims **by plaintiffs** against persons joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, 24, or claims by Rule 19/24 plaintiffs, when inconsistent with §1332 (codifies *Owen Equipment v. Kroger* (U.S. 1978)). LLC citizenship rule: *Carden v. Arkoma* (U.S. 1990) (all members of unincorporated associations). *Exxon Mobil v. Allapattah* (U.S. 2005): one plaintiff meeting amount lets co-plaintiffs (Rule 20) with smaller claims in — but complete diversity still required. (c) Discretionary decline: novel state issue, state claim predominates, all original claims dismissed, exceptional circumstances. §1367(d): limitations tolled while pending + 30 days (*Artis v. D.C.* (U.S. 2018) — stop-the-clock).
- **Traps:** SMJ never waived, raised any time incl. first time on appeal (Rule 12(h)(3)); parties can't consent to it. Defendant's counterclaim: compulsory counterclaim fits §1367(a); permissive needs independent basis. Impleader claim by defendant (Rule 14) is fine under §1367(b) — only claims *by plaintiffs* are barred.

## Removal and remand
- **§1441(a):** defendant removes any action within original jurisdiction to the district embracing the state court. Only defendants (not plaintiff-counterclaim-defendants, not third-party counterclaim defendants — *Home Depot v. Jackson* (U.S. 2019)).
- **Forum-defendant rule §1441(b)(2):** diversity case not removable if any **properly joined and served** defendant is a forum citizen. Procedural, waivable.
- **Timing §1446(b):** 30 days after receipt of initial pleading (formal service required — *Murphy Bros. v. Michetti* (U.S. 1999)); or 30 days after paper showing case became removable; diversity cases not removable > **1 year** after commencement unless plaintiff acted in bad faith (§1446(c)(1)). All served defendants must join/consent (§1446(b)(2)(A)).
- **Remand §1447(c):** procedural defects — motion within 30 days of removal notice; lack of SMJ any time. Remand orders generally unappealable (§1447(d)) except §1442/§1443 removals (then whole order reviewable — *BP v. Mayor of Baltimore* (U.S. 2021)) and CAFA (§1453(c)).
- **Post-removal amendment:** if plaintiff amends to drop all federal claims, court loses supplemental jurisdiction and must remand (*Royal Canin U.S.A. v. Wullschleger* (U.S. 2025)). Post-removal joinder of non-diverse defendant: deny or permit and remand (§1447(e)).

## Personal jurisdiction (constitutional)
- **Rule 4(k)(1)(A):** federal court borrows forum state's long-arm reach. 4(k)(1)(B): 100-mile bulge for Rule 14/19 parties. 4(k)(2): federal claim + defendant not subject to any state's general jurisdiction → national contacts.
- **Traditional bases:** in-state tag service (*Burnham* (U.S. 1990)); domicile; consent (incl. forum-selection clause, appearance); consent-by-registration statutes are consistent with due process (*Mallory v. Norfolk Southern* (U.S. 2023)) — but only if the state statute actually says so.
- **General:** "at home" — individual's domicile; corporation's state of incorporation and PPB (*Goodyear* (U.S. 2011); *Daimler AG v. Bauman* (U.S. 2014); *BNSF v. Tyrrell* (U.S. 2017)). "Substantial, continuous, systematic" business alone is not enough.
- **Specific:** (1) purposeful availment — defendant's own contacts with the forum, not plaintiff's (*World-Wide Volkswagen* (U.S. 1980); *Walden v. Fiore* (U.S. 2014)); stream of commerce split (*Asahi* (U.S. 1987): O'Connor "plus" vs Brennan awareness; *J. McIntyre v. Nicastro* (U.S. 2011) plurality); (2) claim "arises out of **or relates to**" contacts — causation not strictly required (*Ford Motor v. Montana Eighth Jud. Dist.* (U.S. 2021)); nonresident plaintiffs need their own connection (*Bristol-Myers Squibb* (U.S. 2017)); (3) fair play and substantial justice (*Burger King* (U.S. 1985) — defendant must present compelling case). Intentional tort aimed at forum: *Calder v. Jones* (U.S. 1984).
- **In rem/quasi in rem:** minimum-contacts test applies to all (*Shaffer v. Heitner* (U.S. 1977)).
- **Federal statutes:** Fifth Amendment due process does not import state-by-state minimum-contacts limits (*Fuld v. PLO* (U.S. 2025)) (confirm scope before use).

## Service (federal)
- **Rule 4(m):** serve within **90 days** after filing, else dismissal without prejudice or order; good cause = mandatory extension.
- **Rule 4(e) individuals:** state-law methods (forum state or state where served), personal delivery, leaving at dwelling/usual abode with someone of suitable age and discretion **who resides there**, or agent. Corporations 4(h): officer, managing/general agent, authorized agent.
- **Waiver 4(d):** defendant given ≥30 days to return (60 if abroad); if waived, answer due **60 days** from request sent (90 if abroad); unjustified refusal → costs of service.
- **Answer:** 21 days after service (Rule 12(a)).

## Venue and transfer (federal)
- **§1391(b):** (1) district where any defendant resides if all defendants reside in the same state; (2) where a substantial part of events/omissions occurred or property is situated; (3) fallback only if no district under (1)/(2): any district where any defendant is subject to PJ.
- **Residence:** individual = domicile; entity defendant = any district where subject to PJ for this action (§1391(c)(2)); multi-district state: district contacts treated as a state (§1391(d)); non-US-resident defendant may be sued in any district (§1391(c)(3)).
- **Transfer §1404(a):** proper venue, convenience/interest of justice, to district where action might have been brought (or all parties consent). Transferee applies transferor's choice-of-law (*Van Dusen v. Barrack* (U.S. 1964)), even when plaintiff moves (*Ferens v. John Deere* (U.S. 1990)). **§1406(a):** improper venue → dismiss or transfer; transferee's own law applies.
- **Forum-selection clause:** enforced by §1404(a) (or forum non conveniens for non-federal forum); plaintiff's choice gets no weight, private-interest factors ignored, no Van Dusen law carryover (*Atlantic Marine* (U.S. 2013)). Clause doesn't make venue "wrong" under §1406.
- **Forum non conveniens:** dismissal when alternative forum abroad/state court; less favorable law abroad not dispositive (*Piper Aircraft v. Reyno* (U.S. 1981)).

## Erie
- **Rule:** federal court in diversity (and supplemental state claims) applies state substantive law; federal procedure.
- **Framework:** (1) Is there a valid federal statute or FRCP directly on point? FRCP valid if it "really regulates procedure" (*Sibbach*; *Hanna v. Plumer* (U.S. 1965)) → apply it. *Shady Grove v. Allstate* (U.S. 2010): Rule 23 displaces **CPLR 901(b)** (no class actions for statutory penalties) in federal court. *Berk v. Choy* (U.S. 2026): Rule 8 displaces state affidavit-of-merit requirement. (2) If no direct conflict: Rules of Decision Act / "twin aims" — forum shopping + inequitable administration (*Hanna*), outcome-determinative (*Guaranty Trust v. York* (U.S. 1945)), balance federal interests (*Byrd* (U.S. 1958) — jury allocation).
- **Always state:** elements, burden of proof, statute of limitations and tolling/commencement (*Walker v. Armco* (U.S. 1980): Rule 3 doesn't govern tolling), choice-of-law rules (*Klaxon* (U.S. 1941)), state excessiveness standard applied by district court (*Gasperini* (U.S. 1996) — CPLR 5501(c) "deviates materially" applied by trial judge; appellate review abuse of discretion).
- **Preclusion of federal diversity judgment:** federal common law adopting forum state's rule (*Semtek* (U.S. 2001)).

## Pleading and motions (federal)
- **Rule 8(a):** plausibility (*Twombly* (U.S. 2007); *Iqbal* (U.S. 2009)); legal conclusions disregarded. Rule 9(b): fraud/mistake with particularity; intent generally.
- **Rule 11:** signer certifies; **21-day safe harbor** before filing sanctions motion; law firm jointly responsible.
- **Rule 12(h):** (1) PJ, venue, insufficient process, insufficient service waived if omitted from first Rule 12 motion or not in first responsive pleading/amendment as of course. (2) Failure to state claim, failure to join Rule 19 party, failure to state legal defense — preserved through trial. (3) SMJ never waived.
- **Amendment Rule 15(a):** once as of course within 21 days after serving, or if responsive pleading required, 21 days after service of responsive pleading or Rule 12(b)/(e)/(f) motion, whichever earlier; otherwise consent/leave "freely given."
- **Relation back 15(c):** (A) SOL law allows; (B) same conduct, transaction, occurrence; (C) new party: (B) + within 4(m) period party received notice so no prejudice + knew or should have known it would have been sued but for a mistake concerning proper party's identity — focus on defendant's knowledge (*Krupski v. Costa Crociere* (U.S. 2010)). "John Doe" replacement usually fails (no "mistake").
- **Voluntary dismissal 41(a)(1):** without order before answer or SJ motion; second notice dismissal of same claim = adjudication on merits.
- **Default 55:** clerk (sum certain, no appearance) vs court; appearing party gets 7 days' notice.

## Joinder and class actions (federal)
- **Counterclaims 13(a):** compulsory if same transaction/occurrence and no additional parties outside jurisdiction; lost if not asserted. 13(b) permissive; 13(g) crossclaim permissive (same transaction), then not barred later.
- **Impleader 14:** defendant may bring in one who **is or may be liable to it** for all/part of the claim (indemnity/contribution — not "it's his fault, not mine"); as of right within **14 days** after serving original answer.
- **Rule 19:** required party if (a) complete relief impossible, or absence impairs absentee's interest or risks inconsistent obligations; join if feasible; if not, 19(b) equity and good conscience factors (prejudice, shaping relief, adequacy, plaintiff's alternative forum). Joint tortfeasors are not required parties (*Temple v. Synthes* (U.S. 1990)).
- **Rule 20:** same transaction/series + common question. **Rule 24:** intervention of right (interest, impairment, inadequate representation, timely) vs permissive.
- **Interpleader:** statutory §1335 — ≥ \$500, minimal diversity among claimants, nationwide service (§2361), venue where any claimant resides (§1397), deposit required. Rule 22 — ordinary SMJ/PJ/venue.
- **Rule 23:** (a) numerosity, commonality (common answers — *Wal-Mart v. Dukes* (U.S. 2011)), typicality, adequacy; plus (b)(1) risk of inconsistent adjudications/limited fund, (b)(2) injunctive/declaratory relief for class as whole (no individualized money), or (b)(3) predominance + superiority with individual notice and opt-out (*Eisen* (U.S. 1974) — plaintiff pays notice). Settlement/dismissal of certified class needs court approval after hearing (23(e)). 23(f): petition for permission to appeal certification order within **14 days**. Rigorous analysis may overlap merits (*Comcast v. Behrend* (U.S. 2013)).

## Discovery (federal)
- **Scope 26(b)(1):** nonprivileged, relevant to any claim/defense, **proportional** to needs of case.
- **Required disclosures 26(a):** initial (within 14 days after 26(f) conference), expert (reports for retained experts, 90 days before trial default), pretrial (30 days before trial).
- **Work product 26(b)(3):** documents prepared in anticipation of litigation — discoverable on substantial need + no substantial equivalent without undue hardship; mental impressions/opinions near-absolute (*Hickman v. Taylor* (U.S. 1947)). Experts 26(b)(4): testifying experts may be deposed; drafts and most attorney-expert communications protected; consulting experts only on exceptional circumstances.
- **Limits:** 10 depositions per side, 1 day of 7 hours (30); 25 interrogatories incl. subparts (33); Rule 35 exam requires court order, condition "in controversy," good cause; Rule 36 admissions deemed admitted if no answer within 30 days. ESI loss 37(e): curative measures on prejudice; adverse inference/dismissal only on **intent to deprive**. Subpoena 45: 100-mile limit.

## Summary judgment, trial, post-trial (federal)
- **Rule 56:** no genuine dispute of material fact + entitled to judgment as a matter of law; movant without burden may point to absence of evidence (*Celotex* (U.S. 1986)); view evidence for nonmovant, heightened proof standard counts (*Anderson v. Liberty Lobby* (U.S. 1986)). Due by 30 days after close of all discovery unless otherwise set. 56(d): nonmovant needs discovery.
- **Jury 7th Am.:** legal claims tried to jury; common legal issues tried first (*Beacon Theatres* (U.S. 1959); *Dairy Queen* (U.S. 1962)); civil penalties for common-law-like fraud → jury (*SEC v. Jarkesy* (U.S. 2024)). Demand within 14 days after last pleading directed to issue (Rule 38). Jury 6–12; unanimous unless stipulated (Rule 48).
- **JMOL 50:** 50(a) before submission; renewed 50(b) within **28 days** after judgment, only if 50(a) made. New trial 59: 28 days. Remittitur (plaintiff may refuse → new trial); additur unconstitutional in federal court. **Rule 60(b):** (1) mistake/excusable neglect, (2) new evidence, (3) fraud — within 1 year; (4) void, (5) satisfied, (6) other — reasonable time. Pure legal issue rejected at SJ preserved for appeal without 50 motion (*Dupree v. Younger* (U.S. 2023)); fact-sufficiency issues need 50(b) (*Unitherm* (U.S. 2006)).

## Preclusion
- **Claim preclusion:** (1) final, valid judgment on merits; (2) same claim — transactional (Restatement (Second) of Judgments §24); (3) same parties or privity. Nonparty bound only in *Taylor v. Sturgell* (U.S. 2008) categories (agreement, substantive legal relationship, adequate representation, assumed control, proxy, special statutory scheme). Defendant must plead it; does not bar claims arising from later conduct (*Lucky Brand v. Marcel Fashions* (U.S. 2020)).
- **Issue preclusion:** same issue actually litigated and determined, essential to judgment, party against whom asserted had full and fair opportunity. Nonmutual defensive OK (*Blonder-Tongue* (U.S. 1971)); offensive discretionary — plaintiff could easily have joined? inconsistent prior judgments? procedural opportunities unavailable? small stakes in first action? (*Parklane Hosiery* (U.S. 1979)). No nonmutual offensive preclusion against the U.S. (*Mendoza* (U.S. 1984)).
- **FF&C:** state judgments get same preclusive effect in federal court as in rendering state (28 U.S.C. §1738).
- **NY:** transactional res judicata (*O'Brien v. City of Syracuse* (N.Y. 1981)); issue preclusion requires identity of issue and full and fair opportunity, mutuality abandoned (*B.R. DeWitt v. Hall* (N.Y. 1967); *Schwartz v. Public Administrator* (N.Y. 1969)). NY has **no compulsory counterclaim rule** (CPLR 3019) — but res judicata can bar a later claim that would impair rights under the first judgment.

## Appeals (federal)
- **§1291 final judgment rule.** Exceptions: collateral order (conclusively determines, important issue separate from merits, effectively unreviewable — *Cohen* (U.S. 1949); qualified-immunity denial yes; discovery/privilege orders no — *Mohawk* (U.S. 2009)); §1292(a)(1) injunction orders; §1292(b) district certification (controlling question, substantial ground for difference, material advance) + court of appeals permits within **10 days**; Rule 54(b) partial final judgment with "no just reason for delay"; Rule 23(f); mandamus.
- **Notice of appeal:** 30 days after entry (60 if U.S. party) (FRAP 4(a)); tolled by timely 50(b)/52(b)/59/60 (within 28 days) motions. Jurisdictional for statutory deadlines (*Bowles v. Russell* (U.S. 2007)).
- **Standards:** law de novo; facts clear error (Rule 52(a)(6)), even documentary; discretion abuse.

## NY: Courts and commencement
- Supreme Court = general jurisdiction trial court. NYC Civil Court up to **\$50,000** (since Jan. 1, 2022). County Court/City/District Court limits lower (confirm before use). Appellate Division (4 Depts); Court of Appeals = law questions only (except where AD reverses/modifies facts).
- **Commencement = filing** summons and complaint/summons with notice (CPLR 304); claim interposed on filing (CPLR 203(c)). Serve within **120 days** after filing (CPLR 306-b); extension for good cause or "in the interest of justice" (*Leader v. Maroney, Ponzini & Spencer* (N.Y. 2001)).
- **Pleading:** NY fact pleading ("sufficiently particular," CPLR 3013); **CPLR 3017(c):** personal injury/wrongful death complaint must **not** state amount of damages; defendant may request supplemental statement (response due 15 days). Summons with notice option (CPLR 305(b)) — then defendant demands complaint (CPLR 3012(b)).
- **Answer time CPLR 320(a)/3012:** 20 days after personal delivery in NY; 30 days after service complete if any other method (308(2), (4), out-of-state, Secretary of State).

## NY: Personal jurisdiction
- **CPLR 301 (general):** now bounded by *Daimler*. Registration to do business + designating Secretary of State for service is **not** consent to general jurisdiction (*Aybar v. Aybar* (N.Y. 2021)) — *Mallory* doesn't change this because NY's statute doesn't say registration = consent (confirm current legislative status before use).
- **CPLR 302(a) long-arm (specific; claim must arise from the act):**
  1. transacts **any business** within NY or contracts anywhere to supply goods/services in NY — one purposeful transaction can suffice, physical presence not required (*Kreutter v. McFadden Oil* (N.Y. 1988); *Deutsche Bank Sec. v. Montana Bd. of Invs.* (N.Y. 2006)); repeated use of NY correspondent account can qualify (*Licci v. Lebanese Canadian Bank* (N.Y. 2012)). Articulable nexus/substantial relationship between claim and transaction.
  2. commits tortious act **within** NY — **except defamation**.
  3. tortious act **outside** NY causing injury **in** NY — **except defamation** — if (i) regularly does/solicits business, persistent course of conduct, or derives substantial revenue from goods used/services rendered in NY, **or** (ii) expects or reasonably should expect the act to have NY consequences **and** derives substantial revenue from interstate or international commerce. Situs of injury = where original critical event occurred, not where economic loss felt (copyright online-piracy exception: *Penguin Group v. American Buddha* (N.Y. 2011)).
  4. owns, uses, or possesses real property in NY.
- **302(b):** matrimonial support/maintenance vs nonresident if NY was the marital domicile before separation, defendant abandoned plaintiff in NY, or obligation arose under NY law/agreement executed in NY — and plaintiff is NY resident/domiciliary at commencement.
- **Traps:** Defamation is carved out of (a)(2)/(a)(3) but can fit (a)(1) if the defamation arises from NY business transacted. Long-arm satisfied ≠ due process satisfied — run both. CPLR 302 is narrower than due process in places.

## NY: Service CPLR 308 (natural persons)
- **308(1):** personal delivery in NY. Complete on delivery.
- **308(2) "deliver and mail":** deliver in NY to a person of **suitable age and discretion** at actual place of business, dwelling place, or usual place of abode + mail to last known residence (or first-class to actual place of business marked "personal and confidential," no indication it's from an attorney/about a lawsuit). Delivery and mailing within **20 days of each other**; proof filed within **20 days** of the later act; service **complete 10 days after filing** proof. No due-diligence prerequisite.
- **308(3):** delivery to agent designated under CPLR 318.
- **308(4) "nail and mail":** only if 308(1) and (2) **cannot be made with due diligence**; affix to door of actual place of business, dwelling, or usual abode + mail as in (2); same 20/20/10-day timing. Due diligence is strictly applied (multiple attempts at varied times; inquiry about employment).
- **308(5):** court-ordered method when (1),(2),(4) impracticable (e.g., email) — no due-diligence showing required beyond impracticability.
- **Corporations:** CPLR 311 (officer, director, managing/general agent, cashier, authorized agent) or BCL 306 (Secretary of State). LLCs: CPLR 311-a / LLCL 303.
- **Traps:** 308(2) and 308(4) timing is 20/20/10 — defendant's answer clock (30 days) runs from **completion**, not delivery. "Nail and mail" is 308(4), not 308(2). Service defect objection raised in answer is **waived unless defendant moves for judgment within 60 days** after serving the pleading (CPLR 3211(e)).
- **Default relief:** CPLR 5015(a)(1) excusable default + meritorious defense, within 1 year after service of judgment with notice of entry. CPLR 317: served other than by personal delivery, didn't receive actual notice in time — within 1 year of learning of judgment, max 5 years after entry, meritorious defense.

## NY: Venue
- **CPLR 503(a):** county where **any party resided** at commencement, **or** county where a **substantial part of the events or omissions** occurred; if no party resides in NY, any county plaintiff designates. Domestic corp/authorized foreign corp resides in county of **principal office** (503(c)).
- CPLR 507: real property actions — county where property is situated. CPLR 504: actions vs counties/cities have special venue.
- **Change (CPLR 511):** defendant serves written demand with or before answer; plaintiff may consent within 5 days; else defendant moves within 15 days after serving demand. Improper venue is not jurisdictional.

## NY: Motions
- **CPLR 3211(a):** (1) documentary evidence, (2) no SMJ, (3) capacity, (4) other action pending, (5) release, res judicata, collateral estoppel, SOL, statute of frauds, payment, infancy, etc., (6) counterclaim not properly interposed, (7) failure to state a cause of action, (8) no PJ, (9) no in rem jurisdiction, (10) necessary party not joined, (11) volunteer nonprofit director immunity.
- **3211(e):** **one** pre-answer motion. (1),(3),(4),(5),(6) waived unless raised by motion or responsive pleading; (2),(7),(10) may be raised later. (8)/(9) waived if a 3211 motion is made without them or if not raised in the answer. 3211(c): court may treat as SJ with notice. 3211(g): anti-SLAPP (Civil Rights Law 76-a).
- **CPLR 3212(a):** after joinder of issue; **no later than 120 days after filing the note of issue** unless court sets shorter date (not less than 30 days); late only with leave on **good cause** shown — a satisfactory explanation for the delay, not merits (*Brill v. City of New York* (N.Y. 2004)). 3212(f): facts unavailable to opponent. 3212(g)/(e): partial SJ.
- **Default judgment:** move within 1 year of default or complaint dismissed as abandoned absent sufficient cause (CPLR 3215(c)).

## NY: Statutes of limitations (CPLR Art. 2)
- **CPLR 211:** 20 years — money judgment (211(b)).
- **CPLR 213 (6 years):** contract (213(2)) (UCC sale of goods = 4 years, UCC 2-725); fraud — greater of 6 years from commission or **2 years from discovery** (213(8)); equitable relief where no other period (213(1)); mortgage foreclosure (213(4)); corporate/derivative claims (213(7)).
- **CPLR 214 (3 years):** personal injury (214(5)); property injury (214(4)); conversion/replevin (214(3)); statutory liability (214(2)); nonmedical professional malpractice incl. legal/accounting/architect, regardless of contract theory (214(6)). **CPLR 214-c:** toxic exposure — 3 years from discovery of injury.
- **CPLR 214-a:** medical/dental/podiatric malpractice **2½ years** from act or end of continuous treatment for same condition; foreign object — 1 year from discovery; cancer/malignant tumor failure to diagnose — 2½ years from discovery, max 7 years from act (Lavern's Law 2018).
- **CPLR 215 (1 year):** assault, battery, false imprisonment, malicious prosecution, libel, slander, privacy (Civ. Rights Law 51) (215(3)); actions vs sheriff/coroner (215(1)). 215(8): crime victim may sue within 1 year of termination of criminal action vs same defendant.
- **Wrongful death:** EPTL 5-4.1 — **2 years** from death (Grieving Families Act vetoed four times, most recently Dec. 5, 2025 — confirm status before use).
- **Municipal/public corporations — GML 50-e/50-i:** notice of claim within **90 days** after claim arises; served on the public corporation; no suit until **30 days** after service of notice (40 if served on Secretary of State) and adjustment neglected/refused; action within **1 year and 90 days** after the event (wrongful death: **2 years** after death). Late notice under 50-e(5) — application within the 1-year-90-day period; factors: actual knowledge within 90 days or reasonable time after, excuse, prejudice (*Matter of Newcomb v. Middle Country CSD* (N.Y. 2016) — burden on prejudice). 50-h examination demand stays suit until held. **State:** Court of Claims Act §10 (90-day notice of intention/claim; negligence 2 years if notice of intention served) (confirm before use).
- **Article 78:** 4 months (CPLR 217(1)) — see administrative-law.md.
- **Accrual/tolling:** accrues at injury (no general discovery rule except where statute says); 203(d) time-barred counterclaim usable as recoupment/offset if it arises from same transaction and was not barred when the complaint's claims were interposed; 204(a) stays toll; 207 defendant's absence from NY > 4 months continuous tolls only if jurisdiction could not otherwise be obtained; 208 infancy/insanity (insanity toll max 10 years; med-mal infancy toll max 10 years); 208(b) CSA child sexual abuse claims — until age 55.
- **CPLR 205(a) savings:** if timely action terminates **other than** by voluntary discontinuance, failure to obtain PJ over defendant, dismissal for neglect to prosecute, or final judgment on the merits → new action on same transaction within **6 months** of termination, provided the new action would have been timely when the prior one was commenced and **service is effected within the 6 months**. Neglect-to-prosecute dismissal must state the specific conduct constituting neglect. (205-a: separate FAPA rule for foreclosure.)
- **CPLR 203(f) relation back:** amended claim relates back unless the original pleading gave no notice of the transactions/occurrences to be proved. **New defendant** — *Buran v. Coupal* (N.Y. 1995) adopting *Brock v. Bua*: (1) same conduct/transaction/occurrence; (2) new party **united in interest** with original defendant (e.g., vicarious liability) so charged with notice and not prejudiced; (3) new party knew or should have known that but for a mistake as to identity it would have been sued. Mistake need not be excusable (*Buran*), but a deliberate choice not to sue ≠ mistake.
- **CPLR 202 borrowing:** cause accruing **outside NY** — must be timely under **both** NY and the place of accrual, unless it accrued in favor of a **NY resident** (then NY period only). Economic injury accrues where plaintiff resides and sustains the loss (*Global Fin. Corp. v. Triarc Corp.* (N.Y. 1999)); assignee takes assignor's residence (*Portfolio Recovery Assocs. v. King* (N.Y. 2010)).

## NY: Disclosure, trial, joinder
- CPLR 3101(a): "material and necessary" = useful and reasonable (*Allen v. Crowell-Collier* (N.Y. 1968)). 3101(c) attorney work product absolutely immune; 3101(d)(2) trial prep materials — substantial need + undue hardship. 3101(d)(1)(i): expert disclosure on request (identity, subject, substance, qualifications, summary) — **no expert depositions as of right** (unlike FRCP). 3101(i): surveillance films discoverable.
- Jury: 6 jurors (CPLR 4104); verdict **5/6** (CPLR 4113). Demand by note of issue.
- Joinder: CPLR 1001 necessary parties (court may proceed without on 1001(b) factors); 1002 permissive; 1007 impleader (anyone "who is or may be liable" to defendant); CPLR 901(b) bars class recovery of statutory penalties/minimum damages unless statute authorizes (state court only — *Shady Grove*). CPLR 1411 pure comparative fault; Art. 16 (1601) limits joint liability for non-economic loss of defendant ≤50% at fault.

## NY: Appeals (CPLR 5501, 5513, 5701)
- **CPLR 5701(a)(2):** appeal **as of right** to Appellate Division from most **nonfinal orders** decided on a motion on notice that (i) grant/refuse provisional remedy, (iv) involve some part of the merits, (v) affect a substantial right, etc. — far broader than federal §1291. Not appealable as of right: order from **ex parte** motion (seek vacatur first), order requiring/refusing more definite statement, striking scandalous matter (5701(b)); **Article 78 intermediate orders** (5701(b)(1)). Leave available (5701(c)).
- **CPLR 5513:** notice of appeal within **30 days** after service of judgment/order **with notice of entry** (+5 days if served by mail). Clock doesn't run until served with notice of entry.
- **CPLR 5501(a):** appeal from final judgment brings up nonfinal orders necessarily affecting it; 5501(c): AD reviews facts and law; damages "deviate materially from what would be reasonable compensation." Aggrievement required (CPLR 5511).
- **Court of Appeals:** as of right on **two-Justice dissent on question of law** in final AD order (CPLR 5601(a)), or substantial constitutional question (5601(b)); otherwise by leave (5602). Reviews questions of law only.
- **Traps:** NY interlocutory appeals are routine — a denied 3211 motion is appealable as of right; a federal denial of a 12(b) motion is not.
