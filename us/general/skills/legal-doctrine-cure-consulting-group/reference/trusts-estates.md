# Trusts & Estates — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: intestacy, will execution/revocation/construction, will contests, elective share, non-probate transfers, trust creation/modification/fiduciary duties, future interests/RAP, powers of appointment.
Scope: MEE/UPC/Restatement/UTC rule first; NY distinctions flagged **NY:** (EPTL, SCPA). Verified against nysenate.gov: EPTL 3-2.1(a)(4), 3-4.5, 4-1.3 deadlines, 4-1.1(a), 5-1.1-A(a)(2),(a)(4),(e)(2), 5-3.1 dollar limits, 3-3.5 safe harbors, 3-4.4, 7-1.16, 7-1.17, 7-1.9, 10-6.1(a)(4). Other doctrine from model knowledge; numbers marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Intestacy
- **UPC 2-102 spouse:** entire estate if all decedent's descendants are also spouse's and spouse has no other descendants, or no descendants/parents; first \$300,000 + 3/4 if no descendants but a parent survives; first \$225,000 + 1/2 if all descendants are joint but spouse has other descendants; first \$150,000 + 1/2 if decedent has descendants not of spouse (figures COLA-adjusted under UPC 1-109 — confirm before use).
- **Representation schemes:** (1) strict per stirpes (English) — divide at children's generation even if all dead; (2) modern per stirpes / per capita with representation — divide at first generation with a survivor; (3) **per capita at each generation** (UPC 2-106) — divide at first generation with a survivor, then pool shares of deceased members and divide equally at the next generation.
- **Survival:** UPC 2-104 — heir must survive by 120 hours, proven by clear and convincing evidence; USDA (1940) — "insufficient evidence" of order of death.
- **Special heirs:** adopted child = child of adoptive parents (stepparent adoption preserves inheritance from other natural parent's side under UPC 2-119); half-bloods take equally (UPC 2-107; some states take half); nonmarital child inherits from mother, from father on proof of paternity; posthumous (in gestation) children inherit. Slayer rule (UPC 2-803; *Riggs v. Palmer* (N.Y. 1889)). Advancements: UPC 2-109 — only if written.
- **Escheat** only if no heirs within statutory degree (UPC: descendants of grandparents).
- **NY (EPTL 4-1.1):** spouse + issue → **\$50,000 + 1/2 of the residue to spouse**, balance to issue by representation; spouse, no issue → all to spouse; issue, no spouse → all to issue; then parents (whole, not shared with siblings); then issue of parents; then grandparents / their issue (half to each side, to first cousins; first cousins once removed limited). Half-bloods treated as whole blood (4-1.1(b)). **Representation = per capita at each generation** (EPTL 1-2.16 "by representation").
- **NY disqualifications:** spouse disqualified by abandonment, failure to support, void marriage, divorce/separation judgment against spouse (EPTL 5-1.2); parent disqualified for failure to support/abandonment of child under 21 (EPTL 4-1.4).
- **NY posthumously conceived children (EPTL 4-1.3):** genetic child conceived after death inherits only if decedent consented in writing, written notice is given to the fiduciary within 7 months of issuance of letters (or to distributees within 7 months of death; other steps if no letters), and the child is in utero within 24 months **or** born within 33 months of death (verified nysenate.gov).
- **NY advancements (EPTL 2-1.5):** only if contemporaneous writing by donor or donee acknowledgment.
- **Traps:** NY gives spouse the \$50,000 + half **even when all issue are the spouse's** (UPC would give spouse everything). Parents take over siblings. Stepchildren and in-laws are not heirs.

## Will execution
- **UPC 2-502:** writing, signed by testator (or by another in T's conscious presence and at direction), and either (a) two witnesses who sign within a reasonable time after witnessing T's signing or acknowledgment, or (b) acknowledged before a notary. Holographic: signature and material portions in T's handwriting (2-502(b)). **Harmless error (2-503):** clear and convincing evidence T intended the document as a will. Interested witness: no effect (2-505).
- **Traditional (majority):** signature (location usually anywhere); two witnesses; witnesses sign in T's presence (line-of-sight vs conscious-presence split); substantial compliance in some.
- **NY (EPTL 3-2.1(a)):** (1) signed by T **at the end** (matter after signature is ineffective, but matter before it stays valid unless the after-matter is so material that the will is incomplete); (2) signed or acknowledged in the presence of each witness; (3) **publication** — T declares the instrument to be T's will to the witnesses; (4) **at least two attesting witnesses, within one 30-day period**, who sign at T's request (rebuttable presumption that the 30-day requirement was met). Matter after the signature (other than the attestation clause) is given no effect. Rebuttable presumption of due execution where an attorney supervised. Self-proving affidavit SCPA 1406.
- **NY holographic/nuncupative (EPTL 3-2.2):** valid only for members of the armed forces during armed conflict, persons serving with/accompanying them, and mariners at sea; lapse after discharge (1 year) / 3 years for mariners (confirm before use). **No harmless-error doctrine** in NY; strict compliance.
- **NY interested witness (EPTL 3-3.2):** a disposition to an attesting witness is **void unless there are two other disinterested witnesses**; the witness-beneficiary who would be an intestate distributee keeps the **lesser** of the legacy or intestate share.
- **NY choice of law (EPTL 3-5.1(c)):** will is valid as to execution if it complies with NY law, or law of place of execution, or T's domicile at execution or death (for personal property; real property in NY — any of those sufficing). Formal validity of disposition of NY realty governed by NY law (3-5.1(b)).
- **Traps:** NY requires signature **at the end** and publication — UPC does not. NY has no notarized-will alternative and no holographic wills for civilians.

## Components and extrinsic documents
- **Integration; republication by codicil** (codicil re-dates the will and can cure defects in an earlier will; may "validate" an invalid will in some states).
- **Incorporation by reference:** document in existence when will executed, will manifests intent, and describes it sufficiently. **NY: rejected** for unattested documents (*Booth v. Baptist Church* (N.Y. 1891)); exception only for existing documents such as a lifetime trust instrument.
- **Acts of independent significance** — accepted in NY and UPC 2-512.
- **UPC 2-513:** separate writing for tangible personal property, even if prepared after will. **NY:** not recognized.
- **Pour-over (UTC/UTATA; NY EPTL 3-3.7):** devise to a trust in existence (or executed concurrently) valid even if trust is amendable and amended later; NY requires the trust instrument be executed before or concurrently with the will; trust need not be funded until death (confirm unfunded-trust rule before use).

## Revocation and revival
- **Methods:** subsequent writing executed with will formalities (express or inconsistency); physical act (burning, tearing, cancelling, obliterating, destroying) with intent; operation of law (divorce; marriage/children under pretermission statutes).
- **Presumptions:** will last in T's possession not found → presumed revoked; mutilated found in T's possession → presumed revoked.
- **UPC:** partial revocation by physical act allowed; revocation by cancellation needn't touch words (UPC 2-507). Revival: UPC 2-509 depends on T's intent.
- **Dependent relative revocation (DRR):** revocation disregarded if premised on a mistake of law/fact and T would not have revoked but for mistake.
- **Lost wills:** proponent proves due execution, contents, and that it was not revoked.
- **NY (EPTL 3-4.1):** revocation by later will or writing executed with will formalities, or by physical act of T **or of another in T's presence and at T's direction, proved by two witnesses** in addition to the person who performed it. Alterations after execution are ineffective (will probated as originally written if legible). **Revival (EPTL 3-4.6): revocation of a later will does NOT revive the earlier one** unless the earlier is re-executed or republished by codicil (confirm exception for revocation by later writing expressly reviving). **Lost/destroyed will (SCPA 1407):** prove not revoked, due execution, and all provisions clearly and distinctly by two credible witnesses or a copy/draft.
- **NY revocation by divorce (EPTL 5-1.4):** divorce, annulment, declaration of nullity, or dissolution revokes revocable dispositions **to the former spouse and to relatives of the former spouse**, and fiduciary nominations — in wills, revocable trusts, beneficiary designations, POAs — unless the instrument or court order/separation agreement provides otherwise. (EPTL 3-4.4 is not the divorce rule — it concerns a conservator's sale of specifically devised property: beneficiary traces to proceeds.)
- **NY after-born children (EPTL 5-3.2):** child born after the will, unprovided for and not mentioned, takes a share; if T had other children with gifts, after-born shares ratably in those gifts; if no child took, after-born takes intestate share. No protection for children alive at execution who were omitted.
- **Traps:** NY anti-revival default. Partial revocation by act: NY probates the unaltered text.

## Construction: lapse, ademption, abatement, exoneration
- **Lapse:** beneficiary predeceases T → gift fails; void gift if already dead at execution. **Anti-lapse (UPC 2-603):** devisee who is T's grandparent, descendant of grandparent, or stepchild → surviving descendants take; applies to class gifts; words of survivorship ("if she survives me") defeat it in majority, **but UPC 2-603(b)(3) says mere words of survivorship are not enough** (minority; contested).
- **NY anti-lapse (EPTL 3-3.3):** applies only to gifts to T's **issue, brothers, or sisters**; their surviving issue take per capita at each generation. Also applies to class gifts to such persons. **Residuary lapse (EPTL 3-3.4):** lapsed share of residue passes to other residuary beneficiaries proportionally.
- **Ademption by extinction:** specific gift not in estate at death fails — identity theory (majority; NY) vs intent theory (UPC 2-606: unpaid sale/condemnation/insurance proceeds; **replacement property only for real or tangible personal property** (2-606(a)(5)) — sale of stock and reinvestment is not covered; value if sold by conservator/agent; catch-all (a)(6) pecuniary devise of the disposed item's value if ademption is shown inconsistent with T's plan or intent). **NY:** EPTL 3-4.3 — a conveyance altering but not wholly divesting T's interest is not a revocation; beneficiary takes what remains; EPTL 3-4.4 conservator-sale exception; EPTL 3-4.5 — insurance proceeds on specifically disposed property paid after T's death go to the specific beneficiary (verified).
- **Ademption by satisfaction:** UPC 2-609 and **NY EPTL 2-1.5** — only if writing.
- **Stock:** UPC 2-605 — specific devisee takes additional shares from splits/stock dividends of same entity. Cash dividends before death do not pass.
- **Abatement order (UPC 3-902; NY EPTL 13-1.3):** intestate property → residuary → general → specific (demonstrative treated as specific to extent of source). Pro rata within class. NY: same basic order.
- **Exoneration:** UPC 2-607 and **NY EPTL 3-3.6**: specific devisee takes subject to mortgage; no exoneration unless will expressly directs (general "pay my debts" clause insufficient).
- **Traps:** NY anti-lapse does not cover nieces/nephews, spouses, or stepchildren. A gift "to my sister if she survives me" — survivorship condition defeats anti-lapse.

## Will contests
- **Standing:** interested person who would take more if the will fails. **Capacity:** T must understand (1) nature and extent of property, (2) natural objects of bounty, (3) the disposition being made, (4) how these relate — lower bar than contract capacity. Insane delusion: false belief persisted in against all evidence that affected the disposition.
- **Undue influence:** (1) susceptibility, (2) opportunity, (3) disposition to exert, (4) result showing effect. Presumption where confidential relationship + active participation in procurement + unnatural disposition (majority). **NY:** confidential/fiduciary relationship (attorney, doctor) creates an inference requiring explanation; attorney-drafter-beneficiary (*Matter of Putnam* (N.Y. 1931)) — gift to the drafting attorney raises an inference of undue influence the attorney must explain ("Putnam scenario"). Undue influence is moral coercion constraining T to act against free will (*Matter of Walther* (N.Y. 1959)).
- **Fraud:** in the execution (misrepresents document) vs in the inducement (misrepresents facts). Remedy often constructive trust.
- **No-contest clauses:** UPC 3-905 — unenforceable if probable cause. **NY EPTL 3-3.5:** strictly construed and enforceable, but safe harbors — contest on grounds of **forgery or later revocation** brought with probable cause; objection to jurisdiction; disclosure of information to parties/court; refusal to join a probate petition or sign a waiver; **SCPA 1404 examination** of attesting witnesses, drafter, nominated executors, proponents; construction proceedings; contests by infants/incompetents. Otherwise any contest (even good faith, e.g. undue influence) forfeits the gift.
- **NY SCPA 1404:** before filing objections, any party may examine attesting witnesses, the drafter, nominated fiduciaries, and (upon application) other persons; Uniform Rules limit discovery to 3 years before and 2 years after the will's date (22 NYCRR 207.27; confirm before use). Objections due 10 days after the 1404 transcripts are completed (confirm before use). **SCPA 1408:** court must be satisfied of genuineness, due execution, and competency and freedom from restraint before admitting to probate.
- **NY small estates:** SCPA Art. 13 voluntary administration for personal property ≤ \$50,000 (confirm before use).
- **Traps:** NY no-contest clause triggered by an undue-influence objection filed in good faith; not by 1404 examination.

## Spousal protection and non-probate assets
- **Elective share (UPC 2-202):** percentage of augmented estate scaled by marriage length (up to 50% of marital-property portion after 15 years); augmented estate includes non-probate transfers. Majority (separate-property states): 1/3 of probate estate, often extended by illusory-transfer / fraud-on-rights doctrines. Community-property states have no elective share (but quasi-community property).
- **NY (EPTL 5-1.1-A):** **greater of \$50,000 (or the net estate if less) or 1/3 of the net estate**, computed including **testamentary substitutes** (gifts causa mortis, gifts within 1 year of death, Totten trusts, joint accounts/joint property to extent of decedent's contribution, POD, retirement plans, revocable trusts, transfers with retained life interest). Election within **6 months after letters issue but not later than 2 years after death** (extensions by order before expiration). Waiver/release must be **in writing, subscribed, and acknowledged** like a deed (5-1.1-A(e)(2)). Spouse disqualified under EPTL 5-1.2. Life-estate trusts no longer satisfy the share for post-1992 wills.
- **NY exempt property (EPTL 5-3.1):** set off to surviving spouse (or children under 21) outside the will and ahead of creditors: household items ≤ \$20,000; family bible/pictures/books ≤ \$2,500; farm animals/machinery ≤ \$20,000; one motor vehicle ≤ \$25,000; money (incl. accounts and marketable securities) ≤ \$25,000, reduced by the excess value, if any, of items acquired under (a)(1)-(3) and (5) (verified nysenate.gov).
- **Non-probate:** revocable trusts, life insurance, POD/TOD, joint tenancy, Totten trust (NY EPTL 7-5.2), retirement accounts. **NY Totten trust** may be revoked by will only by express reference to the account (EPTL 7-5.2(2)). **UPC 6-101 / TOD deeds:** NY adopted real property TOD deeds (RPL 424, eff. 2024; confirm before use).
- **Pretermitted spouse (UPC 2-301):** spouse who married T after will takes intestate share of non-spouse portion. **NY:** no pretermitted-spouse statute — relies on elective share.
- **Traps:** NY elective share reaches most non-probate assets; ERISA preemption may override for qualified plans with spousal consent rules.

## Trusts — creation and types
- **Elements:** settlor intent, trust property (res), ascertainable beneficiaries (except charitable/purpose/pet trusts), trustee (court will appoint; trust won't fail), lawful purpose. Oral trust of personalty valid (clear and convincing, UTC 407); land trust needs writing (SOF).
- **Precatory language** ("hope," "wish") creates no trust. **Secret trust** (absolute on face) → constructive trust for intended beneficiary; **semi-secret** (trust on face, beneficiary unnamed) → resulting trust to T's estate (traditional; Restatement (Third) allows proof).
- **Resulting trusts:** failed express trust; purchase-money (payor ≠ title holder, unless family gift presumption). **NY:** purchase-money resulting trusts abolished except limited cases (EPTL 7-1.3; confirm before use).
- **Constructive trust:** remedy for unjust enrichment. **NY elements (*Sharp v. Kosmalski* (N.Y. 1976)):** confidential/fiduciary relationship, promise, transfer in reliance, unjust enrichment.
- **Revocability:** UTC 602 — trust **revocable unless expressly irrevocable** (for post-UTC trusts); common-law majority — irrevocable unless reserved. **NY EPTL 7-1.16: a lifetime trust is irrevocable unless it expressly provides it is revocable**; a revocable lifetime trust may also be revoked by will that specifically refers to it.
- **NY lifetime trust formalities (EPTL 7-1.17):** in writing, executed and acknowledged (or witnessed by two witnesses) by the creator and by at least one trustee unless creator is sole trustee; amendments/revocations executed the same way.
- **Charitable trusts:** indefinite beneficiaries OK; exempt from RAP (as to duration); AG enforces; **cy pres** when purpose impossible/impracticable/illegal (UTC 413 adds wasteful) and general charitable intent (presumed under UTC). **NY EPTL 8-1.1(c)** — cy pres by Supreme or Surrogate's Court; AG a necessary party. Honorary/purpose trusts: UTC 409 — 21 years; pet trusts (NY EPTL 7-8.1).
- **Spendthrift:** restraint on voluntary and involuntary alienation of beneficial interest; exceptions (UTC 503): child support/alimony, supplier of necessaries (common law), government claims, prior-contract creditors in some states. Self-settled: invalid against settlor's creditors (majority; UTC 505; **NY EPTL 7-3.1**); ~20 states allow DAPTs.
- **NY spendthrift default (EPTL 7-1.5):** the beneficiary's right to **income** is inalienable by default (no language needed) unless the instrument permits transfer; principal remainder interests are alienable. Creditors of an income beneficiary may reach income in excess of the amount needed for education and support (EPTL 7-3.4; CPLR 5205(e) — 10% income execution; confirm before use).
- **Discretionary trusts:** creditor cannot compel distribution; beneficiary can sue only for abuse of discretion (bad faith / arbitrary). Support trusts: creditor who provides necessaries can reach.
- **Traps:** NY and UTC diverge on default revocability — a NY lifetime trust silent on revocation is irrevocable. NY spendthrift protection for income is automatic.

## Modification and termination
- **Claflin (majority):** beneficiaries (all consenting, competent) may terminate/modify only if it will not defeat a **material purpose** (spendthrift, support, discretionary, age-based distribution are material purposes). Settlor + all beneficiaries may terminate any trust. UTC 411(a) settlor + beneficiaries; 411(b) beneficiaries alone if not inconsistent with material purpose (spendthrift clause not presumed material in UTC). UTC 412 — equitable deviation for unanticipated circumstances (dispositive or administrative). UTC 416 — modify to achieve tax objectives.
- **NY (EPTL 7-1.9):** creator may revoke/amend on **written, acknowledged consent of all persons beneficially interested**; beneficiaries alone cannot terminate a trust (NY rejects beneficiary-only termination; confirm before use). Trustee **decanting (EPTL 10-6.6)** — trustee with discretion to invade principal may appoint to a new trust (absolute discretion allows broad changes; limited discretion requires substantially similar interests; notice 30 days — confirm before use).
- **Traps:** a spendthrift clause blocks Claflin termination at common law but not automatically under UTC.

## Trust administration — fiduciary duties
- **Loyalty:** self-dealing voidable regardless of fairness (no further inquiry) unless authorized by instrument, consent, or court; conflicts get fairness review. *Meinhard v. Salmon* (N.Y. 1928) — "punctilio of an honor the most sensitive." *Matter of Rothko* (N.Y. 1977) — conflicted executors; appreciation damages.
- **Prudence (UPIA; NY EPTL 11-2.3 Prudent Investor Act):** portfolio as a whole, risk/return, **diversify** unless special circumstances; may delegate investment functions with care in selection, instruction, monitoring; judged at time of decision (not hindsight). Retain-inherited-assets not a safe harbor (*Matter of Janes* (N.Y. 1997) — failure to diversify concentrated stock).
- **Impartiality** between income and remainder; **NY power to adjust** (EPTL 11-2.3(b)(5)) and **unitrust election** (EPTL 11-2.4, 4% of trailing value; confirm before use).
- **Other duties:** inform and report (UTC 813), segregate/earmark, not delegate non-investment discretion, enforce and defend claims.
- **Remedies:** surcharge, removal, tracing, constructive trust. Exculpatory clauses: UTC 1008 — unenforceable for bad faith/reckless indifference. **NY EPTL 11-1.7:** exoneration of a **testamentary** trustee for failure to exercise reasonable care is void as against public policy.
- **NY commissions (SCPA 2307/2308/2309):** statutory percentage schedules (confirm schedule before use).
- **Traps:** a trustee's good faith is not a defense to self-dealing. Delegation to an agent is not a breach if done prudently.

## Future interests and RAP
- **Classification:** reversion, possibility of reverter, right of entry (grantor); vested remainder (indefeasibly, subject to open, subject to divestment), contingent remainder, executory interest (grantee).
- **RAP (common law):** interest must vest or fail, if at all, within lives in being + 21 years; applies to contingent remainders, executory interests, class gifts (all-or-nothing), options, rights of first refusal (some). Traps: fertile octogenarian, unborn widow, slothful executor, administrative contingency.
- **Reform:** USRAP (majority) — 90-year wait-and-see + reformation; some states cy pres.
- **NY (EPTL 9-1.1):** no wait-and-see; common-law RAP (b) plus **rule against suspension of the power of alienation** (a) — lives in being + 21; a trust with a trustee power of sale does not suspend alienability. **NY saving statutes:** EPTL 9-1.2 — age contingency over 21 that would violate RAP reduced to 21; EPTL 9-1.3 — presumptions (valid construction preferred; "widow" means one alive at creation; woman over 55 presumed unable to have a child; administrative contingency presumed to occur within 21 years) (confirm subsection details). Destructibility of contingent remainders abolished; Rule in Shelley's Case and Doctrine of Worthier Title abolished (EPTL 6-5.9 area; 6-5.16).
- **Class gifts:** rule of convenience — class closes when any member is entitled to possession. **Gifts to "heirs":** determined at the death of the named ancestor (UPC 2-711 — at time of possession).
- **Survival for future interests:** common law — no implied survival requirement; **UPC 2-707** — remainder beneficiary must survive to distribution date; substitute gift to descendants. **NY:** no UPC 2-707; interests vest early.
- **Traps:** NY has no wait-and-see; a NY gift violating RAP on paper is void ab initio unless a saving statute applies.

## Powers of appointment
- **General** (exercisable in favor of donee, donee's estate, or their creditors) vs **nongeneral/special**; presently exercisable vs testamentary. General power property is reachable by donee's creditors (presently exercisable; majority) and included in donee's gross estate (IRC §2041).
- **Exercise by residuary clause:** majority / UPC 2-608 — general residuary clause does **not** exercise a power unless the power is general and there is no gift in default, or will manifests intent. Specific-reference requirement imposed by donor must be honored (courts split on substantial compliance).
- **NY (EPTL 10-6.1(a)(4)):** a will disposing of all of the donee's property (a general residuary clause) **exercises the power** unless the will expressly or by necessary implication shows it was not intended; but a donor's specific-reference requirement must be met. Minority rule — tested.
- **Lapse in exercise:** anti-lapse applies to appointees (UPC 2-603; NY via 3-3.3 by analogy — confirm before use). **Capture / allocation / fraud on the power** — appointment to non-objects void.
- **NY creditors (EPTL 10-7.1, 10-7.2):** creditors reach property subject to a presently exercisable general power; also at death if exercised (confirm before use).
- **Traps:** a NY residuary clause silently exercises the power; under UPC it usually does not.
