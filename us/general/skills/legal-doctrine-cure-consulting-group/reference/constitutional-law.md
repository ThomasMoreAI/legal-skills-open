# Constitutional Law — rules, splits, traps

Load when: justiciability, federal/state power, preemption, dormant commerce clause, separation of powers, state action, due process, equal protection, First Amendment, takings, contracts clause, P&I, Eleventh Amendment.
Scope: MBE-only subject (not tested on NYLE); federal rule throughout, sparse **NY:** notes where the NY Constitution gives more. Verified: doctrine from model knowledge through OT2024 decisions; *Trump v. Slaughter* (OT2025) checked against the slip opinion; no numbers requiring source check.

## Justiciability
- **Standing:** (1) injury in fact — concrete and particularized, actual or imminent (*Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife* (U.S. 1992); bare statutory violation insufficient, *Spokeo* (U.S. 2016); *TransUnion v. Ramirez* (U.S. 2021)); (2) causation (fairly traceable); (3) redressability. Must exist for each claim and form of relief. Doctors' stake in FDA mifepristone rules insufficient (*FDA v. Alliance for Hippocratic Medicine* (U.S. 2024)).
- **Third-party standing:** close relationship + hindrance (doctor–patient, seller–buyer for age restrictions, *Craig v. Boren*). **Organizational:** members would have standing, germane interests, no individual participation needed. **Taxpayer:** none, except Establishment Clause challenge to congressional spending (*Flast v. Cohen*) — not executive discretionary spending (*Hein*) or tax credits (*Winn*). **Legislators/states:** generally no generalized grievances; *Massachusetts v. EPA* special solicitude; *United States v. Texas* (U.S. 2023) no state standing to challenge enforcement priorities. Personal stake in 303 Creative-type pre-enforcement: credible threat of enforcement.
- **Ripeness** (hardship + fitness), **mootness** (exceptions: capable of repetition yet evading review — abortion, elections; voluntary cessation; class actions with live class), **political questions** (*Baker v. Carr* (U.S. 1962): textual commitment, no manageable standards — Guarantee Clause, foreign affairs recognition, impeachment procedure (*Nixon v. U.S.* (U.S. 1993)), partisan gerrymandering (*Rucho v. Common Cause* (U.S. 2019)); racial gerrymandering justiciable).
- **Adequate and independent state ground** bars SCOTUS review (*Michigan v. Long*). Advisory opinions barred. Congress may limit appellate jurisdiction (Exceptions Clause) but not dictate rule of decision in pending case.
- **Eleventh Amendment/state sovereign immunity:** bars private suits for damages against states in federal court (and state court, *Alden v. Maine*); exceptions — consent (clear), Congress abrogates under §5 of 14th Am (not Art I powers, *Seminole Tribe* (U.S. 1996); except bankruptcy (*Katz*) and war powers (*Torres v. Texas DPS* (U.S. 2022))), *Ex parte Young* injunctions vs officials (prospective, federal law), suits by U.S. or other states, individual-capacity damages vs officers. Not protected: cities/counties. One state can't be sued in another's courts without consent (*Franchise Tax Bd. v. Hyatt III* (U.S. 2019)).

## Federal legislative power
- **Commerce Clause:** (1) channels, (2) instrumentalities + persons/things in commerce, (3) activities substantially affecting commerce — economic activity may be aggregated (*Wickard*; *Gonzales v. Raich* (U.S. 2005)); non-economic activity can't be aggregated (*Lopez* (U.S. 1995) guns near schools; *Morrison* (U.S. 2000) gender violence civil remedy). Cannot compel activity (*NFIB v. Sebelius* (U.S. 2012) individual mandate) — upheld as tax.
- **Taxing & spending:** tax valid if revenue-raising/reasonably related; **spending** for general welfare with conditions that are unambiguous, related to federal interest, not coercive (*South Dakota v. Dole* (U.S. 1987) 5% highway funds OK; *NFIB* — threat to all Medicaid funds coercive).
- **Necessary and Proper:** means rationally related to enumerated end (*McCulloch v. Maryland* (U.S. 1819); *United States v. Comstock* (U.S. 2010)). Not independent power.
- **§5 of 14th Amendment:** remedial only — **congruence and proportionality** (*City of Boerne v. Flores* (U.S. 1997)); can't expand substantive rights; RFRA invalid as to states. 15th Am §2 — preclearance formula invalid (*Shelby County* (U.S. 2013)). 13th Am §2 reaches private badges of slavery.
- **Tenth Amendment / anti-commandeering:** Congress can't compel state legislatures (*New York v. United States* (U.S. 1992)), state executive officers (*Printz* (U.S. 1997)), or forbid states from repealing laws (*Murphy v. NCAA* (U.S. 2018) sports betting). Generally applicable laws binding states OK (*Garcia*; *Reno v. Condon*). Conditional preemption and spending conditions OK.
- **Other:** war, immigration/naturalization, property clause (plenary over federal lands), bankruptcy, postal, IP, Indian commerce. No general police power except DC, territories, federal lands, military.

## Executive power & separation of powers
- **Youngstown** (U.S. 1952) Jackson tiers: express/implied authorization → maximum; congressional silence → zone of twilight; contrary to Congress → lowest ebb (upheld only if exclusive power, e.g., recognition — *Zivotofsky v. Kerry* (U.S. 2015)).
- **Appointments:** principal officers — President with Senate consent; inferior — Congress may vest in President, courts, department heads (*Morrison v. Olson*; *Edmond*); APJs exercising unreviewable power are principal-officer-like (*Arthrex* (U.S. 2021)). Congress can't appoint executive officers (*Buckley v. Valeo*).
- **Removal:** President may remove principal officers at will; for-cause protection OK for multimember expert agencies (*Humphrey's Executor*) and inferior officers with limited duties (*Morrison*); **single director with for-cause protection unconstitutional** (*Seila Law* (U.S. 2020); *Collins v. Yellen* (U.S. 2021)); dual for-cause layers invalid (*Free Enterprise Fund* (U.S. 2010)). **_Humphrey's Executor_ overruled:** *Trump v. Slaughter* (U.S. June 29, 2026) (6–3) — FTC commissioners' for-cause protection violates separation of powers; officers exercising executive power must be removable at will. Left open: Federal Reserve, non-Article III adjudicators, and offices exercising no executive power. Multimember-agency for-cause protection is no longer a safe MBE answer.
- **Legislative veto / line-item veto invalid:** bicameralism + presentment (*INS v. Chadha* (U.S. 1983); *Clinton v. New York* (U.S. 1998)). Nondelegation: intelligible principle (*Gundy* (U.S. 2019); *FCC v. Consumers' Research* (U.S. 2025) upheld USF).
- **Major questions doctrine:** agency claims of vast economic/political significance need clear congressional authorization (*West Virginia v. EPA* (U.S. 2022); *Biden v. Nebraska* (U.S. 2023) student loans).
- **Chevron overruled:** *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo* (U.S. 2024) — courts exercise independent judgment on statutory meaning under APA §706; agency views get *Skidmore* respect only; prior Chevron-based holdings retain stare decisis. *SEC v. Jarkesy* (U.S. 2024): civil penalties for securities fraud (common-law analogue) require jury — no agency in-house adjudication (7th Am; public-rights exception narrowed). *Corner Post* (U.S. 2024): APA 6-year SOL runs from plaintiff's injury.
- **Immunities:** President absolutely immune from civil damages for official acts (*Nixon v. Fitzgerald*); not for unofficial pre-office conduct (*Clinton v. Jones*); **criminal**: absolute immunity for core constitutional powers, presumptive immunity for other official acts, none for unofficial acts (*Trump v. United States* (U.S. 2024)). Executive privilege yields to specific criminal-trial need (*United States v. Nixon* (U.S. 1974)). Pardon: federal crimes only, not impeachment. Treaties (2/3 Senate) vs executive agreements (no Senate; can't override statute). Self-executing treaty + later statute: last in time wins.
- **Universal injunctions:** federal courts likely lack equitable authority to issue relief broader than necessary to redress plaintiffs (*Trump v. CASA* (U.S. 2025)); class actions remain.
- **State qualifications to federal office:** states can't add term limits (*U.S. Term Limits v. Thornton*); states can't enforce §3 of 14th Am against federal candidates (*Trump v. Anderson* (U.S. 2024)).

## Federalism limits on states
- **Preemption:** express; implied field (pervasive scheme — immigration registration, *Arizona v. U.S.* (U.S. 2012)); conflict (impossibility or obstacle). Presumption against preemption in traditional state fields. Intergovernmental immunity: states can't tax or directly regulate federal government or discriminate against it.
- **Dormant Commerce Clause:** (1) facial or purposeful **discrimination** against out-of-state commerce → virtually per se invalid unless necessary to important non-protectionist interest with no less discriminatory alternative (*Maine v. Taylor* baitfish); (2) nondiscriminatory burden → *Pike v. Bruce Church* balancing (clearly excessive burden). *National Pork Producers v. Ross* (U.S. 2023): no per se "extraterritoriality" rule; Prop 12 upheld; Pike claim requires substantial burden on interstate commerce. **Market participant** exception (*Reeves v. Stake*; not downstream conditions — *South-Central Timber*); government function (*United Haulers* (U.S. 2007) public waste facility). Congress can authorize discrimination.
- **State taxation of commerce:** *Complete Auto* (substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination, fairly related); physical presence not required (*South Dakota v. Wayfair* (U.S. 2018)).
- **Art IV Privileges & Immunities:** protects citizens (not corporations/aliens) from discrimination on **fundamental** interests — commercial livelihood, civil liberties; not recreation (*Baldwin v. Montana* elk hunting). Discrimination OK if substantial reason + substantial relationship (no less restrictive means). Commercial fishing license disparity → violates (*Toomer v. Witsell*); bar admission residency → violates (*Piper*). *McBurney v. Young* (U.S. 2013): FOIA limited to citizens OK.
- **14th Am P or I:** right to travel — new residents treated equally (*Saenz v. Roe* (U.S. 1999) durational welfare limits invalid). Otherwise narrow (*Slaughter-House*).
- **Full Faith and Credit:** judgments with jurisdiction and on merits must be recognized.

## State action
- Constitution binds government only. Private actor state action when: **public function** traditionally and **exclusively** governmental (company town *Marsh v. Alabama*; not shopping malls — *Hudgens v. NLRB* (U.S. 1976); elections *Terry v. Adams*; not utilities, schools, nursing homes; not public-access cable channels — *Manhattan Community Access v. Halleck* (U.S. 2019)); **entanglement** — judicial enforcement of racial covenant (*Shelley v. Kraemer*), symbiotic lessee (*Burton*), joint action, encouragement/coercion; mere licensing/regulation/funding insufficient (*Moose Lodge*; *Rendell-Baker*; *Jackson v. Metropolitan Edison*). Public official's social-media posts are state action only if actual authority to speak for state + purported exercise (*Lindke v. Freed* (U.S. 2024)). Government jawboning of private platforms: coercion violates 1st Am (*NRA v. Vullo* (U.S. 2024)).

## Due process
- **Procedural:** deprivation of life, liberty, or **property** (legitimate entitlement — tenure, welfare, for-cause employment, driver's license); *Mathews v. Eldridge* (U.S. 1976) balancing: private interest, risk of error/value of safeguards, government interest. Welfare termination: pre-termination evidentiary hearing (*Goldberg v. Kelly*); public employee for-cause: pre-termination notice + opportunity to respond, full post-termination hearing (*Loudermill*); disability benefits: post-termination hearing OK (*Mathews*); school suspension: informal notice and hearing (*Goss*). Negligent deprivation isn't a DP violation (*Daniels*). No affirmative duty to protect from private violence (*DeShaney* (U.S. 1989); *Castle Rock v. Gonzales*). Punitive damages ratio >single digit suspect (*State Farm v. Campbell*).
- **Void for vagueness**; **irrebuttable presumptions** disfavored.
- **Substantive DP:** fundamental rights → strict scrutiny (government bears burden: necessary to compelling interest, least restrictive). Otherwise rational basis (challenger bears burden). Fundamental: marriage (*Loving*; *Obergefell* (U.S. 2015)), procreation, contraception (*Griswold*, *Eisenstadt*), family living (*Moore v. East Cleveland*), parental rights (*Troxel*; *Pierce*; *Meyer*), sexual intimacy (*Lawrence* (U.S. 2003) — rational basis with bite), refusing medical treatment (*Cruzan*; no right to assisted suicide, *Glucksberg* (U.S. 1997)), interstate travel, voting (EP). Test for unenumerated right: deeply rooted in history and tradition (*Glucksberg*).
- **Abortion:** no federal constitutional right — *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health* (U.S. 2022) overruled *Roe* and *Casey*; regulation gets rational basis. **NY:** Reproductive Health Act (PHL 2599-aa, 2019) and NY Const art I §11 (Equal Rights Amendment 2024 — includes pregnancy outcomes, reproductive autonomy) (confirm scope before use).
- **Second Amendment:** individual right (*Heller* (U.S. 2008)), incorporated (*McDonald* (U.S. 2010)); test is **text, history, and tradition** — no means-end scrutiny (*NY State Rifle & Pistol Ass'n v. Bruen* (U.S. 2022) — struck NY "proper cause" licensing); law need only be "relevantly similar" to historical analogues in why and how (*United States v. Rahimi* (U.S. 2024) upheld §922(g)(8) domestic-violence order disarmament). **NY:** Concealed Carry Improvement Act (2022) — sensitive-place provisions partly upheld in *Antonyuk v. James* (2d Cir. 2024) (confirm before use).
- **Incorporation:** almost all Bill of Rights incorporated via 14th DP, except 3rd, 5th grand jury, 7th civil jury, 6th vicinage; excessive fines incorporated (*Timbs* (U.S. 2019)); unanimous jury (*Ramos* (U.S. 2020)).

## Equal protection
- Applies to states via 14th, federal via 5th DP (*Bolling v. Sharpe*). Requires **intent** — facially discriminatory, discriminatory application, or discriminatory purpose (*Washington v. Davis* (U.S. 1976); *Arlington Heights* factors; *Feeney* — "because of, not in spite of"). Disparate impact alone → rational basis.
- **Strict scrutiny:** race, ethnicity, national origin; state alienage classifications (exceptions: political/governmental functions — police, teachers, probation officers → rational basis; federal alienage → rational basis; undocumented children → *Plyler* intermediate-ish). Fundamental rights under EP: voting (one person one vote; poll taxes), access to courts (criminal appeal transcripts), interstate travel.
- **Affirmative action:** race-conscious university admissions violate EP/Title VI — *Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard/UNC* (U.S. 2023) (overruling *Grutter* in effect); diversity not measurable compelling interest; race can't be used as a negative or stereotype; applicant's essay about how race affected life may be considered as individual experience. Remedying identified past discrimination by the government actor remains compelling. Race predominance in districting → strict scrutiny (*Alexander v. SC NAACP* (U.S. 2024) — presumption of legislative good faith; plaintiff must disentangle race from politics).
- **Intermediate scrutiny:** sex (exceedingly persuasive justification, *United States v. Virginia* (U.S. 1996)); **nonmarital children** (legitimacy). Substantially related to important interest; government burden. Benign sex classifications compensating actual past discrimination may pass.
- **Rational basis:** everything else — age, wealth, disability, sexual orientation (formally; *Romer* animus), economic regulation. Challenger must show no conceivable legitimate purpose. Animus → fails (*Cleburne* (U.S. 1985); *Romer* (U.S. 1996); *Moreno*). *United States v. Skrmetti* (U.S. 2025): state ban on gender-affirming care for minors classifies by age and medical use → rational basis; upheld.
- **Voting:** Anderson-Burdick balancing for election rules; strict scrutiny for severe burdens; poll tax invalid (*Harper*).

## First Amendment — speech
- **Content-based** (subject matter or viewpoint) → strict scrutiny (*Reed v. Town of Gilbert* (U.S. 2015)); regulation distinguishing on-premises vs off-premises signs is content-neutral (*City of Austin v. Reagan National* (U.S. 2022)). **Content-neutral** time, place, manner → intermediate (narrowly tailored to significant interest, ample alternative channels; need not be least restrictive, *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*). Expressive conduct: *O'Brien* (important interest unrelated to suppression, incidental restriction no greater than essential); flag burning protected (*Texas v. Johnson*).
- **Unprotected/less-protected:** incitement (intent + imminent lawless action + likely — *Brandenburg* (U.S. 1969)); fighting words (narrow; can't be viewpoint-selective — *R.A.V.*); **true threats** — speaker must at least **recklessly** disregard threatening nature (*Counterman v. Colorado* (U.S. 2023)); obscenity (*Miller* (U.S. 1973): prurient interest by community standards, patently offensive, lacks serious value by national reasonable person); child pornography (no *Miller* test; virtual not unprotected, *Ashcroft v. FSC*); defamation (see torts.md); false statements generally protected (*Alvarez* (U.S. 2012) Stolen Valor). Age-verification for sexual material obscene-to-minors → intermediate scrutiny (*Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton* (U.S. 2025)). Violent video games for minors protected (*Brown v. EMA*).
- **Prior restraint:** heavy presumption of invalidity (*Near v. Minnesota*; *Pentagon Papers*); licensing must have narrow objective standards + prompt judicial review; injunctions must be obeyed until vacated (collateral bar rule — *Walker v. Birmingham*).
- **Overbreadth** (substantial relative to legitimate sweep — *United States v. Hansen* (U.S. 2023); facial challenges demanding, *Moody v. NetChoice* (U.S. 2024) — platforms' curation is expressive), **vagueness**.
- **Forums:** traditional public (streets, parks, sidewalks) and designated → content-based strict, TPM intermediate; limited public forum and nonpublic (airport terminals, military bases, mailboxes, jails, school mail) → reasonable and viewpoint-neutral (*Minnesota Voters Alliance v. Mansky* (U.S. 2018) — standards must be capable of reasoned application). Government speech not subject to forum analysis (*Summum*; *Walker v. Texas SCV*), but flag-raising program open to public was not government speech (*Shurtleff v. Boston* (U.S. 2022)). Residential picketing targeted at single home may be banned (*Frisby*).
- **Commercial speech:** *Central Hudson* — lawful + not misleading, then substantial interest, directly advances, not more extensive than necessary (reasonable fit). False/misleading or illegal activity ads unprotected. Compelled factual disclosures OK if purely factual and uncontroversial and not unduly burdensome (*Zauderer*; *NIFLA v. Becerra* (U.S. 2018)).
- **Compelled speech/association:** *Barnette*; *Wooley*; *303 Creative v. Elenis* (U.S. 2023) — state can't compel web designer to create expressive wedding websites; *Janus* (U.S. 2018) — public-employee agency fees; *Americans for Prosperity v. Bonta* (U.S. 2021) — donor disclosure exacting scrutiny requires narrow tailoring; *Boy Scouts v. Dale*.
- **Government employees:** *Pickering* balancing for speech as citizen on public concern; *Garcetti* — pursuant to official duties unprotected; *Kennedy v. Bremerton* (coach's personal prayer was private speech). **Students:** *Tinker* substantial disruption; *Mahanoy* (U.S. 2021) off-campus speech limited authority; *Fraser* lewd; *Hazelwood* school-sponsored; *Morse* drug messages.
- **Campaign finance:** contribution limits OK (closely drawn); expenditure limits strict — corporate independent expenditures protected (*Citizens United* (U.S. 2010)); *FEC v. Cruz* (U.S. 2022) loan-repayment limit invalid.

## First Amendment — religion
- **Establishment Clause:** *Lemon* abandoned — **history and tradition** test (*Kennedy v. Bremerton School District* (U.S. 2022); *Town of Greece v. Galloway* (U.S. 2014) legislative prayer; *American Legion* (U.S. 2019) longstanding monuments presumed valid). Still barred: coercion (school-sponsored prayer — *Lee v. Weisman*, *Engel*), sect preference (*Larson v. Valente* — strict scrutiny for denominational discrimination; *Catholic Charities Bureau v. Wisconsin Labor & Industry Review Comm'n* (U.S. 2025) — tax exemption keyed to religious-doctrine distinctions = denominational discrimination). Religious charter school (Oklahoma, 4–4 affirmance, 2025) — no precedent.
- **Free Exercise:** neutral, generally applicable laws need only rational basis (*Employment Division v. Smith* (U.S. 1990)). Not generally applicable if it permits **individualized exemptions** (*Fulton v. Philadelphia* (U.S. 2021)) or treats **any comparable secular activity** more favorably (*Tandon v. Newsom* (U.S. 2021) — "most-favored-nation"); non-neutral if hostility (*Church of Lukumi*; *Masterpiece Cakeshop*). Then strict scrutiny. Excluding religious schools from generally available aid based on religious status or use violates Free Exercise (*Trinity Lutheran*; *Espinoza* (U.S. 2020); *Carson v. Makin* (U.S. 2022)). Parents' right to opt children out of instruction substantially interfering with religious upbringing → strict scrutiny (*Mahmoud v. Taylor* (U.S. 2025)). Ministerial exception (*Hosanna-Tabor*; *Our Lady of Guadalupe*). Title VII "undue hardship" = substantial increased costs (*Groff v. DeJoy* (U.S. 2023)). RFRA (federal) strict scrutiny; RLUIPA for prisoners/land use.

## Takings
- **Per se takings:** permanent physical occupation (*Loretto*); physical appropriation incl. temporary access regulations (*Cedar Point Nursery v. Hassid* (U.S. 2021) — union access = per se taking); total wipeout of economic value (*Lucas* (U.S. 1992)) unless background nuisance/property principles. **Regulatory:** *Penn Central* (economic impact, investment-backed expectations, character). Denominator = parcel as a whole (*Murr v. Wisconsin*). Temporary moratoria not per se (*Tahoe-Sierra*).
- **Exactions:** essential nexus + rough proportionality (*Nollan*; *Dolan*), incl. monetary exactions (*Koontz*) and legislative permit fees (*Sheetz v. County of El Dorado* (U.S. 2024)).
- **Public use:** broad — economic development OK (*Kelo* (U.S. 2005)). Just compensation = FMV. Direct federal suit available without state exhaustion (*Knick* (U.S. 2019)). Retaining surplus equity from tax foreclosure = taking (*Tyler v. Hennepin County* (U.S. 2023)).

## Contracts Clause & other limits
- **Contracts Clause** (states only, retroactive impairment): private contracts — substantial impairment → significant and legitimate public purpose + reasonable and appropriate means (*Energy Reserves*; *Sveen v. Melin* (U.S. 2018)); public contracts — stricter (reasonable and necessary; *U.S. Trust*). **Ex post facto** — criminal only; **bills of attainder** — both levels.
- **Citizenship:** can't be involuntarily stripped (*Afroyim*).

## Traps
- Standing in a con law fact pattern is often the threshold answer; check each plaintiff and relief.
- DCC: if the state is the **market participant**, discrimination is fine; if Congress authorized it, fine; Art IV P&I still applies to individuals (not corporations).
- 10th Am anti-commandeering vs spending conditions (voluntary) vs generally applicable laws (valid).
- Establishment: don't apply *Lemon* after 2022. Free Exercise: *Smith* unless *Fulton*/*Tandon* triggers.
- EP: disparate impact ≠ intent; affirmative action post-SFFA; alienage by federal government = rational basis.
- Chevron no longer governs; *Loper Bright* is the MBE-safe answer on agency interpretation deference post-2024.
