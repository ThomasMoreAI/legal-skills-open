# Real Property — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: estates/future interests, RAP, co-ownership, landlord-tenant, easements/covenants/servitudes, adverse possession, deeds and recording, mortgages and foreclosure, land-sale contracts, zoning.
Scope: MBE/MEE rule first; NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified: RPAPL 501, 522, 543, GOL 7-108(1-a), RPL 462 checked on nysenate.gov (2026-09); other doctrine from model knowledge; numbers marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Present estates
- **Fee simple determinable** ("so long as," "until") → possibility of reverter, automatic. **Fee simple subject to condition subsequent** ("but if," "provided that" + right of entry) → grantor must act. **Fee simple subject to executory limitation** → divests in favor of third party.
- **Life estate:** waste — voluntary (affirmative), permissive (failure to repair, pay taxes/interest up to income), ameliorative (majority modern: allowed if value increases and no remainderman objection / changed conditions). Open-mines doctrine.
- **Fee tail:** abolished in most states. **NY:** abolished; words that would create a fee tail create a fee simple (EPTL 6-1.2, confirm before use).
- **Traps:** ambiguous language → courts prefer FSSCS over FSD. Restraints on alienation: disabling restraints on fee void; forfeiture restraints on fee void; reasonable use restrictions and rights of first refusal generally valid.

## Future interests and RAP
- **Remainders:** vested (indefeasibly; subject to open; subject to complete divestment) vs. contingent (unascertained taker or condition precedent). Executory interests: shifting (from grantee) and springing (from grantor).
- **Rules:** destructibility of contingent remainders — abolished almost everywhere. **Shelley's Case** — abolished majority. **Worthier Title** — rule of construction majority. **NY:** Rule in Shelley's case abolished; Doctrine of Worthier Title abolished for lifetime and testamentary dispositions (EPTL 6-5.9, confirm before use); destructibility abolished (EPTL 6-5.10, confirm before use).
- **RAP (common law):** interest must vest, if at all, within 21 years after the death of a life in being at creation. Applies to contingent remainders, executory interests, vested remainders subject to open (class gifts: all-or-nothing), options in gross, rights of first refusal (split). Not to: reversions, possibilities of reverter, rights of entry, vested remainders, charity-to-charity gifts. Classic traps: fertile octogenarian, unborn widow, slothful executor/administrative contingency, age contingency > 21.
- **Reform (majority/USRAP):** wait-and-see (90 years) and cy pres/reformation.
- **NY:** EPTL 9-1.1(b) — no estate valid unless it must vest, if at all, not later than 21 years after one or more lives in being at creation, plus any period of gestation. **No wait-and-see** — NY judges validity at creation on what might happen. Savings provisions: EPTL 9-1.2 (age contingency exceeding 21 reduced to 21 where that saves it), 9-1.3 (presume intent to create valid interest; administrative contingency presumed to occur within 21 years; spouse presumed to be a life in being — unborn widow; woman over 55 presumed unable to bear a child — fertile octogenarian). EPTL 9-1.1(a) — separate **rule against suspension of the power of alienation** (lives in being + 21 years); NY applies it to trusts where no one can convey absolute fee. Commercial options: *Symphony Space v. Pergola Properties* (N.Y. 1996) — RAP applies to options to purchase in gross even between commercial parties; options appurtenant to a lease are exempt.

## Concurrent ownership
- **Joint tenancy:** four unities (time, title, interest, possession) + clear right of survivorship language. Severance: conveyance by one JT (converts to TIC as to grantee); mortgage — title theory severs, **lien theory does not** (majority; if the mortgagor dies first, the survivor takes free of the lien — *Harms v. Sprague* (Ill. 1984)); partition.
- **Tenancy by the entirety:** married couples; no unilateral severance; creditors of one spouse cannot reach; divorce converts to TIC.
- **Co-tenant rights:** each may possess all; no rent owed to others absent ouster; adverse possession against cotenant requires ouster; contribution for taxes/mortgage (necessary carrying costs), not improvements (except at partition); accounting for third-party rents.
- **NY:** EPTL 6-2.2(a) — disposition to two or more persons creates a **tenancy in common** unless expressly declared a joint tenancy. EPTL 6-2.2(b) — disposition to **spouses** creates a **tenancy by the entirety** unless expressly declared otherwise; extends to cooperative apartment shares (6-2.2(c)). Divorce converts TBE to TIC. NY partition: RPAPL Art. 9 (heirs-property protections, RPAPL 993, confirm before use). NY TBE: each spouse's interest may be reached by a creditor subject to the other's survivorship right (NY differs from pure-immunity states; confirm before use).

## Landlord-tenant
- **Tenancies:** term of years (no notice to end), periodic (notice = one period, year-to-year = 6 months at common law), at will, at sufferance. Holdover: landlord may evict or bind to new periodic tenancy (majority: year-to-year if original ≥1 yr).
- **Tenant duties:** pay rent; not commit waste; common law no duty to repair beyond avoiding waste. Abandonment: landlord may accept surrender, re-let on tenant's account, or (common law) leave vacant and sue — modern majority imposes **duty to mitigate**.
- **Landlord duties:** deliver possession (majority English rule: actual possession); quiet enjoyment; constructive eviction (substantial interference by landlord, notice, tenant vacates within reasonable time); implied warranty of habitability (residential; tenant may withhold, repair-and-deduct, or damages; non-waivable). Retaliatory eviction barred.
- **Assignment vs. sublease:** assignee in privity of estate (liable for rent while in possession); sublessee not in privity with landlord. Original tenant remains liable in privity of contract. *Dumpor's Case* (consent once = waiver) — majority rejects now.
- **Tort liability:** landlord liable for latent defects known, common areas, negligent repairs, short-term furnished, public-use premises.
- **NY:** RPL 235-b — warranty of habitability in every residential lease; non-waivable (*Park West Management Corp. v. Mitchell* (N.Y. 1979)). **HSTPA 2019:** GOL 7-108(1-a) — security deposit ≤ **one month's rent**; landlord must offer pre-occupancy inspection; within **14 days** after tenant vacates, return deposit with itemized statement or **forfeit any right to retain any portion**. RPL 227-e — residential landlord must in good faith mitigate damages if tenant vacates in violation of the lease (legislatively rejecting *Holy Properties v. Kenneth Cole Productions* (N.Y. 1995) no-mitigation rule for residential; commercial leases in NY still: no duty to mitigate). RPL 238-a — application/background-check fee capped at lesser of actual cost or \$20; late fee capped at lesser of \$50 or 5% of monthly rent, only if rent is more than 5 days late. RPL 226-c — notice of non-renewal or rent increase ≥5%: 30 days (occupancy <1 yr), 60 days (1–2 yrs), 90 days (≥2 yrs). RPL 232-c — landlord's acceptance of rent from a holdover (term > 1 month) creates a **month-to-month** tenancy, not year-to-year. RPL 234 — landlord's attorney-fee clause is reciprocal. RPL 227-a — tenant 62+ entering certain senior/health care housing may terminate lease (confirm age before use). RPL 223-b — retaliation barred. RPL 226-b — residential tenant (4+ unit building) has right to sublet with landlord consent not unreasonably withheld; assignment may be refused without cause (landlord must then release tenant on request, confirm before use). NY eviction: summary proceedings, RPAPL Art. 7; self-help against residential occupants of 30+ days is unlawful (RPAPL 768).

## Easements, profits, licenses
- **Creation:** express (SOF writing); implication from prior use (common ownership, apparent and continuous use, reasonable necessity at severance); necessity (strict necessity at severance, ends when necessity ends); prescription (open, notorious, adverse, continuous for statutory period — no exclusivity requirement); estoppel.
- **Appurtenant** runs with dominant estate automatically; **in gross** — commercial ones transferable.
- **Scope:** reasonable development of dominant use; overburdening → injunction, not termination.
- **Termination:** merger (unity of title), release (writing), abandonment (nonuse + intent — mere nonuse insufficient), estoppel, prescription by servient owner, end of necessity, condemnation, sale to BFP without notice.
- **Licenses:** revocable unless coupled with an interest or irrevocable by estoppel (licensee's substantial expenditure in reliance).
- **NY:** prescriptive period 10 years (tracks CPLR 212(a)). An easement created by **grant** is not extinguished by nonuse alone; abandonment requires clear intent (*Gerbig v. Zumpano* (N.Y. 1960)). Lawn mowing/maintenance across a line is deemed permissive (RPAPL 543(2)) — defeats prescriptive/adverse claims based on such acts.

## Covenants and equitable servitudes
- **Real covenant (damages)** — burden runs: writing, intent, touch and concern, **horizontal privity** (grantor-grantee or landlord-tenant), vertical privity, notice. Benefit runs: writing, intent, touch and concern, vertical privity (relaxed).
- **Equitable servitude (injunction):** writing (or common scheme), intent, touch and concern, notice; **no privity** needed. **Common scheme/implied reciprocal servitudes:** developer's general plan + notice (actual, record, inquiry) binds lots sold without the restriction in their deeds.
- **Defenses:** changed neighborhood conditions (must make benefit essentially valueless — changes outside the subdivision rarely suffice), abandonment/acquiescence, unclean hands, laches, merger. Racial covenants unenforceable (*Shelley v. Kraemer* (U.S. 1948)).
- **NY:** NY requires horizontal privity for the burden of a covenant at law; affirmative covenants to pay money (HOA dues) run under *Neponsit Property Owners' Ass'n v. Emigrant Industrial Savings Bank* (N.Y. 1938). RPAPL 1951 — court may extinguish a restriction that is of no actual and substantial benefit (by reason of changed conditions or otherwise), awarding damages to the benefited party.

## Adverse possession
- **Elements (majority):** actual, open and notorious, exclusive, continuous, hostile/adverse for the statutory period. Tacking allowed with privity (voluntary transfer). Disabilities (minority, insanity, imprisonment) of the owner at **inception** extend the period. No AP against government land (majority). Color of title → constructive possession of whole described parcel.
- **State of mind split:** majority objective (hostility = without owner's permission); minority good-faith or aggressive-trespasser.
- **NY:** 10-year period (CPLR 212(a); RPAPL 501). **2008 amendments (effective 2008-07-07):** RPAPL 501 now requires **claim of right** — "a reasonable basis for the belief that the property belongs to the adverse possessor" (not required where the owner cannot be identified in county records and cannot be located by reasonable means); this legislatively overrules *Walling v. Przybylo* (N.Y. 2006), which had held actual knowledge of true ownership not fatal. Title vests automatically at the end of the period, so pre-2008 law governs claims that fully ripened before the amendment (App. Div. consensus; confirm before use). RPAPL 511–512: possession under a written instrument/judgment (cultivated, improved, enclosed). **RPAPL 522:** without a written instrument, land is possessed only where (1) acts sufficiently open to put a reasonably diligent owner on notice, or (2) protected by a substantial enclosure (except as limited by 543(1)). **RPAPL 543:** de minimis non-structural encroachments — fences, hedges, shrubbery, plantings, sheds, non-structural walls — are **deemed permissive and non-adverse**; lawn mowing or similar maintenance across the line is deemed permissive and non-adverse.
- **Traps:** a fence alone in NY does not establish adverse possession of the strip behind it. Owner's later disability does not toll.

## Conveyancing
- **Land-sale contract:** SOF writing; implied **marketable title** at closing (free of reasonable doubt: encumbrances, defects in chain, zoning violations — not zoning restrictions themselves); seller need not have marketable title until closing. Time not of essence unless stated/circumstances (**NY:** time-of-essence may be imposed by clear, unequivocal notice giving a reasonable time to perform).
- **Equitable conversion:** majority — buyer bears risk of loss after contract (most courts credit seller's insurance proceeds against the price). **NY:** GOL 5-1311 (Uniform Vendor and Purchaser Risk Act) — risk stays with **seller** until legal title or possession passes to buyer; if all or a material part is destroyed without buyer's fault, seller cannot enforce and buyer recovers deposit; immaterial part → enforce with abatement.
- **Seller disclosure:** majority — duty to disclose known latent material defects (residential). **NY:** caveat emptor with exceptions for active concealment and conditions created by seller not discoverable by buyer (*Stambovsky v. Ackley* (App. Div. 1st Dep't 1991)). RPL 462 — Property Condition Disclosure Statement required for 1–4 family residential sales; the former **\$500 credit** alternative was **eliminated** by a 2023 amendment (effective March 2024, confirm date before use); current text mandates the statement.
- **Deeds:** delivery = present intent to pass title (physical transfer to grantee or escrow presumptive); conditional delivery to grantee → absolute (majority). **Death escrow:** deed handed to a third party with instructions to deliver at grantor's death, grantor retaining no power of recall = valid present delivery (grantee holds a future interest; a later deed by grantor conveys nothing). General warranty (present: seisin, right to convey, against encumbrances — breached at delivery; future: warranty, quiet enjoyment, further assurances — breached on eviction, run with land), special warranty, quitclaim. **Merger:** contract obligations merge into deed except collateral promises. **NY:** statutory short forms RPL 258 (full covenant and warranty; bargain and sale with/without covenant against grantor's acts; quitclaim); covenant of seisin etc. per RPL 253 (confirm before use).
- **Recording acts:** race (first to record), notice (subsequent BFP without notice wins regardless of recording), race-notice (subsequent BFP without notice who records first). Notice: actual, record (chain-of-title; wild deeds and later-recorded prior-grantor deeds split), inquiry (possession). Shelter rule: transferee of BFP takes BFP's protection. Donees and devisees not protected (no value).
- **NY:** RPL 291 — **race-notice**: an unrecorded conveyance is void against a subsequent purchaser in good faith for valuable consideration **whose conveyance is first duly recorded**. Mortgages are "conveyances." Lis pendens (CPLR 6501) gives constructive notice to later purchasers/encumbrancers. NY applies inquiry notice from visible possession.
- **Estoppel by deed / after-acquired title:** grantor who later acquires title → passes to grantee (but not against a later BFP from grantor in some states).

## Mortgages
- **Theories:** lien (majority; mortgagee has lien only, no right to possession before foreclosure), title, intermediate. **NY:** lien theory.
- **Transfer of mortgaged property:** "subject to" — grantee not personally liable; "assumes" — grantee primarily liable, original mortgagor secondarily (surety). Due-on-sale clauses enforceable (Garn-St Germain, 12 U.S.C. §1701j-3, exempts certain residential transfers — to spouse/child, on death to relative, into living trust).
- **Transfer of the note:** mortgage follows the note. Holder in due course takes free of personal defenses. **NY:** a plaintiff with standing is the holder or assignee of the note at commencement; the mortgage passes as an incident (*Aurora Loan Services v. Taylor* (N.Y. 2015)).
- **Priorities:** first in time, subject to recording act. **PMM** (purchase-money mortgage) beats prior judgment liens against mortgagor; between PMMs, seller's PMM beats third-party lender's. Future advances: obligatory advances keep original priority; optional advances lose priority to intervening liens of which lender has actual notice (majority). Modification increasing burden → junior lienholder primes the increase. Subordination agreements.
- **Foreclosure:** judicial sale; senior interests survive, junior interests wiped out (must be joined — omitted junior retains its interest). Proceeds: expenses, then foreclosing mortgage, then juniors in order, then mortgagor. Equity of redemption until sale; clogging prohibited. Deficiency judgment (subject to anti-deficiency statutes). Deed in lieu.
- **NY:** foreclosure is **judicial** (RPAPL Art. 13); nonjudicial power-of-sale foreclosure is available only under RPAPL Art. 14 and rarely used (confirm before use). **RPAPL 1304** — 90-day pre-foreclosure notice for home loans; **CPLR 3408** — mandatory settlement conference in residential foreclosures; RPAPL 1303 notices. No post-sale statutory redemption. **RPAPL 1371** — deficiency judgment must be sought by motion within **90 days** after the sale's consummation (delivery of deed); deficiency = debt minus the **higher of** fair market value or sale price; failure to move → proceeds deemed full satisfaction. Limitations: **CPLR 213(4)** — 6 years; accrues for each installment when due and for the whole debt on acceleration. **Foreclosure Abuse Prevention Act (2022):** lender cannot unilaterally de-accelerate to reset the limitations period (abrogating *Freedom Mortgage Corp. v. Engel* (N.Y. 2021) in that respect); CPLR 205-a limits refiling. Equitable mortgage: deed absolute intended as security is a mortgage (RPL 320).
- **Installment land contract:** forfeiture clauses — modern courts limit (restitution, grace periods, treat as mortgage).

## Zoning and land use
- **Takings:** per se — permanent physical occupation (*Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV* (U.S. 1982)), total regulatory wipeout (*Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council* (U.S. 1992)); otherwise *Penn Central Transp. Co. v. New York City* (U.S. 1978) balancing (economic impact, investment-backed expectations, character). Exactions: essential nexus (*Nollan* (U.S. 1987)) + rough proportionality (*Dolan* (U.S. 1994)), applies to monetary exactions (*Koontz* (U.S. 2013)).
- **Nonconforming use:** lawful before ordinance; may continue but not expand; lost by abandonment/destruction; amortization valid if reasonable.
- **Variances:** use variance (unnecessary hardship) vs. area variance (practical difficulty). **NY:** Town Law 267-b / Village Law 7-712-b / General City Law 81-b — **use variance** requires unnecessary hardship: (1) cannot realize a reasonable return (financial evidence), (2) hardship unique to the property, (3) will not alter essential character of neighborhood, (4) hardship not self-created. **Area variance**: board balances benefit to applicant against detriment to community using five factors (character change, feasible alternative, substantiality, environmental effect, self-created — relevant but not dispositive).
- **Traps:** spot zoning; conditional zoning; special-use permits are not variances (use is permitted if standards met). Challenges to NY zoning board determinations: CPLR Art. 78 within 30 days of filing the decision (Town Law 267-c, confirm before use).
