# Business Associations — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: agency, partnerships (general, LP, LLP), LLCs, corporations — formation, promoters, veil piercing, shareholder rights, derivative suits, director/officer duties, conflicts, indemnification, mergers, appraisal, dissolution.
Scope: MBE/MEE rule first (Restatement (Third) Agency, RUPA, ULLCA/majority, MBCA/Delaware); NY distinctions flagged **NY:** (NY Partnership Law, LLC Law, BCL). Verified: BCL 623, 630, 910 and LLCL 609(c) checked on nysenate.gov (2026-09); other doctrine from model knowledge; items marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Agency
- **Formation:** consent + control + acting on principal's behalf; no writing needed (equal dignities rule: authority to sign a SOF contract must be in writing in some states — **NY:** GOL 5-703(2) requires agent's authority to convey real property/lease > 1 yr to be in writing).
- **Actual authority:** express or implied (reasonably believes from principal's manifestations to agent; incidental, custom, prior acquiescence). Terminated by revocation, renunciation, death/incapacity of principal (R3d: effective when agent has notice).
- **Apparent authority:** principal's manifestation **to the third party** + third party's reasonable belief. Lingering authority after termination until third party has notice. **NY:** apparent authority must be traced to the principal's words or conduct, not the agent's (*Hallock v. State of New York* (N.Y. 1984)); third party must reasonably rely and has duty of inquiry when circumstances are suspicious.
- **Inherent agency power / estoppel / ratification:** ratification requires knowledge of material facts, affirmance of the entire transaction, principal existed and had capacity at the time and at ratification; relates back.
- **Contract liability of agent:** disclosed principal → agent not a party; unidentified (partially disclosed) → agent is a party; undisclosed → agent liable, principal liable once discovered (election rule varies). Agent without authority breaches implied warranty of authority.
- **Tort liability:** respondeat superior for employees acting within scope (kind of work hired to do, within time/space limits, at least partly to serve employer); frolic vs. detour; intentional torts within scope if natural incident or authorized force. **NY:** *Riviello v. Waldron* (N.Y. 1979) — scope factors incl. foreseeability of the act. Independent contractors: principal not liable except nondelegable duties, inherently dangerous activities, negligent selection, apparent agency. Employee vs. IC: right to control manner and means.
- **Agent duties:** loyalty (no secret profits, no competing, no usurping), obedience, care; principal may recover secret profits (disgorgement).

## General partnerships
- **Formation:** association of two or more persons to carry on as co-owners a business for profit — **intent to form a partnership not required**; profit-sharing raises presumption (not for debt repayment, wages, rent, annuity, interest, sale of goodwill).
- **RUPA:** partnership is an **entity**; partner property vs. partnership property; partners are agents for apparently carrying on business in the ordinary course; jointly and severally liable for all obligations, but creditor must **exhaust partnership assets** first (RUPA 307); incoming partner not personally liable for pre-admission debts. Default rules: equal share of profits (losses follow profits), equal management, ordinary matters by majority, extraordinary matters and amendment of partnership agreement unanimous, no salary except winding up, no new partner without unanimous consent. Transferable interest = distributions only; charging order.
- **Dissociation vs. dissolution (RUPA):** partner may dissociate at any time (wrongful if breaching agreement or before end of term); at-will partnership dissolves on notice of express will to withdraw; term partnership continues unless half remaining partners agree to wind up within 90 days. Buyout under RUPA 701.
- **Fiduciary duties (RUPA 404):** loyalty (account for benefits, no adverse dealing, no competition) and care (gross negligence/reckless/intentional standard); duty of good faith and fair dealing.
- **NY:** NY still uses the **UPA**-based Partnership Law (NY never adopted RUPA). Aggregate theory: Partnership Law 10 definition; PL 20 partner as agent; **PL 26(a): partners are jointly and severally liable for torts/breaches of trust, but only jointly for debts and contract obligations**; no exhaustion requirement codified as in RUPA (confirm before use). **Dissolution** occurs on any partner's ceasing to be associated (PL 60, 62) — e.g., express will of any partner in an at-will partnership, death, bankruptcy; winding up follows unless continuation agreement. Incoming partner liable for prior debts only out of partnership property (PL 27). Fiduciary standard: "punctilio of an honor the most sensitive" (*Meinhard v. Salmon* (N.Y. 1928)) — duty to disclose a new opportunity arising from the venture.

## LPs and LLPs
- **LP:** at least one general partner (personal liability) and one limited partner; certificate filed. **ULPA 2001:** limited partner not liable even if participating in control. **RULPA (1985) and NY:** limited partner who participates in control may be liable to those who reasonably believed it was a GP (NY Partnership Law Art. 8-A, 121-303, with safe-harbor list; confirm before use). **NY:** LP must publish within 120 days (PL 121-201) (confirm before use).
- **LLP:** registration shields partners from vicarious liability. **NY:** PL 26(b) — LLP partners not liable for partnership debts or others' torts; PL 26(c) — each partner remains liable for own negligent or wrongful acts and those of persons under his **direct supervision and control**. NY LLPs are typically professional firms; registration PL 121-1500, with publication requirement within 120 days (confirm before use).

## LLCs
- **Majority rule:** members not personally liable for LLC obligations; operating agreement governs; default is member-managed; members of member-managed LLC are agents; manager-managed — members are not agents by status. Fiduciary duties of managers/managing members (loyalty, care); may be limited in agreement (Delaware permits elimination; ULLCA restricts). Veil piercing available (often with less weight on formalities). Member dissociation does not dissolve (modern).
- **NY LLC Law:**
  - **LLCL 206 publication:** within **120 days** after articles of organization are filed, publish a copy or notice once a week for **six successive weeks** in two newspapers designated by the county clerk, then file a certificate of publication. **Failure → suspension of authority to carry on business** (not loss of limited liability); cured by later compliance; contracts made during suspension remain valid.
  - **LLCL 417:** members must adopt a written operating agreement within **90 days** after filing articles (confirm before use).
  - **LLCL 401:** member-managed unless articles provide for managers. **LLCL 402:** default voting by majority in interest (pro rata to capital contributions/percentages per agreement); certain acts need majority in interest.
  - **LLCL 409:** managers perform duties in good faith and with the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would use; LLCL 417 allows exculpation except bad faith, intentional misconduct, knowing violation of law, improper personal gain.
  - **LLCL 609(a):** members not liable solely by reason of membership. **LLCL 609(c): the ten members with the largest percentage ownership interests** (measurement date: confirm before use) are jointly and severally liable for unpaid wages/salaries of employees; employee must give written notice within **180 days** after termination of services; action within **90 days** after return of execution unsatisfied against the LLC (parallel to BCL 630).
  - **LLCL 702 judicial dissolution:** when it is **not reasonably practicable to carry on the business in conformity with the articles or operating agreement**; NY LLC Law has **no oppression-based dissolution** comparable to BCL 1104-a.
  - **Derivative suits:** LLC members may sue derivatively (*Tzolis v. Wolff* (N.Y. 2008)).
  - **Foreign LLCs:** unauthorized foreign LLC doing business in NY may not maintain an action in NY courts until authorized (LLCL 808).

## Corporations — formation and promoters
- **Formation:** certificate (NY) / articles (MBCA) filed with the Secretary of State; existence begins on filing. **NY:** BCL 402 contents; BCL 403 — filing by the Department of State is conclusive evidence of formation except in a proceeding by the state.
- **Defective incorporation:** de facto corporation (good-faith colorable attempt + use of corporate powers) and corporation by estoppel; MBCA 2.04 imposes liability on those who purport to act as a corporation knowing there was no incorporation. **NY:** retains de facto and estoppel doctrines in some cases; confirm current App. Div. treatment before use.
- **Promoters:** personally liable on pre-incorporation contracts unless the contract clearly shows the other party looked only to the future corporation; corporation becomes liable only by **adoption** (express or implied by accepting benefits); promoter remains liable after adoption until **novation**. Promoters owe fiduciary duties to the corporation and to later subscribers (secret profits).
- **Ultra vires:** largely abolished; may be raised by shareholders to enjoin, corporation against officers, or AG. **NY:** BCL 203.
- **Foreign corporations:** **NY:** BCL 1312 — an unauthorized foreign corporation doing business in NY cannot maintain an action in NY until it obtains authority and pays fees/taxes; can defend.

## Stock and finance
- **Consideration:** par/no par; watered stock liability for shares issued below par. **NY:** BCL 504 — consideration may be money, other property, labor or services actually received/performed, or (post-1998) binding obligations to pay or perform future services (confirm before use); board's good-faith valuation conclusive absent fraud. BCL 505 — rights and options to purchase shares may be issued on terms fixed by the board (confirm shareholder-approval details before use).
- **Preemptive rights:** **NY:** BCL 622 — for corporations formed after 1998-02-22, **no preemptive rights unless the certificate grants them** (opt-in); older corporations keep statutory preemptive rights unless certificate denies them (confirm before use).
- **Distributions:** MBCA — equity insolvency + balance-sheet test. **NY:** BCL 510 — dividends only out of surplus (net assets exceeding stated capital) and not when insolvent or would render insolvent; **BCL 719** — directors who vote for an unlawful dividend are jointly and severally liable to the corporation (defense: good-faith reliance under BCL 717, or dissent recorded).

## Shareholders
- **Voting:** quorum majority of shares; directors elected by plurality (**NY:** BCL 614); other matters majority of votes cast (**NY:** BCL 614(b) — majority of votes cast unless otherwise required). Cumulative voting only if certificate provides (**NY:** BCL 618). Written consent: MBCA/NY default **unanimous** (NY BCL 615(a); certificate may permit less than unanimous). Proxies revocable unless coupled with interest (**NY:** BCL 609). Voting trusts and voting agreements (**NY:** BCL 620–621).
- **Shareholder agreements (close corporations):** **NY:** BCL 620(b) — a provision in the certificate that improperly restricts the board is valid if all incorporators or all holders of record authorize it and shares are not listed/traded (confirm before use). BCL 616 — supermajority quorum/vote provisions must be in the certificate.
- **Inspection:** **NY:** BCL 624 — shareholder of record for at least **six months** or holder of at least **5%** of any class may, on 5 days' written demand, inspect minutes of shareholder meetings and the record of shareholders; corporation may require affidavit that purpose is not other than the corporation's business and no sale of shareholder lists within 5 years. Annual balance sheet and P&L on request to any shareholder (BCL 624(e)). Common-law right of inspection for proper purpose (books and records) survives.
- **Personal liability:** shareholders generally not liable. **NY BCL 630:** the **ten largest shareholders** (by fair value of beneficial interest) of a domestic corporation, or a foreign corporation when the unpaid services were performed in NY, **whose shares are not listed on a national securities exchange or regularly quoted over the counter**, are jointly and severally liable for **all debts, wages or salaries** due employees for services; employee must give written notice within **180 days after termination of services** (60 days after examining shareholder records, if examined during that period); action within **90 days after return of an execution unsatisfied** against the corporation. "Wages" includes benefits and pension/insurance contributions.
- **Piercing the veil:** majority — domination/alter ego + misuse (fraud, undercapitalization, commingling, disregard of formalities) causing injustice. More often for tort creditors and close corporations/subsidiaries. **NY:** (1) owners exercised **complete domination** of the corporation with respect to the transaction attacked, and (2) such domination was used to commit a **fraud or wrong** against the plaintiff resulting in injury (*Matter of Morris v. New York State Dep't of Taxation & Finance* (N.Y. 1993)); undercapitalization alone not enough — fleet of one-taxi corporations each carrying minimum insurance not pierced absent personal-capacity dealings (*Walkovszky v. Carlton* (N.Y. 1966)); enterprise liability among sibling corporations possible.

## Derivative suits
- **Standing:** contemporaneous ownership (at the time of the wrong and through suit), adequately represents corporation. Recovery goes to corporation.
- **Demand:** MBCA 7.42 — **universal demand** (written demand + 90 days unless rejected earlier or irreparable injury). Delaware — demand excused if futile (*Zuckerberg* (Del. 2021) three-part director-by-director test).
- **NY:** BCL 626(b) — plaintiff must have been a shareholder at the time of bringing the action and at the time of the transaction (or shares devolved by operation of law). BCL 626(c) — complaint must set forth **with particularity** the efforts to secure initiation of the action by the board or the reasons for not making the effort. **Demand futility — *Marx v. Akers* (N.Y. 1996):** excused if the complaint alleges with particularity that (1) a majority of the board is interested in the challenged transaction (self-interest or controlled by an interested party), (2) the directors failed to inform themselves to a degree reasonably necessary about the transaction, or (3) the challenged transaction was so egregious on its face that it could not have been the product of sound business judgment. BCL 626(d) — **court approval** required to discontinue, compromise, or settle; notice to shareholders if court directs. **BCL 627 security for expenses:** unless plaintiff(s) hold **5%** or more of any class or shares with fair value over **\$50,000**, corporation may require security for reasonable expenses. **Special litigation committee:** business judgment protects an SLC's decision to terminate if members are disinterested/independent and used adequate, appropriate investigative procedures; courts do not review the substance (*Auerbach v. Bennett* (N.Y. 1979)).
- **Direct vs. derivative:** harm to shareholder individually (voting rights, dividends declared, preemptive rights) is direct; harm to corporation derivative.

## Directors and officers
- **Board acts** at a meeting with quorum (majority of entire board default) by majority of those present, or unanimous written consent (**NY:** BCL 708). Notice for special meetings; waiver by attendance without protest.
- **Duty of care:** **NY BCL 717** — in good faith and with that degree of care which an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would use under similar circumstances; may rely on information, reports, and opinions of officers, counsel, accountants, and committees believed reliable and competent. **Business judgment rule:** courts will not second-guess good-faith, informed decisions without conflict (*Auerbach v. Bennett* (N.Y. 1979); applied to co-op boards, *Levandusky v. One Fifth Avenue Apartment Corp.* (N.Y. 1990)). Delaware: gross negligence standard for care; *Smith v. Van Gorkom* (Del. 1985). Oversight/*Caremark* (Del. 1996) duty.
- **Exculpation:** DGCL 102(b)(7); **NY BCL 402(b)** — certificate may eliminate or limit directors' personal liability for damages for breach of duty **except** for acts in bad faith, intentional misconduct, knowing violation of law, personal gain of a financial profit or other advantage to which not legally entitled, or BCL 719 violations.
- **Duty of loyalty — interested transactions:** **NY BCL 713:** a transaction between the corporation and a director (or an entity in which a director is a director/officer or has a substantial financial interest) is **not void or voidable by reason of the interest** if (1) the material facts of the interest are disclosed in good faith or known to the board and it is approved by a vote sufficient without counting the interested director's vote (or, if the disinterested directors' votes are insufficient to constitute an act of the board under BCL 708, by **unanimous vote of the disinterested directors**), or (2) disclosed to shareholders entitled to vote and approved by them. **If neither**, the transaction may be avoided unless the party asserting validity proves it was **fair and reasonable to the corporation** when approved. Interested directors count toward quorum (713(c)). MBCA Subchapter F / DGCL 144 — similar safe harbors (DGCL 144 amended 2025, confirm before use).
- **Corporate opportunity:** majority tests — line of business (Delaware *Guth v. Loft* (Del. 1939) / *Broz* (Del. 1996)), ALI — must first offer to the corporation and disclose. **NY:** "tangible expectancy" test (App. Div. case law, confirm before use).
- **Controlling shareholders:** fiduciary duties to minority; entire fairness for self-dealing (freeze-outs). **NY:** freeze-out merger must be fair (fair dealing + fair price) and have an independent corporate purpose (*Alpert v. 28 Williams Street Corp.* (N.Y. 1984)).
- **Loans to directors:** **NY:** BCL 714 — loans to directors require shareholder approval (disinterested shareholders) (confirm before use).
- **Officers:** authority from bylaws/board; president has apparent authority for ordinary business. Removal with or without cause by board (**NY:** BCL 716). Directors removal: shareholders for cause; without cause if certificate/bylaws allow (**NY:** BCL 706; cumulative-voting protection).

## Indemnification (NY BCL 721–726; MBCA 8.50–8.59)
- **Mandatory (BCL 723(a)):** director/officer who was **successful, on the merits or otherwise**, in defense of a covered action is entitled to indemnification.
- **Permissive — third-party actions (BCL 722(a)):** judgments, fines, settlements, reasonable expenses if acted in **good faith** for a purpose reasonably believed to be in (or not opposed to) the corporation's best interests, and in criminal actions had no reasonable cause to believe conduct unlawful. Termination by conviction/plea/settlement does not itself create presumption against.
- **Derivative actions (BCL 722(c)):** only settlement amounts and **reasonable expenses** (not judgments), good-faith standard; **no indemnity** for a threatened action, a pending action that is **settled or otherwise disposed of**, or any matter on which the person was **adjudged liable** to the corporation, unless and only to the extent a court determines on application that indemnity is fair and reasonable.
- **Authorization (BCL 723(b)):** by disinterested quorum of the board, independent counsel opinion, or shareholders. **Court-ordered (BCL 724).** **Advancement** of expenses on undertaking to repay (BCL 723(c), 725). **Non-exclusivity (BCL 721):** bylaws/agreements may expand but no indemnity if a judgment establishes acts committed in bad faith or deliberate dishonesty material to the cause, or personal gain not legally entitled. **Insurance (BCL 726)** permitted with limits.

## Fundamental changes
- **Merger/consolidation:** board adopts plan + shareholder approval. **NY BCL 903:** for corporations formed **after 1998-02-22**, **majority of all outstanding shares entitled to vote**; for older corporations **two-thirds** of outstanding shares unless the certificate provides for majority. Same thresholds for **sale, lease, exchange of all or substantially all assets not in the usual course (BCL 909)**. **Short-form merger (BCL 905):** parent owning **90%** of each class may merge without subsidiary shareholder vote; minority gets appraisal. Surviving corporation takes all assets and liabilities by operation of law (BCL 906).
- **Appraisal — MBCA (MEE rule):** 13.02 — merger (if shareholder entitled to vote), share exchange, disposition of substantially all assets, certain amendments; **market-out** for shares listed on a national exchange or with 2,000+ holders and \$20M+ float, unless shareholders receive something other than cash/market shares (confirm thresholds before use). Procedure: notice of intent to demand payment **before the vote** and not voting in favor (13.21) → corporation's appraisal notice → shareholder demand and deposit of shares. **Fair value** immediately before the action, **no discount for lack of marketability or minority status** (13.01). Exclusivity (13.40): after shareholder approval, no challenge to the action except where (1) not authorized and approved per the statute, articles, bylaws, or authorizing board resolution, (2) procured by fraud, material misrepresentation, or misleading omission, (3) an interested transaction not approved as a director's conflicting-interest transaction would be (8.62/8.63), or (4) approved by non-unanimous written consent without timely notice to the challenger (13.40(b); checked against MBCA-based enactments, Me. 13-C §1341 and N.C.G.S. 55-13-40).
- **Director conflicts — MBCA Subchapter F (8.60–8.63):** safe harbor if (a) qualified directors approve after required disclosure (existence and nature of the conflict plus all material facts), (b) qualified shares approve after disclosure, or (c) transaction fair to the corporation (burden on the interested director). Abstention alone is not disclosure.
- **Appraisal (dissenters') rights — NY BCL 910:** available for **merger or consolidation**, **sale/lease/exchange of all or substantially all assets** (not usual course; exceptions for certain cash sales with distribution within one year, confirm before use), **share exchange (BCL 913)**, and certain certificate amendments that adversely affect shares (BCL 806(b)(6)). **Market-out:** no appraisal in a merger for shares listed on a national securities exchange or designated as a national market system security on the record date. Surviving-corporation shareholders generally no appraisal (confirm scope before use).
- **Appraisal procedure — NY BCL 623:** shareholder must file a **written objection before the vote** (or at the meeting before the vote) — not required if shareholder was not given notice; must not vote in favor; file notice of election to dissent within **20 days** after the corporation gives notice of authorization; corporation makes a written offer within **15 days** after the election period expires or action consummated, whichever later (no later than 90 days after authorization); if no agreement, corporation brings special proceeding in supreme court within **20 days** after the agreement period; court fixes **fair value** as of the close of business on the day before authorization. **BCL 623(k) exclusivity:** appraisal excludes other remedies **except** an action for relief on the ground that the corporate action is **unlawful or fraudulent** as to the shareholder.
- **Amendment of certificate:** board + majority of outstanding shares (**NY:** BCL 803), class votes where class adversely affected (BCL 804).
- **Traps:** a shareholder who votes yes loses appraisal; asset buyer generally not liable for seller's debts (exceptions: assumption, de facto merger, mere continuation, fraud). Delaware appraisal (DGCL 262) does not cover sale of assets.

## Dissolution
- **Voluntary:** board + shareholders (**NY:** BCL 1001, majority of outstanding shares for post-1998 corporations; certificate may permit any shareholder to demand dissolution at will under BCL 1002).
- **Judicial — deadlock (NY BCL 1104):** holders of **one-half** of the voting shares may petition where directors are so divided they cannot act, shareholders are so divided they cannot elect directors, or internal dissension makes dissolution beneficial.
- **Judicial — oppression (NY BCL 1104-a):** holders of **20% or more** of all outstanding shares entitled to vote in an election of directors of a corporation **whose shares are not listed/traded on a securities market** may petition where (1) directors or those in control are guilty of **illegal, fraudulent, or oppressive** actions toward the complaining shareholders, or (2) property/assets are being looted, wasted, or diverted. **Oppression** = conduct that substantially defeats the **reasonable expectations** held by the minority in committing capital to the enterprise, viewed objectively (*Matter of Kemp & Beatley* (N.Y. 1984)) — e.g., termination of employment and cutoff of de facto dividends. Court considers whether liquidation is the only feasible means to protect the petitioner's reasonable return.
- **Buyout election (NY BCL 1118):** the corporation or other shareholders may elect to purchase the petitioner's shares at **fair value**; election as of right within **90 days** after the petition is filed (later with court leave); fair value determined as of the **day before the petition is filed**; courts may apply a discount for lack of marketability but **not a minority discount** (*Matter of Friedman v. Beway Realty Corp.* (N.Y. 1995)).
- **Winding up:** collect assets, pay creditors (known creditors notice; BCL 1007 notice to creditors, confirm deadline before use), distribute remainder to shareholders by preference.
