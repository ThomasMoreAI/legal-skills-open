## Statute and regulator map
- **Rule:** Cannabis Law (ch. 7-A, enacted by the MRTA, L. 2021, ch. 92, signed Mar. 31, 2021): art. 1 findings/definitions (§3); art. 2 Cannabis Control Board (CCB) and Office of Cannabis Management (OCM); art. 3 medical; art. 4 adult-use (§§61–89); art. 5 hemp; art. 6 general provisions (§125 general prohibitions, §136 illicit cannabis, §137 persons forbidden to traffic).
- **Art. 4 sections you will cite most:** §61–64 application/fees/selection; §67 ownership changes; §69 processor; §72 retail dispensary; **§73 microbusiness**; §74 delivery; §76 municipal notice; §78 record keeping and tracking; **§80 no retail interest for cultivators/processors/distributors**; §81 packaging/labeling; §82 lab testing; §85 retail provisions (ID check every sale, price tags, no alcohol); **§86 advertising**; **§87 SEE**; §89 regulations.
- **Who decides:** CCB votes on licenses and adopts regs; OCM staff (Executive Director, Chief Equity Officer, Trade Practices Bureau at TradePractices@ocm.ny.gov) administer. CCB resolutions and OCM advisory opinions are published on cannabis.ny.gov and are the fastest-moving layer.

## True party of interest (TPI), cross-ownership, control
- **Rule:** "True party of interest" (§118.1) covers owners at every level plus persons with a financial interest or controlling interest, and persons receiving payments above the Part 124 thresholds. "Controlling interest" (§118.1) = authority to order or direct management, operations, managers or policies; the definition lists specific powers (budget approval, contract management, hiring, asset control). "Sole control" (§118.1) = a person with real, substantial, and continuing ownership of at least 51%, who executes material contracts, directs operations, and holds voting control — the SEE/CAURD eligibility spine.
- **Thresholds (§124.4, LII text):** an agreement makes the counterparty a TPI if it restricts the licensee's buying or selling, is not arm's-length, lacks termination rights on due notice, or pays above **10% of gross revenue, 50% of net profit, or \$250,000 per calendar year** — the LII text (eff. 9/27/2023) sets the trigger at "the greater of" the three, i.e., the highest of them, based on actual or anticipated annual performance (confirm the formulation survived the 11/5/2025 amendments before use). §124.4(b) applies the same compensation thresholds to goods-and-services providers regardless of how the agreement is labeled.
- **§124.3:** (a) exempt providers (accountants, lawyers, security, contractors, landlords, financiers, non-cannabis goods) may serve many licensees but become TPIs if their interest deepens; (b) **non-exempt consulting/advisory/strategic services** for cultivation, processing, distribution or sale — a provider paid on anything other than a flat fee (revenue share, royalty, profit-based) may not have a goods-and-services agreement with a licensee in a **separate licensing tier**; (c) multiple agreements are **stacked** and held to the strictest prohibition of each.
- **Tier separation (§80; §§123.3(f), 123.5(i), 123.9(j)):** a retailer and its TPIs may not hold any interest in, or a goods-and-services agreement with, a cultivator, processor, distributor, microbusiness, RO, lab, or delivery licensee, and vice versa. OCM Bulletin #1 (3/5/2025) ordered such agreements terminated immediately.
- **Microbusiness (§73; §§123.11–.12):** limited cultivation + processing + distribution + delivery + retail of **its own** products only; may not hold any interest in another license; distributes only its own products, and only to dispensaries.
- **Type 3 processor / branding:** OCM offers "Type 3 – Flower" and a non-plant-touching **"Type 3 – Branding"** license that supplies IP/materials to a plant-touching processor under a **white-label agreement**. §123.5(c): a processor may white-label only for its TPIs or another licensee not otherwise barred. **Retail-branded products are prohibited unless the retailer is a microbusiness or an RO with dispensing (ROD)** (Bulletin #1).
- **Applies to a product when:** SaaS pricing is a % of GMV or sales; the vendor sells to both retailers and supply-side licensees; the vendor also consults (menu pricing, purchasing, marketing strategy); the vendor's contract gives it approval rights over budgets, vendors, hiring, or pricing; a founder or investor also sits in a licensee's cap table.
- **Open questions for counsel:** Is a pure-software subscription an "exempt" §124.3(a) service or "non-exempt" advisory once it recommends pricing or purchasing? Does a transaction-based fee count as "revenue share" under §124.3(b)? Does the vendor need TPI disclosure on the licensee's §120.18 filing?
- **Traps:** a percentage-of-sales SaaS fee plus customers in two tiers = §124.3(b) problem even if the dollar amount is small; "advisory" features (auto-reorder, pricing recommendations) can move a tool out of the exempt bucket; SEE/CAURD licensees lose eligibility if a vendor contract erodes "sole control."

## SEE priority, CAURD, and the licensing-queue litigation
- **Rule:** §87(2): goal of awarding **50%** of adult-use licenses to social and economic equity applicants (communities disproportionately impacted, MWBEs, distressed farmers, service-disabled veterans). §87(3) "extra priority": disproportionately impacted community + income below 80% of county median + a NY marijuana conviction (self or qualifying family member). Part 121 implements. CAURD (Part 116) was the first retail round for justice-involved applicants.
- **Litigation (2023–2026):**
  - ***Matter of Fiore v. N.Y. State Cannabis Control Bd.*** (Sup. Ct. Albany Cnty., Index No. 907282-23; TRO Aug. 7, 2023, PI Aug. 18, 2023, Bryant, J.): service-disabled veterans argued CAURD exceeded the statute; injunction froze most CAURD openings; settled late 2023 (settlement approved Dec. 1, 2023; plaintiffs received licenses).
  - ***Variscite NY One, Inc. v. New York*** (N.D.N.Y. No. 1:22-cv-01013): dormant Commerce Clause challenge to CAURD's NY-conviction requirement; PI Nov. 10, 2022 (Sharpe, J.) froze CAURD licensing in five regions; 2d Cir. (Mar. 2023) narrowed it to the Finger Lakes; settled, approved by the CCB May 30, 2023.
  - ***Variscite NY Four, LLC v. N.Y. State Cannabis Control Bd.*** (2d Cir. Aug. 12, 2025, No. 24-384): dormant Commerce Clause **applies** to NY's cannabis market; "extra priority" for **NY** convictions only is protectionist; plaintiffs had standing to challenge the December-pool extra-priority regime and November-pool precedence, **not** CAURD. Vacated the denial of a PI and remanded (N.D.N.Y. No. 1:23-cv-01599). **Status:** no remand ruling found as of 2026-09-28 (confirm before use — check the docket).
  - **School-proximity error:** OCM reversed its 500-ft measurement guidance in July 2025 (entrance-to-property-line), affecting ~150 dispensaries/applicants; **A10140 signed Feb. 11, 2026** codifies door-to-door measurement for the affected businesses.
- **Traps:** a pending microbusiness applicant's queue position depends on priority rules that are under constitutional attack; don't promise timelines. Watch CCB meeting resolutions for pool changes.

## Advertising and marketing (§86; Part 129)
- **Statute (§86):** ads only where the audience is reasonably expected to be 21+ (advertiser bears the burden with reliable data); nothing designed to appeal to minors; no billboards, unsolicited pop-ups, transit, public property, or within 500 ft of schools, playgrounds, day care, parks, or libraries; the Board may restrict discounts, coupons, and below-market selling that would subvert tax collection.
- **Part 129 (as amended eff. 12/3/2025):** per OCM's Part 129 guidance, retailers **may** run discounts, volume deals, event promotions and **loyalty rewards if the price stays at or above "market value" (1.5× wholesale cost)** — no stacking below the floor, no "spin-the-wheel" gamification. Promotional ads may run in-store and via email, SMS, mailers, the licensee's website, and 21+-restricted third-party sites. **Free product is still barred:** no BOGO-free, free samples, birthday freebies, contests, or "free"/"donated" cannabis; branded merchandise only through an environmental sustainability program approved by OCM (§128.4(a) — confirm before use). Audience-composition test for events, print, broadcast, and electronic ads: 21+ share must at least equal NY's 21+ population share (OCM guidance: **75.9%**, 2023 Census).
- **Traps:** LII's §129.3(9) and (25) still show the older blanket ban on advertising price, discounts, loyalty and coupons — the amended text governs; confirm the current paragraph numbers and the section defining the 1.5× floor before citing. SMS promotions also trigger the TCPA and NY telemarketing rules — see `consumer-protection.md`.

## Packaging, tracking, purchase limits
- **Packaging/labeling:** Cannabis Law §81; Part 128 (amended eff. 12/3/2025).
- **Seed-to-sale:** Cannabis Law §78; §125.8. **NY moved from BioTrack to Metrc**: every licensee credentialed by **Dec. 17, 2025**; retail inventory in by **Jan. 12, 2026**; Retail Item IDs required for distributor-to-dispensary transfers from **Feb. 28, 2026**; **delinquent-payment/credit reporting and COD-list publication run through Metrc from Sept. 15, 2026**. POS/inventory SaaS must integrate with Metrc via licensee-provisioned API keys.
- **Purchase/possession:** Penal Law §222.05(1): a person 21+ may possess, purchase, and transport up to **3 oz of cannabis and 24 g of concentrated cannabis**. Retailers enforce this per transaction; §123.10(g)(11)(iii) bars sales where the consumer's daily transactions exceed the legal possession limits (LII text; confirm paragraph numbering after the 11/5/2025 amendments). ID scan every sale (§85).

## Excise tax (Tax Law art. 20-C, §§492–496-e)
- **§493 (rev. 2024-05-03):** (a) **9% distributor tax** on the distributor's price to the retailer (microbusinesses and ROs selling at retail pay 9% on 75% of the retail price); (b) **9% state retail tax** on the retail price; (c) **4% local retail tax** held in trust for localities; (d) research licensees exempt. The per-milligram THC tax (flower 0.5¢, concentrate 0.8¢, edibles 3¢ per mg) was **repealed effective June 1, 2024** (L. 2024, ch. 59, Part L, amending §§493(a), 496(d), 496-b(a); administration moved to Tax Law art. 28 procedures Dec. 1, 2024 — tax.ny.gov).
- **Traps:** POS systems must separate the 9%/4% retail taxes from state/local sales tax on the receipt; registration under §494; records under §496. Older compliance software may still compute the THC tax.

## Software vendor checklist (NY)
- Flat subscription pricing; avoid a % of licensee revenue or profit; stay under the §124.4 thresholds per licensee, stacked across agreements (§124.3(c)).
- No approval rights over the licensee's budget, pricing, purchasing, hiring, or material contracts (these are "controlling interest" powers).
- If customers span retail and supply-side tiers, keep the product non-advisory, or get counsel to confirm the "exempt" characterization.
- No lender/landlord/consultant bundling that turns the vendor into a TPI; no founder cross-holdings across tiers.
- Marketing features: enforce the market-value floor, block free-product and gamified promos, gate by age, keep audience-composition evidence.
- **Penal Law art. 222:** §222.05 lists the lawful acts and preempts criminal liability for conduct authorized under the Cannabis Law; unlicensed sale is still an offense (§222.45 unlawful sale is a violation, fine up to \$250; criminal sale 3rd–1st degree §§222.50–.65), and civil enforcement against illicit shops runs under Cannabis Law §16-a (emergency relief/injunctions), §138-a (action for unlawful business practices), and §138-b (orders to seal). Vendors should verify a customer's license status before provisioning.

## Federal overlay
- **CSA:** marijuana has been Schedule I (21 U.S.C. §812). **Status (verified 2026-09-28):** DOJ/DEA order announced April 23, 2026, effective April 28, 2026 (91 Fed. Reg. 22714) moved **FDA-approved marijuana products and marijuana subject to a state medical marijuana license** to **Schedule III**, with an expedited DEA registration pathway for state medical licensees (following EO 14370, Dec. 18, 2025). **Adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I.** A formal hearing on broader rescheduling (91 Fed. Reg. 22777) ran June 29–July 15, 2026 before Chief ALJ Derek C. Julius; final briefs were due Aug. 17, 2026; as of Sept. 21, 2026 the ALJ had issued no recommended decision and DEA no final rule. Re-check before any memo.
- **IRC §280E:** disallows deductions and credits (other than COGS) for a trade or business trafficking in Schedule I or II substances. After April 2026, medical-licensed trade is arguably outside §280E; **adult-use NY licensees (including microbusinesses) remain subject**. Mixed medical/adult-use operators (ROs) have an allocation problem — send it to the tax skills (`irc-lookup`, `tax-analyst`).
- **Banking/payments:** FinCEN **FIN-2014-G001** (Feb. 14, 2014) lets banks serve MRBs using "Marijuana Limited/Priority/Termination" SARs. Its predicate, the 2013 Cole Memo, was rescinded in Jan. 2018, and whether FinCEN still stands behind the guidance is a confirm-before-use point. Card networks prohibit cannabis sales; "cashless ATM" and PIN-debit workarounds draw network and enforcement risk. A platform that holds or moves licensee/customer funds without a license risks **18 U.S.C. §1960** (unlicensed money transmitting business) and state money-transmitter licensing; aiding CSA violations remains a theoretical exposure (21 U.S.C. §§841, 846, 856).
- **Hemp:** P.L. 119-37 (signed Nov. 12, 2025) redefines hemp (total-THC basis, 0.4 mg total THC per container for finished products); H.R. 6500 (Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, signed Sept. 2, 2026) delays the naturally derived hemp-cannabinoid provisions to **Dec. 11, 2026**; synthesized cannabinoids not naturally produced by the plant still lose hemp status **Nov. 12, 2026**. This matters for Part 114 products that operators sell next to cannabis.
- **TCPA:** SMS marketing to consumers → `consumer-protection.md` (consent, quiet hours, NY GBL rules).

## Where this stops
- Whether a specific SaaS contract (fees, features, customer mix) makes the vendor a TPI or creates a "controlling interest" — needs counsel's read of the current adopted text and, ideally, an OCM Trade Practices inquiry.
- Microbusiness or SEE eligibility, queue position, and litigation-driven changes to priority rules.
- Federal criminal and money-transmission exposure for payment features, and §280E positions for mixed operators.
- Advertising decisions near the Part 129 lines (loyalty design, floor pricing, influencer/UGC content).
