# Professional Responsibility — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: MPRE items (ABA Model Rules, ABA Model Code of Judicial Conduct), NY attorney-ethics questions (NY RPC, 22 NYCRR Part 1200), fee/engagement rules, trust accounts, advertising/solicitation, UPL, and lawyer use of generative AI.
Scope: ABA Model Rule first (MPRE tests this); NY differences flagged **NY:**. Verified on law.cornell.edu (22 NYCRR): NY 1.5(b)-(d),(g); 1.10(a)-(c),(e) (as amended eff. 2025); 1.15(a),(b),(d),(e); 4.2; 7.1(f),(k); 7.3(a),(e). NY Part 161 (AI) verified against NYSBA notice. Other text from model knowledge; confirm-before-use where marked.

## Exam method
- MPRE answers track the Model Rules' **"shall" vs "may"**: mandatory disclosure is rare (3.3 to a tribunal; 4.1(b) where 1.6 permits and silence = assisting fraud). "Subject to discipline" = violates a Rule; "proper" = permitted. Malpractice/disqualification standards differ from discipline.
- Consent formulas: **informed consent** (1.0(e)); **confirmed in writing** (1.7, 1.9, 1.10, 1.12, 1.18, 1.5(e)); **writing signed by the client** (1.5(c) contingent fee; 1.8(a) business transaction; 1.8(g) aggregate settlement). 1.8(b) needs only informed consent.

## Lawyer-client relationship (1.1–1.4, 1.16)
- **1.1 competence:** legal knowledge, skill, thoroughness, preparation; may become competent by study or associate with a competent lawyer. Comment 8: keep abreast of **benefits and risks of relevant technology**.
- **1.2 allocation:** client decides objectives, settle, plea, jury waiver, testify (criminal), appeal; lawyer controls means after consultation. Limited-scope representation OK if reasonable and client gives informed consent (1.2(c)). Lawyer may not counsel/assist crime or fraud but may discuss legal consequences (1.2(d)).
- **1.3 diligence; 1.4 communication:** promptly inform of decisions requiring consent (e.g. every settlement offer in civil matters unless client pre-authorized acceptance/rejection; every plea offer), keep reasonably informed, explain enough for informed decisions.
- **1.16 withdrawal:** **mandatory** if representation will violate rules/law, lawyer's impairment, or fired. **Permissive** if no material adverse effect; client persists in conduct lawyer reasonably believes criminal/fraudulent; client used services for crime/fraud; repugnant/fundamental disagreement; client fails obligation after warning; unreasonable financial burden; other good cause. Court permission when required by tribunal rules; must continue if ordered. On termination: notice, time to get counsel, return papers/property and unearned fees (1.16(d)); may retain papers only as permitted by law.
- **NY 1.16:** structure differs (1.16(b) mandatory; 1.16(c) permissive with 13 grounds, incl. client "insists upon taking action with which the lawyer has a fundamental disagreement"; 1.16(d) tribunal permission; 1.16(e) return of papers/fees). Substantively similar.
- **Traps:** client consent is needed to settle even if lawyer thinks the offer is excellent. A lawyer fired by the client must withdraw (subject to court permission).

## Fees (1.5)
- **ABA 1.5(a):** fee must not be **unreasonable** (factors: time/labor, novelty, preclusion of other work, customary fee, amount involved and results, time limits, relationship, experience, fixed or contingent). **1.5(b):** scope and basis/rate communicated, **preferably in writing**, before or within reasonable time after commencing (unless regularly represented client on same basis).
- **1.5(c) contingent fee:** in a writing **signed by the client**, stating method, percentages at trial/settlement/appeal, deductions of expenses (before/after fee), and client's liability for expenses; closing statement at conclusion. **1.5(d):** no contingent fee in **domestic relations** (contingent on divorce or amount of alimony/support/property) or for **criminal defense**. (Collecting past-due child support on contingency is permitted — comment.)
- **1.5(e) fee division between firms:** proportional to services **or** each lawyer assumes joint responsibility; client agrees to the arrangement **including the share each lawyer receives**, confirmed in writing; total reasonable. Referral fee without joint responsibility is prohibited.
- **Advance fees and retainers:** advance fees belong to client until earned — deposit in trust (1.15(c)); true "general retainer" to secure availability can be earned on receipt.
- **NY 1.5(a):** "excessive or illegal" fee standard (same factors). **NY 1.5(b):** communicate scope and fee basis — **writing required by 22 NYCRR Part 1215**: letter of engagement or written retainer explaining scope, fees/expenses/billing, and right to Part 137 fee arbitration, **unless** fee expected to be < \$3,000, services of the same general kind previously rendered to and paid by the client, domestic relations matter (Part 1400 governs), or lawyer is admitted elsewhere with no NY office and no material part of the services in NY, or lawyer retained by another lawyer for the client (confirm threshold before use).
- **NY 1.5(d):** shall not charge (1) a contingent fee in a criminal defense matter; (2) a fee prohibited by law; (3) fraudulent billing; **(4) a nonrefundable retainer** — but a reasonable **minimum fee clause** defined in plain language is allowed (*Matter of Cooperman* (N.Y. 1994)); **(5) domestic relations: any fee contingent on securing a divorce or on the amount of maintenance, support, ED, or property settlement, or any fee unless a written retainer agreement signed by lawyer and client is provided per Part 1400** (with a Statement of Client's Rights and Responsibilities; security interests in the marital residence restricted; itemized bills every 60 days — confirm before use).
- **NY contingent fees in PI/med mal:** Appellate Division schedules (Schedule A sliding scale — 50% of first \$1,000, 40% of next \$2,000, 35% of next \$22,000, 25% over \$25,000 — or Schedule B flat 33⅓%) and **Judiciary Law 474-a** medical malpractice cap (30% of first \$250,000, 25% next \$250,000, 20% next \$500,000, 15% next \$250,000, 10% over \$1,250,000) (confirm before use). Retainer and closing statements filed with OCA (confirm).
- **NY 1.5(g) fee division:** proportional **or**, by a writing given to the client, joint responsibility; client agrees after full disclosure of the division **including the share each lawyer receives**, confirmed in writing; total not excessive. Same substance as ABA 1.5(e).
- **NY fee disputes:** Part 137 mandatory arbitration at client's election for amounts \$1,000–\$50,000 (confirm before use).
- **Traps:** "nonrefundable" labels are void in NY regardless of reasonableness. The ABA prefers but does not require a writing for non-contingent fees — NY Part 1215 requires one above the threshold.

## Confidentiality and privilege (1.6, 1.8(b), 1.9(c), 1.18)
- **ABA 1.6(a):** all information **relating to the representation**, whatever the source — broader than privilege. Implied authorization to carry out representation.
- **ABA 1.6(b) permissive exceptions:** (1) prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm; (2) prevent client crime/fraud reasonably certain to cause substantial financial injury **in furtherance of which client used the lawyer's services**; (3) prevent/mitigate/rectify such injury; (4) secure ethics advice; (5) claim/defense in controversy with client, defend against charges, respond to allegations; (6) comply with law or court order; (7) detect conflicts from change of firms (limited info). **1.6(c):** reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized disclosure/access.
- **NY 1.6(a) "confidential information":** information gained during or relating to the representation that is (a) privileged, (b) likely to be embarrassing or detrimental to the client if disclosed, or (c) information the client asked be kept confidential — **excluding** a lawyer's legal knowledge/research and information **generally known in the local community or in the trade**.
- **NY 1.6(b):** lawyer **may** reveal to the extent reasonably believed necessary: (1) prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm; (2) **prevent the client from committing a crime** (any crime — no financial-injury or use-of-services requirement); (3) **withdraw a written or oral opinion or representation** previously given by the lawyer and reasonably believed still relied on by a third person, where lawyer discovered it was based on materially inaccurate information or is being used to further a crime or fraud; (4) secure legal advice about compliance; (5) defend against accusations / establish or collect a fee; (6) comply with law or court order; (7) detect conflicts (confirm numbering before use). **NY has no ABA 1.6(b)(3) "rectify past financial fraud" exception** beyond the 1.6(b)(3) opinion-withdrawal ("noisy withdrawal").
- **Prospective clients (1.18):** duty of confidentiality even if no engagement; disqualified from adverse substantially-related matter only if received information **significantly harmful**; firm avoids imputation by informed consent confirmed in writing, or by screening + limiting intake info + written notice.
- **Privilege vs work product:** privilege (communications for legal advice) is evidentiary; crime-fraud exception. *Upjohn Co. v. United States* (U.S. 1981) — corporate employee communications. **NY privilege:** CPLR 4503.
- **Traps:** 1.6 covers public-record information in the ABA version; NY excludes information "generally known." Permissive disclosure is never mandatory under 1.6 alone.

## Conflicts (1.7–1.12)
- **1.7 concurrent:** directly adverse OR significant risk of material limitation. Consentable if (1) reasonable belief of competent and diligent representation to each; (2) not prohibited by law; (3) not asserting a claim by one client against another **in the same litigation**; (4) each gives **informed consent, confirmed in writing**. Suing a current client even on an unrelated matter = direct adversity.
- **1.8 specific:** (a) business transaction — fair and reasonable terms fully disclosed in writing; advised in writing to seek independent counsel and given chance; client's informed consent **in a writing signed by the client**. (b) use of information to client's disadvantage. (c) no soliciting substantial gifts or preparing instrument giving lawyer/relative a substantial gift unless related. (d) no literary/media rights before conclusion. (e) no financial assistance except court costs/expenses (repayment may be contingent on outcome), costs for indigent clients, and **modest humanitarian gifts to indigent pro bono clients** (ABA 2020). (f) third-party payor — informed consent, no interference, confidentiality. (g) aggregate settlement — informed consent in writing signed by each client. (h) no prospective malpractice limitation unless client independently represented; settling malpractice claim with unrepresented client requires written advice to get counsel. (i) no proprietary interest in the cause of action except lien and contingent fee. **(j) no sexual relations with a client unless a consensual relationship pre-existed.** (k) imputation of (a)–(i) to firm, not (j).
- **NY 1.8(j):** narrower general bar — lawyer shall not (i) require or demand sexual relations as a condition of representation; (ii) employ coercion, intimidation, or undue influence in entering sexual relations incident to a representation; (iii) **in domestic relations matters, enter into sexual relations with a client during the representation**. Pre-existing relationships excepted. Outside domestic relations, a consensual relationship begun during representation is not per se barred (but 1.7 personal-interest conflict analysis applies).
- **NY 1.8(e):** similar exceptions; lawyer may pay court costs and expenses for indigent or pro bono clients, and in contingent-fee matters may advance costs with repayment contingent (confirm text).
- **1.9 former client:** same or substantially related matter, materially adverse → informed consent confirmed in writing. Never use/reveal former client's information to its disadvantage unless generally known.
- **1.10 imputation (ABA):** conflicts of one lawyer imputed to all in firm, **except** personal-interest conflicts not presenting significant risk, and **lateral conflicts cured by timely screening + no fee share + written notice to former client** (1.10(a)(2)). Nonlawyer staff conflicts not imputed if screened.
- **NY 1.10 (verified, amended eff. 2025):** (a) imputation of 1.7, 1.8, 1.9 conflicts; (c) lateral lawyer's former-client conflict is imputed **unless** (1) the lawyer acquired no material confidential information, **or** (2) the firm promptly notifies personnel, implements effective screening, apportions the lawyer no part of the fee, and gives written notice to the former client; **but (c)(3) screening does not cure imputation in litigation, arbitration, or adjudicative matters where the lateral lawyer (i) substantially participated in the management and direction of the matter, or (ii) had substantial decision-making responsibility in the matter on a continuous day-to-day basis.** (c)(4) permits delaying the former-client notice where giving it would reveal 1.6 information. Eff. Jan. 1, 2025 (NYC Bar Formal Op. 2026-1). (e) firm must keep a written record of engagements and a conflict-check system. (Pre-2025 NY barred screening for laterals — older sources are stale.)
- **1.11 former government lawyers:** personally and substantially participated → no private representation in same matter without agency's informed consent confirmed in writing; firm avoids imputation by screening + notice to agency. Confidential government information about a person.
- **1.12 former judges/arbitrators/law clerks:** no representation in matter in which personally and substantially participated unless all parties consent confirmed in writing; law clerk may negotiate employment with a party's lawyer after notifying the judge; firm screening cures.
- **1.13 organization as client:** report up; report out permitted if highest authority fails to act on clear violation reasonably certain to cause substantial injury. Upjohn warning to constituents.
- **Traps:** in NY, screening a lateral litigator who ran the matter at the old firm does not avoid disqualification. Business transactions with clients need the client's signed writing — oral consent fails.

## Safekeeping property (1.15)
- **ABA:** separate trust account in the state where office is situated (or elsewhere with consent); records kept 5 years; lawyer's own funds only to cover bank charges; advance fees deposited and withdrawn as earned; prompt notice and delivery; disputed portion held until resolved, undisputed portion distributed.
- **NY 1.15 (verified):** lawyer is a **fiduciary** and must not misappropriate or commingle; accounts in a **banking institution within New York State** (1.15(b)(1)), titled "Attorney Special Account," "Attorney Trust Account," or "Attorney Escrow Account"; **records kept 7 years** after the events they record (1.15(d)); withdrawals only to a **named payee, not cash**, by check or, with prior written approval of the party entitled, bank transfer (1.15(e)); only an attorney admitted in NY may be a signatory. **IOLA** (Judiciary Law 497) for nominal/short-term funds. Disputed funds held; may seek court resolution. Bounced-check reporting: banks report dishonored escrow checks to the Lawyers' Fund (22 NYCRR Part 1300; confirm).
- **Traps:** NY: no ATM/cash withdrawals from escrow; retention is 7 years, not 5.

## Advocate duties (3.1–3.9)
- **3.1** nonfrivolous claims (criminal defense may make State prove every element). **3.2** expedite. **3.3 candor:** no false statements of fact or law to tribunal (or failure to correct); **disclose directly adverse controlling authority** not disclosed by opposing counsel; no offering known false evidence; reasonable remedial measures incl. disclosure to tribunal if lawyer learns material evidence was false — **duty overrides 1.6** and lasts to conclusion of proceeding (incl. appeal). May refuse evidence reasonably believed false (except criminal defendant's own testimony). Ex parte: disclose all material facts, adverse included.
- **3.4 fairness:** no obstructing access to evidence, falsifying, inducing witness (may pay expenses, reasonable compensation for lost time, expert fee not contingent on outcome); no requesting non-client to withhold information unless relative/employee/agent of client and their interests won't be harmed.
- **3.5** no ex parte with judge/juror; no post-discharge juror contact if prohibited, juror declines, or coercive. **3.6** trial publicity — substantial likelihood of materially prejudicing; safe harbors. **3.7** lawyer as witness — can't be trial advocate if likely a necessary witness, except uncontested issue, fees, or substantial hardship; others in firm may try unless 1.7/1.9.
- **3.8 prosecutors:** probable cause; ensure accused advised of right to counsel; no seeking waivers from unrepresented accused; **timely disclose all evidence tending to negate guilt or mitigate** (broader than Brady — no materiality limit; ABA Formal Op. 09-454); subpoena to lawyers only if essential; extrajudicial statements; **(g)–(h) new, credible, material evidence of innocence → disclose to court/authority and, if in jurisdiction, investigate; clear and convincing evidence of innocence → seek to remedy**. **NY 3.8(c)–(d):** adopted comparable post-conviction duties (confirm text).
- **Traps:** 3.3 remedial duty trumps confidentiality; 1.6 does not. Adverse **controlling** (same jurisdiction) authority only.

## Third persons and others (4.1–4.4)
- **4.1:** no false statement of material fact or law; puffery/estimates of value and a client's settlement intentions are not "material fact" (comment). **4.2 no-contact:** lawyer can't communicate about the subject with person known to be represented in the matter without that lawyer's consent or legal authorization; parties may talk directly; for organizations — current constituents who supervise/direct/regularly consult the lawyer, have authority to obligate, or whose act may be imputed; **former employees are not covered** (cmt. [7]), but the lawyer may not seek the organization's privileged information. **4.3** unrepresented persons: no implying disinterest; correct misunderstandings; no legal advice except to get a lawyer if interests conflict. **4.4(b)** inadvertently sent document → promptly notify sender (return/no-read is outside Model Rules).
- **NY 4.2(b):** lawyer **may cause a client to communicate** with a represented person (unless not legally competent) and may counsel the client about it, **provided the lawyer gives reasonable advance notice to the represented person's counsel.** **NY 4.2(c):** lawyer acting pro se or represented may communicate with a represented person with reasonable advance notice to that person's counsel. ABA only says parties may communicate directly (comment 4) without a notice requirement, and a scripted end-run violates 8.4(a).
- **Traps:** the "consent" in 4.2 is the other lawyer's, not the represented person's.

## Law firms, UPL, and nonlawyers (5.1–5.7)
- **5.1** partners/managers ensure reasonable measures for compliance; supervisors responsible if they order/ratify or know and fail to remediate. **5.2** subordinate follows supervisor's reasonable resolution of arguable question. **5.3** nonlawyer assistants — **includes vendors and AI tools** (Op. 512). **5.4 independence:** no fee-sharing with nonlawyers (exceptions: death benefit to estate, purchase of practice, retirement/profit-sharing plan for nonlawyer employees, court-awarded fees shared with nonprofit that referred); no partnership with nonlawyers practicing law; no nonlawyer ownership/directorship of a professional corporation. (ABA did **not** adopt nonlawyer ownership/ABS; AZ and UT regulatory sandboxes are state-specific; DC permits limited nonlawyer partners.)
- **5.5 UPL / multijurisdictional:** temporary practice if associated with local lawyer; related to pending/potential proceeding where admitted pro hac vice; ADR related to home practice; reasonably related to home practice; in-house and federal-law practice permitted (registration may be required). No systematic and continuous presence or holding out.
- **5.6** no agreements restricting right to practice after termination (except retirement benefits), and no settlement restricting practice. **5.7** law-related services.
- **NY 5.4:** same bar on nonlawyer fee sharing/ownership; NY never permitted ABS. **NY 5.7–5.8:** limited "contractual relationships" with nonlegal professionals (listed professions) for referrals and services; no fee sharing/ownership. **Judiciary Law 478** (practice or holding out without admission — unlawful); **484** (non-attorneys may not prepare pleadings/papers for others); **485** (misdemeanor); **495** (corporations and voluntary associations may not practice law or furnish lawyers — exception for legal aid organizations; confirm). NY in-house counsel registration (22 NYCRR Part 522); foreign legal consultants (Part 521).
- **Traps:** paying a nonlawyer marketing firm a percentage of fees is prohibited fee sharing in both regimes.

## Information about legal services (7.1–7.5)
- **ABA (2018 consolidation):** 7.1 no false or misleading communication (truthful statements that create unjustified expectations can mislead). **7.2(b):** no giving anything of value for a recommendation except reasonable cost of ads, qualified referral service fees, purchase of practice, **reciprocal non-exclusive referral agreements disclosed to client**, and **nominal gifts** as thanks not expected as payment. Communications must include name and contact of at least one responsible lawyer/firm. **7.2(c):** "certified specialist" only if certified by ABA-accredited or state-approved body and name is clearly identified. **7.3 solicitation:** no **live person-to-person** contact for pecuniary gain (in-person, live phone, real-time video) **unless** recipient is a lawyer, family, close personal or prior professional relationship, or **routinely uses the type of legal services for business**; no solicitation (any medium) if the target has said no, or involves coercion/duress/harassment. Targeted written/electronic solicitation permitted (no ABA label requirement post-2018). Prepaid/group plans permitted.
- **NY 7.1 (verified in part):** detailed content rules; **"Attorney Advertising" label** required on most advertisements other than radio/TV/billboard/directory/periodical ads or in-person under 7.3(a)(1) — on first page or website home page (7.1(f)); **all advertisements pre-approved by the lawyer/firm and a copy retained for at least 3 years after initial dissemination** (7.1(k)); computer-accessed communications retention rules (confirm before use). Disclaimer "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome" with testimonials/results (7.1(d)–(e); confirm).
- **NY 7.3 (verified in part):** no solicitation by **in-person, telephone, or real-time interactive computer-accessed communication** unless recipient is a close friend, relative, former client, or existing client (7.3(a)(1)); **no solicitation relating to a specific incident involving potential personal injury or wrongful death claims before the 30th day after the incident** (15th day if a filing is legally required within 30 days) (7.3(e)); written solicitations **filed** with the attorney disciplinary committee and retained (7.3(c)); limits on solicitations to those the lawyer knows are unable to exercise reasonable judgment. **NY 7.4:** no claim of "specialist"/"specializes" unless certified by an approved organization, with required disclaimer (confirm). **NY 7.5:** firm names — no trade names (7.5(b); confirm current rule).
- **Traps:** NY's 30-day PI moratorium applies to all solicitations including mailed letters; the ABA has no waiting period (FL has its own).

## Integrity of the profession (8.1–8.5)
- **8.1** bar admission/discipline — no knowing false statement, no failing to correct a misapprehension, no failing to respond to lawful demand (except 1.6 info). **8.2** no knowing/reckless false statements about judges' integrity. **8.3 reporting:** **must** report another lawyer's violation raising a **substantial question as to honesty, trustworthiness, or fitness**; judges likewise; not required if 1.6 protects the information or learned in a lawyer-assistance program. **8.4 misconduct:** violate/attempt/assist another to violate rules; criminal act reflecting adversely on honesty/fitness; dishonesty, fraud, deceit, misrepresentation; conduct prejudicial to administration of justice; implying improper influence; assisting judge's misconduct; **(g) harassment or discrimination related to the practice of law** (ABA 2016). **8.5** choice of rules: tribunal's rules for litigation; otherwise where conduct occurred or predominant effect.
- **NY 8.3(a):** must report to a tribunal or other authority empowered to investigate knowledge that another lawyer committed a violation raising a substantial question as to honesty, trustworthiness, or fitness; exceptions for confidential information and lawyer-assistance programs. **NY 8.4(g):** unlawful discrimination in the practice of law (NY amended 2022 to cover harassment — confirm text before use). **NY 8.4(h):** "any other conduct that adversely reflects on the lawyer's fitness."
- **Traps:** the duty is to report, not to investigate; a lawyer's own client's confidences need not be reported.

## Judicial conduct (ABA Model Code of Judicial Conduct 2007; NY 22 NYCRR Part 100)
- **Rule 2.9 ex parte:** prohibited except scheduling/administrative/emergency (no substantive advantage + prompt notice to all), written advice of disinterested expert on law with notice and chance to respond, consult court staff and other judges (not those with disqualifying interest), separate settlement conferences **with parties' consent**, as authorized by law. Judge may not independently investigate facts.
- **Rule 2.11 disqualification** whenever impartiality might reasonably be questioned, incl. personal bias/knowledge; judge, spouse, or relative within **third degree** is a party, lawyer, material witness, or has more than de minimis interest; economic interest of judge or family member residing in household; campaign contributions above threshold; public statements committing to a result; prior involvement as lawyer/judge/witness. **Remittal (2.11(C)):** except for personal bias, parties and lawyers may agree, **outside the judge's participation**, to waive; agreement on the record.
- **Rule 3.1–3.14 extrajudicial:** no conduct undermining independence; 3.6 no membership in invidiously discriminating organizations; **3.7 fundraising — may solicit only from family members and judges not under the judge's supervisory/appellate authority**; 3.8 fiduciary only for family members; 3.9 no arbitration/mediation for private parties; 3.10 **no practice of law** except self-representation and uncompensated advice to family; 3.13 gifts — may accept ordinary social hospitality, gifts from friends/relatives, items of little value, and gifts incident to spouse's business; public reporting for others (e.g. gifts from persons not likely to appear).
- **Rule 4.1 political:** no endorsing candidates, no personal solicitation of campaign contributions (campaign committee may) — upheld in *Williams-Yulee v. Florida Bar* (U.S. 2015); "announce clause" invalid under *Republican Party of Minnesota v. White* (U.S. 2002); due process requires recusal on extreme campaign support, *Caperton v. A.T. Massey Coal Co.* (U.S. 2009).
- **NY:** Part 100 (Rules Governing Judicial Conduct); Advisory Committee on Judicial Ethics opinions; Commission on Judicial Conduct. Fiduciary/family exceptions similar.
- **Traps:** a judge's personal bias cannot be remitted. A judge may not ask a litigant's lawyer to donate to the judge's favored charity.

## Generative AI and professional duties
- **ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024):** (1) **competence (1.1)** — reasonable understanding of the tool's capabilities and limits; independently verify output (hallucinations); (2) **confidentiality (1.6)** — assess risk that inputted information will be disclosed or accessed; **informed consent required before inputting client information into a self-learning tool** that may use it outside the firm; **boilerplate in an engagement letter is not informed consent**; (3) **communication (1.4)** — disclose GAI use when the client asks, when it bears on fees, or when output influences a significant decision; (4) **meritorious claims and candor (3.1, 3.3, 8.4(c))** — verify citations and factual/legal assertions; (5) **supervision (5.1, 5.3)** — firm policies, training, vendor due diligence; (6) **fees (1.5)** — hourly billing only for actual time spent (incl. prompting and review), **cannot bill for time saved** or for learning a generally used tool; flat fees must remain reasonable; tool costs are overhead unless a disclosed per-use charge.
- ***Mata v. Avianca, Inc.* (S.D.N.Y. 2023):** counsel filed a brief with ChatGPT-fabricated cases and stood by them after the court questioned them; Judge Castel imposed Rule 11 sanctions (\$5,000 penalty, notice letters to the client and to judges falsely named). Lesson: the duty is independent verification; subjective bad faith was found from continued advocacy after warning signs.
- **NY:** **22 NYCRR Part 161 (Use of Artificial Intelligence Technology), eff. June 1, 2026** — AI use in preparing court papers permitted; **no statewide disclosure mandate**; the signing attorney must review and independently ensure no fabricated or fictitious cases, statutes, or other material (tied to Part 130 frivolous-conduct sanctions and RPC 3.3). Individual judges' part rules may impose disclosure — check the assigned part. Proposed CPLR 2107 disclosure bill (S2698, 2025) not enacted as of this writing (confirm before use). NYSBA Task Force on AI report (2024) is guidance, not rule.
- **Traps:** confidentiality risk turns on whether the tool retains/trains on inputs — an enterprise tool with no-training/no-retention terms may not need client consent for ordinary use; a public consumer chatbot does. Fabricated citations violate 3.3(a)(1) and 1.1 even without intent to deceive; 8.4(c) requires dishonesty.
