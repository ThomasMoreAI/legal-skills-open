# Family Law — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: marriage validity/annulment, divorce grounds, property division, maintenance/alimony, child support, custody/visitation/relocation, interstate jurisdiction (UCCJEA/UIFSA), premarital/marital agreements, parentage/surrogacy.
Scope: MEE/UMDA/majority rule first; NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified: EPTL/DRL cross-refs against nysenate.gov where noted; 2026 support caps from secondary sources only (confirm before use); other doctrine from model knowledge.

## Marriage — formation and validity
- **Rule:** Validity governed by law of the place of celebration unless it violates strong public policy of the domicile (Restatement (Second) Conflicts §283).
- **Elements (ceremonial):** 1. capacity (age, not already married, not within prohibited degree) 2. license 3. solemnization by authorized officiant.
- **Void vs voidable:** void (bigamy, incest) = nullity, attackable by anyone, anytime, even after death; voidable (nonage, incapacity, impotence, fraud, duress) = valid until annulled, only by the aggrieved spouse, can be ratified.
- **Common-law marriage:** minority of states (e.g. CO, TX, IA, others); requires capacity + present agreement to be married + cohabitation + holding out. All states recognize one validly formed where contracted.
- **Constitutional:** *Loving v. Virginia* (U.S. 1967); *Obergefell v. Hodges* (U.S. 2015) — same-sex marriage is a fundamental right; *Zablocki v. Redhail* (U.S. 1978) — strict scrutiny for direct burdens on marrying.
- **Putative spouse:** good-faith belief in validity → equitable property/support rights (UMDA §209; CA).
- **NY:** Common-law marriage abolished 1933, but NY recognizes one validly formed in another state. Marriage Equality Act (2011) — DRL 10-a. Minimum age 18, no exceptions (DRL 15-a as amended 2021; confirm before use). License required (DRL 13), 24-hour wait after issuance (DRL 13-b), license valid 60 days (confirm before use); failure to obtain a license does not void a solemnized marriage (DRL 25). Solemnization by an officiant listed in DRL 11 (clergy, judges, mayors, clerks, certain others); DRL 12 requires the parties solemnly declare in the officiant's presence and at least one witness that they take each other as spouses.
- **NY void (DRL 5, 6):** incestuous (ancestor/descendant, siblings of whole or half blood, uncle-niece, aunt-nephew — first cousins permitted); bigamous (prior spouse living, unless prior marriage annulled/dissolved; DRL 6; absent-spouse dissolution under DRL 220–221 — confirm details before use). **NY voidable (DRL 7, 140):** under-age, mental incapacity, incurable physical incapacity, consent by force/duress/fraud, incurable mental illness for 5+ years.
- **NY fraud annulment (DRL 140(e)):** fraud must go to the essence and be material to the aggrieved spouse; action barred if parties voluntarily cohabited with full knowledge of the fraud (DRL 140(e)). Proof beyond the parties' own admissions required (DRL 144). Limitations period: confirm before use.
- **Traps:** Declaring a marriage void does not bar property/support relief in NY — court may make ED and maintenance awards in annulment actions (DRL 236(B) applies to annulments). Prior-marriage bigamy is not cured by the first spouse's later death in most states; parties must remarry.

## Divorce — grounds, defenses, procedure
- **Rule:** All states now have no-fault divorce (irreconcilable differences / irretrievable breakdown / living separate and apart). Fault grounds survive in many: adultery, cruelty, desertion, imprisonment, habitual drunkenness.
- **Fault defenses (largely obsolete):** recrimination, condonation, connivance, collusion, provocation.
- **Jurisdiction:** divorce status = in rem; one spouse's domicile suffices (*Williams v. North Carolina* (U.S. 1942)). Property division and support = in personam; need personal jurisdiction over defendant (**divisible divorce**: *Estin v. Estin* (U.S. 1948); *Vanderbilt v. Vanderbilt* (U.S. 1957)). Durational residency requirements upheld (*Sosna v. Iowa* (U.S. 1975)).
- **NY grounds (DRL 170):** (1) cruel and inhuman treatment endangering physical/mental well-being; (2) abandonment 1+ year; (3) imprisonment 3+ consecutive years after marriage; (4) adultery (defenses DRL 171 — procurement/connivance, forgiveness or voluntary cohabitation after knowledge, 5-year limit from discovery, plaintiff's own adultery; corroboration required); (5) living apart 1+ year under a separation judgment; (6) living apart 1+ year under a written, acknowledged separation agreement; (7) **irretrievable breakdown for at least 6 months**, sworn by one party.
- **NY DRL 170(7) limits:** no judgment until economic issues (equitable distribution, maintenance, counsel fees, child support, custody/visitation) are resolved by the parties or determined by the court and incorporated. The sworn statement is generally not contestable as a fact issue in practice (Appellate Division split history; confirm before use).
- **NY residency (DRL 230):** 2 years continuous; or 1 year if married in NY, resided as spouses in NY, or cause arose in NY; or cause arose in NY and both residents at commencement. Supreme Court has exclusive divorce jurisdiction — Family Court cannot grant divorce.
- **NY counsel fees (DRL 237):** rebuttable presumption that counsel fees be awarded to the less monied spouse.
- **Traps:** A NY divorce requires resolution of all ancillary issues under 170(7). Ex parte foreign divorce is valid as to status but cannot cut off the non-appearing spouse's support/property rights.

## Property division
- **Community property (9 states: AZ, CA, ID, LA, NV, NM, TX, WA, WI):** property acquired during marriage by either spouse's labor = owned 1/2 each; most divide equally at divorce (CA, LA, NM mandatory equal).
- **Equitable distribution (majority, incl. NY):** court classifies marital vs separate, values, then distributes fairly — not necessarily equally.
- **Classification:** separate = premarital, gift/inheritance from third party, personal-injury pain-and-suffering, property acquired in exchange for separate; marital = acquired during marriage regardless of title. **Commingling/transmutation** can convert separate to marital. Tracing burden on the spouse claiming separate.
- **Appreciation:** active appreciation of separate property (due to marital funds or either spouse's efforts) = marital; passive (market) = separate.
- **Professional degrees/licenses:** almost all states hold a degree is not property; reimbursement alimony instead.
- **Pensions:** marital portion (coverture fraction); divided by QDRO. Military retirement: USFSPA; VA disability not divisible (*Howell v. Howell* (U.S. 2017)). Social Security not divisible.
- **Valuation date:** varies (filing, trial). Goodwill: enterprise goodwill divisible; personal goodwill split among states.
- **NY (DRL 236(B)):** marital property = acquired by either or both during marriage and before execution of a separation agreement or commencement of a matrimonial action, regardless of title. Separate property (236(B)(1)(d)): pre-marriage; inheritance, gift from party other than spouse; compensation for personal injuries (not lost-earnings portion); property acquired in exchange for or increase in value of separate property **except to the extent the appreciation is due in part to contributions or efforts of the other spouse**; property described as separate by a valid written agreement.
- **NY appreciation:** *Price v. Price* (N.Y. 1986) — appreciation of separate property attributable to the non-titled spouse's direct or indirect contributions (incl. homemaker) is marital; passive appreciation stays separate.
- **NY enhanced earning capacity:** *O'Brien v. O'Brien* (N.Y. 1985) held a medical license acquired during marriage is marital property; *McSparron v. McSparron* (N.Y. 1995) (no merger of license into career). **Abrogated by the 2016 amendment to DRL 236(B)(5)(d)(7):** court shall not consider as marital property the value of a spouse's enhanced earning capacity from a license, degree, celebrity goodwill, or career enhancement — but **shall consider** the other spouse's direct or indirect contributions to the development during the marriage of that enhanced earning capacity in distributing marital property (text verified on nysenate.gov; applies to actions commenced on or after Jan. 25, 2016 — confirm effective-date detail before use).
- **NY distribution factors (236(B)(5)(d)):** income/property at marriage and at commencement; duration and age/health; need of custodial parent to occupy home; loss of inheritance/pension rights; maintenance award; direct/indirect contributions incl. homemaker; liquidity; future financial circumstances; tax consequences; wasteful dissipation; transfers in contemplation of action; domestic violence; companion animals' best interest (2021); any other factor. Court must state factors in writing (236(B)(5)(g)).
- **Traps:** NY cutoff is **commencement of the action** (or a separation agreement), not separation in fact. Fault is generally irrelevant to ED in NY except egregious conduct that "shocks the conscience" (*O'Brien*; *Howard S. v. Lillian S.* (N.Y. 2010) — adultery alone not egregious). Title is irrelevant to classification.

## Spousal support (alimony / NY "maintenance")
- **Rule (UMDA §308):** need and ability to pay; forms: temporary (pendente lite), rehabilitative, reimbursement, permanent (declining), lump-sum. Termination on death of either; remarriage of payee terminates in most states; cohabitation may modify/terminate by statute.
- **Modification:** substantial change in circumstances; not retroactive before filing. Lump-sum/property settlement not modifiable.
- **NY — Maintenance Guidelines Act (2015):** temporary (DRL 236(B)(5-a)) and post-divorce (236(B)(6)) use the same formula on payor income up to the **income cap** (\$241,000 eff. 3/1/2026 per secondary sources; adjusted biennially by CPI — confirm before use).
  - **Where child support is also paid and payor is the non-custodial parent:** (a) 20% of payor income − 25% of payee income; (b) 40% of combined income − payee income; award = lesser; if ≤0, zero.
  - **No child support / payor is custodial parent:** (a) 30% of payor income − 20% of payee income; (b) 40% of combined − payee income; award = lesser.
  - Income over the cap: discretionary, on the statutory factors, with written findings. Low-income: self-support reserve adjustment.
  - **Post-divorce duration advisory schedule:** marriage 0–15 yrs → 15–30% of marriage length; 15–20 yrs → 30–40%; 20+ yrs → 35–50%. Marriage length = date of marriage to commencement.
  - Maintenance is computed **before** child support (payor income reduced by maintenance for CSSA).
- **NY termination:** death of either; payee remarriage terminates (DRL 236(B)(9)(b)); payee cohabitation + holding out as spouse → may annul/modify (DRL 248). Modification on substantial change in circumstances, including extreme hardship for agreements (for agreement-based maintenance) — confirm standard before use.
- **Tax:** post-2018 divorces — alimony not deductible/not income (TCJA).
- **Traps:** NY applies the formula even to high earners up to the cap; ALWAYS compute maintenance first, then child support. NY "maintenance" is not called alimony.

## Child support
- **Models:** income shares (majority, ~40 states, incl. NY) vs percentage-of-obligor-income (minority) vs Melson formula (few).
- **Rule:** guideline amount = rebuttable presumption; deviation requires written findings. Duty runs to majority (18, or HS graduation) in most states; support can't be waived or bargained away to the child's detriment. Modifiable on substantial change; **not retroactively modifiable** for accrued arrears (Bradley Amendment, 42 U.S.C. §666(a)(9)).
- **Enforcement:** wage withholding, license suspension, tax intercept, contempt; *Turner v. Rogers* (U.S. 2011) — no automatic right to counsel in civil contempt, but procedural safeguards required.
- **NY — CSSA (DRL 240(1-b); FCA 413):** combined parental income (gross less FICA, NYC/Yonkers tax, maintenance paid) × percentage: **1 child 17%; 2 = 25%; 3 = 29%; 4 = 31%; 5+ = no less than 35%.** Pro-rated by each parent's share of combined income. Cap on combined parental income: \$193,000 eff. 3/1/2026 per secondary sources (formerly \$183,000; set by SSL 111-i(2)(b), adjusted biennially — confirm before use). Above cap: apply percentage and/or the DRL 240(1-b)(f) factors, with reasons.
- **NY add-ons:** child care (work/school), health insurance and unreimbursed medical (mandatory, pro rata); educational expenses (discretionary). Self-support reserve and poverty-level minimums (\$25/month floor; confirm before use).
- **NY duration:** support to **age 21** (DRL 32; FCA 413), unless emancipated earlier (self-supporting, marriage, military, or unjustified abandonment of parent — constructive emancipation for child 17+).
- **NY agreements (DRL 240(1-b)(h)):** agreement/stipulation must recite that parties were advised of CSSA, that the basic amount presumptively yields the correct amount, state the presumptive amount, and if deviating, the reasons. Non-compliant provision is unenforceable.
- **NY modification (DRL 236(B)(9)(b)(2); FCA 451):** substantial change; OR 3 years since order entered/modified/adjusted; OR 15%+ change in either party's gross income (involuntary, and must have sought work commensurate with ability). Parties may opt out of the 3-year and 15% bases in a written agreement. Retroactive only to date of application.
- **Traps:** NY age 21, not 18. A NY agreement silent on the CSSA recitals is vulnerable. Parent's voluntary reduction in income → court may impute income.

## Custody and visitation
- **Rule:** best interests of the child (UMDA §402 factors: wishes of parents and child, interaction/relationships, adjustment to home/school/community, mental and physical health, domestic violence). Legal vs physical; sole vs joint. Parental presumption over third parties.
- **Constitutional:** *Troxel v. Granville* (U.S. 2000) — fit parent's decisions get special weight; third-party (grandparent) visitation statute too broad as applied. *Palmore v. Sidoti* (U.S. 1984) — race cannot decide custody. *Stanley v. Illinois* (U.S. 1972); *Santosky v. Kramer* (U.S. 1982) — termination of parental rights needs clear and convincing evidence.
- **Modification:** substantial change in circumstances + best interests; many states bar modification within 2 years absent endangerment (UMDA §409).
- **Relocation:** splits — presumption for custodial parent (minority), burden on relocating parent, or pure best interests.
- **NY:** best interests, totality of circumstances; *Eschbach v. Eschbach* (N.Y. 1982) — factors include stability, prior agreements, quality of home environment, parental guidance, fitness, child's wishes (weighted by age/maturity), keeping siblings together; no single factor controls. Domestic violence must be considered (DRL 240(1)(a)). Joint custody inappropriate where parents are embattled (*Braiman v. Braiman* (N.Y. 1978)).
- **NY relocation:** *Tropea v. Tropea* (N.Y. 1996) — no threshold "exceptional circumstances" test; each request decided on best interests with all relevant factors (reasons for move, relationship with each parent, effect on contact, economic/emotional/educational enhancement, feasibility of preserving relationship). Relocating parent bears burden by preponderance.
- **NY third parties:** *Bennett v. Jeffreys* (N.Y. 1976) — nonparent must show extraordinary circumstances (surrender, abandonment, persistent neglect, unfitness, extended disruption) before best interests is reached; DRL 72(2) — grandparents may show extraordinary circumstances incl. 24-month care. Grandparent visitation standing DRL 72(1) (death of parent or equitable circumstances). **Non-biological partner:** *Brooke S.B. v. Elizabeth A.C.C.* (N.Y. 2016), overruling *Alison D. v. Virginia M.* (N.Y. 1991) — partner who proves by clear and convincing evidence a pre-conception agreement to conceive and raise the child together has standing as a "parent" under DRL 70.
- **NY forum:** Supreme Court (with matrimonial action) and Family Court (FCA Art. 6) have concurrent custody jurisdiction.
- **Traps:** a child's preference is a factor, not controlling. A NY court may not order joint custody over one parent's reasonable, sustained opposition when parents cannot cooperate.

## Interstate jurisdiction
- **UCCJEA (all states but MA; NY DRL Art. 5-A, DRL 75 et seq.):** initial jurisdiction priority: 1. **home state** (lived with parent 6 consecutive months immediately before commencement, or was home state within 6 months and a parent still lives there; infants <6 mo: since birth); 2. significant connection + substantial evidence (only if no home state or home state declines); 3. more appropriate forum declined; 4. default/vacuum. **Exclusive continuing jurisdiction** in the decree state until it determines no significant connection remains or all parties have left. **Temporary emergency jurisdiction** (child present + abandoned or emergency). Declining: inconvenient forum; unjustifiable conduct. Personal jurisdiction over parent not required for custody.
- **PKPA (28 U.S.C. §1738A):** federal full faith and credit to custody decrees consistent with it; no private federal cause of action (*Thompson v. Thompson* (U.S. 1988)).
- **UIFSA (all states; NY FCA Art. 5-B):** child support — broad long-arm bases; one-order world; issuing state keeps **continuing exclusive jurisdiction** to modify while obligor, obligee, or child resides there (or parties consent in writing to another state). Non-issuing state may modify only if all left issuing state and petitioner is a nonresident of the forum (the "play away" rule), or parties consent. Duration of support governed by the **issuing state's law** and cannot be changed by modification. *Kulko v. Superior Court* (U.S. 1978) — sending child to live with other parent is not minimum contacts.
- **FFCCSOA (28 U.S.C. §1738B):** full faith and credit to child-support orders; consistent with UIFSA.
- **Hague Abduction Convention / ICARA:** return to habitual residence unless exception (grave risk, 1-year + settled, consent/acquiescence, mature child's objection); *Monasky v. Taglieri* (U.S. 2020) — habitual residence totality; *Golan v. Saada* (U.S. 2022) — ameliorative measures not mandatory.
- **Traps:** NY's age-21 duration survives a move if NY issued the order (duration fixed by issuing state). Home state beats significant connection.

## Premarital and marital agreements
- **UPAA (1983, ~28 states):** unenforceable only if challenger proves (1) not voluntary, or (2) unconscionable **when executed** AND before execution no fair and reasonable disclosure, no waiver of disclosure, and no adequate knowledge. Spousal-support waiver that leaves spouse eligible for public assistance → court may order support. Child support cannot be adversely affected. No consideration required; must be in writing and signed.
- **UPMAA (2012, few states):** adds access to independent counsel / plain-language waiver explanation if unrepresented.
- **Minority/common law:** also unconscionable at time of enforcement (second look); *Simeone v. Simeone* (Pa. 1990) — contract law, no review of reasonableness.
- **Counsel:** *In re Marriage of Bonds* (Cal. 2000) — lack of counsel a factor not dispositive.
- **NY (DRL 236(B)(3)):** agreement before or during marriage valid if **in writing, subscribed by the parties, and acknowledged or proven in the manner required to entitle a deed to be recorded**. *Matisoff v. Dobi* (N.Y. 1997) — unacknowledged postnuptial agreement unenforceable even if both concede signing; *Galetta v. Galetta* (N.Y. 2013) — acknowledgment certificate missing required language is fatal (cure question left open). Maintenance terms must be fair and reasonable when made and not unconscionable at final judgment (236(B)(3)); child-support terms must satisfy CSSA recitals. Set aside also for fraud, duress, overreaching; *Christian v. Christian* (N.Y. 1977) — manifestly unfair agreement from overreaching. Waiver of elective share requires the same acknowledgment (EPTL 5-1.1-A(e)).
- **Traps:** NY acknowledgment is a validity requirement, not a mere evidentiary one. Unconscionability of maintenance terms is judged at two times in NY.

## Parentage
- **Presumptions:** marital presumption (child born during or within 300 days after marriage); holding-out; voluntary acknowledgment of paternity (VAP) — rescindable within 60 days, then only for fraud/duress/material mistake (42 U.S.C. §666(a)(5)). *Michael H. v. Gerald D.* (U.S. 1989) — state may bar biological father from rebutting marital presumption. Unwed father's rights depend on relationship (*Lehr v. Robertson* (U.S. 1983)).
- **UPA (2017):** gender-neutral; de facto parent; assisted reproduction — consenting intended parent is parent; gestational and genetic surrogacy with safeguards.
- **NY paternity:** FCA Art. 5; standard clear and convincing; equitable estoppel can bar a man from denying (or a mother from asserting) paternity against child's best interests (FCA 418(a), 532(a); *Matter of Shondel J. v. Mark D.* (N.Y. 2006)). Acknowledgment of paternity rescindable within 60 days (FCA 516-a).
- **NY Child-Parent Security Act (eff. 2/15/2021; FCA Art. 5-C):** judgment of parentage for children born via assisted reproduction (intended parents, incl. non-genetic); legalizes **compensated gestational surrogacy** with requirements (carrier 21+, independent counsel paid by intended parents, health insurance, court-approved agreement, surrogate's right to make health decisions). **Genetic (traditional) surrogacy agreements remain unenforceable** (DRL Art. 8; confirm scope before use). Donor is not a parent.
- **Traps:** marital presumption applies to same-sex spouses (*Pavan v. Smith* (U.S. 2017) — birth-certificate parity). NY estoppel runs to the child's best interests, not the adult's.

## Domestic violence and Family Court (NY)
- **FCA jurisdiction:** Art. 4 support; Art. 5 paternity; 5-A/5-B interstate; 5-C CPSA parentage; Art. 6 custody, guardianship, adoption-related, PINS related (Art. 7); Art. 8 family offense (concurrent with criminal court; "intimate relationship" covers non-family); Art. 10 child abuse/neglect. No divorce, no ED.
- **Family offense orders:** temporary ex parte; final order up to 2 years, or up to 5 years on aggravating circumstances (FCA 842; confirm before use).
- **Traps:** Family Court is a court of limited statutory jurisdiction — cannot entertain ED or divorce; Supreme Court can hear everything.
