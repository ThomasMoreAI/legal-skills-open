# Conflict of Laws — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: which state's law governs (torts, contracts, property, wills, marriage), domicile, characterization, renvoi, public policy, borrowing statutes, recognition/enforcement of sister-state and foreign judgments, divorce jurisdiction.
Scope: MEE rule first (First Restatement vested rights, Restatement (Second) most significant relationship, governmental interest); NY distinctions flagged **NY:** (NY = interest analysis for torts, center of gravity for contracts). Verified against statute text: GOL 5-1401, CPLR 202. Other doctrine from model knowledge; items marked (confirm before use) were not source-checked.

## Method: how to write a conflicts answer
1. Is there a **true/actual conflict**? If laws are the same, or only one state has an interest (false conflict), no choice needed. **NY:** first step is always whether an actual conflict exists (*Matter of Allstate Ins. Co. (Stolarz)* (N.Y. 1993)).
2. **Characterize** the issue (tort/contract/property/procedure; substance vs procedure).
3. Apply the **forum's** choice-of-law approach (federal court in diversity uses forum state's rules — *Klaxon* (U.S. 1941); after §1404(a) transfer, transferor's — *Van Dusen*).
4. Check escape devices: public policy, renvoi, procedure characterization, depeçage.
5. Constitutional limits: state must have significant contacts creating state interests so choice isn't arbitrary or unfair (*Allstate v. Hague* (U.S. 1981); *Phillips Petroleum v. Shutts* (U.S. 1985)).

## Domicile
- **Rule:** physical presence + intent to remain indefinitely (or no present intent to leave). Everyone has exactly one domicile. Domicile of origin persists until a new one is acquired (First Restatement; U.S. rule — not the English revival rule).
- Minors: parent's (custodial parent if separated). Incompetents: generally cannot change domicile. Spouses: may have separate domiciles.
- Corporations: state of incorporation (and PPB for diversity citizenship). Residence ≠ domicile (a person can have several residences; CPLR 202 uses **residence**).
- **Traps:** intent is measured at time of presence; declared intent vs conduct — conduct wins (voting, taxes, home, family). Temporary absence (military, school, prison) doesn't change domicile absent intent.

## Characterization, substance/procedure, renvoi, depeçage
- **Characterization:** forum characterizes by its own law (First Restatement). Manipulable — an escape device (e.g., treating a tort as contract).
- **Substance vs procedure:** forum applies its own procedure. Statutes of limitations traditionally **procedural** (forum SOL applies) — hence borrowing statutes; Restatement (Second) §142 (1988 rev.): forum applies its own SOL barring the claim unless exceptional circumstances; if forum SOL permits, apply it unless no substantial forum interest and the claim is barred by the state with the more significant relationship. Burden of proof and presumptions: usually substantive if they affect outcome. Damages caps/measure = substantive.
- **Renvoi:** reference to "whole law" of foreign state including its choice-of-law rules. Generally **rejected** (forum applies foreign internal law) except: title to land (situs's whole law), validity of decedent's movables (domicile's whole law), and where purpose is uniformity (Restatement (Second) §8). **NY:** a contractual choice of NY law refers to NY substantive law, excluding NY's conflicts rules (*Ministers & Missionaries Benefit Bd. v. Snow* (N.Y. 2015)).
- **Depeçage:** different issues in one case may be governed by different states' laws (routine under interest analysis; e.g., *Babcock* — Ontario rules of the road but NY guest-liability law).

## Torts — approaches
- **First Restatement (vested rights):** law of the **place of the wrong** = place of the last event necessary for liability (usually **injury**). Still followed by a handful of states.
- **Restatement (Second) §145:** state with the **most significant relationship** under §6 principles, considering (a) place of injury, (b) place of conduct, (c) domicile/residence/nationality/place of incorporation/business of parties, (d) place where relationship is centered. §146 personal injury: presumption for place of injury unless another state has more significant relationship. §6 factors: needs of interstate system, relevant policies of forum and other interested states, justified expectations, basic policies of the field, certainty/predictability/uniformity, ease of determination.
- **Governmental interest analysis (Currie):** identify each state's policy and whether applying it furthers that policy on these facts. False conflict → apply interested state. True conflict → forum law (Currie) or comparative impairment (California). Unprovided-for case (neither interested) → forum law / lex loci.
- **Better law (Leflar):** five choice-influencing considerations incl. application of the better rule of law.

## NY torts — interest analysis
- ***Babcock v. Jackson* (N.Y. 1963):** abandoned lex loci; NY host and guest on a trip from NY into Ontario — Ontario guest statute not applied; NY had the dominant interest (center of gravity/grouping of contacts + interests). ***Tooker v. Lopez* (N.Y. 1969):** Michigan guest statute not applied to NY domiciliaries.
- **Conduct-regulating vs loss-allocating** (*Padula v. Lilarn Properties* (N.Y. 1994); *Schultz v. Boy Scouts of America* (N.Y. 1985); *Cooney v. Osgood Machinery* (N.Y. 1993)):
  - **Conduct-regulating** rules (standards of care, rules of the road, safety statutes such as Labor Law §240/§241) → law of the **place of the tort** (locus has the predominant interest). *Padula*: NY Labor Law scaffold statutes didn't apply to a NY-domiciled worker injured in Massachusetts.
  - **Loss-allocating** rules (guest statutes, charitable immunity, contribution, vicarious-liability caps, damages caps, spousal/intrafamily immunity) → apply the **Neumeier rules**.
- ***Neumeier v. Kuehner* (N.Y. 1972) rules (loss allocation):**
  1. Parties **share a common domicile** → law of the common domicile (car registered there, for guest statutes). (*Schultz*: common NJ domicile → NJ charitable immunity applied to claims vs Boy Scouts.)
  2. **Split domicile, and the conduct/injury occurred in one party's domicile**: if defendant's conduct occurred in defendant's home state and that law protects him, he isn't held liable under plaintiff's domicile law; if plaintiff is injured in plaintiff's home state whose law permits recovery, defendant who came there can't interpose his home law. → effectively **law of the place of the tort** when it coincides with one party's domicile.
  3. **All other split-domicile cases** (incl. tort in a third state): **law of the place of the tort (lex loci)** normally applies, unless displacing it will advance the relevant substantive law purposes without impairing the smooth working of the multistate system or producing great uncertainty for litigants. (*Neumeier*: Ontario guest statute applied — Ontario guest, NY driver, accident in Ontario.)
- **Contribution/indemnity among joint tortfeasors:** loss-allocating; with parties in different states, often third Neumeier rule; *Cooney*: Missouri workers' comp exclusivity (bar on contribution vs employer) applied — accident in Missouri; reliance interests of employer significant.
- **Traps:** Neumeier rules are **not** for conduct-regulating rules. Where parties share a domicile, "domicile" for corporations is complicated — courts may look to the parties' relationship's seat. Public policy is rarely enough to reject another state's loss-allocation law chosen under Neumeier (*Cooney*: public policy exception requires the proponent to show the foreign law violates a fundamental NY principle **and** enough NY contacts).

## Contracts
- **Party choice — Restatement (Second) §187:** honored if the issue is one the parties could have resolved by explicit provision; otherwise honored unless (a) chosen state has **no substantial relationship** to parties/transaction and no other reasonable basis, or (b) application would be contrary to a **fundamental policy** of a state with a **materially greater interest** that would be the governing law absent the choice.
- **No choice — §188:** most significant relationship considering place of contracting, negotiation, performance, subject-matter location, domicile/place of business of parties. Specific: land → situs (§189–190); life insurance → insured's domicile (§192); casualty insurance → principal location of insured risk (§193).
- **UCC 1-301:** parties may choose law of a state bearing a **reasonable relation** to the transaction; otherwise forum applies UCC to transactions bearing an appropriate relation to forum. Specific UCC rules override (e.g., 9-301 perfection).
- **First Restatement:** validity → place of making; performance → place of performance.
- **NY:** "center of gravity"/grouping of contacts (*Auten v. Auten* (N.Y. 1954) — separation agreement made in NY between English domiciliaries governed by English law); with interest analysis where policies are clear (*Zurich Ins. v. Shearson Lehman* (N.Y. 1994)); §188 contacts; place of contracting and performance weigh most (*Allstate (Stolarz)*). Insurance: state understood to be the principal location of the insured risk.
- **NY — GOL 5-1401:** parties to a contract/transaction **aggregating not less than \$250,000** may choose **NY law** whether or not the contract bears a reasonable relation to NY. Excludes contracts for labor or personal services; transactions for personal, family, or household services; and matters governed by UCC 1-301(c). Effect: NY courts enforce the clause **without any conflicts analysis** (*IRB-Brasil Resseguros v. Inepar Invs.* (N.Y. 2012)). **GOL 5-1402:** contracts **≥ \$1,000,000** with NY choice-of-law clause and consent to NY jurisdiction → foreign or nonresident party may sue in NY; forum non conveniens dismissal barred (with exceptions). (confirm 5-1402 details before use)
- **Traps:** A NY choice-of-law clause governs contract claims, not necessarily tort claims (fraud, etc.) unless the clause is broad enough ("arising out of or relating to" — construed narrowly in NY) (*Finance One v. Lehman Bros.* (2d Cir. 2005)) (confirm before use). Choice of law ≠ choice of forum; forum-selection clauses are separately enforceable (*Brooke Group v. JCH Syndicate* (N.Y. 1996)) (confirm before use).

## Property, wills, trusts, estates
- **Real property:** situs law for title, conveyance validity, liens, succession (via situs's whole law).
- **Personal property inter vivos:** Restatement (Second) — most significant relationship, usually situs at time of transfer.
- **Succession:** land → situs; movables → decedent's **domicile at death**.
- **NY — EPTL 3-5.1:** (b)(1) disposition of **real** property (incl. validity/effect) → law where land is situated; (b)(2) **personal** property → law of decedent's **domicile at death**. (c) **Formal validity**: a will is admissible if in writing and signed and executed per NY law, the law of the place where executed, or the law of testator's domicile at execution or death (for personal property/NY realty — confirm scope before use). (h) A testator not domiciled in NY may elect in the will that NY law govern disposition of property situated in NY. Spousal right of election (EPTL 5-1.1-A) available to surviving spouse of NY domiciliary; nondomiciliary estate elects under domicile law, except for NY property under 3-5.1(h) election (confirm before use).
- **Trusts:** inter vivos trusts of movables — law designated by settlor if substantial relation; administration — place of administration.

## Family law
- **Marriage validity:** valid where celebrated (lex loci celebrationis) → valid everywhere, unless contrary to strong public policy of the domicile (polygamous, incestuous by close degree). **NY:** recognizes marriages valid where contracted unless expressly prohibited by NY positive law or abhorrent to natural law — uncle-niece marriage valid in Rhode Island recognized (*Matter of May's Estate* (N.Y. 1953)). Common-law marriages valid where contracted are recognized in NY though NY doesn't permit them.
- **Divorce jurisdiction:** ex parte divorce valid if **one spouse domiciled** in the rendering state; entitled to FF&C, but domicile finding can be collaterally attacked by a spouse who did not appear (*Williams v. North Carolina I & II* (U.S. 1942, 1945)); a spouse who **appeared** is precluded (*Sherrer v. Sherrer* (U.S. 1948)); strangers bound by what rendering state would permit (*Johnson v. Muelberger* (U.S. 1951)).
- **Divisible divorce:** court without PJ over absent spouse can dissolve marriage but cannot cut off alimony/property rights (*Estin v. Estin* (U.S. 1948); *Vanderbilt v. Vanderbilt* (U.S. 1957)). Custody: UCCJEA home state (6 months) (DRL Art. 5-A in NY). Support: UIFSA continuing exclusive jurisdiction (FCA Art. 5-B in NY).

## Public policy exception
- **Rule:** forum may refuse to apply otherwise-applicable foreign law contrary to the forum's strong public policy. Narrow; results in dismissal, not application of forum law (in traditional approach).
- **NY:** refuse foreign law only if it would "violate some fundamental principle of justice, some prevalent conception of good morals, some deep-rooted tradition of the common weal" (*Loucks v. Standard Oil Co.* (N.Y. 1918) (Cardozo)); proponent of exception bears burden to show sufficient NY contacts and fundamental policy (*Cooney*). Wrongful death damages cap of place of accident refused as against NY public policy (NY Const. art. I, §16) (*Kilberg v. Northeast Airlines* (N.Y. 1961)).
- **Traps:** public policy does **not** apply to sister-state **judgments** (FF&C — *Baker v. General Motors* (U.S. 1998); *Fauntleroy v. Lum* (U.S. 1908)); it may apply to sister-state **laws** within due-process limits.

## Statutes of limitations and NY borrowing statute
- **CPLR 202:** a cause of action **accruing outside NY** cannot be commenced after the **shorter** of NY's period or the period of the place where it accrued — **except** where it accrued in favor of a **NY resident**, NY's period alone applies.
- **Accrual place:** personal injury — where injury occurs; purely economic injury — where plaintiff **resides and sustains the economic impact** (*Global Fin. Corp. v. Triarc Corp.* (N.Y. 1999)); assignee stands in shoes of out-of-state assignor (*Portfolio Recovery Assocs. v. King* (N.Y. 2010)). Foreign tolling provisions are borrowed with the foreign period.
- **Traps:** "resident" not "domiciliary" — a NY resident plaintiff escapes the shorter foreign period; a nonresident suing on a foreign-accrued claim must satisfy **both**. A NY-accrued claim uses NY's period regardless of plaintiff's residence. In federal diversity court, CPLR 202 applies (substantive under Erie/Guaranty Trust).

## Recognition and enforcement of judgments
- **Sister-state judgments — FF&C (U.S. Const. art. IV, §1; 28 U.S.C. §1738):** must be given the preclusive effect the rendering state gives them if (1) rendering court had SMJ and PJ, (2) judgment on the merits, (3) final. No public policy exception; no re-examination of merits even for errors of law (*Fauntleroy*). Jurisdiction collateral attack barred if the issue was litigated or the defendant appeared (*Durfee v. Duke* (U.S. 1963)); a defaulting defendant may attack PJ. Fraud: extrinsic fraud only. Equitable decrees: FF&C but enforcement mechanisms follow forum (*Baker*). Sister state need not apply the rendering state's **statute of limitations** for enforcement of judgments.
- **NY enforcement procedure:** CPLR Art. 54 (Uniform Enforcement of Foreign Judgments Act) — file authenticated copy + affidavit with a county clerk → treated as NY judgment; not available for default judgments on nonappearance (then sue on judgment, e.g., CPLR 3213 motion for SJ in lieu of complaint) (confirm before use).
- **Foreign-country money judgments — NY CPLR Art. 53** (Uniform Foreign Country Money Judgments Recognition Act; NY adopted the 2005 revised Act effective 2021) (confirm before use): final, conclusive, enforceable where rendered. **Mandatory nonrecognition (CPLR 5304(a)):** system lacking impartial tribunals/due process; no PJ; no SMJ. **Discretionary (5304(b)):** insufficient notice; fraud; repugnant to NY/US public policy; conflicts with another final judgment; contrary to forum-selection agreement; seriously inconvenient forum (jurisdiction based only on service); substantial doubt about integrity of rendering court; specific proceeding lacked due process; **defamation judgment** unless it provides at least the free-speech protections of US and NY constitutions (confirm grounds list before use). Recognition by action, counterclaim, cross-claim, affirmative defense, or CPLR 3213 motion. No PJ over the debtor in NY is required to recognize a foreign-country judgment (*Lenchyshyn v. Pelko Elec.* (4th Dep't 2001)). Foreign country judgments are **not** entitled to FF&C — comity only.
- **Traps:** FF&C applies to sister states, not foreign countries. SPEECH Act (28 U.S.C. §4102) bars federal/state recognition of foreign defamation judgments not meeting First Amendment protections.
