# Administrative Law — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: challenging a NY state/local agency action (Article 78), SAPA rulemaking or adjudication, licensing, agency power/delegation, FOIL records requests, Open Meetings Law; federal APA only for contrast.
Scope: NY-first (NYLE tests NY administrative law); federal APA brief for contrast. Verified against nysenate.gov text: CPLR 217(1), 7803, 7804(c); SAPA 202 (comment period, revised rules, emergency rules, expiry, 202(8) challenge period); POL 89(3)–(4) FOIL deadlines and fees; POL 104 notice. Other doctrine from model knowledge; items marked (confirm before use) were not source-checked.

## NY agency power and separation of powers
- **Delegation:** Legislature may delegate with reasonable safeguards/standards; agencies may fill interstices but not make fundamental policy choices.
- ***Boreali v. Axelrod* (N.Y. 1987)** — Public Health Council's indoor-smoking regulations invalid; factors (not rigid): (1) agency balanced competing economic/social concerns using its own ideas of sound public policy rather than implementing legislative policy; (2) wrote on a **clean slate**, creating its own comprehensive rules without legislative guidance; (3) acted in an area where the **Legislature repeatedly tried and failed** to reach agreement; (4) no special **expertise** required. Applied: NYC soda portion-cap rule invalid (*Matter of NY Statewide Coalition of Hispanic Chambers of Commerce v. NYC Dept. of Health* (N.Y. 2014)); NYC flu-vaccine rule for child care upheld (*Matter of Garcia v. NYC Dept. of Health* (N.Y. 2018)).
- **Deference to agency interpretation:** pure statutory reading with no specialized knowledge → **little deference**, court decides (*Kurcsics v. Merchants Mut. Ins.* (N.Y. 1980)); interpretation involving specialized knowledge, operational practices, or data → deference if not irrational or unreasonable. Agency's reading of its **own regulation** → deference unless irrational. **Federal contrast:** *Chevron* overruled — courts exercise independent judgment on statutory meaning (*Loper Bright v. Raimondo* (U.S. 2024)); *Auer* deference survives only for genuinely ambiguous regulations after exhausting tools (*Kisor v. Wilkie* (U.S. 2019)).

## SAPA — rulemaking (Art. 2)
- **"Rule"** (SAPA 102(2)): agency statement of general applicability implementing/applying law, incl. amendment/repeal; excludes internal management rules, forms, interpretive statements/policy statements that are merely explanatory. An agency "policy" applied rigidly as a fixed general principle without regard to facts is a **rule** that must go through SAPA — invalid if not promulgated (*Matter of Schwartfigure v. Hartnett* (N.Y. 1994)) (confirm before use).
- **Notice of proposed rule making** published in the **State Register** (SAPA 202(1)); contents: statutory authority, text/summary, contact, regulatory impact statement (202-a), regulatory flexibility analysis for small business/local government (202-b), rural area flexibility analysis (202-bb), job impact statement (201-a).
- **Comment period:** no adoption until at least **60 days** after publication of the notice, or **5 days** after the last public hearing if a hearing is required, whichever is later (SAPA 202(1)(a)). Public hearing required only if statute requires (otherwise discretionary).
- **Expiration:** notice of proposed rule expires if the rule isn't adopted within **365 days** after the later of publication or the last hearing (SAPA 202).
- **Revised rule:** substantial revisions require a notice of revised rule making and additional comment period: the notice of revised rule making must appear in the State Register at least **45 days** before adoption, and comments are accepted for at least 45 days after publication (SAPA 202(4-a)). Assessment of public comment required on adoption.
- **Emergency rules** (SAPA 202(6)): on finding immediate adoption necessary to preserve public health, safety, or general welfare and compliance with notice would be contrary to public interest; effective on filing (or later date); **max 90 days** after filing unless readopted; each readoption **max 60 days**.
- **Consensus rule making** (202(1)(b)): for noncontroversial rules; any objection forces regular process.
- **Effective date:** on publication of notice of adoption in State Register or later date stated (SAPA 203).
- **Challenge:** substantial compliance with SAPA procedures suffices; action to invalidate rule for procedural noncompliance must be commenced within **4 months** after effective date (SAPA 202(8)). Substantive challenge to a quasi-legislative rule: **declaratory judgment** action (not Article 78 certiorari); standard is whether rule has a rational basis and is not unreasonable, arbitrary, or capricious; challenger bears heavy burden.

## SAPA — adjudication (Art. 3) and licensing (Art. 4)
- **Adjudicatory proceeding** (SAPA 102(3)): activity before an agency in which a determination of legal rights/duties/privileges of named parties is **required by law** to be made only on a record and after an opportunity for a hearing. SAPA hearing rights attach only where statute or due process requires a hearing.
- **Notice (SAPA 301):** reasonable notice incl. time, place, nature, legal authority, statutes/rules involved, short plain statement of matters asserted. Opportunity to present written argument, evidence, cross-examine.
- **Evidence (SAPA 306):** agency **need not observe rules of evidence** (except privileges); irrelevant/immaterial/unduly repetitious evidence may be excluded; **hearsay admissible** and hearsay alone may be substantial evidence if sufficiently relevant and probative (*People ex rel. Vega v. Smith* (N.Y. 1985)); official notice of judicially noticeable facts and generally recognized technical facts within agency's competence with notice to parties. Decision must be supported by and in accordance with **substantial evidence**.
- **Record (SAPA 302); presiding officer impartiality (303);** ex parte communications barred (307(2)). **Decision (SAPA 307):** in writing or stated on record, with **findings of fact and conclusions of law** (or reasons) — permits meaningful review. Agency head may reject ALJ findings but must explain.
- **Due process:** hearing required when protected property/liberty interest (tenured employment, licenses, benefits) — *Mathews v. Eldridge* (U.S. 1976) balancing; pre-termination opportunity to respond for tenured public employees (*Cleveland Bd. of Educ. v. Loudermill* (U.S. 1985)).
- **Licenses (SAPA 401):** when licensee timely files for **renewal**, existing license does not expire until application finally determined (401(2)). **Summary suspension** (401(3)): permitted if agency finds public health, safety, or welfare **imperatively requires emergency action** and incorporates the finding in its order; proceedings for revocation/other action must be **promptly instituted and determined**. Otherwise revocation, suspension, annulment, or withdrawal requires notice and opportunity for hearing.

## CPLR Article 78 — overview
- **Replaces** certiorari, mandamus, prohibition (CPLR 7801). Against a "body or officer" (7802(a)) — state and local agencies, officers, and courts/judges (for prohibition/mandamus); not the Legislature's legislative acts.
- **Not available** (7801) for a determination (1) **not final**, or (2) which can be adequately reviewed by **appeal** to a court or another body/officer. Criminal-court orders generally not reviewable except prohibition in narrow circumstances.
- **The questions (CPLR 7803)** — the four core questions:
  1. **Mandamus to compel** — (1) "whether the body or officer **failed to perform a duty enjoined upon it by law**." Lies only for a **ministerial**, nondiscretionary act where petitioner has a **clear legal right** (*Klostermann v. Cuomo* (N.Y. 1984)) — can compel a decision, not a particular discretionary outcome.
  2. **Prohibition** — (2) "whether the body or officer proceeded, is proceeding or is about to proceed **without or in excess of jurisdiction**." Extraordinary; lies against judicial/quasi-judicial action only where there's a clear legal right and the act is outside jurisdiction or in excess of powers — **not** mere legal error correctable on appeal; court weighs gravity of harm and availability of other remedies (*Matter of Holtzman v. Goldman* (N.Y. 1988); *Matter of Rush v. Mordue* (N.Y. 1986)).
  3. **Mandamus to review** — (3) "whether a determination was made in **violation of lawful procedure**, was affected by an **error of law** or was **arbitrary and capricious** or an **abuse of discretion**, including abuse of discretion as to the **measure or mode of penalty** or discipline imposed." Used for determinations made **without** a required evidentiary hearing.
  4. **Certiorari (substantial evidence)** — (4) "whether a determination made as a result of a **hearing held, and at which evidence was taken, pursuant to direction by law** is, on the entire record, supported by **substantial evidence**."
  - (7803(5) addresses Education Law §4404(3) state review officer determinations — not core.)
- **Traps:** The substantial-evidence question applies **only** where a hearing with evidence was **required by law** (statute, regulation, or due process). A voluntary/informal hearing → arbitrary-and-capricious review, not substantial evidence.

## Article 78 — standards of review
- **Arbitrary and capricious:** action taken **without sound basis in reason** and generally **without regard to the facts**; if rational basis exists, court may not substitute its judgment (*Matter of Pell v. Board of Educ.* (N.Y. 1974)). Agency departing from its own precedent without explanation = arbitrary (*Matter of Charles A. Field Delivery Serv.* (N.Y. 1985)).
- **Penalty:** set aside only if **so disproportionate to the offense as to be shocking to one's sense of fairness** (*Pell*). Court remits to agency for a new penalty rather than imposing its own.
- **Substantial evidence:** "such relevant proof as a reasonable mind may accept as adequate to support a conclusion or ultimate fact" — less than preponderance; court may not weigh evidence or reject credibility choices (*300 Gramatan Ave. Assocs. v. State Div. of Human Rights* (N.Y. 1978)). Hearsay can suffice.
- **Error of law:** court decides (subject to Kurcsics deference rules).
- **Grounds limited to agency's stated reasons:** a determination can be upheld only on the grounds the agency invoked (*Matter of Montauk Improvement v. Proccacino* (N.Y. 1976)) (confirm before use).

## Article 78 — procedure
- **Statute of limitations CPLR 217(1):** **4 months** after the determination becomes **final and binding** upon petitioner, **or** after respondent's **refusal, upon demand**, to perform its duty (mandamus to compel) — unless a shorter period is set by the authorizing law (e.g., zoning board of appeals — 30 days under Town Law §267-c / Village Law 7-712-c; SEQRA challenges follow underlying action) (confirm specific short periods before use).
  - **Final and binding:** agency reached a **definitive position** on the issue that inflicts an **actual, concrete injury**, and the injury is not prevented or significantly ameliorated by further administrative action or steps available to the complainant (*Matter of Walton v. NYS Dept. of Correctional Servs.* (N.Y. 2007); *Essex County v. Zagata* (N.Y. 1998)). Runs from when petitioner is notified/aggrieved.
  - **Mandamus to compel:** petitioner must make a demand within a **reasonable time** (laches; courts often measure by ~4 months after the right to demand arose), then 4 months from refusal.
  - Request for reconsideration does **not** extend the period unless agency actually reconsiders on new facts.
- **Declaratory judgment vs Article 78:** court looks at substance — if the claim could have been brought as Article 78, the **4-month** period applies to a declaratory action (*Solnick v. Whalen* (N.Y. 1980)). Challenges to quasi-legislative acts (rules, legislation) → declaratory judgment with its own (often 6-year, CPLR 213(1)) period; but procedural challenges to legislative-type acts may take 4 months.
- **Exhaustion:** must exhaust available administrative remedies (*Watergate II Apts. v. Buffalo Sewer Auth.* (N.Y. 1978)). **Exceptions:** constitutional challenge to agency action (facial), agency action **wholly beyond its grant of power**, resort to agency would be **futile**, or pursuit would cause **irreparable injury**.
- **Standing:** injury in fact, **different in kind or degree from the public at large** (for land-use/environmental), within the **zone of interests** protected by the statute (*Society of Plastics Indus. v. County of Suffolk* (N.Y. 1991)); proximity to project gives presumption of injury in land use; organizational standing requires a member with standing, germane interests, no individual participation needed.
- **Venue/court (CPLR 506(b)):** Supreme Court in a county within the judicial district where respondent made the determination, refused to perform, or where proceedings were brought/taken, or where respondent's principal office is. Proceedings against a **Supreme Court justice, County Court judge**, and certain state bodies → directly in **Appellate Division** (CPLR 506(b)(1), 7804(b)).
- **Pleadings/timing (CPLR 7804):** notice of petition + petition + affidavits served **at least 20 days** before the return date (order to show cause alternative); answer at least **5 days** before (7804(c)); reply at least **1 day** before. Commenced by **filing** (CPLR 304). 7804(f): respondent may move to dismiss on objections in point of law; if denied, court must permit answer (unless facts fully presented — *Matter of Nassau BOCES v. Board of Educ.* (N.Y. 1984)) (confirm before use). Summary determination as on SJ (7804(h): trial only if triable fact issue).
- **Transfer to Appellate Division — CPLR 7804(g):** if the petition raises a **substantial evidence (7803(4))** question, Supreme Court must first dispose of other objections that could **terminate** the proceeding (SOL, standing, exhaustion, jurisdiction, lack of hearing) and then **transfer** the case to the Appellate Division for determination. Appellate Division reviews all issues de novo as if originally filed there. If transfer was improper (no substantial-evidence issue), AD retains and decides anyway.
- **Relief (CPLR 7806):** judgment may grant the relief to which petitioner is entitled; **damages/restitution only if incidental** to the primary relief and could be recovered against respondent in the same capacity in Supreme Court (e.g., back pay). Remittal to agency is usual remedy.
- **Appeals:** Supreme Court's final Article 78 judgment appealable as of right; **intermediate (nonfinal) orders in Art. 78 are not** (CPLR 5701(b)(1)).

## FOIL — Public Officers Law Art. 6 (§§84–90)
- **Scope:** "agency" = state or municipal department, board, bureau, division, commission, committee, public authority, public corporation, etc.; **excludes the judiciary** (courts/court records governed by other law) and the **State Legislature** (separate duties under POL §88). "Record" = any information kept, held, filed, produced, or reproduced by, with, or for an agency in any physical form. Agency need not create a record it doesn't possess (§89(3)(a)), but must extract electronic data if doing so requires less work than creating a new record (confirm before use).
- **Presumption of access:** all records open unless within an **exemption (§87(2))**, construed **narrowly**; burden on agency to show **particularized and specific justification** (*Matter of Fink v. Lefkowitz* (N.Y. 1979); *Matter of Gould v. NYC Police Dept.* (N.Y. 1996)); requester's purpose/status irrelevant. Blanket denials improper; redact and release remainder.
- **§87(2) exemptions (may deny):** (a) specifically exempted by state/federal statute; (b) unwarranted invasion of personal privacy (§89(2)); (c) would impair present/imminent contract awards or collective bargaining; (d) trade secrets or commercial info whose disclosure would cause **substantial injury** to competitive position of the submitting enterprise; (e) compiled for **law enforcement** purposes and disclosure would interfere with investigation/proceedings, deprive right to fair trial, identify confidential source, or reveal non-routine investigative techniques; (f) endanger life or safety; (g) **inter-agency or intra-agency materials** — **except** statistical/factual tabulations, instructions to staff affecting the public, **final agency policy or determinations**, and external audits; (h) examination questions/answers before final administration; (i) disclosure would jeopardize agency IT/critical infrastructure security; plus later-added categories (confirm list before use).
- **Police discipline:** Civil Rights Law §50-a **repealed June 2020** — law enforcement disciplinary records now FOIL-able subject to limited redactions (§87(4-a),(4-b); §89(2-b)) (confirm before use).
- **Timeline (§89(3)(a)):** within **5 business days** of receiving a written request reasonably describing the record: make available, deny in writing, or **acknowledge** receipt with a **reasonable approximate date** for grant/denial (reasonable under the circumstances); if not granted within **20 business days** of acknowledgement, state in writing the reason and a date certain within a reasonable period. Failure to comply = constructive denial.
- **Administrative appeal (§89(4)(a)):** within **30 days** of denial, appeal in writing to the head/chief executive/governing body; it must respond within **10 business days** — explain reasons in writing or provide access. Copies of appeals and determinations sent to the **Committee on Open Government**. Constructive denial of appeal if no timely response. **Exhaust** the appeal before suing (unless agency failed to advise of appeal rights).
- **Judicial review (§89(4)(b)):** Article 78 (4 months from appeal denial). **Fees (§89(4)(c)):** court **may** award reasonable attorney's fees and costs if petitioner **substantially prevailed** and agency **failed to respond within statutory time**; court **shall** award if petitioner substantially prevailed and agency had **no reasonable basis** for denying access.
- **Costs:** up to \$0.25 per page for copies ≤ 9x14 inches; actual cost otherwise; no charge for search/review time except limited employee time for electronic records over 2 hours (§87(1)(b)) (confirm before use).
- **Traps:** FOIL and civil discovery are separate — a litigant may FOIL records from an agency adversary; pending litigation doesn't bar FOIL (*Matter of Farbman & Sons v. NYC Health & Hosps. Corp.* (N.Y. 1984)). Privacy balancing is for the agency; names/addresses may be withheld if for commercial/fund-raising use (§89(2)(b)(iii)).

## Open Meetings Law — Public Officers Law Art. 7 (§§100–111)
- **Scope:** every meeting of a **public body** (entity of two or more members performing a governmental function, incl. committees/subcommittees) open to the general public. "Meeting" = official convening of a quorum for conducting public business, **in person or by videoconference** (§102(1)); informal gatherings of a quorum to discuss public business count — no action required. Quorum by email/serial conversations can be a problem.
- **Notice (§104):** meeting scheduled at least **one week** ahead → notice to news media and conspicuous public posting at least **72 hours** before; other meetings → notice to extent practicable at a reasonable time before. If agency website exists, post notice online; records to be discussed made available (to extent practicable) before/at meeting (confirm details before use).
- **Videoconferencing (§103-a, 2022):** permitted only under adopted local resolution and procedures; quorum must be physically present in one or more locations open to the public unless extraordinary circumstances (confirm before use).
- **Executive session (§105):** convened **only in an open meeting** by **majority vote of the total membership**, on a motion identifying the **general area** of subject matter. Permitted subjects: (a) matters imperiling public safety if disclosed; (b) identity of law enforcement agent or informer; (c) information on current or future investigation/prosecution of a criminal offense that would imperil law enforcement; (d) discussions regarding **proposed, pending, or current litigation**; (e) **collective negotiations** under Civil Service Law Art. 14 (Taylor Law); (f) medical, financial, credit, or employment history of a **particular person or corporation**, or matters leading to appointment, employment, promotion, demotion, discipline, suspension, dismissal, or removal of a particular person or corporation; (g) preparation, grading, or administration of exams; (h) proposed acquisition, sale, or lease of **real property** or acquisition of securities, but **only when publicity would substantially affect the value**. Formal votes may be taken in executive session only where not appropriating public money (confirm before use). Attorney-client privileged consultation is separately exempt under §108(3) (matters made confidential by law).
- **Exemptions (§108):** judicial or quasi-judicial proceedings (**except** proceedings of **zoning boards of appeals**); deliberations of political committees, conferences, and caucuses; matters made confidential by federal or state law.
- **Minutes (§106):** open-session minutes (motions, proposals, resolutions, formal votes) available within **2 weeks**; executive-session minutes (only of final action taken, if any) within **1 week**.
- **Enforcement (§107):** Article 78 or declaratory/injunctive action; court may, **upon good cause shown**, declare void any action taken in violation, in whole or in part; must award costs and attorney's fees to successful petitioner if action taken in violation or body voted in private session improperly (fees mandatory in some circumstances since 2021 amendment; confirm before use). Unintentional failure to give notice does not alone void action.

## Federal APA — contrast only
- **Rulemaking §553:** notice in Federal Register, comment, concise general statement of basis and purpose; exempt: interpretive rules, policy statements, procedural rules, good cause; 30-day delayed effective date (exceptions). Logical outgrowth test for final rule. Formal rulemaking (§§556–557) only when statute requires "on the record after opportunity for an agency hearing" (*U.S. v. Florida East Coast Ry.* (U.S. 1973)).
- **Adjudication §554:** formal only when statute requires on-the-record hearing; ALJ, burden on proponent (§556(d)).
- **Review §706:** arbitrary and capricious — hard look, reasoned explanation, consider important aspects (*Motor Vehicle Mfrs. v. State Farm* (U.S. 1983)); substantial evidence for formal proceedings; statutory questions — independent judgment (*Loper Bright* (U.S. 2024)). Reviewability: final agency action (*Bennett v. Spear* (U.S. 1997)); not committed to agency discretion by law (§701(a)(2)); exhaustion required only where statute or agency rule requires and makes action inoperative pending appeal (*Darby v. Cisneros* (U.S. 1993)). Limitations: 28 U.S.C. §2401(a) 6 years accrues when plaintiff is injured (*Corner Post v. Fed. Reserve* (U.S. 2024)). Major questions doctrine (*West Virginia v. EPA* (U.S. 2022)). Agency civil penalties for common-law-analog claims → Seventh Amendment jury (*SEC v. Jarkesy* (U.S. 2024)).
- **Key NY contrasts:** NY Article 78 is a single proceeding with statutory "questions" and a 4-month SOL (federal APA: 6 years); NY transfers substantial-evidence cases to the Appellate Division; NY Boreali factors vs federal nondelegation/major questions; NY SAPA comment period 60 days (federal §553 sets none).
