# Secured Transactions (UCC Art. 9) — rules, NY distinctions, traps

Load when: security interests in personal property — scope, attachment, perfection, priority, proceeds, fixtures, buyers and lien creditors, default, repossession, disposition, strict foreclosure.
Scope: uniform revised Art. 9 (2001, with 2010 amendments) — the MEE rule; NY distinctions flagged **NY:**. Verified: doctrine from model knowledge; numbers are uniform-text numbers; items marked confirm-before-use were not source-checked.

## Scope (9-109) and classification
- **Covers:** consensual security interests in personal property and fixtures; sales of accounts, chattel paper, payment intangibles, promissory notes; agricultural liens; consignments (commercial). **Excludes:** real estate interests (but a note secured by a mortgage is Art. 9 collateral), landlord's liens, wage assignments, most insurance (except health-care receivables), judgment liens, deposit accounts in consumer transactions.
- **Lease vs. disguised security interest (1-203):** bright-line — security interest if lease not terminable by lessee **and** (term ≥ remaining economic life, lessee bound to renew for economic life or become owner, or option to renew/buy for nominal consideration).
- **Collateral types** classified by debtor's primary use of goods: consumer goods (personal/family/household), equipment (residual), inventory (held for sale/lease, raw materials, work in process), farm products. Intangibles: accounts, chattel paper, instruments, investment property, deposit accounts, letter-of-credit rights, commercial tort claims, general intangibles (residual — IP, software, payment intangibles).
- **Traps:** same item can be inventory to a dealer and consumer goods to the buyer. A car used by a lawyer "mostly for work" = equipment.

## Attachment (9-203)
- **Elements:** 1. value given; 2. debtor has rights in the collateral (or power to transfer); 3. one of: authenticated (signed/record) security agreement describing collateral, secured party possession under agreement, control (deposit accounts, investment property, electronic chattel paper, LC rights), or delivery of certificated security.
- **Description (9-108):** reasonably identifies — by category or UCC type suffices; **supergeneric "all assets" is insufficient in the security agreement** (9-108(c)) though fine in a financing statement (9-504). Commercial tort claims need specific description (not by type), and after-acquired commercial tort claims cannot be covered (9-204(b)). In **consumer transactions**, description by UCC type alone is insufficient for consumer goods, security entitlements, securities accounts, commodity accounts (9-108(e)).
- **After-acquired property (9-204):** effective, except consumer goods not acquired within **10 days** after secured party gives value, and commercial tort claims. **Future advances** (9-204(c)) may be secured.
- **Automatic attachment to proceeds (9-203(f), 9-315)** and supporting obligations.
- **Traps:** a financing statement is not a security agreement; an oral agreement + no possession = no attachment. Composite-document rule: several writings together may satisfy.

## Perfection
- **Filing** (default method; 9-310): financing statement with debtor's name, secured party name, indication of collateral (9-502). Filing is effective for **5 years** (9-515); continuation statement only within the **6 months before lapse**; lapse → unperfected and deemed never perfected against a purchaser for value (9-515(c)). Filing may precede attachment and the security agreement.
- **Name errors (9-506):** seriously misleading unless a search under the correct name in the filing office's standard search logic would disclose it. Registered organization: name on public organic record. Individual: 2010 amendments — Alternative A (name on unexpired driver's license) or B. **NY:** NY adopted the 2010 amendments effective 2014-12-17; confirm NY's individual-name alternative before use.
- **Name change (9-507(c)):** existing filing remains effective for collateral acquired before and within **4 months** after the change; must amend within 4 months to cover later-acquired collateral.
- **Where to file (9-301, 9-307):** law of **debtor's location** governs perfection of nonpossessory interests. Registered organization (corp, LLC, LP) → **state of organization**. Individual → **principal residence**. Unregistered organization → place of business / chief executive office if more than one. Goods covered by certificate of title → law of issuing state (9-303). Possessory → location of collateral. Fixture filings → real property records where land is. Deposit accounts → bank's jurisdiction (9-304).
- **Relocation (9-316):** perfection continues until earliest of lapse, **4 months** after debtor relocates to another jurisdiction, or **1 year** after transfer of collateral to a person located in another jurisdiction.
- **Possession (9-313):** goods, instruments, negotiable documents, money (money: **only** possession), tangible chattel paper, certificated securities. Perfected only while possessed.
- **Control:** deposit accounts (**only** control as original collateral, 9-312(b)(1)); investment property (control or filing); electronic chattel paper, LC rights.
- **Automatic perfection (9-309):** **PMSI in consumer goods** (except goods subject to certificate-of-title law — cars need title notation); casual/isolated assignment of accounts not a significant part of assignor's outstanding accounts; sale of payment intangibles/promissory notes. Temporary automatic: instruments, certificated securities, negotiable documents for **20 days** on new value (9-312(e)); proceeds 20 days.
- **Certificate-of-title goods (9-311):** perfect by notation on the title; filing ineffective — **except inventory held for sale by a dealer**, which is perfected by filing.
- **PMSI definition (9-103):** seller-financing or enabling loan actually used to acquire the collateral. Dual-status rule in non-consumer transactions; consumer transactions left to courts.
- **NY:** filing office for most collateral is the NY Department of State; fixture filings and timber/minerals in the county real property records (NYC: City Register) (confirm before use).

## Priority
- **General rule (9-322(a)(1)):** among perfected interests, **first to file or perfect**, whichever earlier (a pre-filing lender can win even if the other attached first). Perfected beats unperfected; among unperfected, first to attach.
- **PMSI super-priority (9-324):**
  - **Non-inventory goods (equipment, consumer goods):** PMSI beats earlier-filed interest if perfected **when debtor receives possession or within 20 days after**.
  - **Inventory:** PMSI perfected when debtor receives possession **and** the PMSI holder sends authenticated notification to prior filed inventory secured parties **before** debtor receives the inventory; notice good for **5 years**. Priority extends to identifiable cash proceeds received on or before delivery to buyer, chattel paper/instruments under 9-330. **No 20-day grace for inventory.**
  - Livestock (farm products): similar notice rule, 6 months.
- **Lien creditors (9-317):** unperfected interest is subordinate to a person who becomes a lien creditor (incl. **bankruptcy trustee**, 11 U.S.C. §544(a)) before the interest is perfected or a financing statement filed and security agreement authenticated. **PMSI relation-back (9-317(e)):** PMSI filed within **20 days** after debtor receives delivery beats intervening lien creditors and buyers.
- **Buyers:**
  - **Buyer in ordinary course (9-320(a))** takes free of a security interest **created by its seller**, even if perfected and even if the buyer knows of it (unless buyer knows the sale violates the security agreement). BIOC = good faith, from a person in the business of selling goods of that kind, not in bulk/as security for debt. Farm products buyers excepted (Food Security Act governs).
  - **Consumer-to-consumer ("garage sale") buyer (9-320(b)):** takes free of a PMSI perfected automatically if buyer buys without knowledge, for value, for personal/household use, **before a financing statement is filed**.
  - **Buyers of non-ordinary-course goods (9-317(b)):** take free of **unperfected** interest if they give value and receive delivery without knowledge.
  - **Shelter:** a subsequent buyer from a protected buyer takes that buyer's rights.
  - Buyer takes subject to security interest created by a remote prior owner (the "created by its seller" limit): e.g., consumer who buys a used car from dealer takes subject to a lien created by the car's prior owner if perfected.
- **Future advances (9-323):** generally take priority of the original filing; as against a lien creditor, the security interest is subordinate only to the extent it secures an advance made **more than 45 days** after the person becomes a lien creditor, unless that advance was made without knowledge of the lien or under a commitment entered into without knowledge (9-323(b)); buyers not in ordinary course similar (45 days or knowledge of the purchase, 9-323(d)–(e)).
- **Chattel paper / instruments (9-330):** purchaser who gives new value and takes possession in ordinary course without knowledge of a violation beats a filed interest.
- **Deposit accounts (9-327):** control beats non-control; bank with control beats others except a secured party that becomes the bank's customer.
- **Accessions (9-335)**, **commingled goods (9-336)** (perfected interests share pro rata by value), **Agricultural/possessory liens (9-333):** artisan's/mechanic's possessory lien for services/materials beats perfected security interest unless the lien statute says otherwise.
- **Federal tax liens:** secured party generally wins if perfected before tax lien filed; 45-day window for future advances (IRC §6323(d)).
- **Traps:** 9-322 counts **filing**, not attachment — the first to file wins even if it lent last. PMSI in inventory requires notice to prior filers; PMSI in equipment does not.

## Proceeds (9-315)
- Security interest continues in collateral despite disposition unless secured party authorized disposition free of it; attaches to **identifiable proceeds** (lowest intermediate balance rule for commingled cash).
- Perfection in proceeds: automatically perfected for **20 days**; continues after 20 days if (1) filed financing statement covers original collateral, proceeds are collateral in which a security interest could be perfected by filing in the same office, and proceeds not acquired with cash proceeds; (2) identifiable cash proceeds; or (3) perfected independently within 20 days.
- Priority in proceeds: same time of filing/perfection as original collateral (9-322(b)), subject to 9-322(c) exceptions for deposit accounts/investment property/non-filing collateral.
- **Trap:** equipment bought with cash proceeds of inventory — continued perfection requires filing covering equipment (the "acquired with cash proceeds" exception).

## Fixtures (9-334)
- Security interest in goods that become fixtures may be perfected by ordinary filing or **fixture filing** (in real property records).
- **General rule:** subordinate to conflicting real property interests (mortgagee, owner) **except**:
  1. **PMSI** fixture filing made **before goods become fixtures or within 20 days after** → beats prior-recorded real estate interests (not construction mortgages).
  2. Fixture filing perfected before the real estate interest is of record (first-to-record).
  3. **Readily removable factory/office machines, equipment not primarily used in operating the real property, and replacement domestic appliances that are consumer goods** — perfected by any method before becoming fixtures beats real estate interests.
  4. Lien creditors on the real estate who obtained liens after perfection by any method.
- **Construction mortgage (9-334(h)):** recorded before goods become fixtures and goods become fixtures before completion → mortgage wins even against PMSI fixture filing.
- **Remedy on default:** removal, but secured party must reimburse encumbrancer/owner for physical injury (not diminution in value) and may be required to post security.

## Default and enforcement (Part 6)
- **Default** is defined by the agreement. Secured party may reduce claim to judgment, foreclose, or use Art. 9 remedies cumulatively.
- **Repossession (9-609):** self-help **without breach of the peace** (debtor's contemporaneous objection generally bars proceeding; entry into closed dwelling/garage, deception with police, confrontation = breach). Secured party liable for repo agent's breach (non-delegable). Judicial process (replevin) otherwise. May render equipment unusable on premises.
- **Disposition (9-610):** every aspect — method, manner, time, place, terms — **commercially reasonable**; public or private; secured party may buy at public sale, and at private sale only if the collateral is of a kind customarily sold on a recognized market or subject to widely distributed standard price quotations.
- **Notice (9-611, 9-612, 9-613, 9-614):** reasonable authenticated notification to debtor, secondary obligors, and (non-consumer goods) other secured parties/lienholders of record. **10 days** before disposition is a safe harbor in **non-consumer** transactions (9-612(b)). Not required for perishable collateral, collateral threatening to decline speedily, or collateral of a recognized market. Debtor may waive only **after default** (9-624).
- **Application of proceeds (9-615):** (1) reasonable expenses of retaking, holding, preparing, disposing, and (if agreed) attorney's fees; (2) satisfaction of the secured obligation; (3) subordinate security interests/liens that made authenticated demand before distribution; (4) **surplus to debtor**; debtor liable for **deficiency** (except sale of accounts/chattel paper).
- **Deficiency when secured party noncompliant (9-626):** non-consumer transactions — **rebuttable presumption rule**: presumed collateral worth the debt unless secured party proves compliant sale would have yielded less. Consumer transactions — left to courts (some apply absolute bar). Low-price dispositions to secured party/related persons: deficiency computed on what a proper disposition would have brought (9-615(f)).
- **Transferee at disposition (9-617):** takes all debtor's rights, free of the security interest and subordinate interests; good-faith transferee takes free even if secured party failed to comply.
- **Strict foreclosure (9-620):** acceptance of collateral in full or partial satisfaction requires debtor consent **after default** (express in authenticated record; for full satisfaction, may be implied by no objection within **20 days** after proposal is sent) and no objection from other notified secured parties within 20 days. **Partial strict foreclosure not allowed in consumer transactions.** **Mandatory disposition (9-620(e)):** if debtor has paid **60%** of cash price (PMSI in consumer goods) or 60% of loan (non-PMSI consumer goods), secured party in possession must dispose within **90 days** after taking possession unless debtor waives after default.
- **Redemption (9-623):** debtor or other secured party may redeem any time before disposition/contract to dispose or strict foreclosure by paying the full obligation (accelerated amount) plus expenses.
- **Remedies for noncompliance (9-625):** actual damages; **consumer goods** — statutory minimum damages of the credit service charge plus 10% of principal (or time-price differential plus 10% of cash price) regardless of loss.
- **Traps:** debtor cannot waive disposition-notice or commercial-reasonableness rights pre-default. Secured party who keeps collateral without following 9-620 has not strictly foreclosed.

## Bankruptcy overlay (MEE crossover)
- Trustee as hypothetical lien creditor (§544(a)) defeats unperfected interests. Preference (§547): transfer on account of antecedent debt within 90 days (1 year insiders) while insolvent that improves creditor's position; perfection more than **30 days** after attachment is treated as a transfer at perfection (§547(e)); PMSI enabling-loan safe harbor if perfected within **30 days** after debtor receives possession (§547(c)(3)). Automatic stay (§362) halts repossession.
- **NY:** NY Art. 9 is substantially uniform; NY Debtor and Creditor Law Art. 10 (Uniform Voidable Transactions Act, adopted 2019 effective 2020, confirm before use) governs fraudulent transfers.
