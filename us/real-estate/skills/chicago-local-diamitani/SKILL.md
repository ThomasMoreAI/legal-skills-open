# Chicago Local Resources

## Overview

A consolidated guide to Chicago-specific resources across public safety, legal assistance, tenant rights, and community services. Merges Crime Tracker Chicago (crime data and safety awareness), ChicagoESQ (justice system navigation), Tenny: Chicago (tenant rights and housing law), and Chicago 211 Resource Assistant (social services and community resources).

## When to Use

- Looking up crime statistics and safety information for specific Chicago neighborhoods
- Understanding Chicago's justice system: courts, legal aid, police oversight, and your rights
- Navigating tenant-landlord issues under Chicago's Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance (RLTO)
- Finding social services: housing assistance, food programs, utility assistance, mental health, and more
- Understanding your legal rights during interactions with Chicago police
- Searching for free or low-cost legal assistance in Chicago
- Filing complaints about housing conditions, discrimination, or landlord violations
- Connecting with community organizations for support and advocacy

## Steps

### 1. Crime & Public Safety (Crime Tracker Chicago)

- Search crime data by neighborhood, police district, or community area
- Understand crime categories and reporting trends over time
- Access resources:
  - CPD CLEARpath: online reporting for non-emergency incidents
  - Community policing offices and beat meeting schedules
  - CAPS (Chicago Alternative Policing Strategy) engagement
  - Victim advocacy services and crime victim compensation
- Safety planning: neighborhood-specific awareness, community alerts, local safety apps

### 2. Justice System Navigation (ChicagoESQ)

- **Court system overview:**
  - Circuit Court of Cook County structure and locations
  - Types of cases: criminal, civil, housing, traffic, family, small claims
- **Legal rights:**
  - Miranda rights and police interactions in Illinois
  - Search and seizure protections under Illinois law
  - Rights during arrest, booking, and detention
- **Legal assistance:**
  - Cook County Public Defender's Office
  - Legal Aid Chicago (formerly LAF) — free civil legal services
  - CARPLS legal hotline (312-738-9200) — free legal advice
  - Illinois Legal Aid Online — self-help resources and forms
- **Police oversight:**
  - COPA (Civilian Office of Police Accountability) — file complaints
  - Chicago Police Board — disciplinary cases
  - Consent decree monitoring and compliance updates

### 3. Tenant Rights (Tenny: Chicago)

- **Chicago RLTO (Residential Landlord and Tenant Ordinance):**
  - Applies to most rental units except owner-occupied buildings with 6 or fewer units
  - Key protections: security deposit rules, habitability standards, anti-lockout, anti-retaliation
- **Security deposits:**
  - Landlord must hold deposit in a separate, interest-bearing Chicago account
  - Provide receipt and disclosure of bank within 14 days
  - Return within 45 days of move-out with itemized deductions
  - Penalty for violations: 2x deposit + attorney's fees
- **Repairs and habitability:**
  - Landlord must maintain the unit in compliance with Chicago Building Code
  - Tenants have the right to withhold rent (with proper procedure) if conditions aren't fixed
  - Process: written notice → 14 days for non-emergency repairs → remedies if not fixed
- **Eviction protection:**
  - Landlord cannot lock out, shut off utilities, or remove belongings without court order
  - Just-cause eviction protections in covered units
  - Resources: Lawyers' Committee for Better Housing, Rentervention (online tenant help tool)
- **Lease and rent increases:**
  - Understanding lease terms, renewals, and termination notice requirements
  - Chicago does not have rent control, but RLTO requires proper notice for changes

### 4. Community Resources (Chicago 211)

- **Housing assistance:** Emergency shelter, rental assistance, foreclosure prevention, housing counseling
- **Food programs:** Food pantries, SNAP enrollment assistance, meal programs for seniors and children
- **Utility assistance:** LIHEAP (energy), water assistance, internet subsidy programs
- **Health services:** Free/low-cost clinics, mental health services, substance use treatment, dental care
- **Employment services:** Job training, unemployment benefits, career counseling, GED/ESL programs
- **Family support:** Childcare assistance, parenting resources, youth programs, domestic violence services
- **Transportation:** Reduced-fare transit passes, paratransit services, mobility assistance
- **Contact:** Dial 211 (24/7), visit 211metrochicago.org, or text your ZIP code to 898211

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Not knowing RLTO coverage** — Many tenants don't realize the RLTO applies to them. Check whether your building is covered — it affects your rights significantly.
2. **Waiting too long to file complaints** — Tenant rights claims and police complaints have strict deadlines. Act promptly.
3. **Using 911 for non-emergencies** — Use 311 for city services and non-emergency issues; 911 only for active emergencies.
4. **Assuming all legal aid is free** — Free services have income eligibility limits. Know them before you go.
5. **Not documenting everything** — Photos, written correspondence, and a paper trail are essential for tenant disputes, police complaints, and legal cases.
