# Coverage contract: employer-side Israeli hiring automation

Scope of this skill: drafting job advertisements, publishing them on Israeli boards, screening
candidates, scheduling interviews, drafting offer letters, and handling candidate personal data.

It does NOT draft the statutory written notice of employment terms (הודעה לעובד על תנאי עבודה),
which is a separate instrument; the skill states that the duty exists and routes the user to the
statute or to counsel. Everything downstream of the first day of work (dismissal, hearings, wage
slips, disciplinary process) is also out of scope.

Rule for rate-driven items: where the skill states or computes an amount that varies by a
category (age band, seniority band, day type, employee type, sector), every row of the official
table is its own Must-cover item below. A single summary row for a multi-row official table is
treated as a coverage gap, not as coverage.

## Must cover (core)

### A. The job advertisement

1. Every enumerated ground in section 2(a) of the Equal Employment Opportunities Law,
   5748-1988, present in the operational checklist and in the validator: מין, נטייה מינית,
   מעמד אישי, הריון, טיפולי פוריות והפריה חוץ גופית, הורות, גיל, גזע, דת, לאום, ארץ מוצא,
   **מקום מגורים**, השקפה, מפלגה, שירות מילואים.
   Rationale: an omitted ground is an unflagged unlawful advertisement. מקום מגורים is the
   ground Israeli ads breach most often ("תושבי המרכז בלבד").
   Source: Equal Employment Opportunities Law 5748-1988, s.2(a).
2. Section 8 duty specific to advertisements: a job advertisement must be worded in both the
   masculine and the feminine form, and this is a standalone offence separate from s.2.
   Rationale: an ad can be non-discriminatory in substance and still breach s.8 on wording.
   Source: same law, s.8 (and s.2 for the substantive prohibition).
3. Grounds that are NOT in s.2(a) and must not be attributed to it: מראה חיצוני (nowhere in
   the statute) and מוגבלות (Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law 5758-1998).
   Rationale: a wrong statutory citation is a wrong legal conclusion.
   Source: Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law 5758-1998, ss.8-9.
4. The genuine-occupational-qualification carve-out and its limits.
   Source: Equal Employment Opportunities Law, s.2(c).

### B. Screening, interviewing and evidence

5. Section 9 burden-of-proof reversal, stated operationally: in a hiring or promotion claim the
   employer carries the burden once the candidate shows they met the published requirements,
   and the burden also shifts where the employer asked the candidate for information about a
   protected ground.
   Rationale: this converts "keep a record of the screening criteria and the decision" from
   good practice into the employer's only defence.
   Source: Equal Employment Opportunities Law, s.9.
6. A defensible screening record: the published requirements, the scoring rubric, per-candidate
   scores, the reason for rejection, and who decided. Retained long enough to defend a claim.
   Source: s.9 (above), read with the limitation period for a labour claim.
7. The collision between item 6 and privacy purpose limitation, resolved explicitly: evidence
   retention is a distinct lawful purpose from "keeping the good ones on file", and must be
   disclosed at collection as such.
   Source: Privacy Protection Law 5741-1981, s.2 and s.8(b) (use limited to the purpose).
8. Prohibition on demanding a criminal-record certificate or register information from a
   candidate; only bodies authorised by the statute may receive it, and asking the candidate to
   produce their own extract is not a workaround.
   Source: Criminal Records and Rehabilitation of Offenders Law 5779-2019 (replacing the
   5741-1981 law), the sections restricting who may receive register information and
   prohibiting requesting it from the data subject.
9. Prohibition on requiring a genetic test or genetic information as a condition of employment.
   Source: Genetic Information Law 5761-2000, s.29.
10. Questions that may not be asked at interview, tied to the ground they touch: pregnancy and
    family plans, marital status and children, religious observance, political affiliation,
    military unit, health conditions unrelated to the role. Plus the questions that MAY be
    asked: duration of service where relevant to experience, ability to perform the role's
    essential functions, and legal eligibility to work in Israel.
    Source: s.2(a) read with s.9 (the asking itself shifts the burden).
11. Work eligibility of a non-Israeli candidate: employing a foreign national requires an
    employer permit and a work visa, employing without one is an offence, and the check is
    lawful and mandatory notwithstanding the ban on ארץ מוצא questions.
    Source: Foreign Workers Law 5751-1991, ss.1-2 (permit and offence) and the written-contract,
    medical-insurance and accommodation duties in ss.1B-1E.
12. Candidates under 18: minimum working age, the pre-employment medical fitness certificate,
    prohibited hours and night work, and the shortened deadline for the written notice of terms.
    Source: Youth Labour Law 5713-1953, ss.2, 11, 20-24; Employment of Youth (medical
    examinations) regulations.

### C. Offer letter and the statutory notice of terms

13. The EXISTENCE of the written notice of employment terms (הודעה לעובד על תנאי עבודה) as a
    duty separate from an offer letter, and the fact that an offer letter does not discharge it.
    **Do not state its deadline here.** The deadline was not verified against a primary source
    in the cycle that wrote this checklist, and `references/adjacent-duties.md` forbids stating
    a deadline for any of these rows from memory. Read the statute before writing a number.
    Source: Notice to Employee and to Candidate Law (Employment Terms and Screening and Hiring
    Procedures) 5762-2002.
14. A prompt to the employer to confirm, against its OWN separate notice document, that the
    notice carries every particular the statute prescribes. **This skill does not draft that
    notice and must not embed its particulars as lines of the offer-letter template**, which
    would produce an instrument that looks like the statutory notice without being one. Direct
    the employer to the statute or to counsel for the particulars.
    Source: same law.
15. Screening-process duties owed to candidates by an employer covered by the Act: a written
    update on the progress of the screening process at fixed intervals and a written notice of
    non-selection after the decision.
    Source: same law, the chapter on הליכי מיון וקבלה לעבודה (added by amendment).
16. Financial sanction exposure for failing to give the notice, which is administrative and does
    not require the employee to sue.
    Source: Increased Enforcement of Labour Laws Law 5772-2011 and its schedules.
17. Delivery of the workplace sexual-harassment תקנון and identification of the ממונה at hire.
    Source: Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law 5758-1998, s.7; Prevention of Sexual Harassment
    (Employer's Duties) Regulations 5758-1998, regs. 6-8 (תקנון duty above the employee-count
    threshold, appointment of a ממונה, distribution to employees).

### D. Rate and bracket tables, enumerated row by row

18. Minimum wage, one item per band, not a single adult figure:
    a. adult full-time monthly rate and the derived hourly rate, with the date the current rate
       took effect;
    b. reduced youth rates set as a percentage of the adult rate, in bands by age. The
       percentages were NOT verified against a primary source in the cycle that wrote this
       checklist, so they are deliberately not stated here. Read them at the statute or Kol
       Zchut before quoting one.
    e. apprentice (חניך) rate;
    f. adjusted minimum wage for an employee with a disability, set by permit rather than by the
       standard rate.
    Source: Minimum Wage Law 5747-1987, s.16 and the Minimum Wage (Adjusted Wage for an Employee
    with a Disability) regulations; Youth Labour Law.
19. Pension, one item per component: employee 6%; employer tagmulim 6.5%; employer severance
    component 6%; total 18.5%; optional severance top-up of 2.33% to reach 8.33%; the
    qualification periods (day one for an employee with an existing pension, six months for one
    without); the age thresholds; the salary base.
    Source: Extension Order for Comprehensive Pension Insurance in the Economy, as amended
    effective 1 January 2017.
20. Severance and Section 14, as separate items: the 8.33% statutory accrual; the general
    approval under s.14 and that it releases only to the extent actually deposited, from day one,
    on full salary; the completion the employer owes where only 6% was deposited.
    Source: Severance Pay Law 5723-1963, s.12 and s.14, and the General Approval under s.14.
21. Annual leave, one item per seniority band, in calendar days: years 1 to 5 = 16; year 6 = 18;
    year 7 = 21; year 8 = 22; year 9 = 23; year 10 = 24; year 11 = 25; year 12 = 26; year 13 = 27;
    year 14 and above = 28. Plus the conversion to working days on a five-day and a six-day week,
    and the rule for an employee who worked less than a full year.
    Source: Annual Leave Law 5711-1951, s.3, as amended by Amendment 15.
22. Sick pay, one item per day band: day 1 unpaid; days 2 and 3 at 50%; day 4 onward at 100%;
    accrual 1.5 days per month; ceiling 90 days. Plus the separate quotas for a sick child, a
    sick spouse, a sick parent, and bereavement leave charged against sick days.
    Source: Sick Pay Law 5736-1976, ss.2-4, and the companion sick-leave statutes.
23. Convalescence pay, one item per seniority band and per sector: private-sector daily rate and
    public-sector daily rate for the current convalescence year; days by seniority (year 1 = 5;
    years 2 to 3 = 6; years 4 to 10 = 7; years 11 to 15 = 8; years 16 to 19 = 9; year 20 and
    above = 10); the eligibility condition of one completed year; pro-rating by scope of position;
    the fact that the extension order is often published after the year it governs, so a
    retroactive completion may be owed.
    Source: General Extension Order on convalescence pay in the private sector, current order.
24. Overtime and rest-day pay, one item per band: weekday first two hours 125%; weekday third
    hour onward 150%; ordinary hours on the weekly rest day or a festival 150%; first two
    overtime hours on a rest day 175%; third hour onward on a rest day 200%; the daily threshold
    at which overtime starts; the weekly overtime ceiling.
    Source: Hours of Work and Rest Law 5711-1951, ss.16-17.
25. The permit requirement for employing on the weekly rest day, as a separate item from the
    rate: rest-day work is prohibited without a permit from the Minister of Labour, and paying
    150% does not cure its absence. Compensatory rest must also be given.
    Source: Hours of Work and Rest Law, ss.7, 9, 9A and 12.
26. Notice period, one item per band and per employee type.
    Monthly-salaried: months 1 to 6 = one day per month; months 7 to 12 = six days plus 2.5 days
    for each month beyond the sixth; after one year = one month.
    Hourly or daily paid: year 1 = one day per month; year 2 = 14 days plus one day per two
    months; year 3 = 21 days plus one day per two months; after three years = one month.
    Plus the mutual application of the period and payment in lieu.
    Source: Advance Notice of Dismissal and Resignation Law 5761-2001, ss.3-4.

### E. Candidate personal data

27. The CV collection as a database under the Privacy Protection Law, with the notice-at-
    collection duty stated in the terms the statute uses: whether there is a legal duty to supply
    the data, the purpose of collection, and to whom the data will be delivered.
    Source: Privacy Protection Law 5741-1981, s.11.
28. Purpose limitation on retention and the separate consent needed to keep a CV for future
    openings.
    Source: same law, s.2(9) and s.8(b).
29. The data-security duties that apply to every database regardless of registration, including
    the security-level classification and the incident-reporting duty.
    Source: Privacy Protection (Data Security) Regulations 5777-2017.
30. Transfer of candidate data outside Israel, which is what happens the moment a foreign ATS or
    job board holds the CVs.
    Source: Privacy Protection (Transfer of Data to Databases Abroad) Regulations 5761-2001.
31. The current enforcement regime, including the privacy protection officer duty and the
    administrative-fine powers, stated with the amendment identified rather than refused.
    Source: Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13, in force 14 August 2025; Privacy Protection
    Authority guidance.

## Should cover (advanced)

32. Equal pay obligations and the annual gender pay-gap report an employer above the statutory
    employee threshold must publish.
    Rationale: it shapes what salary band an offer letter may lawfully contain.
    Source: Equal Pay for Female and Male Employees Law 5756-1996, s.6A (Amendment 6, 2020),
    threshold of 518 employees.
33. Appropriate representation of persons with disabilities for larger employers, and the duty to
    appoint a person responsible for employment of persons with disabilities.
    Source: Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law 5758-1998, s.9 and the accompanying
    directives.
34. Reasonable accommodation at the screening and interview stage, not only after hire.
    Source: same law, s.8(e).
35. Pre-employment medical examinations and psychometric or personality testing: no general
    statutory authorisation, so each is governed by consent, proportionality and the ban on
    health questions unrelated to the role.
    Source: Privacy Protection Law, s.2; Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law, s.8.
36. Polygraph testing of candidates and its treatment by the labour courts.
    Source: labour court case law; no statutory authorisation.
37. Protection of a candidate or employee who is pregnant or undergoing fertility treatment,
    including the dismissal-permit regime that attaches shortly after hire.
    Source: Employment of Women Law 5714-1954, s.9.
38. Trust and management positions excluded from the Hours of Work and Rest Law, and why a global
    overtime clause for such a role is a different question from one for a covered employee.
    Source: Hours of Work and Rest Law, s.30(a)(5)-(6).
39. Global overtime clauses: the conditions under which they are enforceable and the risk that a
    shortfall is re-characterised across the whole period.
    Source: Hours of Work and Rest Law, s.16; labour court case law on שעות נוספות גלובליות.
40. Whether a collective agreement or extension order applies to the sector, which changes the
    minimum terms the offer letter must meet.
    Source: Collective Agreements Law 5717-1957, ss.25-33.
41. Travel expense reimbursement and its current daily ceiling under the extension order.
    Source: General Extension Order on travel expenses.
42. Employment of a candidate through a manpower contractor and the nine-month transfer rule.
    Source: Employment of Employees by Manpower Contractors Law 5756-1996, s.12A.
43. Recruitment of a candidate who is a reservist, and the protections that attach.
    Source: Discharged Soldiers (Return to Work) Law 5709-1949; Equal Employment Opportunities
    Law, s.2(a) (שירות מילואים).
44. Job-board reality: which Israeli boards expose a documented programmatic interface and which
    only have a human employer portal, refreshed rather than assumed.
    Rationale: an invented endpoint is a silent failure in an automation skill.

## Out of scope (explicit)

45. Dismissal, the שימוע hearing and severance settlement on termination.
    Rationale: this skill ends at the offer letter; termination is a separate advisory posture.
46. Payroll computation, wage slips, income tax and National Insurance withholding.
    Rationale: a licensed accountant or payroll bureau territory, and a different skill.
47. Reviewing or opining on an executed employment contract or a non-compete clause.
    Rationale: that is legal advice on a specific instrument and reserved to a licensed lawyer.
48. Candidate-side activity: job searching, CV writing, interview preparation, salary negotiation.
    Rationale: this skill is employer-side; a candidate-side skill has opposite incentives.
49. Immigration sponsorship strategy and visa applications beyond the employer permit check.
    Rationale: reserved practice and fact-specific.
50. Determining whether a given engagement is employment or independent contracting.
    Rationale: a re-characterisation question decided by the labour courts on the facts.

**Out of scope (explicit), with rationale:**

- Full drafting of the notice of employment terms. Out of scope: this skill drafts the job ad
  and the offer letter. The notice is a different document with prescribed statutory content,
  and getting its particulars wrong is worse than routing the user to counsel. The DUTY is in
  scope and is now stated; the DRAFTING is not.
- Payroll computation, tax withholding and Bituach Leumi. Out of scope: the skill states the
  employer-cost components an offer letter must name, not the monthly payslip. Payroll skills
  in the catalogue cover this.
- Termination, dismissal hearings (שימוע) and severance disputes. Out of scope: this skill ends
  at the offer letter. Only the accrual rates that belong in an offer letter are covered.
- Collective-agreement interpretation for a specific sector. Out of scope: the skill now flags
  that an agreement may raise every floor, which is the actionable part; identifying and
  reading the applicable agreement is a counsel task.
