# Money, tax, and employment after a death

Detail behind the short pointers in SKILL.md. Every statement here is anchored in
`evidence.json`. Where a point is not settled by a primary source, it says so. Do not fill a
gap with a plausible figure, form number, or deadline.

## 1. Severance when the deceased was an employee

Section 5(a) of the Severance Pay Law, 5723-1963: the employer pays severance to the
deceased's שאירים as if it had dismissed the employee. The defined שאירים are the spouse at
the time of death (including a common-law partner living with them) and a child who is a
dependent under the National Insurance Law; failing those, children and parents who were
mainly supported by the deceased, and siblings who lived in the home for at least twelve
months and were wholly supported. Section 5(b): where the recipients are not the spouse or a
dependent child, the money is deposited with the regional labour court, which divides it.
Section 5(c): severance paid under the section is not part of the estate.

**Temporary provision, section 5(b1) (Amendment 34, "Swords of Iron").** For an employee who
died during the "determining period", the שאירים are only the spouse and a dependent child,
and if there are none, the heirs by law or the beneficiaries under the will take their place.
The determining period runs from 7 October 2023 until one year after the end of the
home-front special-situation declaration made on that date. That declaration was not extended
past 28 October 2025: Yalkut HaPirsumim 13830 (16 September 2025), p. 9858, extended it to
28 October 2025, and on 27 October 2025 the Defence Minister adopted the IDF recommendation not
to extend it again (JDN, 27.10.2025; the Israel Democracy Institute reports the same), so the period ends
on 28 October 2026 unless the Labour Minister extends it by order under section 5(b1)(4). A
new declaration made on 28 February 2026 is, on the statutory definition, a different
declaration, so it does not by itself move that date; it could only ground an extension order.
Confirm with the employer or a lawyer for any death near the boundary.

Practical effect: an adult, self-supporting child of a widowed parent who died inside the
period may be entitled to the severance as an heir. Outside the period, such a child is not a
שאיר under section 5 and generally has no claim to this money, but where the date is near the
boundary, confirm before concluding.

**How the money is actually paid (Kol-Zchut, severance to survivors).**
- Entitlement requires that the deceased worked at least one year with the same employer or at
  the same workplace.
- If all the severance was deposited in the deceased's pension insurance, the survivors
  withdraw it from the fund. If only part was deposited, the employer pays the rest. Whether a
  deposit counts in place of severance is governed by section 14 of the Law: a payment to a
  pension fund replaces severance only as far as a collective agreement or the Labour
  Minister's approval provides.
- The fund releases the money only after the employer transfers all deposits, including for
  the last month, and reports the cessation and its cause (death). Above the tax-exempt ceiling,
  release waits for Tax Authority approval.
- The employer fills in its part of Form 161 and hands it to the survivors.
- The deceased's debts cannot be set off against this severance.
- Last salary and vacation redemption are different: section 5(c) carves out only severance
  paid under section 5, so those other sums are owed to the estate.

## 2. When the deceased was an EMPLOYER (caregiver, housekeeper)

Kol-Zchut's heirs guide: where a nursing caregiver or housekeeper was employed shortly before
the death, the death constitutes dismissal of the worker, who is owed everything that flows
from the end of employment: the last salary, notice pay, unpaid recuperation pay, annual
leave redemption, and severance. These are usually paid out of the estate, except severance
money already deposited in the pension fund. A foreign caregiver employed for at least one
continuous year is entitled to severance, from the fund where it was deposited or from the
estate for any part not deposited.

These are estate debts. Settle them before division (see the section 128 regime in SKILL.md).
For the payroll arithmetic of a foreign caregiver, route to `foreign-caregiver-payroll`.

## 3. Money that moves without the order

Kol-Zchut's heirs guide:
- Savings products such as provident funds and life insurance can carry named beneficiaries.
  Those sums go only to the named beneficiaries, even if the will says otherwise, and the
  beneficiaries claim them regardless of the order process.
- Under conditions, money in a bank account or an insurance, provident or pension plan can be
  withdrawn without an order, mainly where the balance is very small. The conditions are in
  Finance Ministry circulars 2014-2-3 and 2017-9-8. This skill does not state the thresholds;
  check the circulars or ask the institution.

Claim these in parallel with the order, not after it. Eligibility for survivor pensions and
life-insurance claims belongs to `israeli-survivor-benefits-navigator`.

## 4. The deceased's income tax, VAT, and National Insurance files

What the sources establish:
- Income Tax Ordinance, section 120(a): where a person dies, the legal personal
  representative is liable for the tax the deceased would have owed and is responsible for
  every act the deceased would have had to do under the Ordinance. Section 120(f) defines the
  legal personal representative to include an heir, the estate administrator, and the
  executor of the will.
- Section 120(b): from the date of death, the estate's taxable income is treated as the
  heirs' income according to their shares.
- Form 2805 (gov.il): heirs or administrators declare themselves to the Tax Authority, for the
  purpose of closing the deceased's file and continuing to handle estate income. It is
  returned to the assessing office named in the notice letter.
- VAT: the only official page is the general request to close a business at VAT (Form 18).
  It does not describe a death-specific route.

Section 120 makes the representative responsible for the deceased's tax filings and for
paying that tax; it does not by itself say the heirs pay it from their own money. Where
personal exposure is a question, route it to the adviser.

What this skill does NOT state: any filing deadline for the year-of-death return, how estate
income is computed, or who signs for a deceased dealer at VAT. Send the user to the assessing
office and to a רואה חשבון or יועץ מס.

## 5. Real estate: tax on a later sale, and dividing among heirs

- Land Taxation Law, section 4: inheritance is not a sale, so no betterment or purchase tax
  is due at the moment of inheritance.
- Later sale by an heir: the heir steps into the deceased's shoes. Section 5(c)(2) sends the
  purchase date and purchase value to sections 26 and 37; for a death on or after
  1 April 1981 the purchase value is the value that would have applied had the deceased sold
  (section 26(a)(2)). There is no step-up to market value at death.
- Section 49B(5) exempts the sale of an inherited qualifying residential apartment when all of
  these hold: the seller is the deceased's spouse, a descendant, or a descendant's spouse; the
  deceased owned only one residential apartment before death; and the deceased would have
  been exempt had they sold it while alive.
- Section 5(c)(4): the first division of estate property among the heirs is not treated as a
  sale, whether before or after the order is registered. If an heir pays money, or money's
  worth that is not an estate asset, the part paid for is treated as sold. This is the
  sibling-buyout trap: "I keep the flat and pay my brother from my own savings" is treated as a
  sale of the brother's share, reportable and potentially taxed, unless an exemption such as
  section 49B(5) covers it.
- Section 5(c)(4) also treats the estates of two deceased who were spouses, or a parent and
  child, as one estate for this rule while neither division has finished (the common case of a
  father who died years ago and a mother now).
- The heir's own home: when the heir sells their own apartment under the single-apartment
  exemption (section 49B(2)), section 49C disregards an inherited apartment that meets
  section 49B(5)(a) and (b), and an inherited share of no more than one half (section 49C(3)
  and (4)). Section 49C(4) incorporates only conditions (a) and (b) of section 49B(5): the heir
  is the deceased's spouse, descendant, or descendant's spouse, and the deceased owned only one
  residential apartment. Outside those two routes the inherited apartment counts: for example
  an heir who received more than half of an apartment from a deceased who owned two or more, or
  a sibling or other relative who inherited more than half, may lose the exemption on their own
  home. Confirm with a יועץ מס before selling either apartment.
- Section 5(c)(3): the estate administrator, or each heir where none was appointed, carries the
  reporting duties of sections 73, 76, 90A and 91 for these transactions.

Any sale, buy-out or division with outside money: confirm with a יועץ מס before signing.

## 6. Execution (Hotzaa LaPoal) files against the deceased

Execution Regulations, 5740-1979, regulation 10A:
- A new file against a deceased debtor must name the administrator or the heirs. Until an
  administrator appointment, a succession order, or a probate order is produced, only
  registration-only measures may be taken.
- A file already open when the debtor died is limited the same way, unless the registrar
  orders otherwise.
- Two years after the file was opened or the death became known, with no such order produced,
  the creditor is sent a notice to show cause why the file should not be closed. With no cause
  shown within 30 days, the file is closed (or the deceased is struck off a multi-debtor file).
  The creditor can reopen once an order exists or an attachable asset is found.

The debt still ranks against the estate under section 104 of the Succession Law. It does not
reach the heirs' own money beyond the section 126 to 129 regimes.

## 7. Death abroad

- National Insurance Law, section 266 covers burial in Israel of anyone who died in Israel and
  of an Israeli resident who died abroad. Section 267: where an insured person, their spouse,
  or their child died and was buried abroad, the grant is paid to the person designated,
  capped at what burial in Israel would have cost.
- Kol-Zchut's heirs guide: if the deceased held an Israeli ID and died abroad, the death must
  be recorded in the population registry before the application for an order is filed.
  Repatriation logistics are not covered here.

## 8. Digital accounts and credit cards

No Israeli statute on digital accounts was located. What the providers publish:
- Google: a request form for a deceased user's account
  (https://support.google.com/accounts/troubleshooter/6357590).
- Apple: a Legacy Contact the account holder set up in life
  (https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631).

Practical order: keep the deceased's mobile line active until two-factor codes for the bank
app and email have been moved, then cancel. Credit cards are separate from the bank's
standing orders and are often issued by a separate company: notify each issuer, and move or
cancel the recurring charges sitting on the card.
