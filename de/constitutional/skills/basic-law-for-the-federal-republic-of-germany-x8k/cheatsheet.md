# Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany - Cheat Sheet

## Quick Reference Guide

---

## Constitution at a Glance

**Full Name**: Grundgesetz für die Bundesrepublik Deutschland (Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany)

**Adopted**: May 23, 1949 by the Parliamentary Council

**Entered into Force**: May 24, 1949

**Length**: 146 Articles (plus numerous amendments)

**Structure**:
- Preamble
- I. Fundamental Rights (Articles 1-19)
- II. Federation and Länder (Articles 20-37)
- III. Bundestag (Articles 38-49)
- IV. Bundesrat (Articles 50-53)
- IVa. Joint Committee (Article 53a)
- V. Federal President (Articles 54-61)
- VI. Federal Government (Articles 62-69)
- VII. Federal Legislation (Articles 70-82)
- VIII. Execution of Federal Laws & Administration (Articles 83-91)
- VIIIa. Joint Tasks (Articles 91a-91b)
- IX. Judiciary (Articles 92-104)
- X. Finance (Articles 104a-115)
- Xa. Defense State (Articles 115a-115l)
- XI. Transitional & Final Provisions (Articles 116-146)

---

## Core Constitutional Principles (Article 20)

### 1. Demokratie (Democracy)
- All state authority emanates from the people (Article 20(2))
- Exercised through elections, voting, and special organs
- Representative democracy: Bundestag, Land parliaments
- Limited direct democracy at Land level

### 2. Bundesstaat (Federalism)
- Federal Republic composed of Länder (states)
- Division of powers between Federation and Länder
- Länder participate in federal legislation through Bundesrat
- Residual powers belong to Länder (Article 70)

### 3. Rechtsstaat (Rule of Law)
- State bound by law (Article 20(3))
- All state action requires legal basis (Vorbehalt des Gesetzes)
- Legal certainty and prohibition of arbitrariness
- Judicial protection of rights (Article 19(4))

### 4. Sozialstaat (Social State)
- State obligation to ensure social justice
- Social security, equalization, protection of the weak
- Not directly enforceable but guides interpretation

### 5. Republik (Republic)
- Head of state (Federal President) is elected
- No hereditary monarchy
- Temporary office with limited powers

## Fundamental Rights (Articles 1-19)

### Hierarchy of Rights

**Tier 1 - Absolute (Cannot be restricted)**:
- Human dignity (Article 1(1))

**Tier 2 - Highly Protected**:
- Right to life (Article 2(2) combined with Article 104)
- Freedom of conscience (Article 4(1))
- Prohibition of torture, death penalty (Article 102)

**Tier 3 - Qualified Rights (Restrictable with proportionality)**:
- General freedom of action (Article 2(1))
- Freedom of faith/religion (Article 4)
- Freedom of expression, press, information (Article 5)
- Assembly (Article 8)
- Association (Article 9)
- Privacy of correspondence, postal, telecom (Article 10)
- Freedom of movement (Article 11)
- Professional freedom (Article 12)
- Inviolability of home (Article 13)
- Property, inheritance, expropriation (Article 14)
- Citizenship, extradition (Article 16)
- Right of petition (Article 17)

**Tier 4 - Special Reservation Rights**:
- Equality (Article 3) - Specific discriminations to be eliminated by law

### Proportionality Test (Verhältnismäßigkeit)

All restrictions of fundamental rights must pass:

1. **Suitability (Geeignetheit)**: Measure must advance the legitimate aim
2. **Necessity (Erforderlichkeit)**: Measure must be the least restrictive means
3. **Proportionality in narrow sense (Angemessenheit)**: Benefits must outweigh the restriction

**Exception**: Human dignity (Article 1) cannot be restricted or balanced.

## Key State Organs

### 1. Bundestag (Federal Diet)

**Function**: Primary legislative body, directly elected

**Composition**:
- At least 598 members (current: 735)
- Half elected by majority vote in constituencies
- Half elected by proportional representation from Land lists
- 5% threshold for party representation

**Term**: 4 years (Article 39(1))

**Powers**:
- Primary legislation (Article 77)
- Budget approval (Article 110)
- Chancellor election (Article 63)
- Government oversight (Article 44-45)
- Constitutional amendments (Article 79)

### 2. Bundesrat (Federal Council)

**Function**: Represents Länder governments at federal level

**Composition**:
- Members of Land governments or their delegates
- Number of votes per Land based on population (3-6 votes)
- Each Land casts unified votes

**Powers**:
- Participation in federal legislation (Articles 76-78)
- **Consent laws (Zustimmungsgesetze)**: Must approve (tax, finance, federal-state relations)
- **Objection laws (Einspruchsgesetze)**: Can raise objections, overrideable by Bundestag
- Administrative supervision (Article 84)

**Consent Required For**:
- Laws affecting Länder finances or administration
- Tax laws
- Constitutional amendments
- Most finance-related laws

### 3. Federal President (Bundespräsident)

**Function**: Head of state, ceremonial and reserve powers

**Election**: By Federal Convention (Bundesversammlung) - Bundestag members + equal number of Land delegates

**Term**: 5 years, one re-election allowed

**Powers**:
- Represents Federation internationally (Article 59)
- Signs and promulgates laws (Article 82)
- Appoints and dismisses Chancellor (Articles 63-64)
- Appoints federal judges, ministers, civil servants (Article 60)
- May dissolve Bundestag under specific conditions (Article 63(4), 68)
- Exercises pardoning power (Article 60(2))
- Reserve powers in emergencies (Article 115a)

### 4. Federal Chancellor (Bundeskanzler/in)

**Function**: Head of Government, sets government policy

**Election**: By Bundestag upon proposal by Federal President
- Absolute majority required (Article 63(2))
- If no majority after 3 ballots, relative majority suffices (Article 63(3))
- If still no election, Federal President may dissolve Bundestag or appoint minority Chancellor

**Term**: 4 years (coincides with Bundestag term)

**Powers**:
- **Richtlinienkompetenz**: Determines general government policy guidelines (Article 65 sentence 1)
- Selects ministers (Article 64(1))
- Heads cabinet meetings (Article 65 sentence 3)
- Represents government domestically and internationally

**Accountability**:
- **Constructive vote of no confidence** (Article 67): Bundestag can remove Chancellor only by simultaneously electing successor with absolute majority
- **Vote of confidence** (Article 68): Chancellor can request Bundestag confidence vote; if lost, Federal President may dissolve Bundestag

### 5. Federal Constitutional Court (Bundesverfassungsgericht)

**Function**: Guardian of the Constitution, highest court for constitutional matters

**Composition**:
- 16 judges (8 elected by Bundestag, 8 by Bundesrat)
- Two Senates (8 judges each)
- 12-year terms, no re-election
- Cannot be members of Bundestag, Bundesrat, Government, or similar Land organs

**Jurisdiction** (Article 93):
1. Constitutional disputes between federal organs
2. Federal-State disputes
3. Constitutional complaints by individuals (Verfassungsbeschwerde)
4. Abstract and concrete norm control (judicial review of laws)
5. Election disputes
6. Party bans (Article 21(2))
7. Other matters assigned by law

**Key Decisions**:
- Can declare laws unconstitutional and void
- Can order state organs to act or refrain from acting
- Decisions bind all constitutional organs

## Federal-State Relations

### Legislative Competences

| Category | Federation | Länder |
|----------|------------|---------|
| **Exclusive** (Art. 71, 73) | Full authority | Only if authorized by federal law |
| **Concurrent** (Art. 72, 74) | Can legislate | Can legislate until Federation does |
| **Residual** (Art. 70) | No authority | Full authority |

### Exclusive Federal Competences (Article 73)

- Foreign affairs, defense, civil defense
- Federal citizenship
- Freedom of movement, passports, immigration, extradition
- Currency, monetary system, weights and measures
- Customs union, trade, foreign commerce
- Postal services, telecommunications
- Federal railways, air transport
- Intellectual property, copyright
- Combating international terrorism
- Statistics
- Weapons, explosives
- Nuclear energy (peaceful use)

### Important Concurrent Competences (Article 74)

- Civil law, criminal law, judicial procedure
- Civil status, associations
- Residence of foreigners, refugees
- Public welfare, social security
- Economic law (industry, energy, crafts, etc.)
- Labor law, workplace regulation
- Agriculture, forestry, food
- Land use, water management
- Education, scientific research

### Execution of Federal Laws

| Execution Type | By Whom | Federal Oversight |
|----------------|---------|-------------------|
| **Land matter** (Art. 83) | Länder as own matter | Limited to legality |
| **Federal commission** (Art. 85) | Länder | Full supervision (legality + merit) |
| **Direct federal** (Art. 86-89) | Federal authorities | Federal Government |

## Legislative Process

### Step-by-Step Procedure

1. **Initiation** (Article 76)
   - Federal Government, Bundestag members, or Bundesrat can introduce bills
   - Government bills must first go to Bundesrat

2. **Bundesrat Review** (Article 76(2))
   - 6-week period for opinion (can be extended to 9 weeks)
   - For "urgent" bills: 3 weeks (or 6 weeks with extension)

3. **Bundestag Consideration**
   - Three readings required
   - Amendments possible

4. **Bundesrat Participation** (Articles 77-78)
   - **Consent laws (Zustimmungsgesetze)**: Must approve (tax, finance, federal-state relations)
   - **Objection laws (Einspruchsgesetze)**: Can object within 2 weeks
     - Bundestag can override with majority (absolute majority if Bundesrat had 2/3 majority)

5. **Mediation Committee** (Article 77(2))
   - If Bundesrat requests, committee of Bundestag + Bundesrat members
   - Tries to find compromise
   - If no agreement, Bundesrat can still object

6. **Final Adoption**
   - Law is adopted if:
     - Bundesrat approves, OR
     - No mediation requested and no objection, OR
     - Objection overruled by Bundestag

7. **Promulgation** (Article 82)
   - Federal President signs and promulgates
   - Published in Bundesgesetzblatt (Federal Law Gazette)
   - Enters into force 14 days after publication unless otherwise specified

## Financial Constitution

### Revenue Distribution

**Federal Taxes**:
- Customs duties, fiscal monopolies
- Excise taxes (most)
- Transport taxes
- Capital transfer taxes
- Supplementary income/corporation taxes
- EU-related taxes

**Land Taxes**:
- Wealth taxes
- Inheritance taxes
- Motor vehicle taxes
- Beer taxes
- Gambling taxes

**Shared Taxes** (with distribution):
- Income tax: ~42.5% Federation, ~42.5% Länder, ~15% municipalities
- Corporation tax: 50% Federation, 50% Länder
- Value-added tax: ~53% Federation, ~47% Länder

### Key Financial Principles

1. **Expenditure Responsibility** (Article 104a): Each level bears its own expenses
2. **Financial Equalization** (Article 107): Stronger Länder support weaker ones
3. **Borrowing Limits** (Article 115): Restrictions on federal and Land borrowing
4. **Budget Autonomy** (Article 109): Federation and Länder are autonomous in budget matters

## Emergency Provisions (Section Xa)

### Defense State (Verteidigungsfall)

**Trigger** (Article 115a):
- Armed attack on federal territory OR
- Imminent armed attack
- Determined by Bundestag + Bundesrat (2/3 majority each)

**If Bundestag cannot act**: Joint Committee can determine (2/3 majority)

**Key Changes During Defense State**:
- Chancellor gains command of armed forces (Article 115b)
- Expanded federal legislative powers (Article 115c)
- Simplified legislative procedure (Article 115d)
- Joint Committee can act as parliament (Article 115e)
- Extended federal police powers (Article 115f)

**Safeguards**:
- Federal Constitutional Court's independence preserved (Article 115g)
- All emergency measures have time limits (Article 115k)
- Bundestag can terminate defense state at any time (Article 115l)

---

## Constitutional Amendments

**Procedure** (Article 79):
1. Requires law explicitly amending Basic Law text
2. **Majority**: 2/3 of Bundestag members + 2/3 of Bundesrat votes
3. **Eternity Clause** (Article 79(3)): Cannot amend:
   - Human dignity (Article 1)
   - Democratic principle (Article 20(1))
   - Federal principle (Article 20(1))
   - Rule of law principle (Article 20(3))
   - Social state principle (Article 20(1))
   - Division into Länder
   - Länder participation in legislation

## Judicial System

### Court Hierarchy

**Federal Constitutional Court (BVerfG)**
- Constitutional disputes
- Constitutional complaints
- Judicial review of laws
- Final interpreter of the Basic Law

**Federal Supreme Courts** (Article 95):
- Federal Court of Justice (BGH) - Civil and criminal
- Federal Administrative Court (BVerwG)
- Federal Finance Court (BFH)
- Federal Labor Court (BAG)
- Federal Social Court (BSG)

**Land Courts**:
- Constitutional courts
- Superior courts
- Regional courts
- Local courts

### Access to Justice

**Article 19(4)**: "Whoever is violated in his rights by public authority may have recourse to the courts"

- **Constitutional complaint** to Federal Constitutional Court for basic rights violations
- **Administrative courts** for disputes with administration
- **Civil courts** for private law disputes
- **Specialized courts** for specific subject matters

## Key Constitutional Principles for Problem-Solving

### 1. Human Dignity First
- Always start analysis with Article 1
- Any measure that treats a person as a mere object or means to an end is unconstitutional
- Dignity cannot be balanced against other interests

### 2. Proportionality Always
- For any restriction of rights, apply the three-step test:
  1. Suitability: Does it work?
  2. Necessity: Is it the least restrictive option?
  3. Proportionality: Do benefits outweigh costs?

### 3. Federalism Matters
- Check which level has competence
- Remember Bundesrat participation requirements
- Consider cooperative federalism mechanisms

### 4. Democracy Chain
- Trace all state action back to democratic legitimacy
- Identify breaks in the legitimacy chain
- Ensure proper authorization and accountability

### 5. Rule of Law Constraints
- Requires legal basis for all state action
- Demands clarity and predictability
- Prohibits arbitrariness
- Guarantees judicial protection

### 6. Eternity Clause Limits
- Cannot amend core constitutional principles
- Human dignity is absolutely protected
- Federal structure is unchangeable
- Democratic and rule of law principles cannot be abandoned

## Common Exam/Problem Types and Approaches

### Type 1: Fundamental Rights Violation
**Approach**:
1. Identify the affected right
2. Determine if it's absolute or restrictable
3. If restrictable, apply proportionality test
4. Check for proper legal basis
5. Consider human dignity implications

### Type 2: Federal-State Competence Dispute
**Approach**:
1. Classify the legislative/administrative matter
2. Check Articles 70-75 for competence
3. Determine if exclusive, concurrent, or residual
4. Assess Bundesrat involvement requirements
5. Consider execution provisions (Articles 83-89)

### Type 3: Legislative Procedure Question
**Approach**:
1. Identify type of law (consent vs. objection)
2. Trace procedure through Articles 76-78
3. Check Bundesrat participation
4. Assess mediation committee role
5. Verify final adoption requirements

### Type 4: Emergency Powers Analysis
**Approach**:
1. Determine if defense state criteria are met
2. Check who can declare it and with what majority
3. Identify which normal provisions are modified
4. Apply Section Xa specific rules
5. Verify eternity clause compliance

### Type 5: Financial Constitution Issue
**Approach**:
1. Identify revenue source and spending responsibility
2. Check Articles 104a-108 for distribution
3. Apply fiscal equalization principles
4. Assess borrowing limits
5. Consider EU financial constraints

## Quick Checklists

### For Any Constitutional Problem:
- [ ] Which constitutional principle is most relevant?
- [ ] Which articles apply?
- [ ] What is the factual context?
- [ ] Who are the affected parties?
- [ ] What state action is involved?
- [ ] Does it involve fundamental rights?
- [ ] Does it involve federal-state relations?
- [ ] Does it involve institutional powers?
- [ ] Does it respect the eternity clause?
- [ ] What case law exists on this issue?

### For Fundamental Rights Analysis:
- [ ] Which specific right is affected?
- [ ] Is the right absolute or restrictable?
- [ ] Is there a legal basis for the restriction?
- [ ] Does the restriction pass the proportionality test?
  - [ ] Suitability (Geeignetheit)
  - [ ] Necessity (Erforderlichkeit)
  - [ ] Proportionality in narrow sense (Angemessenheit)
- [ ] Does the restriction respect human dignity?
- [ ] Are there less restrictive alternatives?

### For Legislative Competence:
- [ ] Is this matter listed in Article 73? → Exclusive federal
- [ ] Is this matter listed in Article 74? → Concurrent
- [ ] Has the Federation legislated in this area?
- [ ] If concurrent, can Länder still legislate?
- [ ] Is this a matter for joint tasks (Articles 91a-91b)?
- [ ] What is the Bundesrat's role?

## Final Tips

1. **Always start with the text**: The Basic Law is relatively short and precise - read the relevant articles first
2. **Check the preamble**: It provides important interpretive context
3. **Remember Article 1**: Human dignity is the foundation of everything
4. **Bundesrat is key**: Many constitutional disputes involve federal-state relations
5. **Proportionality is universal**: Apply it to all rights restrictions
6. **Eternity clause is absolute**: Never propose or accept amendments to Articles 1 or 20 core principles
7. **Federalism is complex**: The Basic Law creates a carefully balanced system - respect the balance
8. **Case law matters**: The Federal Constitutional Court has developed extensive jurisprudence interpreting the Basic Law

---

**Remember**: The Basic Law is not just a set of rules, but a living constitutional order that has evolved through interpretation and practice. Understanding its patterns, principles, and mental models is essential for proper application.
