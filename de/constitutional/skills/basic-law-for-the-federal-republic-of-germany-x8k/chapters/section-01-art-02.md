# Article 2: Personal Freedom
**Right to the free development of personality**

## Core Idea
Article 2 guarantees the general right to free development of personality, which is the broadest freedom right in the Basic Law. Combined with Article 1 (human dignity), it forms the constitutional foundation for individual autonomy and self-determination.

## The Text
**Article 2**
[Personal freedoms]
(1) Everyone shall have the right to the free development of his personality in so far as he does not violate the rights of others or offend against the constitutional order or the moral code.
(2) Everyone shall have the right to life and to inviolability of his person. The freedom of the person shall be inviolable. These rights may be interfered with only pursuant to a law.

## Frameworks Introduced

### General Freedom of Action (Paragraph 1)
- **Right to Free Development**: Everyone has right to free development of personality
- **Limits**: Only limited by rights of others, constitutional order, or moral code
- **General Clause**: Acts as catch-all for freedoms not specifically enumerated

### Physical Integrity and Personal Freedom (Paragraph 2)
- **Right to Life**: Fundamental right to life
- **Inviolability of Person**: Right to physical integrity and bodily inviolability
- **Personal Freedom**: Freedom from physical coercion and detention
- **Limitation Clause**: These rights may be interfered with only pursuant to a law

## Key Concepts

### Free Development of Personality
- **Autonomy**: Right to make fundamental life choices free from state interference
- **Self-Determination**: Right to determine one's own path in life
- **Personal Identity**: Protection of individual identity and integrity
- **Scope**: Includes freedom of action, lifestyle choices, personal decisions

### Limits on General Freedom
- **Rights of Others**: Cannot violate rights of other individuals
- **Constitutional Order**: Cannot offend against democratic constitutional order
- **Moral Code**: Cannot violate basic moral principles of the community
- **Balancing**: General freedom must be balanced with other constitutional values

### Right to Life
- **Protection**: Absolute protection of human life
- **State Duty**: Positive obligation to protect life (not just negative obligation to not take life)
- **Scope**: From beginning to end of life (interpreted by Constitutional Court)
- **Limitations**: Life can be limited only in extreme cases (self-defense, state of emergency)

### Inviolability of Person
- **Physical Integrity**: Protection against bodily harm and interference
- **Personal Liberty**: Freedom from detention and coercion
- **State Monopoly**: Only state may restrict personal freedom, and only by law

## Mental Models
- Use **general freedom as default** — if not specifically regulated, action is constitutionally protected
- Think of **personality as process** — free development is ongoing, not static
- Apply **right to life as absolute** — highest protection, but with narrow exceptions
- Use **physical integrity as barrier** — State cannot interfere with person without legal basis

## Anti-patterns
- **Absolute freedom**: General freedom can be limited by rights of others and constitutional order
- **State paternalism**: State cannot dictate life choices beyond constitutional limits
- **Moral legislation**: Laws based solely on morality (not harm) may violate Article 2
- **Preventive detention**: Detention without clear legal basis violates personal freedom

## Key Takeaways
1. General freedom of action protects all autonomous behavior not specifically regulated
2. Free development of personality is core of human autonomy
3. Right to life has highest protection
4. Personal inviolability protects against physical interference
5. Limitations require clear legal basis

## Connects To
- **Article 1 (Human Dignity)**: Free development is expression of human dignity
- **Article 19 (Limitations)**: General limitation rules apply
- **Section IX (Judiciary)**: Courts protect general freedom from state interference
