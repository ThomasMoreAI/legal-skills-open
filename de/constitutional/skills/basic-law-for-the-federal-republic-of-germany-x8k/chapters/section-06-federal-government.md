# Section VI: The Federal Government
**Articles: 62-69**

## Core Idea
The Federal Government consists of the Federal Chancellor and the Federal Ministers. It is the executive branch of the Federation, determining and implementing government policy. The Chancellor principle gives the Chancellor strong leadership powers within the Government.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Composition (Article 62)
- **Federal Government**: Shall consist of the Federal Chancellor and the Federal Ministers
- **Collective Body**: The Federal Chancellor and the Federal Ministers shall together form the Federal Government

### Federal Chancellor (Articles 63-69)

#### Election (Article 63)
- **Proposal**: The Federal President shall propose a candidate for Chancellor to the Bundestag
- **Election**: The Chancellor shall be elected by the Bundestag without debate upon the proposal of the Federal President
- **Majority**: The person receiving the votes of a majority of the members of the Bundestag shall be elected
- **Second Ballot**: If the proposed candidate fails to receive the required majority, the Bundestag may elect a candidate receiving the votes of a majority of its members within fourteen days
- **Third Ballot**: If no candidate is elected in the second ballot, a candidate receiving the largest number of votes in a subsequent ballot shall be considered elected
- **Dissolution**: If no candidate is elected, the Federal President may either appoint the candidate receiving the largest number of votes or dissolve the Bundestag

#### Oath of Office (Article 64)
- **Oath**: The Federal Ministers shall be appointed and dismissed by the Federal President upon the proposal of the Federal Chancellor
- **Oath Text**: The Federal Chancellor and the Federal Ministers shall take an oath of office before the Bundestag

#### Chancellor Principle (Article 65)
- **Direction**: The Federal Chancellor shall determine and be responsible for the general policy guidelines
- **Ministerial Autonomy**: Within these limits each Federal Minister shall conduct the affairs of his department autonomously and on his own responsibility
- **Cabinet Principle**: The Federal Government shall take its decisions by a majority vote and shall conduct its affairs in accordance with the rules of procedure adopted by the Federal Government and approved by the Federal President
- **Dispute Resolution**: In case of differences of opinion between Federal Ministers, the Federal Government shall decide

#### Military Command (Article 65a)
- **Civilian Control**: In time of defense the command over the Armed Forces shall pass to the Federal Chancellor

#### Constructive Motion of No Confidence (Article 67)
- **Motion**: The Bundestag may express its lack of confidence in the Federal Chancellor only by electing a successor by the vote of a majority of its members and requesting the Federal President to dismiss the Federal Chancellor
- **Effect**: The Federal President must comply with the request and appoint the successor
- **Purpose**: Ensures government stability by requiring constructive alternative

#### Vote of Confidence (Article 68)
- **Proposal**: If a motion of the Federal Chancellor for a vote of confidence is not supported by the majority of the members of the Bundestag, the Federal President may, upon the proposal of the Chancellor, dissolve the Bundestag within twenty-one days
- **Limitation**: The Federal President may not dissolve the Bundestag if another Chancellor is elected within this period

#### Incompatibilities (Article 66)
- **Chancellor**: The Federal Chancellor may not hold any other remunerated office, nor engage in any trade or profession, nor be a member of the board or supervisory board of a company conducted for profit
- **Ministers**: The same shall apply to the Federal Ministers

#### Substitution (Article 69)
- **Chancellor Substitution**: The Federal Chancellor shall appoint a Federal Minister to act for him
- **Duration**: A Federal Minister may act for the Federal Chancellor only temporarily

## Mental Models
- Use **Chancellor principle** — Chancellor determines policy guidelines and is responsible
- Think of **constructive no confidence** — removing Chancellor requires electing successor
- Apply **ministerial autonomy** — Ministers have autonomy within Chancellor's guidelines
- Use **government stability** — Constructive no confidence prevents frequent government changes

## Anti-patterns
- **Ministerial independence**: Ministers cannot act against Chancellor's guidelines
- **Destructive no confidence**: Cannot remove Chancellor without constructive alternative
- **Chancellor dictatorship**: Chancellor must respect ministerial autonomy within guidelines
- **Government without confidence**: Chancellor needs Bundestag confidence for legitimacy

## Key Takeaways
1. Federal Government consists of Chancellor and Ministers
2. Chancellor determines policy guidelines and is responsible to Bundestag
3. Constructive motion of no confidence requires electing successor
4. Vote of confidence can trigger Bundestag dissolution
5. Chancellor principle ensures strong executive leadership
