# Section I: Fundamental Rights
**Articles: 1-19**

## Core Idea
Fundamental Rights form the first section of the Basic Law and establish the inviolable rights of the individual against state power. These rights are not granted by the State but are inherent to the person, with human dignity (Article 1) as their foundation and highest value.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Human Dignity as Foundation (Article 1)
- **Inviolability**: "The dignity of man shall be inviolable. To respect and protect it shall be the duty of all state authority."
- **Foundation**: Human dignity is the foundation of all fundamental rights and the constitutional order
- **Binding**: All state authority (legislative, executive, judicial) must respect and protect human dignity

### Rights Structure
- **Absolute Rights**: Some rights (human dignity, Art. 1; essence of rights, Art. 19.2) cannot be limited
- **Limitable Rights**: Most rights can be limited by or pursuant to a law, but limitations must respect the essence of the right
- **General Limitations Clause** (Article 19.1): Rights may be limited by law only if the law is general and does not apply to a single case

### Rights Protection
- **Direct Applicability**: Fundamental rights are directly applicable law (Article 1.3)
- **Judicial Protection**: Everyone whose rights are violated by public authority may have recourse to the courts (Article 19.4)
- **Constitutional Complaint**: Constitutional Court may be accessed for violations of fundamental rights

## Key Rights Categories

### Personal Rights
- **Human Dignity** (Art. 1): Inviolable, foundation of all rights
- **Personal Freedom** (Art. 2.2): Freedom of the person; limitations only by law with formal requirements
- **Life and Physical Integrity** (Art. 2.2): Right to life and bodily integrity

### Equality Rights
- **Equality Before Law** (Art. 3.1): All persons shall be equal before the law
- **Non-Discrimination** (Art. 3.3): No discrimination based on sex, parentage, race, language, homeland and origin, faith, religious or political opinions, or disability
- **Equal Rights for Men and Women** (Art. 3.2): Men and women shall have equal rights

### Freedom Rights
- **General Freedom of Action** (Art. 2.1): Everyone has the right to free development of personality
- **Freedom of Faith, Conscience, Religious Profession** (Art. 4): Freedom to believe or not believe, to change religion, to manifest religion
- **Freedom of Expression** (Art. 5.1): Freedom to express and disseminate opinions in speech, writing, and pictures
- **Freedom of the Press** (Art. 5.1): Freedom of the press and reporting by radio and film
- **Freedom of Art and Science** (Art. 5.3): Freedom of arts, sciences, research, and teaching
- **Freedom of Assembly** (Art. 8): Right to assemble peacefully and unarmed
- **Freedom of Association** (Art. 9): Right to form associations and societies
- **Freedom of Movement** (Art. 11): Freedom to move and settle throughout Germany
- **Freedom of Profession** (Art. 12): Freedom to choose occupation, workplace, and training

### Family and Education Rights
- **Marriage and Family** (Art. 6.1): Marriage and family under special protection of the State
- **Parents' Rights** (Art. 6.2): Care and upbringing of children is natural right and primary duty of parents
- **Mothers' Protection** (Art. 6.4): Every mother entitled to protection and care of the community
- **Education** (Art. 7): State supervision of schools; private schools permitted; religious instruction
- **Children's Rights** (Art. 6.2): State watches over care and upbringing

### Property and Economic Rights
- **Property** (Art. 14.1): Property and right of inheritance guaranteed; content and limits defined by laws
- **Social Obligation** (Art. 14.2): Property entails obligations; its use should also serve public weal
- **Expropriation** (Art. 14.3): Expropriation only for public weal, by law, with just compensation
- **Economic Initiative** (Art. 12.1): Freedom to choose profession includes right to engage in economic activity

### Procedural Rights
- **Right to be Heard** (Art. 103.1): Everyone entitled to be heard in court
- **No Punishment without Law** (Art. 103.2): Act may be punished only if it was criminal under law before committed
- **Ne Bis In Idem** (Art. 103.3): No one may be punished more than once for same act under general criminal laws
- **Right to Petition** (Art. 17): Right to address written petitions to competent authorities
- **Right to Asylum** (Art. 16a.1): Politically persecuted persons shall enjoy right of asylum

### Special Guarantees
- **Inviolability of Home** (Art. 13): Home is inviolable; searches only by judge, with formal requirements
- **Secrecy of Correspondence** (Art. 10): Privacy of postal and telecommunications secrecy
- **Citizenship** (Art. 16.1): No German may be deprived of citizenship; loss only by law and against person's will
- **No Extradition** (Art. 16.2): No German may be extradited to foreign country

## Mental Models
- Use **human dignity as interpretive key** — all constitutional interpretation must respect human dignity
- Think of **rights as limitations on state power** — fundamental rights create barriers against state interference
- Apply **proportionality test** — limitations on rights must be suitable, necessary, and proportionate
- Use **essence protection** — limitations cannot affect the essence of a right (Art. 19.2)

## Anti-patterns
- **Absolute state power**: No state action can violate human dignity (Art. 1)
- **Single-case laws**: Limitations on rights must be general laws, not individual measures (Art. 19.1)
- **Essence violation**: Limiting a right to the point of making it meaningless violates Art. 19.2
- **Discrimination**: Any discrimination based on enumerated grounds violates Art. 3.3

## Key Takeaways
1. Human dignity is inviolable and the foundation of all fundamental rights
2. Fundamental rights are inherent, not granted by the State
3. Rights bind all state authority (legislative, executive, judicial)
4. Most rights can be limited but limitations must respect their essence
5. Judicial protection is available for all rights violations

## Connects To
- **Preamble**: Fundamental rights implement constitutional values
- **Section IX (Judiciary)**: Courts protect fundamental rights
- **Section II (Federation)**: Länder must respect fundamental rights
- **All Sections**: Fundamental rights guide interpretation of entire Basic Law
