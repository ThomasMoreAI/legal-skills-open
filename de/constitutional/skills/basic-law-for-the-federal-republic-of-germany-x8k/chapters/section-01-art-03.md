# Article 3: Equality Before the Law

## Core Idea
Article 3 establishes equality as a fundamental constitutional principle, prohibiting discrimination and guaranteeing equal treatment before the law. It is a key expression of the democratic and social State principles.

## The Text
**Article 3**
[Equality before the law]
(1) All persons shall be equal before the law.
(2) Men and women shall have equal rights. The State shall promote the implementation of equal rights and work towards the elimination of existing disadvantages.
(3) No one may be prejudiced or favored because of his sex, his parentage, his race, his language, his homeland and origin, his faith or religious or political opinions. No one may be prejudiced because of his disability.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Formal Equality (Paragraph 1)
- **Equal Before Law**: All persons shall be equal before the law
- **General Principle**: Applies to all state action (legislative, executive, judicial)
- **Scope**: All persons, not just citizens

### Gender Equality (Paragraph 2)
- **Equal Rights**: Men and women shall have equal rights
- **State Obligation**: State must actively promote implementation of equal rights
- **Positive Duty**: State must work towards elimination of existing disadvantages
- **Substantive Equality**: Goes beyond formal equality to require active measures

### Non-Discrimination (Paragraph 3)
- **Protected Grounds**: Sex, parentage, race, language, homeland and origin, faith or religious or political opinions, disability
- **Negative Duty**: No one may be prejudiced (disadvantaged) because of these grounds
- **Positive Duty**: No one may be favored because of these grounds
- **Absolute Prohibition**: Discrimination based on enumerated grounds is constitutionally prohibited

## Key Concepts

### Equality as Constitutional Principle
- **Rule of Law**: Equal treatment is requirement of rule of law
- **Democratic Principle**: Equality is essential for democratic participation
- **Social State**: Equality promotes social justice
- **Universal Application**: Applies to all state action and all persons

### Types of Equality
- **Formal Equality**: Equal treatment in law (Art. 3.1)
- **Substantive Equality**: Equal opportunities in fact (Art. 3.2)
- **Non-Discrimination**: Protection against differential treatment (Art. 3.3)
- **Proportional Equality**: Equal treatment of equal cases, unequal treatment of unequal cases

### Gender Equality Measures
- **Positive Actions**: State may take measures to promote actual equality
- **Quotas**: Permissible if aimed at eliminating disadvantages
- **Obligation**: State has constitutional duty to eliminate gender disadvantages

### Protected Characteristics
- **Sex**: Biological sex (distinct from gender identity)
- **Parentage**: Family origin, birth status
- **Race**: Ethnic or racial origin (interpreted broadly)
- **Language**: Language spoken or used
- **Homeland and Origin**: Place of birth, national origin
- **Faith or Religious Opinions**: Religious beliefs or non-belief
- **Political Opinions**: Political views and affiliations
- **Disability**: Physical or mental impairments

## Mental Models
- Use **equality as default** — unequal treatment requires special justification
- Think of **discrimination as suspicion** — differential treatment based on protected grounds is presumed unconstitutional
- Apply **proportionality in equality** — unequal treatment requires legitimate aim and proportional means
- Use **substantive equality** — formal equality is not enough; State must eliminate disadvantages

## Anti-patterns
- **Formalism**: Treating formal equality as sufficient ignores substantive disadvantages
- **Neutrality fallacy**: "Neutral" laws can have discriminatory effects
- **Single-ground focus**: Discrimination often involves multiple grounds (intersectionality)
- **State inaction**: State has positive duty to eliminate disadvantages, not just refrain from discrimination

## Key Takeaways
1. All persons are equal before the law
2. Men and women have equal rights; State must promote equality
3. Discrimination based on enumerated grounds is prohibited
4. Equality requires both formal and substantive measures
5. State has positive duty to eliminate disadvantages

## Connects To
