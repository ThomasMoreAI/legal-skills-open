# Article 1: Human Dignity

## Core Idea
Human dignity is the supreme constitutional value and the foundation of all other fundamental rights. It is inviolable and must be respected and protected by all state authority.

## The Text
**Article 1**
[Human dignity – Human rights – Binding force of fundamental rights]
(1) The dignity of man shall be inviolable. To respect and protect it shall be the duty of all state authority.
(2) The German people therefore acknowledge inviolable and inalienable human rights as the basis of every community, of peace and of justice in the world.
(3) The following basic rights shall bind the legislature, the executive and the judiciary as directly applicable law.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Inviolability of Human Dignity (Paragraph 1)
- **Absolute Protection**: Human dignity shall be inviolable
- **Universal Duty**: To respect and protect it shall be the duty of **all** state authority
- **Scope**: Applies to legislature, executive, and judiciary at all levels (federal and Land)

### Foundation of Rights (Paragraph 2)
- **Human Rights Recognition**: German people acknowledge inviolable and inalienable human rights
- **Basis of Community**: Human rights are the basis of every community
- **Basis of Peace and Justice**: Human rights are foundation of peace and justice in the world

### Binding Force (Paragraph 3)
- **Direct Applicability**: Fundamental rights are directly applicable law
- **Tripartite Binding**: Bind legislature, executive, and judiciary
- **Immediate Effect**: Rights can be invoked directly before courts

## Key Concepts

### Human Dignity as Supreme Value
- **Not Grantable**: Human dignity is inherent to the person, not granted by the State
- **Not Waivable**: Cannot be surrendered or renounced
- **Absolute**: Cannot be limited or balanced against other interests
- **Universal**: Applies to all human beings, not just Germans

### State Duty to Protect
- **Positive Obligation**: State must actively protect human dignity, not just refrain from violating it
- **All State Authority**: Applies to all branches (legislative, executive, judicial) and all levels (federal, Land, local)
- **Preventive and Reactive**: Duty includes preventing violations and remedying them

### Human Dignity in Jurisprudence
- **Object Formula**: Human dignity is violated when a person is treated as a mere object or means to an end (BVerfGE 30, 1)
- **Minimum Standard**: Guarantees a minimum of human existence in dignity
- **Autonomy**: Respect for individual self-determination and moral integrity

## Mental Models
- Use **human dignity as constitutional north star** — all interpretation must be guided by human dignity
- Think of **dignity as absolute barrier** — no state action, regardless of purpose, can violate human dignity
- Apply **object formula** — person treated as object = dignity violation
- Use **minimum existence guarantee** — State must ensure conditions for dignified existence

## Anti-patterns
- **Utilitarian balancing**: Human dignity cannot be balanced against other interests
- **Majority rule**: Human dignity protects minorities and individuals against majority
- **State sovereignty**: Human dignity limits state power absolutely
- **Relative dignity**: All human beings have equal dignity; no hierarchy of human worth

## Key Takeaways
1. Human dignity is inviolable and the supreme constitutional value
2. All state authority has duty to respect and protect human dignity
3. Human dignity is the foundation of all fundamental rights
4. Human dignity is absolute and cannot be limited
5. Human dignity applies to all human beings universally

## Connects To
- **All Fundamental Rights**: Human dignity is foundation for Articles 2-19
- **Section IX (Judiciary)**: Courts must protect human dignity in all cases
- **Section II (Federation)**: Federal and Land authorities must respect human dignity
- **Section XI (Eternity Clause)**: Human dignity is explicitly protected from amendment (Art. 79.3)
