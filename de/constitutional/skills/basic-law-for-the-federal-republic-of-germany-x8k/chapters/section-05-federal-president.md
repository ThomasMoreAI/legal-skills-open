# Section V: The Federal President
**Articles: 54-61**

## Core Idea
The Federal President is the Head of State of the Federal Republic of Germany, representing the Federation domestically and internationally. The office is primarily ceremonial with limited political powers, but has important reserve functions in the political system.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Election (Article 54)
- **Electoral Body**: The Federal President shall be elected without debate by the Federal Convention
- **Composition of Federal Convention**: The Federal Convention shall consist of the members of the Bundestag and an equal number of members elected by the legislatures of the Länder
- **Eligibility**: Any German who is entitled to vote in Bundestag elections and has attained the age of forty may be elected
- **Term**: The Federal President shall hold office for a term of five years. Re-election for a consecutive term shall be permissible only once
- **Majority**: The person receiving the votes of a majority of the members of the Federal Convention shall be elected. If no candidate achieves this in two ballots, the person receiving the largest number of votes in the next ballot shall be elected
- **Oath**: The Federal President shall take an oath of office before the Bundestag and the Bundesrat

### Incompatibilities (Article 55)
- **No Other Offices**: The Federal President may not be a member of the Government or of a legislative body of the Federation or a Land
- **No Remunerated Activity**: The Federal President may not hold any other remunerated office, nor engage in any trade or profession
- **No Directorships**: The Federal President may not be a member of the board or supervisory board of a company conducted for profit

### Powers and Duties

#### Representative Powers (Article 59)
- **International Representation**: The Federal President shall represent the Federation in international relations
- **Treaties**: He shall conclude treaties with foreign states on behalf of the Federation
- **Accreditation**: He shall accredit and receive ambassadors

#### Domestic Powers
- **Promulgation** (Art. 82.1): The Federal President shall promulgate laws after countersignature
- **Appointment Powers**: The Federal President shall appoint and dismiss federal judges, federal civil servants, and officers, as provided by law (Art. 60.1)
- **Pardon** (Art. 60.2): The Federal President shall exercise the power of pardon on behalf of the Federation
- **Dissolution** (Art. 63.4): If no candidate for Chancellor receives the required majority, the Federal President may dissolve the Bundestag

#### Reserve Powers
- **Appointment of Chancellor** (Art. 63): The Federal President shall propose a candidate for Chancellor to the Bundestag. If the proposed candidate fails to be elected, the Federal President may propose another candidate or dissolve the Bundestag
- **Emergency Powers**: In case of defense (Art. 115b et seq.), the Federal President declares the state of defense and exercises certain powers

### Substitution (Article 57)
- **Substitution Order**: The President of the Bundesrat shall act for the Federal President if the latter is prevented from doing so, or if his office falls vacant before the expiry of his term
- **Order of Succession**: The order of succession shall be determined by a federal law

### Counter-Signature (Article 58)
- **Requirement**: Orders and directions of the Federal President shall require for their validity the countersignature of the Federal Chancellor or the competent Federal Minister
- **Exceptions**: This shall not apply to the appointment and dismissal of the Federal Chancellor, the dissolution of the Bundestag, or the nomination of a candidate for Chancellor

### Immunity (Article 60.4)
- **Immunity**: The Federal President may not be held legally accountable for official acts performed in the exercise of his office
- **Scope**: Does not apply to acts performed outside official duties

### Impeachment (Article 61)
- **Grounds**: The Bundestag or the Bundesrat may impeach the Federal President before the Federal Constitutional Court for willful violation of the Basic Law or any other federal law
- **Procedure**: Requires a two-thirds majority in the Bundestag or the Bundesrat
- **Consequences**: The Federal Constitutional Court may declare the President guilty and remove him from office

## Mental Models
- Use **President as constitutional symbol** — represents the Federation with limited political power
- Think of **reserve powers** — President has important functions in constitutional crises
- Apply **ceremonial primacy** — President's role is primarily representative
- Use **counter-signature as check** — prevents presidential overreach

## Anti-patterns
- **Political interference**: President must remain above party politics
- **Unilateral action**: Most presidential acts require counter-signature
- **Absolute immunity**: Immunity applies only to official acts
- **Partisan election**: President is elected by Federal Convention, not directly by people

## Key Takeaways
1. Federal President is Head of State, elected by Federal Convention for 5-year term
2. President represents Federation domestically and internationally
3. Most presidential acts require counter-signature by Chancellor or Minister
4. President has reserve powers in Government formation and constitutional crises
5. President can be impeached by Bundestag or Bundesrat for willful constitutional violations
